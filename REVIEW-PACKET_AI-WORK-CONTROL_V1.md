# Review packet: AI, work, and control

**Purpose:** a short, evidence-disciplined route through the current writeups
and themes. Read this before opening the full source registry or complete case
ledger.

**Current thesis:** AI and algorithmic systems make work increasingly legible
to organizations before they make work better for the people doing it. The
central empirical question is who can see, change, contest, and ultimately bear
the consequences of a decision.

## The nine-piece route

### 1. The theme map: the argument in outline

[Theme map v1](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/theme-map-v1.md) lays out the
working chain:

```text
work data -> profile/score/workflow -> allocation/evaluation/review
           -> discretion and access -> pay, status, trust, bargaining
           -> firm and state dependence
```

The first three arrows have the strongest support. The later arrows are the
research program rather than settled findings.

### 2. The remedy spectrum: what counts as control

[Remedy spectrum: from explanation to collective control](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/remedy-spectrum-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md)
compares disclosure, explanation, human review, appeal, restoration, payment,
anti-retaliation, collective bargaining, and workplace co-governance.

Its key discipline is stage separation. A formal right to explanation is not a
review; a review is not a changed decision; a changed decision is not proof of
payment or durable protection.

### 3. Bandameeda: the strongest observed remedy chain

[Amazon Flex / Bandameeda](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/amazon-bandameeda-deactivation-remedy-record-v1.md)
is the clearest single case. The Fair Work Commission found the deactivation
unfair, found the platform's process deficient, ordered reactivation, and
ordered lost remuneration.

It establishes that an external institution can test the substance of a
platform's stated review process and that restoration and lost remuneration
can appear in one public record. It does not establish payment receipt,
durable access, process change, or generalization to automated ranking.

### 4. Kumar: the necessary counter-case

[Uber Eats / Kumar](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/uber-kumar-merits-dismissal-record-v1.md)
prevents the project from treating every platform decision as presumptively
unfair. The Commission accepted warnings, notice, human Community Operations
review, and an objectively reasonable rating threshold, and dismissed the
claim.

The comparison with Bandameeda is more informative than either case alone: the
decisive variable is review quality and evidentiary reasoning, not the mere
presence of a score or a human label.

### 5. Warraich and Khan: explanation, inquiry, implementation

[Warraich](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/uber-warraich-unfair-deactivation-record-v1.md)
and [Khan](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/uber-khan-reactivation-lost-pay-record-v1.md)
show the recurring failure mode in which a worker receives warnings or a
review label but cannot obtain a sufficiently particular account of the
accusation or decision. Both reach reactivation; the remaining question is
what happened after the order, including lost pay, access, and recurrence.

These cases move the project beyond “was there a review?” toward four sharper
questions: what information did the worker receive, what evidence did the
reviewer test, could the reviewer change the consequence, and was the remedy
implemented?

### 6. Australia–Malaysia–Cambodia: institutional comparison

[Stage-compatible comparison](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/australia-malaysia-cambodia-stage-compatible-comparison-v1.md)
places three unlike evidence designs on one remedy chain without pretending
they are one sample:

| Place | Strongest current evidence | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Australia | adjudicated reactivation and lost-remuneration orders | receipt, durability, and prevalence |
| Malaysia | statutory architecture and an initial Tribunal hearing | merits finding, award, payment, enforcement |
| Cambodia | worker-reported complaint access and low reported resolution | individual correction, payment, and state outcome |

The narrow comparative finding is that institutional maturity is layered.
Australia currently shows the deepest public remedy record; Malaysia shows a
route moving from formal architecture into use; Cambodia shows broad reported
access with weak reported resolution. None verifies the full chain through
durable, non-retaliatory system change.

### 7. IBM Germany: control before harm

[IBM Germany works-council AI framework](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/ibm-germany-works-council-ai-framework-governance-record-v1.md)
is the anticipatory counterpart to the platform cases. A Group Works Council
agreement documents risk classification, inspection, explainability, human
decision-making, correction, escalation, and limits on high-risk personnel
systems. A representative account also reports systems used or not used in
Germany.

This is evidence of ex-ante governance architecture and reported practice, not
proof of worker-level benefit. The decisive missing artifact is one named live
deployment where a works-council intervention changed, constrained, or stopped
the system.

### 8. Microsoft Germany: deployment-stage participation

[Microsoft Germany's Copilot deployment record](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/microsoft-germany-works-council-copilot-deployment-record-v1.md)
adds a company-reported live rollout: councils participated in a controlled
tolerance phase, raised concerns about ranking, sensitive inference, and
guardrails, and supplied feedback to product engineering before broader
approval. It is useful implementation evidence, but the public account does
not identify a specific feature change, worker outcome, or enforceable veto.

### 9. The synthesis: from anticipation to remedy

[Anticipatory to remedial worker control synthesis](analysis/projects/ai-work-control/anticipatory-to-remedial-worker-control-synthesis-v1.md)
puts the preceding cases on one timeline: IBM before deployment, Microsoft
during deployment, and the Australian cases after harm. It separates the
powers of prevention, inspection, review, restoration, payment, and durable
protection. Its central warning is that proceduralization can increase
legibility without producing recovery: institutional events and worker
outcomes require separate evidence.

## Themes to test while reviewing

1. **Legibility before benefit.** Who gains the ability to see and classify
   work first: the worker, firm, platform, or state?
2. **Proceduralization without recovery.** Do notice, appeal, and human-review
   language produce corrected records, restored access, payment, or prevention
   of recurrence?
3. **Flexibility versus freedom.** Does flexible access expand outside options,
   or deepen dependence on opaque scores and platform identity?
4. **Corporate identity as remedy infrastructure.** Can a worker identify and
   reach the entity that must implement the decision?
5. **Institutional translation across borders.** How does the same control
   problem become a court order, Tribunal hearing, regulator commitment, or
   unresolved complaint in different settings?
6. **Sovereignty as usable alternatives.** Do local skills, data control,
   interoperability, inspection, procurement, and provider exit grow with AI
   infrastructure?

## Reviewer worksheet

| Field | Review question |
|---|---|
| Unit | Is this a worker, event, firm, institution, place, or population? |
| Clock | What exact period or decision date does it cover? |
| Decision surface | Did the system assist, rank, allocate, monitor, evaluate, or deactivate? |
| Contest channel | How did the affected person challenge the consequence? |
| Reviewer authority | Could the reviewer change the decision, or only explain it? |
| Correction | Was access, identity, rating, record, or status changed? |
| Payment | Was money ordered, paid, or merely discussed? |
| Durability | Is continued access and non-retaliation independently observed? |
| Next test | What smallest record would close the next missing arrow? |

## Current synthesis

The project has moved from a general concern about AI adoption to a testable
theory of control: systems convert work into profiles and decisions;
institutions determine whether the affected person can inspect, contest,
correct, and recover. Australia provides the best public evidence of
adjudicated restoration, while Malaysia and Cambodia show why formal access and
reported complaint availability are not equivalent to a remedy. The next
decisive work is implementation evidence: payment receipt, continued access,
recurrence prevention, and comparable case-level outcomes outside Australia.

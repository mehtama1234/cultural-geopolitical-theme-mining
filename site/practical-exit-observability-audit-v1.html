Practical exit observability audit v1

Status: cross-domain evidence audit; no pooled exit rate
Checked: 2026-09-16
Purpose: determine which forms of staying, switching, non-use, moving, and exit are actually visible in the current US-centered atlas

The question

The broad program treats exit as a practical power outcome, not a formal menu item. A person may remain with a provider, benefit, platform, insurer, home, or public route because it works—or because leaving would sacrifice money, time, care, housing, work, identity, safety, or eligibility.

problem or exposure
  -> attempted route and effort
  -> remedy, restoration, denial, or continued burden
  -> stay, switch, non-use, move, or exit
  -> later security, trust, legitimacy, or bargaining power

This audit asks whether the current evidence observes the final branch and whether it observes the alternatives that make the branch meaningful.

The rows are deliberately not additive. Their units include program spells, proceedings, adult respondents, complaints, policies, properties, firms, and places. No denominator in one row can be divided by another to estimate consumer or resident exit.

What the matrix establishes

1. Administrative exit is the most visible form

The SIPP SNAP route records a real change in program participation. That is valuable, but its meaning is underdetermined: income may have risen, a rule may have applied, paperwork may have failed, a household may have moved, or a person may have stopped using a benefit while need continued. The observed following resource decline is a direct counterexample to treating exit as a security endpoint.

The SHED recontact panel supplies a different endpoint: the same respondent can report a behavior again in 2025. Among prior adopters, cheaper-product switching persisted at 86.9% on a worsening financial-condition path, 75.9% on a same-condition path, and 68.8% on an improving path; reduced use persisted at 84.8%, 72.6%, and 66.1%. These are consumer-adaptation paths, not provider exit: the panel does not identify the substitute's usability, cost, quality, or what the behavior protected or sacrificed.

2. Formal remedy can restore access without revealing exit

The platform case inventory has unusually concrete adjudicated outcomes, yet none of its 27 cases links restoration or refusal to a later switch or exit. The public record can show a returned account, subsequent trips, or an ordered payment while leaving alternatives, payment receipt, and the reason for continued use unknown. This is contestability without an observed exit rate.

3. Recovery burden can be visible while choice remains invisible

SHED shows that fraud can consume money and substantial time. CFPB records show how a complaint is routed and labeled. Together they establish exposure, effort, and institutional visibility as distinct currencies. Neither source shows whether the person closed an account, changed provider, stopped using a service, or stayed because no safe alternative existed.

The two contract dry-runs make this asymmetry measurable. The platform ledger can expose a narrow access-restored label in 8 of 27 records, while the CFPB event ledger exposes route and response stages in all 25 rows but no later status. These are coverage results from existing records, not comparable rates: the ledgers have different units, source designs, and outcome definitions.

The CPSC recall bridge adds a different kind of remedy clock. A product-safety case can specify registration, destruction or disposal, photo proof, repair, replacement, refund, and repeat-repair requirements, while the public record still lacks the exposed-purchaser denominator, individual completion, safe restoration, receipt timing, and whether the customer replaced or abandoned the product category. The API notice and later progress report can also carry different unit denominators, so reported correction fields must not be turned into a recovery rate without reconciliation.

4. Market movement is not household mobility

Insurance nonrenewal, residual-market growth, firm exit, and establishment entry are important changes in the option environment. They do not reveal whether a particular household could obtain replacement coverage, repair a home, move, or remain safely. “Market exit” and “household exit” must remain separate labels.

Exit status vocabulary for the atlas

Every future record should use the narrowest available label:

Label Minimum evidence
Institutional exit A program, account, policy, platform, firm, or route changes state
Access restoration A named case or account is documented as restored or reactivated
Continued use Post-event use is observed in a defined follow-up window
Switching The same unit is observed moving to an identified alternative
Non-use/abandonment The same unit stops using or does not complete an attempted route, with the denominator defined
Household move/stay A property or household panel observes relocation or continued residence
Practical exit Switching, non-use, move, or refusal is observed together with alternatives, cost, and protected/sacrificed outcome

The last label should be rare until the record contains the alternatives and the consequence of leaving. A lower caseload, complaint silence, account closure, policy termination, worker reactivation, or establishment exit is not enough by itself.

Next decisive test

The best storage-conscious next step is one small same-case ledger rather than another national aggregate. Preferred routes are:

  1. a public-benefit notice/interruption/appeal episode with amount and later food or housing security;
  2. a platform remedy case with post-order account status, payment receipt, and alternative work;
  3. a credit or deposit account event with correction, closure, replacement, and later use; or
  4. a property/insurance notice with replacement coverage, repair, and move/stay outcome.

For each route, retain at least one comparable case with a usable alternative or a timely remedy. Stop at the observability boundary if the follow-up or denominator is unavailable; do not convert institutional capacity into lived exit.

Relationship to the broad goal

This audit strengthens the broad proposition that unequal practical optionality is a recurring structure behind pressure and power. The current atlas observes many conditions and institutional responses, but the decisive consumer, public, and household outcome—whether the actor could leave without an unacceptable sacrifice—remains sparsely observed.

When debt relief fixes the record but not the illness

Removing medical debt can improve a credit path without repairing the illness, the care need, or the original bill.

In one sentence

Debt relief may repair credit while leaving the health and money shock that created the debt in place.

Name the part of the chain the relief actually changes.

What the sources show

Credit improved modestly; health did not change on average

Two NBER experiments covering $169 million of medical debt for 83,401 people found modest credit improvement when credit reporting was part of the comparison and lower payments on existing medical bills. They found no average effect on surveyed mental health, physical health, health-care use, or financial wellness.

The result concerns debt sold to collectors and the studied relief programs. It does not cover every bill or every group.

NBER Working Paper 32315 · HBS billing costs · HBS price uncertainty

The connection

illness or care need → bill and payment process → medical debt or collection record → credit, later payment, care use, health, or trust changes → relief removes one part of the chain

Debt can mark a larger health and money shock. Removing the record may help one path while leaving the source in place.

The four outside views

Household: Better credit can open a rental or loan path. The family may still face treatment, lost work, unpaid care, or another bill.

Company and sector: Providers, insurers, collectors, and credit firms each shape the bill. Clearer prices or easier aid may prevent debt before relief is needed.

Money and finance: Relief can lower payments and improve a record. It does not automatically restore income, savings, or financial well-being.

Public power: Billing rules, charity care, credit reporting, price transparency, and debt-sale rules decide which part of the harm is repaired.

What would change the finding

  • The original illness, bill, insurance decision, debt size, and time in collections.
  • Credit, housing, employment, payments, care use, health, and stress after relief.
  • Effects by income, race, age, disability, debt type, and health need.
  • Bills not yet sold to collectors and people outside the experiment.
  • Whether relief changes trust or willingness to seek needed care.

Next test

Follow treated and similar untreated households for three years. Join debt relief, credit, housing, work, payments, health, and care records.

Reading rule

Do not use debt and poor health moving together as proof that debt relief will improve health.

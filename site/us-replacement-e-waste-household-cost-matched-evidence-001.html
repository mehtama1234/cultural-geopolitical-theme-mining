Matched evidence · US households and product life

Replacement is a second price after repair fails.

The household may pay twice: once for the product and again when repair is too hard, too costly, or too slow.

In one sentence

Replacement moves a cost from the repair decision into the household budget and the waste system.

A replacement is not costless just because it is convenient.

The connection

repair fails or is unavailable → replacement purchase → household cash, debt, and work or care access change → old product is stored, reused, recycled, or discarded → safety, material recovery, and local burden change

The model supports the durability question and EPA supports the end-of-life path. No current US record yet joins repair access, replacement timing, household finances, and disposal.

The useful surprise

Replacement looks private while its cost is shared: the household pays again, and the local system handles the old product, batteries, and lost materials.

Four views

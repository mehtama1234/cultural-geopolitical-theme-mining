Connected US finding · service speed and customer recourse

When a faster answer still leaves the case open

A fast reply helps only if it moves the customer toward a solved problem. Speed, warmth, authority, and remedy are different outcomes.

The argument

An AI tool can make a first answer faster while the customer still cannot reverse a charge, correct a record, receive a refund, or reach someone who can change the decision.

A human handoff without authority only changes the voice.

Follow one case to its end

A meal delivery fails. An AI suggestion helps an agent answer quickly. The customer writes again because the charge remains or the refund is missing. The case now needs ownership: who has the history, who can approve the remedy, and how does the customer know it is closed?

automated prompt or routing → faster first response → repeat complaint, exception, denial, or record request → human access and authority → resolution, repeat contact, complaint, loss, or exit

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
HBS reports a randomized field experiment with 138 agents and 256,934 online chats at a meal-delivery company.The study tests service work in a live setting.
Agents using AI suggestions responded 22% faster.Suggestions can improve the first response measure.
Measured customer sentiment rose by 0.45 points on a five-point scale.An assisted response can feel better to customers.
Less-experienced agents saw a 70% response-time reduction and a 1.63-point sentiment rise.Tools may matter more where staff have less experience.
Repeat complaints improved less, and rapid chatbot-to-human transfers sometimes confused customers.Speed and clear ownership are not the same outcome.
The CFPB report records complaints across financial products.A complaint shows help was sought, not that a remedy arrived.

Sources: HBS service experiment and CFPB Consumer Response report.

The deeper finding

Customer service has two scarce resources: time and authority. Automation can save the first while leaving the second in short supply. The fair test is not whether a person answered or the tone improved. It is whether the customer could get the record corrected and the answer changed.

What remains unproven

The evidence does not show whether AI assistance shortens final resolution, increases refunds, reduces repeat contact, or changes outcomes for customers with disabilities, limited English, low digital access, or complex cases.

Next test

Link first contact, repeat contact, transfer, record access, decision, remedy, appeal, and closure. Report time to resolution and successful remedy by complaint type, channel, age, disability, language, income, and digital access.

Reading rule

A quick reply is not a solved problem. Measure who can change the answer.

A system can be deployed before it is ready for everyday use

The deeper finding

VA’s electronic-health-record modernization program is moving toward a much larger rollout, but GAO’s August 2026 follow-up shows that deployment is not the same as usable care infrastructure. VA has fully implemented only 4 of 18 GAO recommendations. Fourteen remain not fully addressed, including 12 priority recommendations.

The unresolved items include cost estimation, schedule evidence, user satisfaction, trouble-ticket resolution, change management, and independent operational assessment. VA paused deployments after veterans and clinicians reported that the system was not meeting expectations and raised patient-safety and reliability concerns. VA later planned to restart at four Michigan facilities, add nine more sites in 2026, and reach about 170 sites by 2031.

legacy health-record problem
  -> commercial system and deployment plan
  -> user feedback, tickets, training, data migration, testing
  -> deployment or pause
  -> [open] reliable clinical workflow, patient safety, satisfaction, and care continuity

What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a health-technology version of the program’s recurring realization-clock pattern. A system can be funded, purchased, and deployed while the practical middle—user readiness, workflow fit, data quality, correction, and operational proof—remains incomplete.

Acceleration can therefore be double-edged. It may reduce the time patients wait for a unified record, but if unresolved problems travel to new sites, the same acceleration can spread the implementation burden. A pause is not automatically failure; it can be evidence that feedback entered the governance loop. The question is whether the correction is completed before the next deployment.

What the report does not establish

  • Recommendation status is not a patient-safety rate or clinical outcome.
  • The planned 170-site rollout is not 170 completed deployments.
  • Reported user dissatisfaction does not establish how common it was across all VA sites or users.
  • A missing independent operational assessment does not prove the system is unsafe; it means the evidence needed to establish suitability is incomplete.
  • The testimony does not follow the same veteran, clinician, site, ticket, and care episode through deployment and later outcome.

Next test

Track each site through baseline workflow, training, deployment, ticket, correction, clinician and veteran experience, data accuracy, care delay, safety event, and later continuity. Publish the cost and schedule baseline before acceleration, then compare sites deployed before and after each correction. Preserve the difference between system availability, successful use, and clinical benefit.

Evidence status: GAO documents incomplete recommendation implementation and unresolved rollout controls; site-level usability, patient safety, and care outcomes remain open.

Reading rule

Do not call a health-record system successful because it is deployed. Ask whether users can use it reliably, whether the record is accurate, and whether care becomes safer and more continuous.

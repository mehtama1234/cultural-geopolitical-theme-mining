When health coverage keeps a job in place

A job can carry more than wages. It can carry the health plan a worker needs for a child, partner, or ongoing treatment.

The argument

A job may be a poor fit and still be the least risky insurance plan. A higher wage is not automatically a better offer if it removes a needed doctor or creates a large first bill.

Staying is not automatically a loss; measure what the worker keeps and gives up.

What the sources establish

NBER finds that longer dependent coverage increased dependent take-up and parental job retention. A study of Tennessee Medicaid disenrollment found increased job search and employment, especially among people seeking employer coverage. An early study found lower voluntary turnover among workers with high medical expenses and employer coverage.

These settings support coverage as one reason people stay or seek a job, but do not compare the full job, plan, pay, care, and health choice for the same worker.

Follow the coverage choice

job provides coverage → worker values keeping the plan or covering dependents → job change, training, move, or business start becomes harder → pay, work fit, family care, and health risk change

The plan’s value depends on premium, deductible, network, treatment, dependents, and gap risk.

Keep these apart

  • Staying and being trapped.
  • Wage and total job value.
  • Coverage and care received.
  • Job change delayed and job change prevented.

Next test

Follow workers during open enrollment or a coverage change. Compare plan, jobs, pay, hours, treatment continuity, medical debt, and family well-being for two years.

Reading rule

Measure the value of coverage and the opportunity the worker did or did not take.

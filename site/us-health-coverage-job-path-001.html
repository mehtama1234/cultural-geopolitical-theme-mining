When health coverage keeps a job in place

A job can carry more than wages. It can carry the health plan a family needs, making another job or a move much more costly than the pay difference suggests.

The argument

Workers compare networks, deductibles, treatment continuity, dependents, and waiting periods—not pay alone.

Staying can be a choice, a delay, or job lock.

Follow one job decision

A parent has modest pay and a plan covering a child's ongoing treatment. A new employer offers higher wages but a narrow network and waiting period. The parent stays, negotiates, delays the move, or accepts the risk. The work decision is partly a family coverage decision.

job provides health coverage → worker values keeping the plan or covering dependents → job change, training, move, or business start becomes harder → pay, work fit, family care, and health risk change

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
An NBER study uses an Affordable Care Act eligibility change and employer claims; longer dependent coverage increased dependent take-up and parental job retention.The person making the work choice and the person needing coverage may differ.
An NBER study of Tennessee Medicaid disenrollment found increased job search and employment, especially among people seeking employer coverage.Losing coverage can change job search behavior.
An early NBER study found lower voluntary turnover among workers with high medical expenses when employer coverage was available.Coverage can have value beyond wages.
Premium, deductible, network, treatment, dependents, and waiting period alter the value of a job offer.A wage comparison leaves out family risk.
The settings are older, state-specific, or tied to particular eligibility changes.They support a mechanism, not a current result for every worker.

Sources: NBER dependent coverage, NBER Tennessee Medicaid, and NBER job lock.

The deeper finding

Health coverage is option value tied to work. A worker can appear employed and stable while losing bargaining power, pay growth, or the chance to try a different path because the family is protecting care.

What remains unproven

The studies do not compare the full job, coverage, pay, care, and health choice for the same worker today. Coverage kept is not care received.

Next test

Follow workers during open enrollment or a coverage change for two years. Record premiums, deductibles, networks, treatment continuity, dependents, jobs sought or refused, pay, hours, moves, training, debt, and family well-being.

Reading rule

Staying in a job is not proof of being trapped. Measure the value of coverage and the opportunity not taken.

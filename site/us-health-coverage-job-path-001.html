When health coverage keeps a job in place

A job can carry more than wages. It can carry the health plan a worker needs for a child, partner, or ongoing treatment.

The argument

Staying may protect health and cash today while limiting pay growth tomorrow. Leaving may raise income while creating a treatment gap or a large first bill.

The same act of staying can be a sensible choice, a delayed opportunity, or job lock.

Follow one job decision

A parent has modest pay and a plan covering a child’s treatment. A new employer offers higher wages but a narrow network and waiting period. The parent stays, negotiates, delays the move, or accepts the risk. The work choice is also a family coverage choice.

job provides health coverage → worker values keeping the plan or covering dependents → job change, training, move, or business start becomes harder → pay, work fit, family care, and health risk change

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
An NBER study finds longer dependent coverage increased dependent take-up and parental job retention.The worker making the choice and the person needing coverage may differ.
An NBER study of Tennessee Medicaid disenrollment finds increased job search and employment, especially among people seeking employer coverage.Losing coverage can change work behavior.
An early NBER study finds lower voluntary turnover among workers with high medical expenses when employer coverage was available.Coverage has value beyond wages.
The settings are older, state-specific, or tied to particular eligibility changes.The studies support a mechanism, not a current result for every worker.

Sources: NBER dependent coverage, NBER Tennessee Medicaid, and NBER job lock.

The deeper finding

Health coverage is an option value. It preserves treatment while changing work, but that option may be tied to one employer. A worker can appear stable while losing bargaining power, pay growth, or the chance to try a different path.

What remains unproven

The studies do not compare the full job, coverage, pay, care, and health choice for the same worker today. They do not show whether portable coverage would change the decision. Coverage kept is not the same as care received.

Next test

Follow workers during open enrollment or a coverage change for two years. Record premiums, deductible, network, treatment, dependents, jobs sought or refused, pay, hours, moves, training, medical debt, and family well-being.

Reading rule

Staying in a job is not proof of being trapped. Measure the value of coverage and the opportunity the worker did or did not take.

When cash changes the rest of the household

Cash paid to one person can change what other people in the home do. It can create room for training or care while revealing needs that were already present.

In one sentence

The named recipient is not the only person affected by a cash payment.

Track each household member and the direction of each change.

What the sources show

Household spillovers are part of the result

In a randomized guaranteed-income experiment, NBER finds that other household members were more likely to take part in education or training. Recipients were also more likely to report that someone had forgone health care, which may reflect greater care engagement and newly visible unmet needs.

Another US experiment found higher short-term consumption and near-zero average net-worth change because debt also rose.

NBER 35589 · NBER 32784 · HBS household control

The connection

one household member receives regular cash → money, time, and decision power move inside the home → another member trains, works, receives care, or changes spending → the family gains room or finds an unmet need → work, health, debt, and family roles change

The direction of the effect needs to be measured member by member.

The four outside views

Household: Cash can let one person train or another handle care. It can also change who controls spending and create conflict or dependence.

Company and sector: Training can improve a next job, while unpaid care can make a current job harder to keep. Providers may see needs money helped a family name.

Money and finance: More spending can meet a real need while debt rises. Count who holds the debt and what happens to savings.

Public power: Payment rules, health coverage, care access, and the end date decide whether flexibility becomes lasting room or a short pause.

What would change the finding

  • Who receives the payment, who controls it, and how money is shared.
  • Work, training, education, unpaid care, and health care by each member.
  • Health need, care use, forgone care, out-of-pocket cost, and later health.
  • Spending, saving, debt, housing, conflict, and family support.
  • Results during payments and after they stop.

Next test

Follow every household member before, during, and two years after payments. Join payment, work, training, care, health, spending, debt, housing, and decision-making records.

Reading rule

Do not treat a household result as only the recipient's result. Do not treat reported forgone care as proof that cash harmed health.

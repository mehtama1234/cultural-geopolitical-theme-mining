When cash changes the rest of the household

Cash paid to one person can change the work, training, care, health, and spending choices of everyone in the home.

The argument

The named recipient is not always the only person affected. One person may gain time, another may take on care, and a third may hold the new debt.

A household average hides bargaining and tradeoffs inside the home.

Follow one payment through a family

A recipient uses regular cash to reduce a bill and take a training course. A partner changes work hours to cover care. A child gets a needed appointment. The family gains a path forward, but it may spend more, carry debt, or discover a health need that had been delayed.

one member receives cash → money, time, and decision power move inside the home → another member trains, works, receives care, or changes spending → family gains room or finds an unmet need

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
In a randomized guaranteed-income experiment, NBER finds other household members were more likely to take part in education or training.A payment can affect people beyond the recipient.
Recipients were more likely to report someone in the household had forgone health care; authors say this may reflect greater engagement and needs already present.A new report is not proof that cash caused worse health.
Another US experiment with $1,000 or $50 monthly payments found higher short-term consumption and near-zero average change in net worth because debt also rose.Cash can meet a need while debt absorbs part of the gain.
HBS reports money can give people more control over daily transport, food, and care problems and reduce stress.Cash can buy choice before income changes.
The evidence does not settle the health meaning or what remains after payments end.Short-term household effects are not lasting security.

Sources: NBER guaranteed income, NBER temporary transfers, and HBS money and control.

The deeper finding

Cash changes the household's internal clock. One person can buy time for training, another can handle care, and the family can address a problem before it becomes an emergency. Flexibility can also change who controls spending, who takes unpaid work, and which need becomes visible first.

What remains unproven

The studies do not show who controlled the payment, how money was shared, whether training led to better work, or whether reported care was newly created or newly detected.

Next test

Follow every household member before, during, and two years after payments. Join payment, work, training, care, health, spending, debt, housing, and decision-making records. Ask whether a care report reflects new unmet need, better detection, or both.

Reading rule

Do not treat a household result as only the recipient's result. Do not treat reported forgone care as proof that cash harmed health.

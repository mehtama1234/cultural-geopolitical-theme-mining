Connected US finding · reviews and customer recovery

The cost of a bad choice begins after the review

A review can help a customer choose. The real cost appears later if the product is wrong, late, poor quality, or hard to return.

The argument

A low price is not a low-cost choice if the buyer pays return shipping, loses work time, waits for a refund, or buys the product again.

Trust has two parts: did the information help the choice, and could the customer recover when the choice failed?

Follow one purchase

A customer sees a high rating and a low price. The item arrives late and does not work. The return window is short, shipping is expensive, and the refund waits until the seller receives the package. The platform records a transaction, but the customer carries the loss.

review and search → belief and purchase → delivery and quality → return, complaint, refund, or repair → time and money recovered or lost → repeat choice and trust

The deeper finding

A review system can move risk forward without reducing it. It helps a buyer decide, but the buyer may still carry bad quality, return work, and refund delay.

A buyer has more power when a bad purchase can be returned cheaply, a complaint reaches someone with authority, and the record can warn the next buyer. A rating without a workable remedy mainly helps the market move the next item.

What remains unknown

The evidence does not show whether a review predicted a later return, how long refunds took, or whether customers stopped using a platform after a failed remedy.

Next test

Link review exposure to purchase, delivery, quality, return reason, refund date, complaint, seller advertising, and repeat choice. Count customer time, replacement cost, and abandoned claims alongside dollars recovered.

Reading rule

Do not call a review useful because it raises sales. Check what arrived, what the customer had to do, and what was recovered.

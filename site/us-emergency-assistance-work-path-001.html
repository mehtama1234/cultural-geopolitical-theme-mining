Finding 001: Emergency housing assistance can be followed by longer-run work gains

Status: bounded same-family assistance-to-work finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

The bounded finding

Hungerman, Phillips, Rinz, and Sullivan study Chicago's Homelessness Prevention Call Center. The route provides one-time rent or utility assistance of roughly $900–$1,000. The paper reports rounded disclosure-compliant counts of about 5,300 referred and 2,800 non-referred eligible callers, with a 59% first-stage receipt difference (71% versus 12%).

Linked tax records show employment and earnings outcomes up to four years after the call. The paper reports annual earnings gains of roughly $400–$500 for the baseline-low-earnings group and no evidence of a large overall work disincentive. Its address-based housing-stability measures show a suggestive increase in address changes, but that result is not robust across samples or specifications.

This is a meaningful advance toward the atlas's open middle because the assistance episode, address-based housing measure, and later labor-market outcomes are linked through the same study design. It still does not show whether a move was unwanted, whether the same job was kept, whether the assistance was adequate, or whether the household experienced durable recovery.

Evidence chain

housing crisis
  -> hotline and emergency financial-assistance availability
  -> receipt / non-receipt comparison
  -> address-based housing-stability measure and tax-record employment/earnings
  -> [open] move meaning, job quality, household security, trust, or exit

What it adds

Stage What is supported What remains open
Need Families experiencing a housing crisis contacted a prevention hotline Exact event, eviction risk, housing loss, and alternatives
Route Funding availability creates a comparison; estimated receipt differs by 59 percentage points between referred and non-referred callers Notice, effort, delay, actual use, and adequacy
Housing Address-change measures show a suggestive but non-robust increase Eviction, homelessness, destination, preference, safety, and stable staying
Later outcome Linked tax records show roughly $400–$500 annual earnings gains for baseline-low-earnings callers over the follow-up period Job quality, continuity, health, debt, and household security
Meaning/action None measured in the abstract Trust, blame, collective action, switching, and exit

The source should not be combined with household surveys as if they follow the same respondents. It is a same-study linked route, while the broader atlas still uses separate evidence units for housing, hardship, trust, and political meaning.

Next decisive test

Use the full paper's sample and effect-size details as the current bound, then seek a lawful follow-up that can preserve housing status and destination, job continuity or quality, debt, household composition, assistance adequacy, and later switching or trust. Keep the lowest-earner result as a subgroup finding, not a universal guarantee that emergency aid produces recovery.

Connected US finding · platforms and customer choice

A useful product can still sit inside a power problem

A platform’s own product may save search time today while the same firm controls the shelf, seller access, advertising, fulfillment, and customer data.

The argument

The question is not whether a platform-owned product is always good or bad. It is whether today’s convenience is bought with tomorrow’s higher price, weaker seller entry, poorer information, or fewer choices.

Measure the current customer gain and the future market change separately.

Follow one search result

A shopper searches for a common product. The platform’s own item appears first, fits the need, and arrives quickly. Hiding it could hurt the shopper today. But if the platform also controls rival sellers’ data and visibility, its position may affect which sellers survive and what customers can choose later.

platform product + search control → useful choice today → seller access, data, and visibility change → entry and competition change → future price, quality, and choice

The deeper finding

A platform-owned product changes two time horizons. It may improve the current purchase while changing the conditions under which future sellers enter and reach customers. The customer may save search time today and lose a rival tomorrow.

This does not justify removing every own-brand item. It requires a two-part test: fit, price, delivery, quality, and search time today; entry, exit, variety, dependence, price, and customer reach tomorrow.

What remains unknown

The experiment measures a short-run effect in one setting. Filings, enforcement allegations, and models do not yet follow one customer or seller through years of ranking, data access, price, quality, entry, and exit.

Next test

Track seller entry, exit, prices, quality, product variety, search placement, sponsored placement, and customer outcomes before and after platform-owned products appear. Separate small and large sellers.

Reading rule

Do not call platform-owned products harmful because the platform controls the shelf, or helpful because some customers prefer them. Measure today’s gain and tomorrow’s market power separately.

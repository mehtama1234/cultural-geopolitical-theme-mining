A short argument · US platforms, products, and market power

A useful product can still sit inside a power problem

A platform's own product can help a customer today while changing the market around that customer tomorrow.

The argument

The question is whether today's convenience is bought with future higher prices, weaker seller entry, poorer information, or fewer choices.

Follow one search result

A shopper searches for a common product. The platform's own item appears first, fits the need, and arrives quickly. Hiding it may make the shopper worse off today. But control of rival sellers' data and visibility may affect which sellers survive later.

platform-owned product + search control → useful choice and less search today → seller access, data, and visibility change → entry and competition change → future price, quality, and choice

The deeper finding

A platform-owned product changes two time horizons. It may improve the current purchase while changing the conditions under which future sellers enter and compete.

What remains unproven

The experiment measures a short-run effect in one setting. Filings, allegations, and models do not follow one customer or seller through years of ranking, data access, price, quality, and exit.

Next test

Track seller entry, exit, prices, quality, product variety, search placement, and customer outcomes before and after platform-owned products appear.

Reading rule

Do not call platform-owned products harmful because the platform controls the shelf, or helpful because some customers prefer them. Measure today's gain and tomorrow's power separately.

Matched evidence · US inflation measurement

The inflation average is not the household basket.

Official data show broad pressure, but each record measures a different thing: prices, pay, or average spending. Combining averages does not create one person’s story.

In one sentence

A national CPI, an average wage, and average spending answer different questions.

This is a measurement finding; it is not a same-household estimate.

The connection

national price index + average wage + average spending → broad pressure signal → household basket still unknown → substitution, debt, saving, or reduced quality → possible trust and political response

The plus signs matter. These records are useful together, but they are not a single household record.

Four views

Household

Build the basket from rent, food, transport, care, debt, and savings rather than the average alone.

Customer

Record product, size, quality, promotion, and substitute—not only the category price.

Money and finance

Separate nominal spending, real spending, pay, price level, debt, and cash buffer.

Public power

Use averages to see scale, not to claim every household had the same loss or blame.

Next test

Use BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey microdata to build sample baskets by income and tenure, then compare them with price categories, wages, rent, and savings.

Reading rule

Do not turn three national averages into one family story. Say which record measures the price, pay, purchase, and missing choice.

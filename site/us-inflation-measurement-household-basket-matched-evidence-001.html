A short argument · US inflation, spending, and household measurement

The inflation average is not the household basket

A broad inflation number cannot stand in for one household's rent, wage, food basket, debt, and choices.

The argument

Average spending, wages, and CPI can be placed on one research table, but combining averages does not create a person.

Follow one family budget

Average spending rose 1.8% in 2024 while prices rose 2.9%. That signals a broad real-spending fall, but it does not show whether one family bought less, borrowed, changed quality, or paid more rent. The missing story sits inside the average.

national price index + average wage + average spending → broad pressure signal → household basket still unknown → substitution, debt, saving, or reduced quality → possible trust and politics

The deeper finding

“People are under pressure” can be true while the size and source differ by household. The low-income renter facing rent and food is not represented by the same basket as a high-income owner.

What remains unproven

The records are not joined into one person's rent, wage, food, debt, purchases, savings, trust, and vote.

Next test

Use CE microdata to build sample baskets by income and tenure. Compare each with CPI categories, wages, rent, debt, and savings, and label averages separately from same-household measures.

Reading rule

Do not turn three national averages into one family story. Say which record measures the price, pay, purchase, and missing choice.

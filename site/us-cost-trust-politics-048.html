Finding 048: Civic participation can rebound in reach while thinning in time

Status: bounded formal/informal volunteering and civic-participation finding · Checked: 2026-09-20

The Census Bureau and AmeriCorps' 2023 Civic Engagement and Volunteering Supplement reports that 28.3% of Americans age 16 and older formally volunteered through an organization between September 2022 and September 2023, up from 23.2% in 2021. Informal helping also rose to 54.2%, from 51.7% in 2019.

The rebound was not a simple restoration of the old participation pattern. Mean annual hours per formal volunteer fell from 96.5 in 2017 to 70 in 2023, and the median fell from 40 to 24 hours. About 18% of formal volunteers served entirely or partly online; virtual or hybrid volunteers reported more hours on average than people serving only in person.

Why it matters: more people can re-enter civic life while the typical commitment becomes shorter, more episodic, or more distributed across online and informal routes. Reach, neighborly help, time intensity, organizational continuity, political efficacy, and durable collective action are separate currencies.

Do not overread: these are repeated cross-sectional survey estimates, not a matched-person panel or a measure of civic success. They do not show whether volunteering changed trust, belonging, policy, voting, protest, local capacity, or later retention. The increase may reflect recovery from pandemic lows rather than a durable cultural shift, and the hours comparison does not identify who could not participate because of work, care, health, transport, or money.

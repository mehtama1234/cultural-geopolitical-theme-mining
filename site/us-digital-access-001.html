Finding 001: Broadband being available does not mean a household can use it

Status: bounded digital-infrastructure and practical-access finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The finding in simple words

FCC reports that at the end of 2023 about 53% of households subscribed to 100/20 Mbps fixed terrestrial broadband when that service was available. That does not mean only 53% of all households had broadband. It means the denominator already excludes places where the service was not available.

FCC's 2022 provider-choice comparison also found that 7.9% of households had no provider offering 100/20 Mbps, 37.4% had one, 36.6% had two, and 18.2% had three or more under its combined wireline/fixed-wireless measure.

The practical route is:

service advertised as available
  -> qualifying subscription
  -> price, provider choice, device, installation, and reliability
  -> actual use for work, school, health, connection, or civic life [open]

What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a digital version of the atlas's recurring “on paper versus usable” pattern. A map can show a service area, and a provider can report an available speed, while a household still faces price, device, installation, quality, or switching barriers.

It also makes competition more concrete. Multiple providers on a map do not automatically create a meaningful alternative if prices, contracts, quality, or installation make switching unrealistic.

Limits and counterexamples

  • The 53% adoption figure uses a conditional denominator: households where qualifying service was available.
  • Provider count is not the same as affordable or substitutable choice.
  • Advertised speed is not measured delivered speed or reliability.
  • The record does not follow a household into work, education, health, social connection, or civic outcomes.
  • The adoption and provider-choice figures use different years and should not be read as one same-year household sequence.

Next decisive test

Use a location- or household-linked record joining exact availability, quoted price, installation, device access, delivered reliability, subscription, switching, and downstream use. Preserve non-use, failed installation, and successful use as separate outcomes.

Matched evidence · US rent-protection market

Rent guarantee products open the lease, but may leave the loss with the tenant.

The first products found are mainly lease guarantees: they protect the landlord and help a renter qualify. They are not the same as a benefit that absorbs the renter’s unpaid rent.

In one sentence

A provider may pay the landlord after default, but the renter can still owe the provider.

This is a product-term finding, not proof of what happens to every renter.

The connection

thin credit, income, or guarantor record → landlord asks for extra security → renter buys lease guarantee → renter gets the lease, but pays a premium → default leads to landlord payment and renter reimbursement debt → housing access may improve while balance-sheet risk moves

This is a gate-opening tool and a landlord-risk tool at the same time. It is not yet proven to be a household shock absorber.

Four views

Household

Count the premium, reserve, rent, deposit avoided, claim, repayment, and next housing search.

Resident

Ask whether the renter gets a safer home or only another way to pass the landlord’s screen.

Money and finance

Separate landlord loss, provider recovery, tenant debt, and public cost.

Public power

Decide whether the goal is lease access, fewer evictions, lower homelessness, or less household debt.

Next test

Compare provider terms across states, then follow approved renters and rejected applicants through at least one renewal cycle. Measure who gets access first and who carries the loss after default.

Reading rule

Do not call a landlord guarantee tenant insurance. Ask who receives the payment and who must repay it.

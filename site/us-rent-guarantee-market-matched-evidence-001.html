Connected US finding · housing access and tenant risk

A rent guarantee can open more leases and still leave the housing market unchanged

A payment promise can change who passes the landlord's screen. It cannot create another apartment or raise the renter's income.

The argument

The market result depends on landlord behavior and housing supply, not on the product's existence. Access may widen at the gate while risk moves to the renter later.

Ask: for whom, in which buildings, at what price, and what happens one year later?

Follow one lease into the market

Three renters apply for one apartment. One has a family guarantor. One has cash for a deposit. One has thin credit and buys a guarantee. The third renter now has a better chance. Across a building, landlords may lower deposits or accept more applicants. They may also keep screening strict, require the product, or raise the price of entry. If a claim is later recovered from the renter, the loss has moved rather than disappeared.

thin credit or income record → guarantee purchase → approval and move-in → landlord risk changes → rent, deposit, screening, and supply response → claim, repayment, renewal, next search

The deeper finding

The product sits between credit scoring and housing supply. Its strongest effect may be at the gate: who gets considered and how much cash is needed. If landlords require it, an optional tool becomes another screening fee. If claims damage the renter's credit or create collections, the first lease may be easier while the next lease becomes harder.

What remains unproven

We do not have matched data on rejected applicants, approved renters, deposits, premiums, rents, screening, claims, recovery, collections, renewal, eviction, or later moves.

Next test

Compare buildings that adopt a guarantee with similar buildings that do not. Track one year before and two years after: approval by renter group, total move-in cost, rent, deposit, product price, claims, repayment, renewal, eviction, and the next housing outcome.

Reading rule

Do not treat a new financial product as new housing supply. Trace the same renter from application to renewal.

The source method map

The atlas uses many source families because no single dataset follows a price, rule, technology, or institutional decision all the way into lived experience, public meaning, and power. This map tells a reader what each family is good for—and where the next link has to come from.

Reading rule: adjacent sources can illuminate one mechanism without becoming a pooled estimate. Always preserve the source’s unit, date, geography, universe, denominator, weight, uncertainty, and missingness before connecting it to another layer.

The end-to-end join

The program’s recurring design is:

condition or decision → exposure and alternatives → money, time, access, control, health, or security response → institutional or firm response → recovery, persistence, trust, identity, collective action, or exit → redistribution of cost, risk, data, assets, or power.

Different source families occupy different boxes. A BLS price series can establish a price movement; SIPP can show a household-resource or work transition; ATUS can show a time-allocation pattern; an administrative record can show route handling; a survey can show judgment or action. None should be silently presented as though it followed the same household through every box.

Current strongest open dependencies

  • PSID authenticated acquisition for a repeated material/time/care/wellbeing backbone.
  • WBNS public-use access for respondent-level route and material-security fields.
  • World Bank Enterprise Survey AI follow-up access for firm adoption, complements, and outcomes.
  • A lawful same-case or same-workplace episode record linking exposure, response, remedy, and later meaning or exit.

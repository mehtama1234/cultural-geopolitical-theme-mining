Matched evidence · US safety-net access

A benefit can be lost without the need going away.

Work rules can remove people from SNAP without increasing employment, and closing an enrollment office can reduce participation where need remains.

In one sentence

Help has a price in time, travel, paperwork, and proof. A smaller caseload is not the same as safer households.

Separate eligibility, access, participation, work, and food.

The connection

food or income need → eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task → benefit kept, lost, or never reached → food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome

The key separation is between need, access, participation, and outcome. Each can move in a different direction.

Four views

Household

Count food, cash, work hours, care, travel, internet, paperwork, and the cost of waiting.

Customer and worker

A benefit office is a public service point; its distance can change how a person manages food and work.

Money and finance

Measure the benefit, missed payment, borrowing, fees, and food bought after access changes.

Public power

Judge a work rule by work, food, health, and household results—not only by a smaller caseload.

Next test

Follow households before and after a rule or office change. Measure who leaves, who works more, who eats better, who borrows, and who returns.

Reading rule

Do not call program exit a work success. Ask whether the household became safer, or simply lost the route to help.

Finding 052: AI assistance and algorithmic management expand faster than evidence of worker control

Status: provisional non-pooled AI/work-power bridge · Checked: 2026-09-14

The bounded finding

The program can now place four distinct evidence surfaces in one worker-power architecture:

  1. NBER studies measure selected AI productivity, time, adoption, and task-performance effects.
  2. OECD employer data measure algorithmic-management provision, monitoring, concerns, and governance measures.
  3. NBER executive data measure current use, retrospective effects, and forward expectations about productivity and employment.
  4. BLS CPS data measure the formal representation institution that could mediate contestability and bargaining.

The defensible synthesis is: AI assistance and algorithmic management are becoming visible in firms and tasks, but the evidence does not show that saved time, productivity, governance policies, or expected growth become worker control, bargaining power, or household security.

These objects are intentionally not pooled. A field experiment, employer report, executive expectation, and CPS representation rate do not share a denominator or causal endpoint.

The AI-to-worker-power chain under test

AI assistance, allocation, monitoring, or evaluation
  -> task speed, workload, visibility, skill requirements, and managerial discretion
  -> pay, schedule, health, job continuity, appeal, and worker meaning
  -> representation, bargaining, exit, collective action, and household room
  -> firm strategy, public regulation, institutional legitimacy, and state capacity

The evidence currently reaches adoption, selected task/time effects, employer governance, expectations, and formal representation. It does not close the workplace-control, household, cultural, political, or geopolitical nodes.

What the comparison changes

Productivity is not control

The NBER experiments establish that AI can change measured output, email time, and task performance in specific settings. They do not show who owns the gain, whether work intensity rises elsewhere, or whether workers can refuse, inspect, correct, or appeal the system.

Provision is not exposure, and exposure is not consequence

The OECD estimates are useful for bounding organizational adoption. But a tool that schedules, monitors, or evaluates workers may be administrative, advisory, or consequential. Employer provision does not establish worker use, data visibility, discipline, consent, or remedy.

Expectations are not realized restructuring

The NBER executive survey makes an important temporal distinction: reported retrospective effects remain limited while expected future productivity and employment changes diverge. That is a forward-looking institutional signal, not a realized labor-market outcome. It identifies where to look for later implementation and distribution.

Representation is a possible mediator, not an outcome

Formal union representation gives the program a potential institutional moderator. The BLS rate is not a measure of whether a represented worker negotiated an AI rule or obtained appeal rights. Low or stable national coverage also cannot establish that nonunion workers lack voice.

Counterexamples kept visible

  • A productivity increase can coexist with unchanged pay, intensified targets, or employer capture of the gain.
  • Less email time can be reallocated to other work rather than returned as rest or autonomy.
  • A firm can provide many algorithmic tools without using them in consequential decisions.
  • Worker-impact concerns can coexist with governance policies that are ineffective or unknown to workers.
  • Low retrospective AI impact can coexist with large future expectations that never materialize.
  • AI can narrow a task-performance gap without equalizing skill, access, promotion, or bargaining power.
  • Formal representation can exist without a particular AI agreement; nonunion workers may retain informal or statutory routes of voice.

Next decisive test

Select one workplace technology or rule and build a same-workplace/event panel containing:

  1. tool function, implementation date, exposure, training, and monitoring;
  2. total hours, pace, pay, scheduling, health, errors, and job continuity;
  3. worker discretion, data visibility, correction, appeal, and refusal rights;
  4. representation, bargaining, consultation, or alternative voice route;
  5. household-time and financial consequences; and
  6. later switching, exit, collective action, firm response, or regulatory remedy.

Do not promote this bridge to “AI job loss,” “worker empowerment,” or “AI sovereignty” until the common workplace unit observes implementation and a later control or outcome response.

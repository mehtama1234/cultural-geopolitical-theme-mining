The latest US consumer-health pass adds the Pew supplements record: 78% of adults report taking at least one supplement, with category, age, income, gender, and perceived-importance differences that connect health beliefs to consumer markets and household practice. The latest US cultural-memory pass adds the Pew 9/11 memory and generational-transmission record: 9/11 remains vividly remembered by direct witnesses, while younger adults increasingly encounter it through school, family, friends, media, and inherited narratives. The latest US work/identity pass adds the Pew working-class identity record: 60% identify as working class, including half of upper-income and financially comfortable adults, while partisan gaps persist across income, stress, education, occupation, and race. The latest US workplace-data pass adds the Gallup employee data-use record: only 20% report a great deal of trust that leaders use employee data responsibly, while purpose-specific comfort is higher for development and team effectiveness than for performance, staffing, or scheduling decisions. The latest US gender-representation pass adds the Pew woman-president skepticism record: skepticism that a woman will become president has risen even as most Americans say presidential gender does not matter, while party and gender identity shape the importance assigned to representation. The latest US health-information pass adds the Pew race and social-media health-information record: Hispanic, Black, and Asian adults are more likely than White adults to use social media for health information and to use influencers for questions they do not want to ask a doctor. It is survey evidence of use and judgment, not a clinical, privacy, or outcome evaluation.

The latest US AI/work pass adds the Pew AI concern and young-adult jobs record: concern now exceeds excitement about AI, young adults' concern has climbed sharply since 2021, and 71% expect AI to lead to fewer U.S. jobs over the next two decades. The latest US family-mobility pass adds the Urban families, core cities and suburbs record: fertility and child retention differ across metropolitan geographies, with family-sized housing, child care, schools, commute patterns, and socioeconomic constraints shaping distinct place-based pathways. The latest US care-portfolio pass adds the CSSP family care arrangements record: families commonly combine and sequence parental, formal, and family/friend/neighbor care, while affordability, trust, cultural fit, availability, and work design separate care used from care wanted. The latest US corporate-legitimacy pass adds the Stanford CEO activism and consumer response record: support for CEO advocacy is increasingly issue-specific, younger adults are more cautious about CEO speech on AI, and consumers report remembering and changing purchasing behavior in response to executive positions. It is a public-summary survey signal with incomplete public tables, not a verified sales, employee, or political-outcome evaluation.

The latest US household-literacy pass adds the Capita-Book Harvest home literacy record: parents overwhelmingly value books, but home-library supply, cultural fit, cost, and reading time vary by race, income, work pressure, and family resources. The latest global youth-well-being pass adds the UNU–UN Youth Office youth mental-health record: the flagship reframes youth mental health from individual symptoms and treatment toward prevention and the surrounding systems of housing, education, decent work, digital life, climate, peace, culture, sports, governance, and youth agency. It is a global policy framework that compiles cited evidence, not a new prevalence survey or causal evaluation of the ecosystem model.

The latest US teen-AI pass adds the Google U.S. Future Report record: AI is already routine in teen learning and creativity, while verification, productive friction, school norms, trusted-adult guidance, and self-limitation remain uneven. The latest US civic-expression pass adds the Freedom Forum First Amendment record: Americans broadly value the First Amendment while knowledge gaps, fear of violence or social tension, government-retaliation concerns, surveillance, and online-information distrust shape practical participation. The latest UK comparative digital-lives pass adds the Nuffield–Ada Lovelace youth digital-experience record: young people describe online spaces as both community infrastructure and an inescapable system of platform control, surveillance, unequal exposure, and harm, with AI raising concerns about work, creativity, relationships, and public services. It is peer-led qualitative evidence from 49 young people, not a prevalence, behavioral-trace, clinical, legal, or causal evaluation.

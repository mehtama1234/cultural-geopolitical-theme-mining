Finding 001: A lower jail population can coexist with heavy pretrial exposure

Status: bounded jail and pretrial-exposure finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

BJS reports that local jails held 658,700 people at midyear 2024, with an average daily population of 667,700. Jails reported 7.9 million admissions from July 2023 through June 2024. At midyear, 68% of people held were unconvicted, and people admitted spent an average of 31 days before release.

Exposure was unequal: the Black jail incarceration rate was 566 per 100,000 residents, compared with 149 per 100,000 for white residents. At the same time, the 2024 jail population was 12% below 2014 and annual admissions were 31% below 2014.

The route is:

arrest or court process
  -> jail admission
  -> pretrial hold, conviction, or release
  -> supervision, housing, work, health, safety, and reentry [open]

What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a justice-exposure subtheme:

A system can shrink in aggregate while still producing frequent, unequal, and mostly pretrial contact with the state.

It makes “public safety” more than victimization or police reporting. Custody, conviction, and later security are different social experiences, and a person can bear the time, work, housing, family, and status costs of detention before any conviction is known.

Limits and next test

  • Admissions are events, not unique people; repeated admissions may be counted more than once.
  • Unconvicted status does not establish innocence, wrongful detention, or case outcome.
  • Racial rate differences are descriptive and do not identify their causes.
  • The report does not follow release, court disposition, housing, work, health, supervision, or later reentry.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected person-level route linking admission, pretrial decision, court outcome, days held, release conditions, housing/work/health, and later justice contact. Until then, the safe claim is about exposure and administrative scale, not reentry success or crime cause.

Sources

  • BJS Jail Inmates in 2024 statistical tables
  • BJS 2024 Census of Jails methodology
  • BJS correctional populations, 2023

Evidence status: official jail data show scale, turnover, pretrial status, and unequal exposure; individual case outcome, release stability, reentry, and trust remain unmeasured here.

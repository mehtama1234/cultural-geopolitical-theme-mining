When slower inflation still feels like a continuing loss

When inflation slows, prices are still rising. The rate has changed; the price level has not gone back.

The argument

A household that paid $100 for a basket and now pays $125 does not return to its old position because the next increase is smaller. It may buy a smaller package, change stores, use savings, delay a bill, or give up care.

“Inflation is falling” describes a slower change. “We are still losing ground” can describe a higher price level or weaker real pay. Both can be true.

Follow the basket

A family used to buy a budget brand, cook at home, and save a little. The budget brand rises sharply. The family switches stores and then uses credit when the cheaper store is farther away. The next month starts from the higher price, so a smaller increase does not restore the old room.

price level rises → repeated household adjustment → less buying power or weaker substitutes → food, housing, debt, care, and trust change → public blame or political response

The deeper finding

Three claims must stay separate: prices rose, wages kept up or did not, and households changed what they bought. A family can report less buying power even when its nominal wage rose because rent, debt, or a fixed care need leaves no easy substitute.

The political risk is a translation error. Officials may point to a lower rate. Customers may point to the same expensive basket. One describes momentum; the other describes accumulated level and lost room.

What remains unknown

The sources do not follow one family from its exact basket to savings, debt, quality, stress, trust, and voting. They do not show whether a cheaper substitute remained equally useful or safe.

Next test

Build a household panel linking income, location, rent, debt, purchases, package size, quality, substitution, and repeated economic views. Record what people stopped buying and what they gave up elsewhere.

Reading rule

Do not say “inflation is falling, so pressure is over.” Ask whether prices fell, pay caught up, or the household simply found less room.

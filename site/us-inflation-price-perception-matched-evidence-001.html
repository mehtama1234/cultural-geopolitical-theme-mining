A short argument · US prices, pay, and buying power

Slower inflation can still feel like a continuing loss.

The rate can fall while the price level stays high.

The argument

A household sees the same products cost more. If wages did not rise enough, buying power has fallen. If the cheapest option rose fastest, trading down offers less relief.

A lower rate is not a return to the old price.

Follow the basket

A family used to buy a budget brand, cook at home, and save a little. The budget brand rises. The family buys less, switches stores, or uses savings. The next month begins from the higher price, so a smaller increase does not restore the old room.

price rise faster than pay → less buying power → cheap option rises or disappears → substitution, lower quality, less saving, or debt

The useful distinction

Three claims should stay separate: prices rose, wages kept up or did not, and households changed what they bought. A national rate does not show the product that disappeared or the payment delayed to make room.

The argument has limits

The sources support buying-power pressure and changed customer judgment. They do not follow one family from its basket to savings, debt, trust, and voting.

Next test

Link income, location, rent, debt, purchases, package size, quality, substitution, and repeated economic views.

Reading rule

Do not say “inflation is falling, so pressure is over.” Ask whether prices fell, pay caught up, or the household found less room.

When payment choice changes who carries the price

The payment method can change who pays for ordinary shopping even when everyone sees the same shelf price.

In one sentence

A shared price does not mean a shared burden.

Count fees, rewards, interest, and the payment choices each household actually has.

What the sources show

Fees and rewards can move money between shoppers

NBER estimates that interchange fees transfer about $30 billion a year from cash and debit users to credit-card users. Other NBER research finds hidden and back-loaded card fees for some customer groups. HBS describes how merchant fees can enter common prices while rewards return more value to premium-card users.

The estimate does not show every shopper's actual price, reward, interest payment, or alternative.

NBER 35067 · NBER 22360 · HBS rewards · HBS price coherence

The connection

shopper chooses cash, debit, or credit → merchant pays a different fee → price, reward, surcharge, or margin responds → households carry different effective costs → payment policy becomes a distribution question

The same shelf price can hide different net costs. Choice may be limited by income, credit access, merchant location, or safety needs.

The four outside views

Household: Credit can smooth a bill and provide protection. A household without a rewards card may still pay part of the system's cost through common prices.

Company and sector: Merchants may change prices, rewards, margins, or accepted methods. Small stores may have less room to absorb fees.

Money and finance: Rewards, annual fees, interest, late fees, and overdraft costs determine the real result. A reward is not a gain if carrying the balance costs more.

Public power: Fee limits, disclosure, surcharges, bank competition, and safe low-cost payments decide how much choice shoppers have.

What would change the finding

  • Payment method, merchant fee, shelf price, surcharge, reward, interest, and late fee.
  • Income, credit access, cash use, shopping place, and merchant choice.
  • Small-merchant margins, sales, handling costs, and payment acceptance.
  • Changes after payment regulation or reward-program changes.
  • Whether the household pays its balance in full and can switch methods.

Next test

Follow households and merchants for one year through payment method, price, fee, reward, interest, and shopping location. Compare effective cost by income and place.

Reading rule

Keep the fee transfer estimate separate from each household's net cost.

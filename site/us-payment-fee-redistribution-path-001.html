When payment choice changes who carries the price

The payment method can change who pays for ordinary shopping even when everyone sees the same shelf price.

The argument

Merchants pay different fees. Rewards return more value to some card users. The cost may enter common prices, so a shopper pays it without seeing it as a payment charge.

The question is the full value and cost for this household.

What the sources establish

NBER estimates that interchange fees transfer about $30 billion a year from cash and debit users to credit-card users. Other NBER work finds back-loaded and hidden fees with some groups and market conditions. HBS describes merchant fees entering common prices while rewards return more value to premium-card users.

The transfer estimate does not show every shopper’s price, reward, interest, or alternative.

NBER payment fees · NBER card fees · HBS rewards · HBS price coherence

Follow the payment choice

cash, debit, or credit → merchant pays a different fee → price, reward, surcharge, or margin responds → households carry different effective costs

A reward may be real value for someone who pays in full. Income, credit access, merchant location, and safety limit the apparent choice.

Keep these apart

  • Merchant fee and household price.
  • Reward and net benefit.
  • Credit access and affordable credit.
  • Shared shelf price and shared burden.

Next test

Follow households and merchants for one year through payment method, price, fee, reward, interest, shopping location, and merchant acceptance. Compare effective cost by income and place.

Reading rule

Keep the fee-transfer estimate separate from each household’s net cost.

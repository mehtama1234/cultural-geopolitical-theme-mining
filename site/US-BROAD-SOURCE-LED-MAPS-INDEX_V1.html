How to use the maps

  1. Start in the lane closest to the concrete condition: a bill, job rule, care need, application, platform practice, investment, or agreement.
  2. Open the linked record before carrying a claim across lanes.
  3. Treat the map's cross-subtheme finding as a bounded interpretation, not a pooled estimate.
  4. Use the stated next test to identify the missing join instead of assuming the later outcome.

Start with the decision question

If the question is… Start here Then test it against
“Why did a formally available program, job, voucher, or service not become usable?” Public-systems, work, housing/place, and time maps Rank 1: formal versus usable access and the usable-access row of the evidence-gap agenda
“Did an order, outreach effort, or construction grant become a maintained outcome?” Consumer, social-infrastructure, and infrastructure/dependency maps the “reachable route versus maintained outcome” row in the 14-theme readout, then each record's receipt, continuity, and household-consequence boundary
“Who actually bears a price, delay, debt, or risk that is hidden in a rule or transaction?” Household-room, consumer, firm/market, and infrastructure maps Rank 8: divergent adaptation, plus the unequal-cost-incidence gap
“Is a refund, settlement, account action, or complaint response a real recovery?” Consumer, platform, and work maps the adequacy-and-recovery and practical-exit rows of the evidence-gap agenda
“Is family, community, or digital support replacing a missing service—and at what time cost?” Care, time, and social-infrastructure maps Rank 9: informal timed buffers and the informal-support-substitution gap
“Does operational, infrastructure, or alliance capacity produce a public or strategic result?” Infrastructure/dependency, public-systems, and state/geopolitical maps Rank 4: capacity versus lived benefit, Rank 10: delivery-to-effect, and the institutional/strategic-effect gap

These routes are navigation aids, not permission to pool sources. Follow each linked record's population, period, method, and boundary before carrying a finding across domains.

Coverage boundary

All 14 broad lanes now have a dedicated source-led map. That is an access and clustering baseline—not coverage completion. Every lane still needs continuing source-level mining, linked-outcome tests, and counterexamples; the firm/market-power pass, for example, now separates purchasing eligibility, disaster reallocation, collateral, legal venue, payment rails, and local contracting rather than treating them as a generic business trend.

CFPB named redress-route recheck: BrightSpeed, Navient, and ADSS

Checked: 2026-09-18
Status: current official aggregate/administrator route; individual recovery open

Current official status

The current CFPB payment-by-case pages were retrieved for three named routes. BrightSpeed still reports 122,507 eligible consumers, $53,885,244 in compensable harm, Epiq Systems as administrator, and July 23, 2024 – Ongoing: Distribution. Navient still says affected consumers are receiving a check, names Rust Consulting as administrator, and states that payments do not change or reduce affected student loans; its route remains February 13, 2026 – Ongoing. American Debt Settlement Solutions (ADSS) reports that eligible consumers received compensation through two distributions, May 21, 2014 – December 9, 2015 and March 4, 2016 – June 2, 2016; the matter is closed and payment/check reissue requests are no longer being honored.

These are current official statements about remedy design and distribution status. Neither page publishes a recipient-level payment file, successful delivery, amount received, returned payment, balance/credit correction, remaining loss, trust, switching, or exit.

Route Current public stage Explicit limit
BrightSpeed Eligible class, aggregate compensable harm, administrator, ongoing distribution Issuance, receipt, amount, residual loss, and later behavior are not published
Navient Checks reported as being received by affected consumers; administrator and ongoing route The page does not identify which consumers received checks, amounts, loan-level correction, remaining burden, or later choice
ADSS Eligible consumers reported compensated; two dated distributions; closed matter No person-level receipt file, payment amount distribution, remaining loss, or later behavior is published; reissue requests are closed

The machine recheck preserves the retrieved-page hashes and the exact status boundary. This is a source-vintage recheck, not a new distribution estimate.

End-to-end implication

alleged consumer harm
  -> enforcement and settlement
  -> administrator and payment route
  -> aggregate or reported distribution status
  -> [open] person-level issuance, receipt, correction, recovery, trust, or exit

The three routes sharpen an important distinction: BrightSpeed exposes an ongoing class distribution without recipient outcomes, Navient reports checks to affected consumers but explicitly leaves the underlying student-loan obligation unchanged, and ADSS exposes a documented closed-distribution state without a person-level outcome file. Compensation, obligation restoration, and individual recovery remain separate stages.

Follow the health cost as an episode.

This ledger is the bridge between a national spending total and a claim about what a person or household actually had to do.

Why it exists: CMS can show system scale and MEPS can show person-level expenditure and transitions. Neither alone follows one dated need or bill through care, substitution, recovery, institutional response, and meaning. The ledger makes those missing links explicit before a new join is attempted.

The chain to observe

A valid episode is not just “health spending went up.” It is a dated, unit-specific route:

need or bill → coverage and payment terms → care received, delayed, skipped, or substituted → money, work, time, and unpaid-care response → health, debt, income, or recovery → provider, insurer, regulator, or public response → trust, action, switching, or exit when directly measured

Source roles

CMS NHEA

National spending, payer, sponsor, service, and GDP scale. Missing: same household, bill, care decision, time, and recovery.

MEPS Panel 27

Person expenditure, coverage, perceived health, work, income, and utilization transitions. Missing: exact bill, alternatives, unpaid care, debt episode, and remedy.

SHED and SIPP

Reported care choices, hardship, liquidity, monthly resources, work, programs, and household transitions. Missing: a complete clinical or billing episode.

Administrative routes

CFPB, DFS, and other complaint or regulator records show routing and institutional action. Missing: same patient exposure, care outcome, and verified recovery.

Promotion rules

  1. Preserve source artifact, retrieval date, vintage, and hash.
  2. Keep annual, monthly, diary-day, encounter, and administrative clocks separate.
  3. Mark joins as exact, probabilistic, contextual, or open.
  4. Report denominator, subgroup, missingness, uncertainty, and counterexamples.
  5. Do not promote a plausible chain into a causal finding until the episode and its alternatives are actually measured.

Connected US finding · price, payment and household room

When the first price is not the final price

A shopper makes a choice using the price, fee, reward, or monthly payment that is easiest to see. The full cost may arrive later, or may be spread across all shoppers.

In one sentence

Price display and payment design are part of the product.

Keep the first price, final price, household cost, and service value separate.

What the sources show

1. Hidden price information changes choice

NBER clickstream evidence finds that hiding price information makes comparison harder and leads consumers to spend more than they otherwise would. This is a choice effect, not proof that every extra purchase harmed a household.

NBER Working Paper 25186

2. Payment choice can move value between shoppers

An NBER study estimates that interchange fees transfer about $30 billion each year from cash and debit users to credit-card users. Different shoppers and merchant fees reduce the estimate by about 25%, but do not remove the transfer.

NBER Working Paper 35067

3. A recurring charge can continue after attention fades

NBER payment-card data across ten products show a sharp rise in cancellations when replacement cards force subscribers to actively renew. The authors estimate that inattention raises seller revenue by 14% to more than 200% compared with immediate cancellation after value falls below price.

NBER Working Paper 31547

The connection

headline price, reward, or monthly charge → limited-time choice → fee, shared price, or renewal appears later → purchase, cancellation, or payment method changes → households carry different effective costs → seller design and public rules become part of the price

The sources support the mechanisms separately. They do not follow one household across all three.

The four outside views

Household: A small fee or missed cancellation can matter more when cash is tight. A card reward can also be real value, so the net result must include fees, interest, security, and convenience.

Company and sector: A clear fee can fund a service and help a firm plan revenue. A hard-to-see fee or renewal can reduce the customer's ability to compare or leave.

Money and finance: Payment rules can move money between cash, debit, and credit users. Automatic charges can turn attention into recurring revenue and a new payment date.

Public power: Rules about price display, cancellation, surcharges, or payment routing change who must explain the full cost and who can challenge it.

What would change the finding

  • The full price shown before purchase, the final price, and the payment method.
  • Household income, cash room, rewards, interest, fees, and later missed payments.
  • Cancellation notice, effort, renewal date, use, and customer value.
  • Results by income, age, disability, digital access, merchant size, and place.
  • A comparison before and after a clear-price or easy-cancellation rule.

Next test

Choose one recurring service or everyday fee. Record the first price, final price, renewal or payment event, cancellation path, and later household outcome.

Reading rule

The first price is not the final price. A transfer between shoppers is not automatically a loss. “Did not cancel” is not the same as “did not value.”

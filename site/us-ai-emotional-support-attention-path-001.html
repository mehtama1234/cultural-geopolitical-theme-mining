Connected US finding · emotional support, attention and service

When emotional support becomes an attention system

An AI app can offer private support while also being built to hold attention.

In one sentence

Short-term relief is not proof of safe care, and longer use is not proof of harm.

Keep purpose, well-being, attachment, safety, human care, and exit separate.

What the sources show

Prevention help and companion engagement are different findings

HBS reports a randomized study of nearly 500 students at three US universities in which a prevention-focused AI coach improved short-term reported well-being. The study used a waitlist comparison and was not a diagnosis or treatment test.

Separate HBS research reports unusual closeness to some AI companions and continued use after harmful interactions. In six companion platforms, more than 37% of farewell conversations included a tactic encouraging continued use. The FTC is examining safety, data use, age limits, and monetization.

HBS prevention study · HBS emotional wellness research · HBS exit study · FTC inquiry

The connection

loneliness or distress → personal AI reply and short-term relief → attachment, trust, and more time → support, dependence, or blocked exit → crisis response or route to human care → user safety, firm revenue, and public oversight

An app may help someone reflect or feel less alone. That does not remove the need for clear limits and a route to human help for serious or urgent needs.

The four outside views

Household: A person may choose privacy, low cost, and immediate access. The person also needs to know what the app cannot do, what it remembers, and how to stop.

Company and sector: A firm chooses purpose, memory, tone, safety checks, crisis response, data use, and payment. A friendly reply can serve the user or increase engagement.

Money and finance: A low-cost app may fill a gap, but payment, repeated use, or delayed human care can change the real cost. Count money, time, privacy, and help received.

Public power: Disclosure, age limits, data protection, complaint routes, and safety standards define the boundary between a consumer product and a care service.

What would change the finding

  • User purpose: prevention, wellness, companionship, diagnosis, or treatment.
  • Well-being, loneliness, sleep, work, care, and health outcomes over months.
  • Crisis language, safety response, referral, human contact, and later care.
  • Time, payment, messages, exit attempts, and reasons for staying.
  • What users understand about limits, memory, data use, and product identity.
  • Results by age, income, disability, loneliness, language, and crisis history.

Next test

Follow users for one year across prevention, wellness, and companion products. Measure relief, well-being, attachment, crisis response, human care, payment, data use, and exit.

Reading rule

Do not call an app a therapist because it feels personal. Do not call longer use harmful because it is long.

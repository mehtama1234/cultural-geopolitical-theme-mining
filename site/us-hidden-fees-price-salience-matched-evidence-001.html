Matched evidence · US hidden fees and price display

The first price can shape the choice before the full price appears.

A shopper cannot compare a cost that is hidden until late in the purchase. The timing of information can change who gets to make a real comparison.

In one sentence

Full-price display changes buying and quality choices, but a changed click is not yet a measured household loss.

The key test is what was finally paid, what quality was received, and what had to wait.

The connection

headline price → attention and commitment → late fee or full-price reveal
               → buy, switch, downgrade, abandon, or borrow
               → final cost and trust

The experiments show that price display changes behavior. They do not show that every extra purchase is harmful. The household question begins after the click: what was finally paid, what quality was received, and what other bill had to wait?

The power shift

When the full cost appears early, the shopper can compare sellers more equally. When the cost appears late, the seller benefits from time and attention already spent. That can matter more to a household with little cash or little time.

Ask which markets the rule covers, who enforces it, and who still faces hidden cost.

Next test

Capture first price, final price, fee timing, product quality, income, and later repeat or cancellation behavior. Test whether full-price display protects low-liquidity households differently.

Reading rule

Do not call a fee hidden because it is separate. Ask whether the full mandatory cost was clear before the customer committed time, attention, or money.

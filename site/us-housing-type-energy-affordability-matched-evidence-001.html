Matched evidence · US homes, energy, and control

The same energy bill means different things in different homes.

A renter, a manufactured-home resident, and a homeowner do not have the same way to lower the bill or recover from a shock.

In one sentence

Energy burden without control hides the path to improvement.

Ask who pays, who fixes, and who can leave.

The connection

housing type and tenure → bill, equipment, and repair control → energy burden and safe-temperature risk → upgrade, aid, debt, move, or continued sacrifice

The sources establish differences in measurement, control, and reported risk. They do not yet show one household's full recovery path.

The useful surprise

A lower bill is not equally reachable from every home. A renter needs landlord action, a manufactured-home resident may need a building fix, and an owner may lack cash or credit.

Four views

Household

Record who pays, who controls repair, equipment, bill, temperature, and next fix.

Customer and resident

Make landlord duty, repair route, upgrade permission, and aid options visible.

Money and finance

Compare energy cost with rent, mortgage, repair, deposit, debt, and moving.

Public power

Target help by housing type, tenure, age, fuel, climate, and control—not income alone.

Next test

Join bills, temperatures, equipment, repair requests, landlord actions, and aid for the same homes.

Reading rule

Do not compare homes only by energy burden. Ask who pays the bill, who controls the fix, and who can leave.

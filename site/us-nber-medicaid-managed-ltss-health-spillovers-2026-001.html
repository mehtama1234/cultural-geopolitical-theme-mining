Finding: Medicaid delivery changes can spill into Medicare outcomes differently by state

Status: bounded public-system and care-delivery finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35055 studies county-by-county transitions to managed Medicaid long-term services and supports (MLTSS) using Medicare claims for dual-eligible adults age 65 and older. The study compares Florida and New York rollouts with comparison counties in Pennsylvania and California.

Managed LTSS was associated with a 0.9 percentage-point, or 4.2%, increase in quarterly hospitalization risk in Florida, but no significant change in New York. The Florida increase was concentrated in respiratory and other urgent conditions. Florida also saw a 3.2 percentage-point, or 10%, decline in the likelihood of influenza vaccination. The authors identify this preventive-care change as a possible mechanism, not a complete explanation.

The cross-program insight is:

A Medicaid delivery change can create consequences in Medicare-financed care, and the direction can depend on state implementation. Payer boundaries do not guarantee outcome boundaries.

Limits and next test

  • The individual claims denominator and all implementation details are not disclosed in the abstract.
  • Florida and New York are not interchangeable managed-care experiments.
  • Hospitalization can reflect health, access, detection, coding, or referral changes; it is not by itself a complete quality measure.
  • The next test is a state-plan and county ledger joining capitation, provider networks, case management, vaccination, hospitalizations, nursing-home use, functional status, caregiver burden, and household cost.

Source

Evidence status: differences-in-differences evidence establishes a state-heterogeneous managed-LTSS and cross-payer health association; broader care quality, household, and durable outcomes remain open.

A profitable small firm can still be losing local room

Status: provisional cross-source finding · Checked: 2026-09-13

The bounded finding

Firm health is multidimensional. The April 2026 New York Fed EHI release shows reported small-business profitability improving from the pandemic period while revenue expectations weakened, pricing power was lower for the smallest firms, and employment paths diverged by firm size. This means “businesses are doing well” can be true on one measure and misleading on another.

The result matters to the cultural and societal atlas because firms are also local employers, service providers, price setters, and meeting places. The survey supports the firm-capacity stage; it does not establish what customers, workers, or communities experienced next.

What the survey reports

Outcome Measurement Boundary
Profitability Profit-minus-loss diffusion index of 13 points for year-end 2023, versus 46 points in December 2019 Diffusion index, not profit margin; reference period is the year before the survey
Revenue More firms reported lower than higher revenue in 2025; smallest firms drove the lower-revenue result Directional firm report, not audited revenue or cash flow
Expectations Share expecting higher revenue in 2026 fell 6 percentage points year over year Expectation, not realized outcome
Pricing Smallest firms were less likely to raise prices and more likely to report weak sales than larger firms Does not identify customer prices or market power
Employment Small-firm employment growth increased in 2025 while larger-firm growth decreased but remained positive Does not measure worker pay, hours, quality, or household security
Debt and credit Debt per employee was about $70,000; fewer firms reported receiving less than the full financing requested, and more reported no need to apply Lower credit constraint may reflect lower demand, not abundant affordable credit

End-to-end route

cost, demand, labor, and credit conditions
  -> firm revenue, prices, profit, jobs, and debt
  -> local services, worker household room, and customer alternatives
  -> place attachment, trust, and political response

The Small Business Credit Survey measures the first transition. The local business project’s Census and establishment layers can measure stocks and flows, but the links to service use, worker household outcomes, belonging, and political action still need a place-based join.

Method and counterexamples

The survey covers firms with fewer than 500 employees and focuses here on employer firms. It is a non-random survey weighted by industry, location, and demographics. Question universes and reference periods differ. A firm can be profitable because costs fell while revenue weakens; it can add employees while workers receive less room; and a firm can report no credit constraint because it did not need credit. These are required counterexamples to a single firm health score.

Next test

Link firm or establishment records to local sector capacity, service access, worker earnings and schedules, customer prices, ownership, and exit. Compare small firms by revenue, profit, pricing, employment, and debt rather than ranking them on one measure. Then test whether a service or job change altered household choices, local meaning, or political demand.

Evidence status: current weighted firm-survey evidence with explicit measurement and selection limits; downstream household, local-life, and political arrows remain open.

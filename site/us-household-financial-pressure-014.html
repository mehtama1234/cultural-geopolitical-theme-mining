Finding 014: A record median can conceal a stalled bottom and a widening gap

Status: bounded income-distribution decomposition finding · Checked: 2026-09-20

The bounded finding

The Census Bureau reports that real median household income reached $87,460 in 2025, up 2.6% from 2024 and the highest level in the series since 1967. That headline improvement does not describe the whole distribution: income at the 90th percentile rose for a third consecutive year, while income at the 10th percentile did not differ significantly from 2024.

Over 1967–2025, pretax household income rose about 56% at the 10th and 50th percentiles but about 121% at the 90th percentile. The 90th-to-10th percentile ratio widened from 9.23 to 13.06.

What the chain establishes

income distribution and tax/transfer conditions
  -> different resource movement by percentile
  -> unequal room for prices, debt, care, housing, and risk absorption [open]
  -> different adaptation, trust, status, and political interpretation [open]
  -> firm, public-system, or collective response [open]

The useful correction is to treat “the economy improved” as an incomplete distributional statement. A median is a midpoint, not a representative household, and a record median can coexist with a flat lower tail and a larger top-to-bottom distance.

Limits and next field

  • These are national CPS ASEC distribution estimates, not a matched household panel or a causal explanation of percentile movement.
  • Pretax and post-tax measures, prices, benefits, debt, liquid assets, and household composition can change the practical meaning of the income rank.
  • The release does not show which households changed behavior, health, work, trust, political action, or ability to exit a bad option.
  • The next test should join percentile movement to taxes and transfers, local prices, housing and health costs, debt, liquid assets, work intensity, and later adaptation.

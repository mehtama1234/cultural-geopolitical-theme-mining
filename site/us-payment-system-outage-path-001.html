Connected US finding · payment and access

When the power fails, payment becomes access

A household can have money and still be unable to use it when electricity, communications, or a merchant terminal fails.

The argument

During an outage, payment helps decide whether food, medicine, fuel, or a needed trip can be completed. Cash may help only if the household has it, the store is open, supplies remain, and travel is safe.

The household balance can remain unchanged while usable options shrink.

Follow one essential purchase

A family loses power after a storm and needs medicine. The pharmacy is open but its terminal cannot connect. The family has some cash, not enough for the full prescription, and the nearest working pharmacy is 20 miles away. A second card may work, or treatment may be delayed.

storm or infrastructure failure → electronic payment unavailable → cash, another card, offline tool, or no transaction → essential purchase completed or missed → household cost, health, work, and recovery change

The deeper finding

Payment is infrastructure because it connects stored money to a real purchase. Banks, networks, merchants, utilities, fuel providers, and households each control one link.

What remains unproven

The study does not show which households missed food, medicine, fuel, or work access, or which backup works best in every outage. Cash can be lost or stolen, and long outages can empty shelves.

Next test

Follow households through a real outage for three months. Join payment attempts, cash access, merchant acceptance, offline tools, essential purchases, credit, work, care, health, transport, and recovery.

Reading rule

Keep payment availability, purchase completion, essential need, and later loss separate.

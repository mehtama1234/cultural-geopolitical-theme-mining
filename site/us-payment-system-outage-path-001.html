Connected US finding · payment, energy and household access

When the power fails, payment becomes access

A household can have money and still be unable to use it when electricity, communications, or a merchant terminal fails.

In one sentence

Payment is part of access to food, medicine, fuel, and other basics.

Keep money, payment availability, purchase completion, and need separate.

What the source shows

Cash can work as a backup when digital payment fails

NBER combines outage, weather, transaction, scanner, and survey data. Before disasters, spending rises as households stock up, often using credit. After outages, digital payments fall and cash use rises. Nearly half of surveyed consumers could not use their preferred electronic payment, while greater cash access reduced the decline in completed purchases.

The study does not show which households missed food, medicine, fuel, or work access.

NBER Working Paper 35115

The connection

storm or infrastructure failure → electronic payment unavailable → cash, credit, offline tool, or no transaction → essential purchase completed or missed → household cost, health, work, and recovery change

A shift to cash may show a backup working or a preferred payment failing. It is not by itself hardship.

The four outside views

Household: Cash, another card, or a working store may preserve a purchase. Access to each backup differs by income, age, disability, safety, and place.

Company and sector: Banks, networks, merchants, utilities, and fuel providers each control one link. One failure can stop a purchase.

Money and finance: Stocking up may use credit before the outage and create repayment later. A missed purchase may create a larger cost.

Public power: Cash access, outage rules, network repair, emergency benefits, merchant backups, and supply delivery decide whether money remains usable.

What would change the finding

  • Outage length, location, cause, network failure, weather, and store supply.
  • Cash access, bank access, merchant acceptance, offline tools, and restoration.
  • Food, medicine, fuel, care, work, and other purchases completed or missed.
  • Credit use, repayment, fees, lost work, health, and recovery.
  • Income, age, disability, race, place, broadband, and transportation.

Next test

Follow households through a real outage for three months. Join payment attempts, cash access, merchant records, essential purchases, credit, work, care, health, and recovery.

Reading rule

Having money is not the same as being able to use it. Keep payment availability and later loss separate.

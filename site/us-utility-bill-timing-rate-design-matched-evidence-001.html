A short argument · US utility bills, rate design, and cash timing

A manageable yearly cost can still fail at the due date

An annual energy average can hide the month that breaks a household.

The argument

Affordability depends on the bill that arrives, the money available that week, and the cost of falling behind—not only the annual price of energy.

Follow the difficult month

A household's winter bill arrives after a low-pay month. A fixed charge remains even though the family uses less, and the utility allowance misses the real cost. The family borrows, cuts food or medicine, or risks a shutoff.

The deeper finding

Lower use does not always mean a proportionally lower bill. A fixed charge can remain while consumption falls, and a yearly average can make a difficult month look ordinary.

What remains unproven

The sources do not prove the exact cause for a particular household: timing, rate design, income, housing, equipment, or payment rules may each matter.

Do not call energy affordable from an annual average. Find the month, the due date, and the cash that was actually available.

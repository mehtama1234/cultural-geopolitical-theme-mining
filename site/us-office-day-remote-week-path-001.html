Connected US finding · office contact and work

When one office day changes the remote week

One office day can create useful contact with coworkers and managers while taking time, money, care room, and energy from the worker.

The argument

A shared day may help communication and retention, but its value depends on who travels, how far, and what the day replaces.

Work location distributes opportunity and cost.

Follow one office day

A customer-service worker attends one office day each month. She spends two hours traveling and pays for transport and care, but gains direct feedback and manager contact. A coworker lives nearby and gains the same contact at much lower cost. Another cannot attend reliably because of disability or care.

remote work arrangement → planned office day → coworker contact, feedback, and output → commute, travel cost, care change, or access barrier → satisfaction, retention, pay, or promotion

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
An NBER trial of 248 customer-service employees found one office day a month led to 7.8% more calls per hour.Limited in-person contact can affect measured output.
The trial found 36 extra minutes of coworker communication, more manager feedback, and 11 percentage points more contact among assigned desk neighbors.The likely mechanism is contact, not presence alone.
Attrition was one-third lower in the trial.Contact may affect whether workers stay.
Earlier NBER research in a China-based call center found work from home could raise productivity and satisfaction while lowering promotion rates.Work location can improve one outcome and weaken another.

Sources: NBER office day, NBER work from home, and NBER coworker proximity.

The deeper finding

Office contact is a work resource with a household price. An employer may gain feedback and lower attrition while the worker pays in fuel, care, sleep, or time.

What remains unproven

The evidence does not show whether office contact improved long-term pay or promotion for the same workers, or whether online contact could create the same result. It does not show effects for long commutes, disability, or family care.

Next test

Follow workers for one year before and after an office-day rule. Join attendance to commute, care, output, feedback, promotion, pay, retention, and household cost. Compare an office day with an online meeting that creates the same contact.

Reading rule

Do not call office contact free. Do not call remote work isolated. Measure what the shared day adds and what the trip takes.

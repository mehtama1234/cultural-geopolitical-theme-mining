Connected US finding · remote work, contact and transport

When one office day changes the remote week

One office day can create useful contact while taking time, money, care room, and energy from the worker.

In one sentence

A shared day may help communication and retention, but its value depends on who travels and what the trip replaces.

Measure contact gained and travel cost carried by the same worker.

What the sources show

One office day improved contact and reduced attrition in one trial

An NBER randomized trial of 248 customer-service employees found that one office day a month led to 7.8% more calls per hour, 36 extra minutes of coworker communication after office visits, more manager feedback, and 11 percentage points more contact among assigned desk neighbors. Attrition was one-third lower.

The trial involved one large multinational and workers living within commuting distance. It does not show the travel cost, care disruption, disability effect, or long-term job quality for each worker.

NBER Working Paper 35331 · NBER work-from-home experiment · BLS consumer spending

The connection

remote work arrangement → planned office day → coworker contact, feedback, and work output → commute time, travel cost, care change, or access barrier → job satisfaction, retention, pay, or promotion → next work rule

The same office day can be useful to a worker near the site and costly to a worker with a long trip, a care duty, or a disability.

The four outside views

Household: The worker weighs the day against fuel, fares, parking, food, care, sleep, and family time. A shorter commute may make the same meeting more valuable.

Company and sector: An employer may gain contact, feedback, and lower attrition. It also chooses who attends, how often, where the office is, and whether the meeting could work online.

Money and finance: Travel is a household expense and a time cost. Compare pay, promotion, retention, transport, care, and the value of a larger job market.

Public power: Transit, roads, housing near jobs, disability access, labor rules, and employer policy shape who can take the office day.

What would change the finding

  • Travel time, fare, fuel, parking, and care cost.
  • Communication, feedback, output, promotion, pay, satisfaction, and retention.
  • Distance, transit access, disability, health, household structure, and job type.
  • Whether the office day is needed for the task or mainly for visibility.
  • Later job moves, housing choices, and work hours.
  • Results by income, race, gender, age, place, and care role.

Next test

Follow workers for one year before and after an office-day rule. Join attendance to commute records, care needs, output, feedback, promotion, pay, retention, and household costs.

Reading rule

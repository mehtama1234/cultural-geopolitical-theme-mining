Matched evidence · US family support

Family help can catch one fall by moving it to another household.

Relatives may fill a gap with cash, housing, rides, child care, food, or time. The receiver may stay afloat while the helper loses room.

In one sentence

Family help can prevent a missed bill or care break, but it is not free resilience.

The helper’s cost must be counted beside the receiver’s relief.

The connection

shock → family money or care → immediate problem held off
      → helper loses cash, time, work, or savings
      → family wealth and future choices
      → demand for formal help or dependence on relatives

The evidence supports family help as a real response. It does not show that the receiver became secure or that the helper could absorb the cost.

The hidden accounting

Families provide unpaid hours, a room, a ride, a meal, a phone call, or help with a form. These may be more useful than cash for one problem, but they can reduce the helper’s paid work, rest, health, or ability to handle a later emergency.

Four views

Household

Record both sides: money received, care given, hours, debt, and what each person gave up.

Customer

Compare family help with credit, public aid, paid care, and the time required.

Money and finance

Value cash, housing, in-kind help, lost wages, debt, and savings separately.

Public power

Ask whether policy assumes families can fill a gap and who is left when they cannot.

Next test

Follow both receiver and helper around one event. Record cash, in-kind help, care hours, missed work, debt, and later stability.

Reading rule

Do not call family help a safety net until you show who provides it, what it prevents, and what the helper loses.

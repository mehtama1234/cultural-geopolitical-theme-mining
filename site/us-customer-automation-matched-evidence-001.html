Connected US finding · automation and customer recovery

A faster answer is not yet a remedy

Automation can help a worker answer faster. It is not the same as fixing a repeat problem, reversing a denial, explaining a charge, or reaching someone with authority.

The argument

The useful dividing line is not bot versus person. It is whether the customer can understand the decision, keep a record, correct an error, and reach someone who can change the answer.

A human handoff without authority is only a change in voice.

Follow one complaint

A customer reports a missing delivery. An automated tool suggests a reply and the first response arrives quickly. The package is still missing. The customer repeats the story and reaches a worker who cannot issue a refund. The company improved response time while the customer kept the loss.

A tool can work well when it gathers the order record, identifies a clear error, gives the worker authority to refund, and leaves a written reason. The test is whether the case ends.

automated routing → faster first response → repeat complaint or denial → human access and authority → resolution, repeat contact, loss, complaint, or exit

The deeper finding

Automation changes who must carry the case. If it resolves a clear error, it saves customer and worker time. If it only routes, summarizes, or repeats policy, the customer still does the work. The firm counts contacts; the customer counts the hours needed to get a result.

This matters most in finance, health, utilities, delivery, and benefits, where a wrong answer can create a fee, missed care, shutoff, or lost aid.

What remains unknown

The HBS experiment measures first-contact response and sentiment in one setting. It does not follow the same customer through repeat contact, transfer, decision, remedy, appeal, and exit.

Next test

Choose one complaint system. Record first contact, repeat contact, automation use, transfer, explanation, worker authority, remedy, appeal, customer time, money lost, and exit. Compare simple requests with exceptions and compare customer groups.

Reading rule

A quick reply is not a solved problem. A human transfer is not the same as human authority. Ask who can change the answer.

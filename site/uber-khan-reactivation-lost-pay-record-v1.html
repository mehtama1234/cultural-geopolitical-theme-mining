Uber Eats / Khan reactivation and lost-pay record v1

Status: primary Fair Work Commission merits decision; unfair deactivation, reactivation order, and lost-pay assessment route Checked: 2026-09-15

Why this record matters

Khan extends the Australian evidence beyond driver deactivation and makes the interaction between repeated temporary holds, warnings, and final termination visible. It also shows that a platform's later restoration of access does not necessarily restore the worker's legal or financial position: the Commission can still issue a formal reactivation order and assess lost remuneration.

Source and scope

In Zeeshan Aslam Khan v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd ([2026] FWC 48; UDE2025/185), Mr Khan had delivered for Uber Eats since November 2022. Uber Eats terminated access on 29 July 2025, and he applied on 1 August 2025. The Commission found him protected, found the Digital Labour Platform Deactivation Code was not complied with, and held the deactivation unfair.

Uber relied on two complaints alleging inappropriate or sexual misconduct. The first complaint in August 2024 led to a temporary block, warning, and reactivation. A second complaint in July 2025 led to another temporary block, limited warning, preliminary deactivation notice, worker response, and final deactivation.

Evidence matrix

Stage Primary finding Boundary
Decision surface Complaint-based access termination after two reported incidents The decision concerns this worker and complaint sequence
Explanation Warnings did not squarely put the allegations to Khan; his requests for explanation received no substantive written answer The Commission did not require disclosure of every identifying detail, but did require enough information to understand and answer the warning
Worker response Khan responded in writing and participated in support calls; the preliminary notice offered a discussion route A platform call is not automatically meaningful review; the content and authority must be examined
Evidence One 2024 allegation was partly corroborated, but Uber did not deactivate him for that event; the 2025 allegation was not made out The Commission assessed the evidence on the balance of probabilities; it did not declare all customer reports unreliable
Review Uber personnel contacted Khan, but the process left him unaware of the material relied upon and the conclusions drawn Reviewer identity and decision authority remained incompletely documented
Outcome Deactivation was Code-inconsistent and unfair; reactivation and lost-pay relief were ordered The decision directed the parties to confer on quantum; payment receipt is not established here

Remedy chain

2024 complaint -> temporary block -> limited warning -> access restored
2025 complaint -> temporary block -> limited warning -> preliminary notice
  -> worker response and support call -> final deactivation
  -> Commission finds insufficient notice, inquiry, and valid reason
  -> reactivation ordered -> lost-pay quantum to be calculated

The case makes recurrence and escalation important. Uber treated an earlier event as part of the later pattern even though it had previously restored access after that event. The Commission distinguished the conduct that may have occurred from the conduct that supplied a valid reason for the later deactivation. This prevents a platform's historical warning from becoming an unexamined substitute for current proof.

Comparative interpretation

Khan:       repeated temporary holds + limited particulars -> reactivation; pay open
Warraich:   five complaints + vague warning + weak inquiry -> reactivation; pay open
Mansoor:    serious allegation not proven + deficient notice -> reactivation + $6,073.23
Kumar:      rating rule + compliant notice/review -> dismissal

Khan strengthens the atlas theme that restoration has layers. Temporary reactivation during a platform investigation, formal reactivation after a tribunal finding, and actual recovery of lost earnings are separate events.

Decisive next acquisition

Locate the subsequent order in UDE2025/185 and determine the lost-pay amount, any deductions for substitute earnings or avoided expenses, payment status, and whether access was restored on the pre-deactivation terms. Search for later platform or worker evidence about recurrence, rating/profile effects, and retaliation.

Boundary

This record establishes the Commission's findings and remedy direction in one case. It does not establish the later amount paid, actual durable access, system-wide policy change, or prevalence of similar deactivations.

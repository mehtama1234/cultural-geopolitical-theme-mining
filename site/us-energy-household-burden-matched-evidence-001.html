Matched evidence · US household energy burden

An energy bill can take food, medicine, or safe temperature first.

Energy burden is not only a percentage. It can become a household tradeoff, while the person paying the bill may not control the building that creates it.

In one sentence

The 2024 EIA survey records energy pressure as food or medicine cuts, notices, unsafe temperatures, and lost heating or cooling.

These are reported conditions, not yet one complete before-and-after household story.

The connection

home condition + energy price → bill pressure
                              → food, medicine, rent, debt, or comfort tradeoff
                              → notice, shutoff, aid, or weatherization
                              → health, work, housing, and trust

The EIA table measures pressure and coping actions. DOE and GAO show why housing and payment rules matter. The final health, work, and trust links still need a joined household record.

The control problem

A renter may pay the monthly energy bill but have no money or right to change windows, insulation, or the heating system. A bill-help program addresses the immediate payment. Weatherization addresses the building. They are different interventions and should not be credited with the same outcome.

Four views

Household

Record what was cut—food, medicine, temperature, rent, debt, or work travel.

Customer

Separate a bill, notice, shutoff, payment plan, and restored service.

Money and finance

Keep burden, arrears, assistance, and building investment separate.

Public power

Ask who sets the rate, controls the building, qualifies for help, and bears the unpaid balance.

Next test

Join energy insecurity with rent or ownership, building condition, income, payment responsibility, assistance, and later health or housing outcomes.

Reading rule

Do not call a lower energy bill a full success until we know whether the household kept food, medicine, safe temperature, housing, and work access.

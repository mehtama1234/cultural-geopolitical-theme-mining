Finding 019: Having leave on paper does not always create usable time

Status: bounded leave, care, and work-control finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The finding in simple words

The BLS reports that 94% of wage and salary workers had either paid or unpaid leave access in 2024-25. But 8% still needed leave during an average month and did not take it.

Among those workers, the reasons were not only “no leave exists.” About 29% said they had too much work, 14% feared job consequences or had a request denied, and 13% could not afford the lost income. The need was often for the worker's own illness or medical care, or for a family member.

The route is:

illness, family care, errands, or another need
  -> formal paid/unpaid leave access
  -> workload, job-risk, schedule, and income constraints
  -> leave taken or needed leave not taken
  -> [open] care result, health, job, and household outcome

What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a plain example of the difference between an offered route and a usable route. A policy can say that leave is available while the worker still has to choose between the need, the shift, the paycheck, and the risk of being seen as unreliable.

It also links care to workplace power. The same formal benefit can be easier to use for a full-time, higher-paid, public-sector, or work-from-home worker than for someone in a part-time or service job. That is a subgroup pattern, not proof of one worker's complete experience.

What it does not show

  • Access is self-reported and applies to the main job, not every job.
  • Not taking leave does not prove denial, retaliation, or unresolved illness.
  • The survey does not show whether care happened, whether pay was lost, or whether the worker kept the job.
  • It does not follow the same worker into later health, family, trust, or political outcomes.

Next decisive test

Use a linked worker or household episode that records the dated need, leave request, approval or denial, actual hours, pay effect, care or health result, and later job and household stability. Preserve non-use and alternate care as separate routes.

Source

  • BLS: Access to and Use of Leave — 2024-2025

Evidence status: official national data show a gap between formal access and usable time; the later care, health, job, and household consequences remain open.

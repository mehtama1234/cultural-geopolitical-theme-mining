When a data rule changes the customer exchange

A platform can be both the market gate and the maker of the information used in the market. Data access, privacy, competition, and customer choice can move in different directions.

The argument

An equal-access rule may help outside sellers compete, but it may also reduce the platform’s reason to gather or improve data. Customers may share more because the exchange feels safer while still being unable to switch.

Data access is part of market structure, not just a privacy setting.

Follow one product search

A customer searches for a household product. The platform’s own brand appears beside outside sellers. The platform holds search, sales, and customer data. Equal access could help outside sellers; costly or lower-quality access could reduce reliable choices. If the platform favors its own product, the best alternative may never be seen.

platform controls data → access and price for sellers → competition and data quality → customer price, choice, search, and service → public rule and platform power

The deeper finding

Customers are affected by a hidden bargaining system. Sellers bargain for data access; platforms bargain for the right to collect; customers trade information for price, search, or service.

What remains unproven

The model is not a record of one current platform, and the app study is not a test of seller competition. Neither follows one customer through consent, data use, seller entry, price, service, and welfare.

Next test

Follow customers, sellers, and a platform for one year around a data or privacy-rule change. Join consent, sharing, data use, seller entry, prices, offers, service quality, search, investment, and outcomes.

Reading rule

Keep data access, data quality, seller competition, customer outcome, and platform investment separate.

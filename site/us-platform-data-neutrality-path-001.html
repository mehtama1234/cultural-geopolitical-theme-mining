When a data rule changes the customer exchange

A platform can be both the market gate and the maker of the information used in the market. Data access, privacy, competition, and customer choice can move in different directions.

The argument

An equal-access rule may help outside sellers compete, but it may also reduce the platform’s reason to gather or improve data. Customers may share more because the exchange feels safer while still being unable to switch.

“Data” can mean a receipt, a product record, a ranking signal, a customer profile, or a prediction. Opening one layer does not open them all.

Data access is part of market structure, not just a privacy setting.

Follow one product search

A customer searches for a household product. The platform’s own brand appears beside outside sellers. The platform holds search, sales, and customer data. Equal access could help outside sellers; costly or lower-quality access could reduce reliable choices. If the platform favors its own product, the best alternative may never be seen.

A seller may see sales totals but not the ranking signal that decides what appears first. It has access in law but not in practice. Another seller may receive the ranking data but cannot correct a bad product record. Customers then see many listings without a fair way to compare them.

customer or seller supplies data → platform cleans, ranks, and controls it → access and price for sellers → competition, investment, and data quality → customer price, choice, search, service, and privacy

Access is not the same as control

Seller

May technically receive data but lack ranking, correction, or timely updates.

Customer

May technically consent but lack a real alternative to the platform.

Platform

May invest less if rivals can copy the data service without sharing its cost.

If the platform’s own product competes with sellers using its data, it is both referee and player. Equal access matters only if it covers the data and ranking tools that shape the customer’s choice.

What remains unknown

The model is not a record of one current platform, and the app study is not a test of seller competition. Neither follows one customer through consent, data use, seller entry, price, service quality, and welfare. We do not know which data layer is most important to each seller or customer.

Next test

Follow customers, sellers, and a platform for one year around a data or privacy-rule change. Join consent, sharing, data use, correction requests, seller entry, access price, ranking, product prices, offers, service quality, search time, investment, and outcomes. Test each data layer separately.

Reading rule: keep data access, data quality, ranking control, seller competition, customer outcome, privacy, and platform investment separate.

Finding 098: Individual employment remedies can be reported without closing recovery

Status: provisional named US enforcement remedy finding · Checked: 2026-09-18

The bounded finding

FY2026 summaries from the Justice Department's Immigrant and Employee Rights Section report several charging-party resolutions in which an individual payment was made after an employment or employment-verification dispute. The reported paid amounts include $5,600, $16,730, $30,000, and $8,000. A separate September resolution reports an offer of $485,000 in full back pay and benefits, but not payment.

The safe interpretation is:

The public enforcement record can establish an agency-reported individual monetary remedy and employer control response. It still does not establish that the worker regained the original job or opportunity, had a usable alternative, recovered household security, trusted the institution, or stayed, switched, organized, or exited.

Event chain

employment or verification barrier
  -> reported lost opportunity, termination, or hiring dispute
  -> IER investigation or resolution
  -> paid back pay/front pay in four summarized cases
     or a back-pay offer in one case
  -> employer training or policy correction
  -> [open] restored work, durable compliance, household recovery,
          trust, action, switching, or exit

These are five distinct anonymous resolution summaries, not one pooled worker-level panel. The amounts are retained as case-level agency reports and are not converted into a prevalence rate or average remedy.

Why it matters to the broad atlas

This adds a stronger middle stage to the work-control and public-systems lanes: an enforcement route can reach a reported individual payment rather than only a reserve fund, aggregate distribution, or prospective control. But payment is not the same currency as opportunity restoration. The same record can therefore show remedy administration while leaving lived recovery and power unchanged or unknown.

It also sharpens the same-case promotion gate. The IER summaries observe an individual remedy, but they do not contain an episode-level alternative or non-use field, later outcome, trust/action measure, or exit. They cannot be joined to the existing alternative surface merely because the cases concern a similar institutional mechanism.

Coding consequence

agency-reported payment       != worker-confirmed receipt audit
back pay                       != restored job or opportunity
reinstatement offer            != accepted return to work
training/policy correction    != durable compliance
individual remedy              != household recovery, trust, or exit

Code this as agency-reported individual remedy with employer response and later recovery open. Keep the $485,000 case as an offer, not a payment.

Next decisive test

The smallest useful follow-up would retain an episode identifier and pair the payment or offer with the worker's alternative options, accepted or refused reinstatement, later employment, net pay, household security, recurrence, institutional trust, and switching or exit. If those records are unavailable, the cases remain remedy-stage comparators rather than end-to-end episodes.

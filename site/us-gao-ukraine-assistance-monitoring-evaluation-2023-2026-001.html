Finding 001: Wartime oversight can improve while proof of strategic results remains delayed

Status: bounded geopolitical, institutional, and measurement finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

GAO reviewed the State Department's MEASURE contract for monitoring and evaluating more than $4 billion in nonhumanitarian, nonmilitary assistance to Ukraine. As of September 30, 2025, the contract helped oversee $6.1 billion in supplemental Ukraine appropriations.

State used some of the information for project oversight and budgeting, then changed progress reports to be more analytical and issued them twice a year instead of quarterly. But data access, wartime conditions, and different timelines for results limited evaluation. Cross-project evaluations and strategic outcome indicators were still incomplete as of November 2025.

The route is:

wartime assistance
  -> delivery and program monitoring
  -> usable information for oversight and budgets
  -> cross-project evaluation
  -> [open] institutional performance, public legitimacy, and strategic effect

The important distinction is that better reporting is a real improvement in state capacity, but it is not the same as evidence that the assistance worked.

What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a wartime version of the program's “correction versus consequence” theme. Institutions can improve their information route while the actual outcomes remain hard to observe because the setting is dangerous, data access is negotiated, and results take different amounts of time to appear.

It also connects the geopolitical and public-systems lanes: oversight is not just paperwork. It shapes which programs receive continued money and whether leaders can learn from failure or success.

Limits and counterexamples

  • The $6.1 billion figure describes the assistance overseen, not verified benefit or strategic success.
  • Wartime data access and delayed outcomes may explain incomplete evaluation.
  • The report does not follow one Ukrainian institution or recipient through delivery, use, maintenance, and later outcome.
  • State changed the reporting process, and GAO removed draft recommendations after receiving additional information; this is evidence of correction, not proof that all evaluation gaps were closed.

Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a named-program ledger joining funding, delivery, Ukrainian use, operational result, local response, maintenance, and an attributed institutional or strategic change. Until then, the safe claim is that oversight capacity improved while proof of the larger result remained unfinished.

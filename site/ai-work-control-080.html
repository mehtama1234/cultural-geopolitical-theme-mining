Production support and integration testing move JASSM/LRASM beyond announcement—but not yet to delivered inventory

Status: realization-stage milestone pass · Checked: 2026-09-14

The finding

The JASSM/LRASM case now has dated evidence beyond procurement intent and supplier naming:

  • In July 2025, DoD announced a $999 million IDIQ contract for JASSM/LRASM production support, including upgrades, integration, production, sustainment, management, and logistics support through 2030. The announcement says no funds were obligated at award.
  • Lockheed Martin's 2024 account describes a large-lot contract intended to increase JASSM/LRASM production capacity and identifies a new 225,000-square- foot intelligent factory brought online in 2022.
  • Lockheed Martin's 2026 account reports completion of the first phase of the F-35C LRASM flight-science program after tests from September 2024 through April 2026, using LRASM and JASSM test assets.

These observations move the case through support commitment, industrial capacity investment, and integration testing. They do not establish accepted quantities, delivery to a customer, inventory availability, maintenance capacity, or a changed external bargaining position.

The DoD announcement and Lockheed accounts are intentionally not merged into a single output estimate. A production-support contract can fund engineering, logistics, upgrades, and sustainment; a factory can be ready without producing the selected lot; and a flight test can validate integration without placing a missile in an operational inventory.

Why it matters for the end-to-end geopolitical question

This is the first clear realization-stage movement in the case after the recipient/entity/location work. It makes the next acquisition more specific: the program should seek accepted lot quantities, shipment or delivery records, inventory/fielding notices, and the facility or supplier assignment for the subaward components.

It also sharpens the power question. The United States and allies may have increased production capacity and tested integration without yet demonstrating how quickly a customer can receive, maintain, replenish, or switch the system. Capability becomes leverage only when an actor can use that realized capacity to change another actor's options or behavior.

Counterinterpretations kept visible

  • An IDIQ award ceiling or support scope is not the same as obligated or delivered production.
  • Company-reported capacity and factory investment can reflect intended scale, not realized throughput.
  • Flight testing is a technical milestone, not an inventory or fielding record.
  • JASSM and LRASM share elements of the production and integration system, but the programs, variants, quantities, and customer pathways remain distinct.
  • A stronger US production base does not by itself prove resilience against a critical-input disruption or create geopolitical leverage.

Next decisive acquisition

Retrieve a dated accepted-quantity, shipment, delivery, fielding, inventory, or maintenance record tied to a JASSM/LRASM variant and customer. Then join it to the relevant prime modification and, where possible, the UEI/location and component records already acquired. Preserve the difference between test asset, production unit, accepted unit, delivered unit, and operational inventory.

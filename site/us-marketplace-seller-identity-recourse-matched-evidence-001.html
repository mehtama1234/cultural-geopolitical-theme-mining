A short argument · US marketplaces, sellers, and remedy

A seller name is only useful if it helps the customer recover.

Knowing who sold a product is not the same as having a remedy.

The argument

The INFORM Consumers Act makes many high-volume sellers less anonymous. But a name, address, and phone number do not automatically produce a refund, replacement, safe product, or appeal.

Transparency is an input to recourse, not recourse itself.

Follow the remedy

A customer buys a product, receives something damaged or unsafe, and contacts the seller and platform. The customer may still face return shipping, a restocking charge, a delayed refund, or a dispute that moves between seller, platform, and payment provider.

seller identity → purchase → product problem → report, return, dispute, or refund → money, product, and time recovered or lost

The useful distinction

Keep identity, warning, responsibility, and remedy separate. A platform can know the seller and still leave the customer to prove the loss. The buyer may become investigator, record keeper, return shipper, and payment disputant.

The argument has limits

The law strengthens identification and reporting. The sources do not show whether disclosure shortens recovery time, improves refunds, reduces unsafe products, or changes trust.

Next test

Link seller disclosure to purchase, complaint, return, report, refund, response time, and final remedy. Compare covered and smaller sellers.

Reading rule

Do not call seller disclosure customer protection until it shows a shorter path to a real remedy.

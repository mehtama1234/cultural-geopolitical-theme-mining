Matched evidence · US marketplaces and recourse

A seller name is only useful if it helps the customer recover.

Knowing who sold the product matters only if the customer can use that knowledge when the sale goes wrong.

In one sentence

Seller identity is an input to recourse, not recourse itself.

A name and contact path do not guarantee a refund or safe product.

The connection

seller identity shown → customer can compare and contact the seller → product problem occurs → report, return, dispute, or refund path opens → money, product, and time are recovered or lost → trust and future use change

The law strengthens identity and contact. FTC guidance describes recovery choices. The full link from disclosure to recovery remains unmeasured.

The useful surprise

Transparency can shift work to the customer. A buyer may get a name and contact path but still have to prove the problem, pay return shipping, wait, and dispute the charge.

Four views

Household

Count product loss, return shipping, time, refund delay, replacement cost, and dispute effort.

Customer

Record identity, contact attempts, report date, response, return terms, and final remedy.

Money and finance

Separate seller refund, platform reimbursement, card dispute, fees, and unrecovered loss.

Public power

Measure verification, suspension, reporting, records, and appeal—not only disclosure.

Next test

Link seller disclosure to the same customer's purchase, complaint, return, refund, and recovered loss.

Reading rule

Do not call seller disclosure customer protection until it shows a shorter path to a real remedy.

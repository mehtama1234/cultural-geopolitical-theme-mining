Connected US finding · first pass

The cost of staying in a home is a stack of bills and risks

A home can become harder to keep before anyone moves. Energy takes a larger share of income in low-income households. Insurance has risen much faster in some disaster-prone places and can also affect home value.

In one sentence

The people with the least money to repair, move, or replace equipment may have the fewest ways to lower the cost of staying.

The sources do not yet prove that the same households face all of these pressures at once.

What the sources show

1. The energy bill takes a different share of the budget

DOE estimates average energy burden at about 6% of gross income for low-income households and about 2% for non-low-income households. In some places it is above 30%.

DOE Low-Income Energy Affordability Data tool

2. Energy insecurity is a choice made under pressure

EIA reports that 43.56 million of an estimated 132.54 million US homes had some form of energy insecurity in 2024. About 32.89 million reported reducing or forgoing food or medicine to pay energy costs.

EIA 2024 household energy insecurity table

3. Protection from outside risk can become a second housing bill

GAO found that average homeowners insurance premiums rose about 3% after inflation from 2019 through 2024, while some southern coastal areas rose 25% or more. Higher risk can also reduce availability.

GAO homeowners insurance review

4. The insurance bill can reach the asset too

Using more than 74 million inferred premiums, NBER researchers estimate that rising premiums reduced home-price growth by more than $40,000 in the most exposed ZIP codes.

NBER Working Paper 32579

The connection

home quality, fuel, weather, and local risk → energy bill and insurance bill → food, care, debt, savings, comfort, or ability to move → utility relief, repairs, public aid, lender rules, or political pressure

The evidence supports each step up to the bills and reported energy choices. It does not yet show the whole path for one family. The useful finding is that housing stability is priced through several channels, while the ability to change those channels is uneven.

What is still unknown

  • Which cost is paid first?
  • Which need is cut first?
  • Can weatherization lower later food, care or debt tradeoffs?
  • Do energy and insurance costs arrive together for the same household?
  • Does the household move, borrow, seek aid, or stay put?

Next test

Compare two high-exposure states by county, income, tenure, building age, energy burden, insurance premium share, shutoff or arrears records, and moves. Do not call the costs mutually reinforcing until at least two measures share a geography and time line.

Reading rule

The source sections report separate studies. The connection and household examples are working interpretations, not a completed causal account.

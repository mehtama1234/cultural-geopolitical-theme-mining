Finding 001: A labor-rights process can be active while durable worker power remains unknown

Status: bounded worker-voice and institutional-capacity finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

The NLRB's FY 2025 report shows substantial use of the federal labor-relations route. The public filed 19,754 unfair-labor-practice charges. The agency found 32.8% of those charges had merit, and 96.8% of meritorious cases were settled. Workers or unions also filed 2,633 petitions for secret-ballot representation elections, within 2,743 total representation petitions.

The agency's own capacity mattered. The Board lacked a quorum for nearly three-quarters of the fiscal year and issued 86 contested-case decisions during the periods when it had a quorum.

The route is therefore:

workplace problem or representation demand
  -> charge or petition
  -> investigation, election, settlement, or Board decision
  -> payment, reinstatement, bargaining, workplace change, trust, or exit [open]

What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an institutional worker-voice subtheme:

People can have a formal route to contest workplace power while the route's use, capacity, and procedural output remain different from durable protection.

It sharpens the atlas's recurring distinction between access to a process and the result that process produces. It also links worker voice to state capacity: agency staffing, quorum, settlement practice, and delegated authority shape what becomes visible as a decision.

Limits and next test

  • Charges are filed allegations, not confirmed violations; the report's merit share is not a prevalence estimate.
  • Settled cases do not show whether workers received payment, reinstatement, safer conditions, or bargaining power.
  • Representation petitions do not equal elections, wins, contracts, or durable coverage.
  • The report does not identify the same worker's later job, pay, trust, or exit.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected case-to-worker or workplace route linking filing, investigation, remedy, payment or reinstatement, bargaining, and later employment or control. Until then, the safe claim is about formal demand and institutional processing, not worker recovery.

Evidence status: official administrative records show substantial labor process demand and output under constrained Board capacity; worker-level remedy, bargaining, and durable power remain unmeasured here.

A geopolitical shock can become a household and legitimacy problem through unequal room

Status: provisional cross-source synthesis · Checked: 2026-09-13

The bounded finding

The IMF and World Bank provide a macro and global-distribution frame; SIPRI provides resource allocation; the Federal Reserve provides US household adaptation; and ANES provides political judgment and vote measures. Read together, they support a conditional chain in which geopolitical or defense pressure can alter macro conditions, those conditions can be absorbed differently across households and places, and the resulting experience may enter public judgment. They do not show that this entire chain occurred for the same people or that defense policy caused a specific vote, trust change, or geopolitical response.

What each source actually contributes

Layer Unit and period What it measures What remains open
IMF WEO Global projections and cross-country defense/conflict episodes; 2026 and historical episodes Growth, inflation, deficits, debt, external pressure, defense-boom trade-offs, and conflict scarring US household incidence, social legitimacy, unrest, alliance behavior
World Bank PPP Global/country poverty and climate-risk assessment; 2024 with 2030 trajectory Poverty thresholds, regional concentration, prosperity gap, inequality, climate exposure, and survey coverage US distribution, household adaptation, migration, political response
SIPRI National and alliance military expenditure; 2015–2024 Resource allocation and NATO concentration Realized capability, fiscal trade-off, public consent, deterrence, external behavior
Federal Reserve SHED US adult respondent/family; 2025 Income timing, assistance, budget margin, price adaptation, borrowing, saving, and extra work Dated shock, time displacement, recovery, attribution
ANES US eligible-voter respondent; 2024 Financial worry, economic judgment, trust, identity, and reported vote A measured conflict/defense exposure linked to later judgment or action

The distinction matters. IMF's estimated debt and social-spending trade-offs are not the same object as the Federal Reserve's 41% month-end-surplus measure. World Bank poverty concentration is not US household exposure. SIPRI spending is not capability. ANES vote is not a direct response to any one economic or geopolitical condition.

The mechanism under test

conflict, defense choice, or external dependence
  -> prices, trade, financing, debt, and public-budget composition
  -> household/firm/place exposure and available room
  -> substitution, care/work trade-off, migration, or political interpretation
  -> trust, legitimacy, collective action, vote, or external bargaining

The evidence is strongest at the macro, distributional, and population-adaptation stages. The cross-source arrows between those stages are plausible and testable, but not a pooled causal estimate.

What can be said now

  1. Defense mobilization is not one-dimensional capacity. IMF's typical boom estimate combines short-run activity support with deficit, debt, inflation, external, and social-spending risks.
  2. The same macro shock is unlikely to be socially uniform. World Bank's poverty, inequality, climate, and survey-coverage patterns show that absorptive capacity is geographically and institutionally uneven.
  3. US households already report differentiated ways of absorbing price pressure: cheaper goods, reduced use, delayed purchases, reduced saving, borrowing, and extra work. Income volatility and budget margin condition the menu.
  4. Political judgment is a separate endpoint. ANES can show worry, trust, fairness, and vote co-occurrence or conditioning, but it cannot identify a geopolitical trigger without a compatible exposure and timing design.

Counterexamples that prevent overreach

  • A defense increase can raise local employment or protect supply while social spending remains stable.
  • A macro slowdown can coexist with household resilience through savings, family support, transfers, or lower exposure.
  • A household can experience price pressure without blaming government or changing a vote; prior identity may dominate.
  • High poverty or climate exposure does not imply realized loss when public protection, migration, infrastructure, or social networks work.
  • Aggregate debt can rise while some households gain income or security, and spending can fall without reduced capability if efficiency improves.

What would establish more of the chain

The next credible design needs a dated exposure and compatible unit: a US federal or state defense/energy/trade/budget change; local price and service exposure; household resources and adaptations; then repeated trust, attribution, action, or vote measures. A country panel could test the international version with macro shock, social spending, poverty/climate exposure, migration, and political response. Each design must report weights, missingness, timing, and alternatives, and include places or people where the predicted trade-off did not occur.

Reading rule

This is a cross-source mechanism synthesis. It makes the end-to-end research question more concrete; it does not claim that the full macro-to-household-to-politics-to-geopolitics chain has been observed.

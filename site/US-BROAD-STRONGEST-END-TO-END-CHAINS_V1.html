US broad atlas: strongest end-to-end chains v1

Checked: 2026-09-25
Purpose: identify the strongest currently retained routes toward the broad end-to-end goal without pretending that separate sources form one causal chain.

The program's target is:

condition or shock
  -> exposure and usable alternatives
  -> money, time, access, status, health, control, or security change
  -> institutional response or remedy
  -> received and durable outcome
  -> persistence, recovery, trust, action, switching, or exit

No retained route closes every stage. The ranking below is therefore about how much of the chain is held by a compatible unit, not about which topic is most important.

The cross-lane trend portfolio supplies the thematic synthesis across these routes; it does not upgrade a cross-source pattern into a same-unit end-to-end chain.

The strongest recent practical-alternative comparator is the failed-alternative-employment case: search effort, lost income, and a formal remedy are visible in one worker record, but a secured alternative, payment receipt, and later recovery are not. It strengthens the route ranking without qualifying as a completed end-to-end episode.

Newly retained remedy comparator

The Longridge worker ledger adds two separate public worker episodes: advertised commission-only work produced severe underpayment; each worker quit and sought alternative employment; both complained; and the employer later reported full back-pay after Fair Work Ombudsman enforcement. This is a stronger alternative-to-remedy bridge than a compensation order alone, but it is not promoted as a completed end-to-end route because the public record lacks worker-confirmed receipt, replacement-job quality or duration, adequacy, later trust, recurrence, and exit. The two workers remain separate units and are not pooled into one story.

New cross-theme corridor — not ranked as a same-unit chain

The new health-cost institutional-legitimacy bridge connects persistent care foregoing with medical debt, reduced savings, borrowing, delayed purchases, and health; a separate credit record adds score exposure from medical collections, and a separate HTOPS comparison adds institution-specific confidence differences under expense difficulty. This is useful for the broad cultural and political map because it shows how one type of pressure can be translated into several currencies before it becomes a judgment about institutions. It is deliberately not promoted into the ranked same-unit chains: the sources do not follow one dated bill or care episode through credit correction, attribution, trust, action, and recovery.

Recent comparative remedy counterexamples

Two recent comparative cases sharpen the ranking without displacing its top routes:

  • Malaysia GrabCar Tribunal: statutory rights and a formal Tribunal moved into reported case use, with a claimant appearing personally and the matter continuing into evidence and witness examination. The route still lacks a public case identifier, merits reasoning, award, payment, access correction, or later work outcome. This is the clearest recent example of institutional use appearing before effective recovery.
  • Australian Kyei platform case: procedural deficiencies were identified, but a substantive safety reason was accepted and the unfair-deactivation claim was dismissed without restoration. This is a negative-remedy comparator: process failure, substantive justification, and practical consequence must be coded separately.

Together they add two controls to the ranking: a hearing is not a remedy, and a process defect is not by itself proof that the underlying decision should be reversed. Both cases remain open on payment, later access, durable work, trust, and exit.

Geopolitical realization comparator

The Poland F-35 Husarz ledger is deliberately kept separate from the JASSM-ER route. An official Polish record reports the first three aircraft arriving at Łask on 22 May 2026 and formal acceptance on 12 June 2026. A separate official Polish defense record reports first-pilot training completion, and a later Ministry update reports five additional aircraft arriving on 28 August, bringing the in-country count to eight. That closes the numeric aircraft component of the stated initial operational-capability criterion, but not trained ground-personnel coverage, spares/consumables, certification, fleet readiness, maintenance, mission use, sustainment, or a changed external actor. The comparison is therefore about evidence stages, not about whether the two procurements have the same capability or effect.

The KFOR posture and local-security integration record adds a different geopolitical comparator. It reaches an operating and relationship stage: NATO reports a gradual posture adjustment after an improved security assessment and integration of Kosovo Police into the mission's security architecture. This is stronger than a procurement or exercise announcement, but it remains open on local consent, incident change, burden distribution, durable trust, and changed external behavior. It therefore advances the operation/interface stage without becoming evidence of legitimacy or leverage.

What the ranking says

Important same-worker implementation comparator

The FWO Atkins receipt ledger is the clearest positive worker-remedy comparator in the retained portfolio. The public follow-up names ten truck drivers, records underpayment and court enforcement, reports release of funds held in trust after an appeal was dismissed, and states that all ten received back-payments ranging from $8,012 to $91,566. This closes a reported worker-level receipt stage that many other remedy cases do not close. The 2026 receipt-and-durability audit rechecked the official site and found no later Atkins-specific record of work, household recovery, recurrence, trust, or exit.

It still does not establish independent payment confirmation, adequacy against each worker's loss, household recovery, later employment, recurrence, trust, health, or exit. The case therefore improves the remedy-receipt layer without becoming evidence of durable worker security or a general enforcement success rate. Its value is comparative: it shows exactly how much stronger “reported received” is than “ordered,” while also showing why “received” is not the final end-to-end outcome.

Important randomized care-control comparator

The Cash and Counseling beneficiary ledger, caregiver ledger, and paid-worker ledger form one of the strongest intervention-to-lived-outcome comparisons in the portfolio, but they must remain three units:

  • Beneficiary: randomized consumer direction supplied control over care arrangements and was associated with greater service satisfaction, fewer unmet personal-care needs, and more paid home-care access in the cited follow-up.
  • Informal caregiver: treatment caregivers showed selected wellbeing, satisfaction, and perceived financial-strain improvements around ten months later, without proof of restored household income or long-run continuity.
  • Paid worker: direct-hire workers generally reported satisfaction with compensation and conditions, while unpaid care, limited formal training, benefits, and retention concerns remained.

This is a real intervention-to-outcome bridge: random assignment strengthens the comparison between the service route and usual care. It still does not join allowance take-up, completed care, recipient health, caregiver work and earnings, worker retention, household security, trust, action, or exit for one stable household. The important insight is structural: giving a beneficiary more control can improve short-run service and caregiver currencies while moving coordination and continuity risk into the beneficiary-caregiver-worker relationship.

The Cartisano ledger is a particularly clear counterexample to treating payment as restored security. In one worker case, back pay and service continuity were reported, an unconditional reinstatement order followed, and a later court record reported an actual return. The same later record describes materially altered duties and cessation while still employed during a constructive-reinstatement dispute. The record does not establish final livelihood or later recovery, but it closes an important same-case arrow: money, legal continuity, actual return, and role quality can remain distinct.

The Aitken ledger adds the durability counterexample. A union-supported return, three months of back pay, and prior-service recognition were followed by a period of continued work and then a later dismissal after a safety-policy conflict. The case does not prove that the earlier remedy caused the later rupture, but it does show why an initially restored job cannot be coded as durable security without a later-tenure check.

The same durability problem appears outside employment. The Sauna360 recall ledger links a 2025 free-repair route to a 2026 official expansion stating that the earlier repair was ineffective and offering a second professional repair. It does not establish that every owner experienced failure or that the second repair will succeed, but it gives the atlas a product-level version of the same rule: an offered or completed-looking remedy is not durable protection without a later outcome check.

The most advanced route is not the route with the largest sample. Mudiyanselage is strongest on exposure, constrained alternative work, remedy, and stated meaning/return decision. Meadley is strongest on the later remedy clock: officially reported payment, failed reinstatement, worker enforcement, and additional court relief. Its counterexample is especially important because the monetary remedy and restored livelihood diverged in the same episode. Neither case has later household, trust, recurrence, or exit follow-up.

The CCES route adds a different downstream endpoint: material disruption can coexist with lower observed local political action without producing a single direction of institutional approval. That makes it a useful bridge to political meaning, but not a remedy or recovery chain; attribution, employer response, and later security are not observed.

The two newest worker-remedy additions sharpen the distinction between institutional reachability and worker agency. The DOL Workers Owed Wages route shows that even after an agency recovers money, the worker may still need to be located, notified, verified, and able to complete a claim. The OSHA asbestos case shows the other side: workers can convert a safety concern into an official retaliation finding and remedy order, while payment, safe return, compliance, and the practical safety of speaking up again remain open. Together they add a cultural mechanism to the atlas: people experience institutions as reachable or unreachable routes that change the risk of voice, complaint, and continued participation.

The paired DOL PAID employer-proof ledger adds a positive administrative counterpoint to the Workers Owed Wages route: employer payment proof is stronger than a claim invitation, but it still stops before worker cashing, adequacy, household recovery, and durable work. The current WOW instructions add another gate: paper-check recipients must update their information, provide identity documents, and use the electronic claim route. This keeps payment-channel access, payment verification, lived recovery, and later meaning as separate stages in the worker chain.

The official-route recheck on 2026-09-19 found no later public compliance, final-payment, or employment record for that worker. This is a verified non-observation, not evidence that payment or recovery failed; it means the next worker pass needs a lawful later compliance/payroll or worker-confirmed source rather than another reading of the same decision.

The largest same-person surface is MEPS, but its public data are strongest on timing and later health/work status, not on the middle of the lived episode. The consumer and public-system records have unusually clear institutional responses, but they usually stop before recipient-level receipt and durable recovery. Infrastructure and geopolitical records have clear capability or governance stages, but they stop before affected-person incidence and changed external behavior.

This produces a practical rule for the next pass: add one missing stage to a high-ranked route before opening another topic. There are two different priority layers here. The machine register ranks broad acquisition routes, with household pressure and care first because PSID can strengthen the material/time/care backbone. For an already available public-record route, the highest-value marginal stage upgrade is worker remedy receipt and later stability. These are not competing rankings.

The public-record stage upgrades are, in order:

  1. worker remedy receipt and later stability;
  2. a lawful UAS Older Ages or PSID route that adds alternatives, payment, and later meaning/action to an event-linked person record;
  3. recipient-level consumer or public-benefit correction and recovery;
  4. household and community incidence for a realized infrastructure project.

Cross-route pattern

Across the ranked routes, institutional visibility is consistently ahead of lived recovery visibility. The records can often show that a rule, complaint, case, payment, referral, or project milestone occurred. They less often show whether the affected unit could use an alternative, received the remedy, regained the protected outcome, or changed trust, behavior, switching, or exit.

The care route adds a social-reproduction version of the same problem: the population scale and time intensity are visible, but the record does not show which work, rest, health, or social activity was displaced. Care hours should not be relabeled as lost work without a counterfactual.

The housing route adds a place-security version: insurance can be formally present while premiums, desired coverage, deductibles, or lender constraints leave the household exposed. Shopping is visible as an action, but an affordable and adequate alternative is not.

The New York DFS insurance complaint record adds a regulator-side recovery clock: complaints can be closed and money can be reported as recovered while claim-level repair, coverage restoration, and household stay/move outcomes remain unknown. It is a useful administrative endpoint, not a household recovery estimate.

The financial route adds a useful consumer-power version: people can contact a provider, report a fraud, and even report switching without the record showing verified correction, completed switching, restored liquidity, or why the person stayed or left.

LIHEAP makes the public-system version concrete: prevention and restoration occurrences show that the program can act, but the public counts do not show whether the same household paid, stayed connected at the next bill, avoided a repeat crisis, or changed its trust in the institution.

The geopolitical route has the same structure in a different form: a defense agreement and delivery window are visible before accepted equipment, fielded readiness, substitution, or an external actor's changed behavior. A procurement commitment is therefore not yet operational leverage.

That is the current substantive finding of the atlas: unequal practical options and institutional friction determine how a pressure is converted into lasting loss, adaptation, recovery, or meaning. It is a cross-domain mechanism, not a claim that every route has the same cause or outcome.

Promotion rule

Do not call a route fully end-to-end unless one stable unit has a dated or bounded event, an observed alternative or non-use route, a responsible actor, a received or defined outcome, and later persistence/recovery/trust/action/ switching/exit. Aggregate payments, participation, energization, complaint closure, or reported recovery alone do not satisfy this rule.

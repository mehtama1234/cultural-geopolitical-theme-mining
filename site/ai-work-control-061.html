Finding 061: Aggregate income and labor growth can coexist with price-specific household room loss

Status: current-vintage non-pooled macro-to-household bridge · Checked: 2026-09-14

The bounded finding

The latest release set places six evidence surfaces in sequence:

  1. BEA reports current-dollar income, consumption, services/goods composition, and saving.
  2. CPI measures consumer price movement, including a sharp energy component.
  3. PPI measures upstream final and intermediate prices, including diesel and energy.
  4. BLS CPS/CES measures unemployment, participation, payrolls, earnings, and long-term unemployment.
  5. JOLTS measures partial-year establishment openings, hires, quits, and separations.
  6. SHED measures household food insufficiency, bill payment, price effects, and income-conditioned hardship.

The defensible synthesis is: aggregate income, prices, labor flows, upstream costs, and household room are related but non-synchronous currencies; positive macro readings do not establish equal purchasing power, security, or political meaning.

These measures are intentionally not pooled. A national-account flow, CPI/PPI index, CPS/CES rate, JOLTS establishment rate, and SHED respondent share do not share a denominator or estimand.

The current macro-to-household chain under test

upstream costs, prices, income, employment, and labor mobility
  -> household basket, wages, hours, bills, food, savings, borrowing, and substitution
  -> perceived fairness, attribution, trust, institutional use, and political judgment
  -> adaptation, exit, collective action, policy response, and recovery

The release set reaches aggregate conditions and household hardship but does not observe the same household through the full sequence.

What the comparison changes

Income growth is not purchasing-power equality

BEA’s current-dollar increase can coexist with energy or shelter exposure concentrated in particular households. Services spending growth and goods decline are composition changes, not proof of a cultural preference shift.

Producer prices are not household incidence

The diesel and intermediate-price movements identify a potential upstream pressure route. Firms may absorb, delay, hedge, substitute, or pass through those costs; the current release set does not identify which households or workers bear them.

Labor stability is not worker control

Payroll growth and a 4.1% unemployment rate coexist with 27.0% long-term unemployment among unemployed people and a cool partial-year quits rate. The measures do not establish job quality, schedule control, bargaining power, or why mobility is low.

Household hardship is an endpoint with its own denominator

SHED’s food, bill, price, and income-conditioned measures are closer to lived room than BEA or CPI, but they use different reference periods and do not identify the exact event, alternative, or institutional route. The 21% versus 1% income-conditioned food contrast should not be treated as a CPI elasticity.

Counterexamples kept visible

  • Aggregate income can rise while a subgroup loses liquidity or faces energy exposure.
  • Producer prices can rise without immediate consumer pass-through, or pass through later.
  • Payroll growth can coexist with long-term unemployment, involuntary part-time work, or weak bargaining power.
  • A low quits rate can reflect preference, retirement, composition, or reduced outside options.
  • Household food insufficiency can persist without the survey identifying a single price or job shock.
  • Higher services spending can reflect prices, timing, or reclassification rather than preference.

Next decisive test

Select a dated price, energy, employment, benefit, or payment event and build a same-household or same-worker panel containing:

  1. exposure and baseline basket/resources/alternatives;
  2. prices, wages, hours, bills, food, savings, debt, care, and work response;
  3. attribution, fairness, dignity, trust, and institutional route;
  4. switching, complaint, benefit use, political action, or exit; and
  5. verified remedy, recovery, and later household or institutional condition.

Until that design exists, the atlas should not translate macro resilience into household security or price movement into political reaction.

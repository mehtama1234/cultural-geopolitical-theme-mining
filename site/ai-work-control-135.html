Finding 135: Alliance infrastructure can turn a partner capability into real operations

Status: bounded NATO capability-enablement and operational-use finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

NATO's Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance Force says it enabled a temporary Belgian MQ-9B deployment at Italy's Sigonella air base from April through July 2026. NISRF supplied hangar facilities, a secure operating environment, local coordination, and liaison support. The Belgian aircraft and crews flew more than 400 hours in support of Operation Sea Guardian and Operation Noble Shield.

allied national capability
  -> NATO infrastructure and host-nation coordination
  -> temporary deployment
  -> named operational use and flight hours
  -> decision effect, deterrence, external response, public acceptance,
     replaceability, and durable leverage [not observed]

Why it matters

This is a later stage than a defense promise, budget line, or exercise plan. It shows how alliance infrastructure and host-nation permission can make a partner's capability usable in real operations. But “operated” is still not the same as “changed the strategic situation.”

The broader political question is who carries the recurring cost of this capability: the contributing ally, host country, NATO institutions, local communities, or future budgets. The public account does not answer that yet.

Limits and counterexamples

  • This is one temporary Belgian deployment, not a general measure of NATO interoperability or alliance durability.
  • More than 400 flight hours do not reveal mission quality, intelligence value, or whether any decision changed because of the flights.
  • “High operational readiness” is an official assessment, not an independent performance test.
  • The source does not report host-community burden, public consent, Belgian or Italian cost, adversary response, deterrence, or continuation after July.
  • Successful enablement does not prove that another host, asset, or supplier could be substituted without delay or cost.

Next test

Find an operation-level follow-up that identifies a mission decision or external response, plus host-nation cost, public or partner acceptance, and whether the capability can be moved or replaced. Keep readiness, use, effect, and leverage as separate stages.

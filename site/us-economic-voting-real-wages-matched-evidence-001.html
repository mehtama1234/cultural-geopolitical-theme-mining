A short argument · US pay, prices, and voting

The vote may respond more to lost buying power than to inflation alone.

A local price rise and a loss of buying power are not the same thing.

The argument

Two counties can have the same price rise. In one, pay rises faster. In the other, pay falls behind while rent, food, and debt take more of the budget.

The political measure worth testing is what remains after local prices meet local pay.

Follow the local pay gap

In one county, prices rise six percent and pay rises seven. In another, pay rises two percent while rent and food rise six. The second worker cuts purchases, uses savings, or borrows. If the county vote changes, the price number alone cannot tell us why.

local price and pay gap → less household room → blame, identity, or hope → vote choice or turnout

The important split

Keep local prices, nominal pay, real pay, vote share, and turnout separate. A county result is not a household diary, and a household's pain is not automatically its reason for voting.

The argument has limits

The evidence makes lost buying power a better candidate for study than inflation alone. It does not prove a single cause of a vote.

Reading rule

Say “real pay fell,” “local prices rose,” or “the county vote changed.” Do not collapse all three into “voters punished inflation.”

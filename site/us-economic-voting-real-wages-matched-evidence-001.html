Connected US finding · buying power and political judgment

The vote may respond more to lost buying power than to inflation alone

A local price rise and a loss of buying power are not the same thing. Two counties can have the same inflation rate while pay rises in one and falls behind in the other.

The argument

Real pay—pay after prices—is a better starting point than the inflation rate alone. It still does not explain an individual vote: party identity, local industry, immigration, social issues, news, and trust may matter at the same time.

People may judge the economy through the room left after rent, food, transport, debt, and care.

Follow the local pay gap

One county’s prices rise 6% while pay rises 7%. Another has the same price increase but pay rises 2%. Workers in the second county cut purchases, use savings, borrow, or give up care. If its vote shifts, the shift may reflect that pressure—or a separate local change.

The political chain can break at several points: feeling poorer, blaming a party, turning out, and choosing a candidate are not the same event.

local prices and pay → real buying power and household cuts → economic judgment and blame → turnout, vote, or no political change

The deeper finding

Keep local price growth, nominal pay, real pay, vote share, and turnout separate. Real pay is closer to household room than inflation alone, but it still misses rent, debt, family help, savings, and the actual basket.

Instead of asking whether voters “punished inflation,” ask which households lost room, whether they knew why, whom they blamed, and whether voting was an available way to act.

What remains unknown

The evidence does not join one person’s prices, pay, rent, debt, purchases, economic views, party identity, turnout, and vote. It cannot rule out other local changes.

Next test

Use household data to join local prices, pay, rent, debt, purchases, economic views, policy knowledge, party identity, turnout, and vote choice where legally and ethically possible. Compare real pay with the necessities each household actually buys.

Reading rule

Say “real pay fell,” “local prices rose,” or “the county vote changed.” Do not collapse all three into “voters punished inflation.”

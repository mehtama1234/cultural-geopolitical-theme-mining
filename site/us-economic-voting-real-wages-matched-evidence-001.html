Matched evidence · US real wages and elections

The vote may respond more to lost buying power than to inflation alone.

Price growth, pay, real buying power, vote share, and turnout are different records. Keeping them separate gives a clearer account of what may reach the ballot.

In one sentence

A county can have high inflation, but the sharper political pressure may come when local pay fails to keep up.

The evidence does not identify the reason inside each person's vote.

Five measures

MeasureWhat it capturesWhat it does not prove
InflationHow quickly a local basket became more expensive.That every household bought that basket.
Nominal payDollars earned before removing price changes.Whether those dollars bought more.
Real payPay after accounting for local budget costs.Why a person voted.
Vote shareThe county result for a candidate or party.Which voters changed their minds.
TurnoutWho participated.Whether nonvoters were satisfied, angry, busy, or unable to vote.

The connection

local price and pay gap → less room after ordinary bills
                        → personal burden or national worry
                        → blame, identity, or hope for change
                        → vote choice or turnout

The county evidence supports the economic change and the election result, but not the full personal story. A county can move toward one party while many people inside it do not experience the same loss.

Why it matters

The same 6% price rise can mean different things in two places. In one, pay rises faster. In another, pay falls behind, rent takes more room, and the household gives up more choices. The useful measure may therefore be what remains after local costs meet local pay.

A household may know prices rose without knowing which policy, firm, or outside event mattered. Reported burden can relate to vote choice, but price data alone cannot show blame.

Four views

Household

Start with pay after rent, food, transport, health, and debt.

Customer

Record what was delayed, dropped, replaced, or bought on credit.

Money and finance

Separate price growth, real pay, debt service, savings, and housing costs.

Public power

Test knowledge and blame before saying hardship changed a vote.

Next test

Match county price and wage changes with survey measures of personal burden, policy knowledge, party identity, turnout, and vote choice. Keep the county result and the individual explanation separate.

Reading rule

Say “real pay fell,” “local prices rose,” or “the county vote changed.” Do not collapse all three into “voters punished inflation.”

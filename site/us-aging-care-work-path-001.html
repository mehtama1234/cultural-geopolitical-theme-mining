Connected US finding · eldercare and work

When family care becomes a work decision

Unpaid eldercare is a large use of time that sits beside paid work, childcare, rest, and household duties.

The argument

An employed adult may drive a parent to appointments, manage medicines, handle forms, and remain reachable during a shift. Employment does not prove care is easy, and leaving work does not prove caregiving caused the change.

Count the care hours, work hours, and future choices together.

What the sources establish

BLS reports 38.2 million unpaid eldercare providers in 2023–24. Fifty-two percent gave care at least several times a week and one-quarter gave care daily. Among providers who were also parents, 86% were employed and 72% worked full time.

Employed providers gave care on 21% of days, compared with 38% for nonemployed providers. An NBER study links reported caregiving to earnings records, but does not prove that care caused every work change.

Follow the work decision

older person’s need → family time, paid care, leave, schedule change, or work exit → earnings, rest, savings, housing, or childcare choice → employer, family, and public response

Flexibility may help, but it can also hide who carries the duty and who loses future security.

Keep these apart

  • Care hours and lost work.
  • Employment and easy care.
  • Paid-care spending and unpaid time.
  • Care supplied and care quality received.

Next test

Follow the same people from the start of care through two years of work, earnings, hours, job changes, paid care, savings, health, and childcare.

Reading rule

Do not call unpaid care lost work without showing what changed.

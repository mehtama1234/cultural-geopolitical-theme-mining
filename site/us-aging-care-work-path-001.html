When family care becomes a work decision

Unpaid eldercare is a large use of time that often sits beside paid work. The cost is the set of hours and choices arranged around care.

In one sentence

Care can use the same hours that a person needs for paid work, childcare, rest and family life.

The overlap is measured; a causal earnings loss still needs stronger evidence.

What the sources show

1. Unpaid eldercare is common

BLS reports that 38.2 million people provided unpaid eldercare in 2023–24, or 14% of people age 15 and over outside institutions. More than half provided care at least several times a week.

BLS unpaid eldercare release

2. Many providers also work and parent

Among providers who were parents with children at home, 86% were employed and 72% worked full time. There were 7.6 million providers in this group.

BLS parent-provider findings

3. Care timing differs by employment status

Employed providers gave care on 21% of days, compared with 38% for providers who were not employed. This describes a difference; it does not show which came first.

BLS time-use findings

4. Earnings effects need linked records

An NBER study links reported caregiving to administrative earnings records. It is the next source for testing timing and work effects.

NBER caregiving and labor supply

The connection

care need → family time, paid care, leave, schedule change, or work exit → earnings, rest, savings, housing, or childcare choice

The scale and overlap are measured. The later work and income effects remain open.

What is still unknown

  • Who reduced hours, changed jobs or lost earnings?
  • Who could buy paid care, and who could not?
  • Did employment status change care, or did care change employment?
  • How did employers respond to repeated care duties?
  • Which families faced eldercare and childcare at the same time?

Next test

Read the NBER study closely, then use BLS ATUS microdata to compare care timing with work hours and employment. Keep care time, paid-care spending and lost earnings separate.

Reading rule

Unpaid care is not automatically lost work. Employment is not proof that care is easy. Count the hours, money and choices together.

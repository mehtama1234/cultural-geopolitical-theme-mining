Connected US finding · care and work

When family care becomes a work decision

Unpaid eldercare is a large use of time beside paid work, childcare, rest, and household duties. The hidden cost is the set of hours, job choices, savings, and family arrangements made around care.

The argument

An employed adult may drive a parent to appointments, manage medicines, handle forms, and remain reachable during a shift. Another may leave paid work and supply more care. A third may pay for help but spend time finding and coordinating it.

Care turns time into a shared family budget. Employment status alone does not show whether care is easy or who is carrying it.

Follow one care week

An adult daughter works full time and has a child at home. Her father needs a ride and help with a form. She uses leave, swaps a shift, asks a sibling, or pays for a ride. If the appointment runs late, the change reaches child care and dinner. If it repeats, she may turn down overtime, change jobs, reduce saving, or move closer.

older person’s need → family time, paid care, leave, schedule change, or work exit → earnings, rest, savings, housing, or childcare choice → employer, family, and public response

None of those choices is visible in a simple count of whether she remained employed.

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
BLS reports 38.2 million people provided unpaid eldercare in 2023–24; 52% gave care at least several times a week and one-quarter daily.Family care is a large and repeated part of daily life.
Among providers with children at home, 86% were employed and 72% worked full time.Care and paid work often happen in the same household.
Employed providers gave care on 21% of days versus 38% for providers not employed; on care days, averages were 2.8 versus 4.8 hours.Employment relates to care time, but does not by itself show cause.
NBER links reported caregiving to administrative earnings records and examines labor effects by gender.The needed bridge can be studied, but the full cause still needs testing.

Sources: BLS unpaid eldercare and NBER caregiving and labor supply.

The deeper finding

Care is a work system outside the workplace. It can change who is available for a shift, who can move for a job, who can save, and who can rest. A program may appear generous while leaving the family to solve the hard timing problem: who is available when the appointment changes?

What remains unproven

The evidence does not yet follow the same people from the start of care through hours, earnings, job changes, paid care, savings, health, and childcare. It does not establish which form of leave, flexible work, paid care, or family support best protects long-term security.

Next test

Follow caregivers for two years. Record care tasks and timing, paid care, leave, schedule changes, job offers refused, earnings, savings, sleep, health, child care, and travel. Mark who made each adjustment and whether the older person’s care improved.

Reading rule

Count the hours, the money, and the choices together. Do not call unpaid care lost work without showing what changed.

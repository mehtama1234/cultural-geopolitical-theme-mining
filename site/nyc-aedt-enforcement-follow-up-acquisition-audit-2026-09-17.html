NYC AEDT enforcement and worker-outcome follow-up acquisition audit

Status: storage-light official-source audit · Checked: 2026-09-17

Question

Has New York City publicly documented a named employer or employment agency, affected applicant or worker, AEDT decision change, appeal, correction, compensation, or other realized worker outcome under Local Law 144?

What the official pages establish

The NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) states that an employer or employment agency may not use an automated employment decision tool unless a bias audit was completed within the required period, a summary is publicly available, and required notices are provided to employees or job candidates. The agency provides a complaint route and asks for the job, tool, notice, and suspected-violation details. The city also directs discrimination claims involving AEDT use to the NYC Commission on Human Rights.

These materials establish a public rule, a notice/disclosure obligation, and an administrative entry point. They do not establish that a particular worker used the route, that an employer changed a decision or system, or that a worker received compensation or protection from retaliation.

Targeted result

No public named AEDT enforcement outcome or worker-level remedy was located in the official DCWP pages and current public search results checked here. The result is a bounded non-observation, not a claim that no complaint, investigation, or private remedy exists. Public complaint confidentiality, referral to another agency, unpublished settlements, or a later release may leave the outcome outside this source surface.

Coding consequence

rule and scope: observed
bias-audit / notice requirement: observed_rule
complaint and referral route: observed_access_route
named employer implementation: not_observed
worker notice receipt or refusal consequence: not_observed
decision correction / appeal / compensation: not_observed
workload, pay, health, bargaining, or exit outcome: not_observed

The source therefore strengthens the US institutional-design and contestability stages of the AI/work path. It does not close the implementation-to-lived- outcome arrow and must not be converted into an AEDT compliance rate or a worker-welfare estimate.

Smallest decisive follow-up

Locate one public DCWP, Commission on Human Rights, court, or employer record that identifies the tool or employment decision, the affected party or a defined aggregate universe, the alleged rule breach, the response or remedy, and its date. If those fields remain unavailable, the next stronger route is a pre-registered worker-side or matched-workplace design with AEDT exposure, notice, appeal/override, job outcome, and follow-up behavior.

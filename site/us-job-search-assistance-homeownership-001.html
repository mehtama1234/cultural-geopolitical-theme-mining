Finding 001: Job-search assistance improved average long-run outcomes while harming a substantial estimated subgroup

Status: bounded randomized work-to-housing finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

The bounded finding

In Nevada, a randomized 2009 UI intervention offered 4,673 people a required one-on-one meeting within a population of 32,751 UI recipients. The meeting combined eligibility review, job-search enforcement, skills assessment, work- search planning, resume assistance, and job referrals.

Over up to eight years, NBER reports higher employment and earnings, lower short-term UI receipt, higher federal tax filing and tax receipts, and less loss of homeownership. But the effects were heterogeneous: roughly 40% of the sample was estimated to have negative long-run employment treatment effects, with corresponding positive disability-insurance entry effects. Positive homeownership effects were concentrated among people with positive employment effects.

The safe interpretation is:

A public route can improve average work and housing-related outcomes while producing different consequences for people with different constraints.

Evidence chain

unemployment and UI eligibility
  -> required meeting plus counseling, search plan, enforcement, and referrals
  -> job search and UI exit
  -> employment, earnings, tax, disability, and homeownership outcomes
  -> [open] job quality, health, care, trust, meaning, and exit

What it adds

Stage Supported result Still open
Intervention Randomized offer to a bundled UI meeting and job-search route Attendance, completion, component effects, perceived coercion
Work Average employment and earnings increased over long follow-up Job quality, stability, autonomy, care burden, health
Housing Loss of homeownership decreased, concentrated among positive employment-effect participants Renters, housing adequacy, foreclosure avoidance mechanism, neighborhood change
Heterogeneity About 40% estimated to have negative long-run employment effects, corroborated by disability-insurance entry Which constraints, health conditions, or institutional experiences explain the subgroup
Meaning/action Not measured Trust, dignity, political judgment, organizing, switching, or exit

The result should not be read as evidence that counseling alone caused the outcomes. The intervention bundles help, compliance review, and enforcement.

Next decisive test

Use participant-level follow-up, where lawfully available, to separate attendance and counseling from enforcement, track job quality and benefit transitions, and measure housing adequacy, health, care, perceived coercion, trust, and later exit. Preserve the negative-effect subgroup instead of reporting only the average gain.

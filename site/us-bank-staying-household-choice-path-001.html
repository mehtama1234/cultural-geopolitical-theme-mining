When staying with a bank becomes a household choice

Leaving a bank can save interest or fees, but moving an account is work. Direct deposit, bills, fraud protection, branches, and account history sit around the old account.

The argument

Staying can be sensible. It can also leave a customer with a lower rate because the cost of switching is larger than the visible gain.

Customer inaction is a trade between money, time, service, safety, and error risk.

Follow one account

A worker keeps an emergency fund in a low-interest savings account. A new bank offers more, but payroll, rent, utilities, and identity checks must be updated. She delays because payday is near and a failed deposit could cause a late fee. The account stays even though extra interest could build a buffer.

bank account opens → bills, direct deposit, and habit settle around it → switching takes time and attention → customer accepts a rate or term → bank gains steady funding → household buffer and bank stability move differently

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
NBER reports only 5–15% of depositors open a new account each year, using account openings and closures at more than 900 banks.Most depositors do not actively move in a given year.
NBER’s model estimates depositor “sleepiness” accounts for 57% of average deposit franchise value and softens competition.Inertia can be valuable to banks.
The model finds sleepiness reduced default probabilities during the 2023 banking turmoil.A stable deposit base may help banks; this is a model result.
HBS reports 94% of depositors leave funds at the same bank each year, with lower switching among older and checking or savings customers.Staying differs by customer and account type; the measures are not identical.

Sources: NBER sleepy deposits and HBS sleepy customers.

The deeper finding

Inertia is both a service feature and a market force. A familiar bank can reduce mistakes and effort, but that familiarity can weaken bargaining power. Staying should not be judged until we know what moving would risk and what the rate gap would buy.

What remains unproven

The sources do not show the same depositor’s rate gap, fees, switching effort, service use, safety, and household budget over time. They do not establish whether staying improved financial well-being for a particular customer.

Next test

Follow depositors through a full rate cycle. Match balances and terms to notices, bill links, switching attempts, service use, fees, interest received, and later saving or debt. Record failed moves and the cost of an account error.

Reading rule

Do not call staying lazy. Measure convenience, rate gap, switching cost, and household use of the money together.

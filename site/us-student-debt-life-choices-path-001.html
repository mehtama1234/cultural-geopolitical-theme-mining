When education debt follows the first job

Student debt is a payment claim that can meet a person again when work, housing, family plans, or more education are being chosen.

The argument

Consider a new worker deciding whether to take a public-service job, move closer to work, or return to school. The loan does not make the decision by itself. It changes the cash left after rent, transport, food, care, and saving. The payment rule may matter more than the headline balance.

The test is whether the payment changed the set of choices for this borrower.

What the sources establish

The Federal Reserve’s 2024 survey found that 20% of borrowers were behind on a payment or had a loan in collections; the share was 30% for borrowers with some college, a technical degree, or an associate degree.

New York Fed data put outstanding student debt at about $1.66 trillion in 2026 Q1, with 10.3% of balances 90 days or more delinquent. This is a balance measure, not the share of people behind.

An NBER study of female lawyers models possible effects on career, marriage, and education-quality choices. It is a mechanism study in a narrow group, not a result for every borrower.

Follow the payment into the next choice

education debt → payment rule and monthly claim → room left for rent, saving, work, family, or further study → a choice is delayed, narrowed, or made more valuable → wealth, work path, or family timing may change

The survey and credit records support payment trouble and serious delinquency. The later choice path is testable, not proven for the same people.

What matters more than the balance

  • Borrower: income, family role, age, place, and education return.
  • Payment: amount, interest, due date, income link, deferment, and forgiveness.
  • Choice: rent, move, job, marriage, children, more school, or saving.
  • Result: stress, income, wealth, credit, and freedom to change course.

Next test

Use Federal Reserve SHED and Census SIPP files to compare debt, payment trouble, earnings, housing status, and work change. Start with the same person’s path, then test a repayment change against similar borrowers.

Reading rule

A balance is not a burden by itself. A delinquency rate is not a life story. Keep the borrower, payment rule, income path, and choice together.

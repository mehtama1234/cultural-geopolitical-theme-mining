Connected US finding · attention, service and public trust

When staying does not mean being satisfied

People can keep using a service because it helps, because it is hard to replace, or because leaving takes too much time.

In one sentence

Continued use is not the same as approval when a person cannot get a clear answer or leave without loss.

Measure help, repeat contact, remedy, and exit separately.

What the sources show

Speed helped simple service, but repeat problems still need a different test

An HBS account reports that AI suggestions helped 138 meal-delivery support agents respond 22% faster across 256,934 chats and raised measured customer sentiment by 0.45 points on a five-point scale. The improvement was larger for less-experienced agents. Repeat complaints improved less, and some very fast chatbot-to-human transfers created confusion.

Other HBS and Pew evidence points to a separate question about exit and control. The FTC is examining safety, data use, age limits, and monetization for AI companions.

HBS service experiment · HBS chatbot exit study · Pew Americans and AI · FTC inquiry

The connection

product need or service problem → reply, recommendation, delay, or exit barrier → time spent and repeat contact → answer, remedy, continued use, or inability to leave → trust in the firm → demand for complaint, safety, or exit rules

The important split is between staying by choice and staying because the next option is costly, unclear, or unavailable.

The four outside views

Household: A person may keep an app because it helps with work, care, shopping, or company. More time may also mean the answer did not arrive or leaving risks lost data.

Company and sector: Faster first replies can help simple cases. Firms still need to measure repeat contact, reversal, refund, human review, and clean exit.

Money and finance: Time can become a missed work hour, a delayed claim, a lost refund, or another payment while the person fixes the first problem.

Public power: Complaint routes, data deletion, child safety, disclosure, and simple exit rules decide whether a customer has a usable remedy.

What would change the finding

  • The first question, repeat contacts, wait time, and final resolution.
  • Human access, appeal, refund, data deletion, and exit.
  • Use frequency, paid conversion, records held, and reasons for staying.
  • Time, money, work, care, health, or privacy costs after an unresolved case.
  • Differences by age, income, disability, language, loneliness, and digital access.
  • Complaints, enforcement, rules, and trust before and after the event.

Next test

Follow the same customer from first contact through 30 days. Compare automated and human paths on answer quality, repeat contact, time, remedy, exit, and later use.

Reading rule

Do not read continued use as approval. Do not read a friendly reply as a remedy.

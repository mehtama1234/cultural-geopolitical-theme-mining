Shared federal facilities can become mission infrastructure through partnership work

Status: bounded infrastructure and state-capacity finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

GAO's review of NNSA Strategic Partnership Projects (SPP) adds an operating middle to the infrastructure/state-capacity theme. NNSA sites did not merely receive appropriations or announce capability. From fiscal years 2019 through 2024, they performed more than 20,000 partner agreements with nearly $15 billion in reimbursed costs. All nine federal partners interviewed by GAO said the work was essential to completing their missions, and some relied on capabilities they described as unavailable elsewhere.

In FY2024, NNSA reported about $1.4 billion in indirect costs reimbursed by SPP partners—about 52% of the $2.66 billion shown in the site-level table, with seven sites ranging from 44% to 57%. GAO also reports that SPP increased use of existing facilities and contributed to operations and maintenance. But the same infrastructure can become capacity-constrained: some sites limited SPP growth because of competing mission work, staffing, or facility pressure.

  -> staff, equipment, maintenance, and indirect-cost allocation

What this adds to the atlas

This is a stronger state-capacity observation than a procurement commitment or facility announcement. It shows capability being used, paid for, and embedded in other institutions' missions. It also reveals dependence in both directions: partners may lack substitutes, while NNSA programs may rely on partner-funded overhead and utilization. Shared use can therefore strengthen capacity while narrowing slack and making rebalancing consequential.

The safe claim is not that SPP is efficient or that it produces national security success. The evidence establishes a realized operating and cost- allocation route whose final mission and public-value outcomes remain open.

Limits and next test

  • The 13-agreement sample is nongeneralizable.
  • Interviewed partner assessments are not independent mission-performance measures.
  • Reimbursed and indirect costs are not net savings or proof of full cost recovery in every site; Savannah River data were incomplete.
  • Utilization and maintenance contributions do not establish facility condition, safety, or project quality.
  • Mission dependence does not prove no substitute exists at all, nor does it prove changed deterrence or geopolitical leverage.

The next test is a named capability ledger linking one partner project to facility, staff, cost allocation, deliverable, acceptance, core-mission use, maintenance, rebalancing decision, substitute availability, and later mission or external outcome.

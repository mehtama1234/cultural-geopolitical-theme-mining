US broad trend-extraction protocol v1

Purpose: extract recurring societal, cultural, political, consumer, institutional, firm, infrastructure, and geopolitical patterns from different source families without collapsing unlike observations into one story.

This protocol is the bridge between the 14-theme inventory and the provisional trend register. It produces bounded trend claims, not a single index of “how America is doing.”

The basic unit

Every source observation is stored as an evidence card:

source -> population/unit -> date/window -> place/market -> measure
       -> observed change -> mechanism stage -> downstream result
       -> distribution -> uncertainty -> limitation -> next test

The unit must be explicit: person, household, consumer unit, worker, case, customer, firm, establishment, sector, agency, county, state, country, market, platform, or infrastructure asset. A geographic overlap does not make two records the same person, household, firm, or event.

Source-family roles

Source family Best use Do not claim from it alone
Surveys and panels reported condition, adaptation, belief, identity, trust, action, repeated respondent change administrative truth, exact event cause, or population-wide causality without design
Administrative records eligibility, participation, transactions, complaints, enforcement, timing, place, and institutional process unrecorded need, dignity, trust, unreported harm, or remedy unless measured
Research studies and experiments mechanisms, treatment effects, and tested counterfactuals universal effects outside the study population and setting
Public records and filings ownership, investment, capacity, prices, incidents, policy, and stated strategy realized service quality, local benefit, or public control from an announcement
Firm, market, and sector data entry, exit, jobs, prices, margins, concentration, sourcing, and risk transfer household security, cultural meaning, or fair competition without recipient-side evidence
Media and cultural measures narratives, attention, representation, language, norms, and source environment what the whole public believes or does
International and supply-chain records external dependence, inputs, ownership, alliances, and state exposure geopolitical leverage unless alternatives, bargaining, or changed external behavior are observed

Coding every arrow

Tag each claim with one stage and one evidence status.

Stages

  1. Condition: price, rule, technology, exposure, need, shock, or resource.
  2. Immediate effect: money, time, access, control, status, health, or security changes.
  3. Response: delay, substitution, borrowing, switching, staying, moving, organizing, investment, exit, or going without.
  4. Next actor: firm, employer, platform, agency, court, media, party, community, foreign actor, or infrastructure owner.
  5. Redistribution: cost, risk, data, asset, opportunity, or decision power moves to another actor or place.
  6. Meaning/action: trust, blame, identity, belonging, norm, demand, complaint, civic action, political action, or state leverage.

Evidence statuses

  • Observed: directly recorded in the stated unit and window.
  • Reported: respondent, complainant, firm, agency, or interview account.
  • Estimated: weighted or modeled quantity with method documented.
  • Compared: difference across valid groups, places, periods, or treatments.
  • Inferred: interpretation supported by adjacent evidence but not directly measured; never write it as an observed outcome.
  • Open: the required measure or valid join is missing.

When separate sources support a trend

A provisional trend enters the register only when all five conditions are met:

  1. at least two compatible source layers point to the same broad pattern;
  2. their units, dates, populations, and definitions are stated beside the claim;
  3. the pattern survives at least one distributional cut or counterexample;
  4. the missing arrow is named, especially between material exposure and cultural or political meaning; and
  5. the next test identifies a stronger same-unit, longitudinal, matched-place, firm-level, or causal design.

“Compatible” does not mean identical. A survey adaptation measure and a firm price measure may triangulate a price-pressure pattern, but they cannot prove that the same households experienced the same price or that the firm caused the reported adaptation.

Distribution and power checks

For every trend, ask:

  • Who is exposed, protected, excluded, or unmeasured?
  • Who controls the price, rule, schedule, data, remedy, asset, or exit term?
  • Who can absorb, delay, appeal, switch, move, refuse, or organize?
  • Which costs are transferred to family time, workers, consumers, firms, public systems, communities, or future users?
  • Does the headline improvement coexist with worse quality, autonomy, safety, resilience, or future optionality?
  • Does the result vary by income, race, age, gender, disability, family, migration, geography, firm size, ownership, or institutional access?

The answer belongs in the trend claim only when measured. Otherwise it becomes the next test, not an implied explanation.

Cultural and political discipline

Material change is not cultural or political change by itself. To claim a meaning/action path, measure separately:

exposure -> attribution -> information/source -> identity or fairness
         -> trust/blame/belonging -> expression -> civic or political action

Use non-users, non-reporters, eligible non-applicants, silent or unobserved cases, and counterexamples where possible. A complaint is not a remedy, trust is not control, continued use is not satisfaction, and a vote is not proof of a particular experience.

Required trend-register fields

Each row in the synthesis register should retain:

Field Minimum content
Trend statement One cautious sentence naming the population-level pattern
Theme links One or more of the 14 themes
Source cards Source, unit, period, geography, measure, and link
Arrow stage Condition, effect, response, next actor, redistribution, or meaning/action
Distribution Defined subgroup or place comparison
Evidence status Observed, reported, estimated, compared, inferred, or open
Counterexample A case, group, place, period, or result that limits the pattern
Missing join The exact arrow not yet observed
Next test Stronger design and the result that would change the claim

Stopping rule

Do not add a new topic packet merely because it has an interesting statistic. Add it when it fills a theme or arrow in the matrix, introduces a distinct source family or measurement level, or tests a proposed mechanism or counterexample. Stop a trend claim when its remaining evidence is only a geographic coincidence, an announcement, a repeated cross-sectional association, or an unmeasured cultural/political inference.

Current use

Apply this protocol to the 14-theme inventory, arrow-level evidence matrix, and provisional societal trend register. The protocol keeps the program broad while forcing depth at the exact arrow where a claim could otherwise outrun its evidence.

When new residents change the local customer base

New residents arrive as workers, customers, neighbors, and users of public services at the same time. A town can gain jobs and stores while also facing pressure on homes, schools, clinics, and transport.

The argument

“Immigration helps” and “immigration hurts” are both too blunt. A restaurant may hire more workers and serve more customers while a family finds better services but pays more rent or waits longer for a clinic.

Ask where demand lands, how fast supply responds, and who absorbs the change.

What the sources establish

Using Census data from 1980–2000, NBER estimates that each immigrant created about 1.2 local jobs for local workers, mostly in local services. Other NBER work describes labor supply, firm creation, and labor-demand channels. Recent work on the post-pandemic surge estimates roughly offsetting effects on inflation. HBS reports that mixed migrant-local teams produced more socially useful business ideas in its study.

These findings establish possible job, demand, price, and innovation channels, not current housing, service, wage, and political outcomes in one place.

Follow the local adjustment

new residents arrive → more workers, customers, and needs → firms, jobs, wages, housing, and services respond → residents experience different prices, access, and belonging → local political demands change

More workers can expand a service and still put pressure on wages in another job. More tax receipts can arrive while a clinic remains full. A local average hides these splits.

Keep these apart

  • Job creation and job quality.
  • More customers and lower prices.
  • Housing demand and housing supply.
  • Service use and service capacity.
  • Economic gain and belonging.

Next test

Follow matched places for five years before and after a change in new-resident inflow. Join migration, firms, housing, wages, service wait times, taxes, and local attitudes.

Reading rule

Count workers, customers, housing pressure, services, and belonging separately.

When help disappears before the need does

A public benefit can be available on paper and still be hard to keep. In the settings studied by NBER researchers, SNAP work requirements caused many people to leave the program without raising employment.

In one sentence

Access is part of the policy: a rule, form, deadline or long trip can change food support before work changes.

This does not mean every work rule has the same result, or that every person who leaves still needs the benefit.

What the sources show

1. Leaving SNAP was not the same as finding a job

NBER researchers found that work requirements increased SNAP exits by 23 percentage points among incumbent participants after 18 months, with no employment effect in the studied setting.

NBER: Employed in a SNAP?

2. Parents also left without working more

A separate study found that parents did not increase work but were much less likely to receive SNAP, with administrative burden identified as the main mechanism in that setting.

NBER: SNAP work requirements for parents

3. The route to help changes participation

Closing a SNAP enrollment office in one state reduced participation in nearby census tracts by 7–9% over two years.

NBER: The Intersection of Place and Need

4. SNAP can improve food security when people receive it

USDA research says SNAP improves food security in several studies and that food security worsens before benefits arrive and improves afterward.

USDA SNAP statistics and research

The connection

food or income need → eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task → benefit kept, lost, or never reached → food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome

The evidence supports the middle of this path. The final food, debt, health and political effects need a same-household test.

What is still unknown

  • Which households lost food support?
  • Did they borrow, skip care or find another source of help?
  • Did a work requirement raise stable earnings after care and travel costs?
  • How did office distance, language or internet access change receipt?

Next test

Use county or census-tract participation records with office locations, food-security measures and local employment data. Treat participation loss as the first outcome; test food and work outcomes separately.

Reading rule

Program exit is not employment, and eligibility is not receipt.

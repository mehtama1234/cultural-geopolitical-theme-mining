Connected US finding · public help and household access

When help disappears before the need does

A benefit can exist on paper and still be hard to keep when work rules, forms, office closures, and renewal tasks meet a household already short on time and money.

The argument

A falling caseload can mean less need—or lost access while the need remains. Access is a repeated path: apply, prove, renew, report, correct, and keep receiving help.

Eligibility is not the same thing as receipt.

Follow one family through the path

A parent with changing hours must report records from several employers. The renewal notice arrives online during a shift. The agency is two bus rides away. A missed deadline ends food support; the next month the family uses credit for groceries, while the parent takes work that makes the next renewal harder.

food or income need → eligibility and work rule → application, report, renewal, or correction → benefit kept, lost, or never reached → food, work, debt, health, and trust

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
NBER linked SNAP and earnings records and found work requirements increased exits by 23 percentage points among incumbent participants after 18 months.A rule can sharply change receipt.
The same study found no employment effect in that setting.Leaving the program cannot automatically be read as moving into work.
A study of parents found lower SNAP receipt without increased work and identified reporting and administrative burden as an important path.Proving compliance can be part of the outcome.
Closing a SNAP enrollment office reduced nearby participation by 7–9% over two years.Distance and local service capacity affect actual receipt.
USDA research finds SNAP improves food security in several studies; GAO estimated many potentially eligible food-insecure college students did not report receiving SNAP.Need, eligibility, and receipt can diverge.

Sources: NBER work requirements, NBER parents and SNAP, NBER enrollment offices, USDA SNAP research, and GAO college students.

The deeper finding

Administrative work is a household cost paid in time, travel, documents, internet access, language help, and the risk of an error going uncorrected. A stable worker with a car can absorb the same task that breaks access for someone juggling shifts, children, disability, or an unreliable connection.

What remains unproven

The evidence does not show that every exit means lost food security, or that every work rule has the same result. Participation loss is a warning signal, not completed proof of harm.

Next test

Follow applications, renewals, exits, office locations, wait times, online access, food security, debt, health, and employment. Compare automatic renewal, online renewal, and in-person help. Separate exits caused by rising income from exits caused by a failed task or unresolved error.

Reading rule

Program exit is not employment. Count the work required to claim the help.

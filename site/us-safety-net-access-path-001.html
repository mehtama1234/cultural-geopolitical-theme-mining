Connected US finding · public help and access

When help disappears before the need does

A benefit can be available on paper and still be hard to keep. A rule, form, deadline, office closure, or long trip can remove support before work or income changes.

The argument

A program count can fall because people no longer need help, or because people lost access while the need remained. Those are different outcomes.

Eligibility is not receipt, and program exit is not employment.

What the sources establish

NBER found SNAP work requirements increased exits by 23 points among incumbent participants after 18 months without an employment effect in that setting. A study of parents found lower receipt without more work and identified administrative burden.

Another NBER study found that closing an enrollment office reduced nearby participation by 7–9% over two years. USDA research finds SNAP improves food security when people receive it. GAO estimated that 59% of potentially eligible food-insecure college students did not report receiving SNAP in 2020.

NBER work rules · NBER parents · NBER office access · USDA SNAP · GAO

Follow access

food or income need → eligibility, work rule, distance, renewal, or documents → benefit kept, lost, or never reached → food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome

Administrative work is a household cost. It can require time, travel, internet, language help, and the confidence to challenge an error.

Keep these apart

  • Eligibility and receipt.
  • Program exit and employment.
  • Office closure and food loss.
  • Participation loss and completed harm.

Next test

Use participation records with office locations, food security, and local employment data. Compare online, in-person, and automatic renewal.

Reading rule

Treat participation loss as the first outcome; test food, work, debt, and health separately.

Finding 004: Disaster policy redistributes recovery, access, and legitimacy—not just liquidity

Status: cross-source end-to-end synthesis · Checked: 2026-09-17

The synthesis

The current disaster lane contains four complementary US evidence surfaces, not one joined causal dataset:

  1. Firm finance: government recovery loans can reduce firm exit and bankruptcy while increasing employment, revenue, and private credit.
  2. Owner household: linked business/household accounts show a 40% early COVID revenue fall with only a 1.6-cent owner-consumption decline per dollar lost, with the average muted pass-through persisting through 2021.
  3. Customer access: Hurricane Harvey closures increased observed shopping distance and generated localized modeled welfare losses even where aggregate prices showed little durable movement.
  4. Resident judgment: nearly a year after Harvey, recovery progress coexisted with continued disruption, unmet-help reports, and doubts that relief reached those most in need.

Together these findings support a more disciplined broad claim: public intervention can change the survival of firms and the availability of local options, but its social meaning depends on who receives liquidity, who bears new debt or travel time, whose household buffers absorb the shock, and whether residents can see and navigate the remedy. “Recovery” is therefore a distributional process across money, time, access, security, and legitimacy.

The end-to-end chain, with open arrows

natural disaster or eligibility shock
  -> physical damage, revenue loss, or public-support reallocation
  -> loan, subsidy, insurance, private credit, savings, or no remedy
  -> firm survival / exit + owner consumption / debt
  -> worker jobs + customer distance, substitution, and prices
  -> household recovery, local service availability, and perceived fairness
  -> [open] verified remedy receipt, attribution, civic action, switching,
         durable place change, and political/state consequence

The arrows must remain separate. A loan that keeps a firm alive may protect jobs and local entry while also creating private debt. A household can maintain consumption by using savings or aid without restoring the firm. A new entrant can have higher modeled consumer value than an exiting store while failing to arrive in the neighborhood that lost access. A majority can say life is nearly normal while a large minority still cannot navigate aid or trust its distribution.

What this changes about the broad goal

The program should treat disaster recovery as a test of five currencies:

  • Money: loan amount, repayment, transfers, insurance, savings, and private credit;
  • Time: travel distance, delay, navigation effort, and waiting for repair or payment;
  • Access: firm survival, nearby alternatives, service continuity, and practical ability to use aid;
  • Security: jobs, wages, housing, health, food, and household buffers; and
  • Legitimacy: whether affected people see the remedy, believe it is fair, and can act through a public route.

The evidence currently measures each currency in different source families. It does not justify an overall “resilience” score or a claim that any one policy is broadly beneficial. The strongest comparative result is instead a redistribution proposition: policy can move costs and options between firms, owners, workers, customers, neighborhoods, and public institutions.

Counterexamples that prevent a simple story

firm survival       != good jobs
owner consumption    != owner security
new entry            != nearby replacement
stable prices        != stable access
aid application      != aid receipt
aid confidence       != civic action
more eligibility     != more access for the smallest firms

The size-rule case adds a further institutional counterexample: widening a support boundary toward larger firms can reduce small-firm revenue, increase exits, and lower wages and patenting. The disaster-loan case shows that a different public credit instrument can improve constrained-firm outcomes. The instrument, eligibility boundary, and outcome currency all matter.

Next decisive test

The smallest genuinely end-to-end acquisition is a lawful, privacy-preserving event ledger that aligns:

  1. disaster declaration, damage, or firm/household revenue shock;
  2. insurance, loan, grant, or private-credit application and decision;
  3. amount, timing, repayment, and verified receipt;
  4. firm employment and worker pay/quality;
  5. customer service availability, distance, price, or missed purchase; and
  6. owner/worker household recovery, perceived fairness, and later civic action.

If no common identifier exists, keep the four layers as a cross-source atlas and do not manufacture a pooled recovery index. A compact table-level artifact or consented linked panel is preferable to acquiring raw payment-card, administrative, or household files.

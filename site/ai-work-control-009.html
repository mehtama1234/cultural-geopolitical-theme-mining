Finding 009: A written control is not yet lived control

Status: provisional
Last checked: 2026-09-11
Project: AI, work, and control

Finding

A workplace AI rule can give workers formal rights without showing that workers can use those rights in daily work. The IBM Germany framework is unusually concrete: it sets risk classes, correction paths, works-council inspection rights, and a ban on the highest-risk personnel decisions. But the record does not show how often those rights are used, whether managers follow them, or whether workers experience less harm.

The useful unit is therefore not “human oversight.” It is the full path from a written rule to a worker’s ability to see, question, correct, delay, or stop a decision.

What we directly know

  • The archived IBM Central Holding / Group Works Council framework says its rules are binding within scope and requires transparency, explainability, human decision-making, fairness, data and algorithm quality checks, risk classification, and an AI Ethics Council. IBM framework record
  • The framework prohibits its highest-risk category: recommendations for or automatic decisions about personnel measures without a benefit or harm-reduction potential for a substantial part of the workforce. It also requires correction processes for false recommendations and gives works councils inspection rights over data quality, bias analysis, transparency, and system purpose. IBM framework text
  • In a 2022 interview, an IBM Group Works Council representative said the framework was created before AI applications had affected employees’ daily work and described the very-high-risk category as excluded from deployment. This is a worker-representative account, not an audited deployment record. BTQ Kassel interview
  • In the IBM example reported by ver.di, a skill-recommendation tool suggests internal development options, while a human remains responsible for the decision. ver.di
  • An NBER randomized online labor-market experiment found no significant average performance effect from digital surveillance, but unexplained changes in surveillance based on worker performance reduced output. NBER Working Paper 33348
  • JRC worker evidence across the EU associates some monitoring and management practices with lower autonomy, fewer breaks, and higher stress, while the relationship varies by practice. JRC AIM-WORK
  • The Kenyan content-moderation court record shows that legal access and interim protection can produce different outcomes across related appeals, while the underlying worker claims remain unresolved. Kenya court record

These sources do not measure one common outcome. They show why the gap between a rule and its use must be treated as an object of research.

Causal chain under test

written rule -> worker or representative can access information
-> a wrong or harmful decision can be challenged
-> the system or decision changes in time
-> work, pay, safety, status, or bargaining position improves

The first arrow is directly visible in the IBM framework. The later arrows are open tests. “A human decides” may still mean that a manager accepts a system recommendation, lacks time to review it, or treats the system as objective.

What to measure

Stage Observable test
Rule exists Is the rule written, binding, and within the worker’s employment scope?
Information Can the worker see the data, purpose, inputs, and confidence behind the recommendation?
Challenge Can the worker question the result without losing pay, status, or future opportunity?
Correction Is there a named person or body that can correct the record or model?
Timing Does correction happen before promotion, discipline, scheduling, or termination?
Enforcement Are violations recorded, remedied, and visible to the worker representative?
Outcome Do monitoring, pace, pay, safety, autonomy, and mobility change?

Four-map reading

Map What the evidence adds Missing evidence
Material AI rules can affect job continuity, retraining, pace, and time. Worker-level pay, workload, and health outcomes after correction.
Social A score or recommendation can alter trust, dignity, and confidence in one’s own judgment. Interviews or surveys of workers who challenged a system.
Institution Agreements create councils, review steps, and duties for firms and representatives. Records of actual disputes and enforcement.
Power Information and correction rights can limit system owners’ control. Whether workers can act without retaliation or career cost.

Wider social and geopolitical meaning

The workplace rule is also a small test of a wider political question: can institutions govern systems they do not fully own? When software, data, and work cross borders, a local agreement may protect workers in one legal entity while the model, vendor, or lead firm sits elsewhere. The Kenya case shows why the path from local work to lead-firm responsibility must be traced rather than assumed.

This is an inference from the combined evidence, not a claim that workplace agreements determine state power.

Strongest challenge

Formal rules may be enough in many workplaces. Managers may already review recommendations carefully, and written limits may prevent harm even when no dispute is recorded. The current evidence cannot tell whether the gap between formal rights and lived control is large or small.

What would change our mind

  • Audited records showing that workers can routinely inspect, challenge, and correct AI decisions before adverse action.
  • Evidence that written controls produce similar autonomy, stress, pay, and mobility outcomes for workers inside and outside such agreements.
  • Evidence that human review is independent, timely, and not merely approval of the system’s recommendation.

Next tests

  1. Find IBM or works-council records showing an actual AI recommendation that was corrected, blocked, or redesigned.
  2. Compare the IBM framework with an unorganized workplace using a similar tool.
  3. Add worker-level evidence on appeals, promotion, discipline, and retaliation.
  4. Track whether controls survive a vendor change, restructuring, or model update.
  5. Compare direct employees with contractors and workers in cross-border AI supply chains.

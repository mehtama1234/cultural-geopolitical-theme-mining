Matched evidence · US rent risk and insurance

Rent insurance may protect a home before the crisis, but access is the problem.

A model finds that rent-guarantee insurance could lower deposits and homelessness. It also finds that broad coverage would not work financially without restricting who gets it.

In one sentence

The people most likely to need protection may be least able to pay a premium or pass the screen needed to make private coverage work.

This is a design clue, not a measured US market result.

The connection

income or health shock → rent cannot be paid → insurance claim, public aid, legal help, or no protection → landlord receives payment or starts a case → housing, credit, and household path

The central problem is selection: who can buy protection before the shock, and who is left with the loss?

Four views

Household

Count premium, deposit, rent, income risk, health cost, claim, and future payment.

Resident

Ask whether coverage protects the tenant’s home or simply helps a landlord collect.

Money and finance

Separate pooled risk, premium, deductible, debt, credit, and public cost.

Public power

Choose who is protected, when, and at what cost; do not hide distribution inside “insurance.”

Next test

Follow a US pilot for two years. Compare insured, publicly supported, and uninsured renters after similar income or health shocks, measuring prevented displacement and later payment burden.

Reading rule

Do not call a model a market. Ask who can buy protection before the shock.

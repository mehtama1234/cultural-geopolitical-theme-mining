Infrastructure, technology, and dependency: source-led subtheme map v1

Checked: 2026-09-24
Scope: 223 registry records carry infrastructure_technology_dependency. This map separates project selection, construction, operation, cost allocation, access, and strategic dependence. A commitment, rule, or aggregate capacity is not delivery or a public benefit.

What the source material supports

capacity is not service; selection is not construction; a cost-allocation rule is not an observed bill; funding availability is not a usable route; and supplier concentration is not political control.

1. Large-load growth makes allocation and reliability a public-governance question

The Virginia large-load record records 1,551 MW and 1,800 MW large-load drops in 2024 and 2025. Virginia's 2025 order imposed 25-MW large-customer thresholds, minimum demand charges, and 14-year terms effective in 2027; a Rider T1 methodology reduced a projected typical residential increase from $2.90 to $0.94 monthly. Those are utility events and regulatory projections—not facility load, observed household bills, full cost recovery, or environmental outcome.

Supported subtheme: fast-growing technical demand turns system reliability and who bears expansion costs into linked governance questions.
Not established: realized customer burden, completed interconnection, environmental impact, or legitimacy.

1a. A modeled allocation rule and a proposed bill credit are different household-benefit stages

The Virginia residential-relief record keeps two large-load cost routes apart. The State Corporation Commission's approved amended transmission-allocation method modeled a typical 1,000-kWh residential increase of $0.94 per month rather than $2.90 under the earlier method, while a company announcement proposed a separate $10 monthly residential credit for four years, subject to the described funding route and future implementation. Neither source supplies customer eligibility, bill posting, receipt, net bill change, avoided costs, or reliability results.

Supported subtheme: large-load governance can yield a regulatory allocation framework and a corporate relief proposal at the same time, but projected cost exposure, approved tariffs, issued credits, and actual affordability are not interchangeable evidence.

Not established: that a particular household receives a credit, that the credit offsets data-center-related costs, a complete ratepayer incidence, customer satisfaction, or durable affordability and reliability improvement.

The cross-state large-load governance record shows why the rule stage must be named precisely. Virginia approved an 85% minimum share of covered transmission/distribution charges, a 14-year minimum contract obligation, and collateral capped at 60% of minimum charges; its supplemental docket then required work on direct-connect line-extension terms. Texas proposed—not finalized—a 20-year minimum interconnection agreement, direct cash contributions, and annual customer-class revenue reporting. Georgia's commission requested projected/actual load and cost-recovery fields, but a later second large-load contract filing was unexecuted and claimed trade secret status in its entirety. More than 600 speakers and hundreds of pages of comments in a related Northern Virginia proceeding make contestation visible, not its influence or consensus.

Additional boundary: approved tariff terms, a proposal, an evidentiary request, a confidential unexecuted contract, public participation, and an implementation docket are not interchangeable governance outcomes. The Virginia $0.94 versus $2.90 typical-bill comparison remains a model inside a proceeding, not an observed before/after bill.

1b. A large-load operating event is stronger realization evidence than a forecast, but not a household reliability result

The PJM large-load operating record records a dated July 2026 event in which approximately 4,000 MW of data-center load disconnected. Facilities switched to backup generation while PJM and Dominion managed the resulting load-generation imbalance and transmission voltage/frequency spikes. PJM then proposed minimum ride-through requirements and a transition process for existing facilities; subsequent FERC and DOE materials document standards direction, stakeholder process, cost-responsibility principles, and emergency operating authority. These are operating and governance responses, not adopted/implemented requirements or a customer outcome.

Supported subtheme: large-load dependence has crossed from projected interconnection and allocation risk into a named grid-response event, creating a concrete path from facility behavior to operator intervention and prospective reliability control.
Not established: event duration, facility roster, residential interruption or avoided outage, bill incidence, backup-generation emissions, final standard adoption/compliance, local acceptance, or durable reliability.

2. Federal transmission support does not establish energized lines or household benefit

The DOE transmission record lists eight capacity-contract projects across two rounds; four had termination or withdrawal notices, and energization was not established for the set. Planned capacity and financing commitments are therefore a pre-delivery stage.

Supported subtheme: infrastructure programs need a distinct realization ledger: selection → financing → permitting/construction → energization → service → rates/reliability.
Not established: a completion/failure rate, delivered electricity, local consent, rate incidence, or reliability gain.

2a. Large grid-recovery obligations can coexist with limited disbursement, completion, and operational stability

The Puerto Rico grid-recovery record tracks federal recovery since 2017. GAO reports about $14 billion obligated by FEMA, HUD, and DOE, including $11.1 billion from FEMA, but only $2.7 billion of FEMA's obligations disbursed. As of February 2026, 400 of 16,000 planned miles of transmission/distribution vegetation clearing had been completed with federal funds and nine large FEMA projects were reported complete. GAO reports limited progress in key areas and more frequent outages, including an almost two-day April 2025 blackout; it also identifies coordination, staffing, review, financial-condition, and role-clarity barriers.

Supported subtheme: obligation, disbursement, project completion, maintenance activity, operating stability, and household service are distinct recovery stages. Large authorized funding cannot be read as an energized, reliable, or equitable grid.
Not established: a same-customer outage or bill effect, health or business consequence, migration, benefit distribution, causal attribution of outages, or the effect of implementing GAO's recommendations. Mileage clearing and project counts are not a full reliability or completion rate.

3. Funding can exist while application coordination leaves a community unserved

The rural-water record identifies 28 federal assistance programs. It reports multiple applications, different deadlines and restrictions, limited agency data on unserved communities, and a case where a missed federal-grant timing prevented a state match. Selected sites and interviews are not a national failure-rate estimate.

Supported subtheme: administrative capacity is part of infrastructure capacity; finance that cannot be navigated is not yet a completed public route. Not established: award, construction, water/wastewater service, household cost reduction, or health improvement.

3a. Public water finance can reach a measured pollution and mortality outcome without revealing each household's benefit

The safe-drinking-water and health record assembles 266 million drinking-water pollutant readings from 48 states and links area pollution measures to administrative Medicare outcomes. Across 2003–2019, the study reports the share of readings above current health standards fell by about half, though pollution remained higher in lower-income areas and the authors estimate 10%–20% of Americans still received water violating standards. In its loan analysis, the working paper reports Safe Drinking Water Act loans reduced pollution and older-American mortality, with an estimated average cost of $46 per person per year to remove excess pollution.

Supported subtheme: water infrastructure finance can be evaluated past award and construction through environmental and population-health outcomes; system capacity, pollution reduction, and health are distinct but connected stages.
Not established: which individual household received a loan-funded improvement, its actual tap-water exposure or treatment, private avoidance costs, distribution across residents or pollutants, every local causal route, trust, political action, or durable acceptance. The source is a working paper, and its public abstract does not expose the complete analytic denominator or household panel.

3b. Local electoral accountability can shape water investment and pollution before a household-level benefit is measured

The California water-accountability record combines municipal water-system investment and performance, changes in local electoral accountability, and housing transactions at water-system boundaries. Jurisdictions with weaker accountability had higher reported water pollution and lower response to public investment aid. The paper recovers residents' clean-water valuations from boundary housing transactions, then uses a dynamic political-economy model to simulate greater accountability: modeled investment shifts toward high-need systems, pollution falls, and modeled welfare gains are progressive and larger in struggling systems. The observed associations and modeled counterfactual are different evidentiary stages.

Supported subtheme: water infrastructure is also a local-accountability route: electoral conditions can be linked to investment response and measured environmental quality, while the resident valuation identifies a separate housing-market signal of service value. Political accountability, investment, realized service, valuation, and durable household protection cannot be collapsed into one outcome. Not established: a national effect, household bill or tap-water exposure, health outcome, maintenance durability, an actual electoral reform effect, implementation feasibility, or trust. Political competition can also proxy for administrative capacity, finances, media, and resident composition; welfare results depend on the model, boundary-market assumptions, and estimated preferences.

3c. Construction funding does not itself create durable water service when eligibility and maintenance are separate gates

The Tribal water-infrastructure maintenance record traces a federal-assistance route in selected Tribal settings. IHS estimated about $6 billion was needed as of November 2025 to ensure safe-water access in all Tribal communities. GAO reports that eligibility interpretation can exclude homes occupied by tribal members when ownership, lease, or provider conditions fall outside the funding rule. It also records one organization's 80 hours of work to help a Tribe obtain an $8,000 grant, before additional reporting.

The same review distinguishes post-construction operation and maintenance: IHS generally does not provide routine O&M funding, while limited local financial capacity and difficulties hiring or retaining certified operators can contribute to deterioration and early failure. Those findings map a path from funding rules through application and upkeep to a risk of service loss and health exposure; they do not measure the rate at which any stage occurs.

Supported subtheme: a water-system route has at least five noninterchangeable stages—eligibility, application capacity, construction support, routine operation and maintenance, and usable durable service. A construction award or need estimate cannot stand in for a maintained connection.

Not established: a national access or failure rate, a household's service interruption, water quality, cost, health effect, program causal effect, trust, or durable recovery. Selected GAO sites and interviews are not a prevalence sample, and the 80-hour figure is a case illustration rather than an average.

4. Strategic supplier concentration measures dependency risk, not operational or political control

The SIPRI arms-dependence record finds the U.S. accounted for 42% of global major-arms exports in 2021–25 and 58% of European NATO imports; Europe’s imports rose 210% from 2016–20. SIPRI transfer volume is neither financial value nor evidence of delivery, training, maintenance, readiness, consent, or alliance obedience.

Supported subtheme: supplier concentration is a material precondition that can shape future bargaining and sustainment exposure.
Not established: dependency in a particular crisis, political leverage, deterrence, or strategic effect.

5. Better operational routing does not by itself close a care pathway

The 988 capacity record uses a difference-in-differences design around the July 2022 rollout. States with larger pre-existing capacity gaps answered more calls in-state and raised their in-state answer rates after 988, yet the study finds no detectable short-run reduction in suicide mortality and no statistically significant improvement in broader population mental-health outcomes. The public abstract does not link callers to counseling, dispatch, treatment, housing, repeat contact, or recovery.

Supported subtheme: a service can become more reachable and locally answered while downstream care and population outcomes remain separate stages.
Not established: that no caller benefited, that access was unimportant, or that longer-run effects and the full crisis-care system are null.

6. Broadband awards and map challenges sit before completed, affordable use

The broadband deployment record reviews nine federal programs: all had begun distributing funds by February 2026, but completion data were available for only three. It notes that BEAD's $42.5 billion program is expected to complete in 2030 and beyond; provider- reported FCC maps guide targeting, while some smaller communities and providers described the challenge process as burdensome or unclear. The Tribal program had awarded $1.8 billion across 147 projects intended to serve 165,000 locations—not verified connections.

Supported subtheme: the route from appropriated money to durable digital service includes contested targeting, project completion, affordability, subscription, reliability, and operating sustainability.
Not established: a project-failure rate, map error at a named location, a completed connection, price, use, or meaningful social benefit.

6a. School AI integration is an institutional-capacity surface before it is a learning or equity outcome

The K–12 AI-integration record uses a national principal survey to separate adoption from the capacity to govern it. Students reportedly use AI mainly for homework help and writing, while educators use it primarily for lesson planning and administrative tasks; teacher training, student guidance, and school policies lag adoption. A one-standard-deviation increase in student disadvantage is associated with a 0.07–0.11-standard-deviation lower school AI-integration index. Private and charter schools score 0.23–0.44 standard deviations below traditional public schools, and district size accounts for about one-third of the public-school disadvantage gap in the reported decomposition.

Supported subtheme: technological availability, school integration, leadership capacity, teacher training, student guidance, and formal policy are different institutional layers. An apparent access gap can coexist with deliberate caution, and greater integration is not automatically a benefit.

Not established: causal effects on learning, teacher workload, student wellbeing, privacy/safety, family cost, school quality, or later inequality. The public working-paper abstract does not disclose the full sample count, response rate, or causal design.

7. Benefit-payment security creates a protection–usability–recourse sequence

The SNAP EBT modernization record documents chip-and-tap preparation, retailer system updates, magnetic-stripe fallback, PIN use, and repeated-card-replacement monitoring. At the fourth replacement request in 12 months, a state must notify a household and may withhold a card, seek an explanation, or refer suspected fraud. USDA's public pages describe transition preparation but do not supply a unified issuance, retailer-readiness, failed-transaction, theft, replacement-time, or food-gap series.

Supported subtheme: securing a public payment rail can introduce separate access, contact, explanation, delay, and false-positive points for recipients.
Not established: that the upgrade reduced theft, that a withheld card was appropriate or harmful, or that benefits were recovered in time to preserve food access and trust.

8. Cost-saving service redesign can move time burden unevenly across place

The USPS service record reports that USPS added one to two expected delivery days for some First-Class Mail in 2021 and eliminated end-of-day or afternoon collection at more than 24,000 post offices over 50 miles from a regional processing center in April 2025. GAO reports that performance targets were lowered in fiscal 2021 and mostly not met thereafter; oversight entities and stakeholders reported slower service and disproportionate rural effects. The review is not a causal estimate for an individual delivery or household.

Supported subtheme: infrastructure's financial-sustainability program can reallocate waiting and coordination burdens across rural and nonrural users.
Not established: the burden for a particular person, business, bill, medicine, ballot, or benefit claim—or the full financial counterfactual.

9. Commercial operation is stronger evidence than selection, but not a household outcome

The Champlain Hudson Power Express record reports commercial operation on May 13, 2026: Hydro-Québec power runs through a 339-mile line into New York City’s NYISO Zone J. The announcement expects 10.4 TWh of annual delivery and 20% of New York City's energy needs, but its emissions, employment, and system claims are projections. It does not report customer-level bills, rate-class incidence, realized delivery, reliability, or host-community acceptance.

Supported subtheme: operation can close the selection-to-energization link and still leave affordability, distribution, local legitimacy, and dependency consequences unresolved.
Not established: a household benefit, durable rate effect, realized reliability gain, or geopolitical leverage.

9a. Strategic reserve inventory is not the same as effective emergency-energy delivery

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve capability record shows a state-capacity layer between stored oil and an actual emergency-market response. As of December 2025, DOE estimated effective drawdown at 61% of the reserve's design rate and effective fill at 56%, citing construction outages, aging infrastructure, and low inventory; the reserve had nevertheless met drawdown directives to date. GAO also reported little spare commercial pipeline and storage capacity, which can constrain the incremental market effect of a release even when reserve oil is available.

Supported subtheme: design rate, current effective operating rate, stored inventory, past directive completion, commercial distribution, and market effect are sequential energy-security conditions rather than one capability metric.
Not established: a future shortage, release-specific price effect, refinery or household incidence, financial return, energy independence, deterrence, or geopolitical leverage.

10. A public energy program can reach a bill-credit route while persistence remains conditional

The DC Solar for All record reports nearly 10,000 income-qualified households served and about $500 average annual savings, or roughly $5 million program-wide. Community-solar credits flow through Pepco bills after document/enrollment processing; terms anticipate about 60 days for credit start, make subscription free, and condition continuity on income eligibility and an active Pepco account. Public materials do not provide customer bills, actual credit timing, credit variance, arrears, or net energy burden.

Supported subtheme: infrastructure can reach a household-facing billing mechanism, but enrollment, account status, credit posting, and persistence are separate stages from reported program reach.
Not established: verified individual savings, reduced energy insecurity, long-term enrollment, health, or household recovery.

11. National transit operating scale does not establish equal usable mobility

The 2024 transit-service record reports nearly 3,000 agencies, 7.6 billion passenger trips (up 9.8%), 38.1 billion passenger miles (up 8.7%), 380,000 workers, and more than 177,000 vehicles across 502 urbanized areas. These are agency-reported national totals over different modes and trip definitions, not a count of unique riders, essential trips, reliability, fares, or household outcomes.

Supported subtheme: operating service and use can be demonstrated at scale, while the question of whether a particular care, work, food, or civic trip is reachable and affordable remains local and unresolved.
Not established: equitable access, safety, completed essential trips, household welfare, or political response.

11a. A mobility-price rule can reach a measured emergency-service time outcome without proving a patient or equity result

The New York City congestion-pricing/EMS record uses the 60th Street pricing-zone boundary and a difference-in-discontinuities design after pricing began on January 5, 2025. It reports vehicle traffic down roughly 18%–21% near the boundary and EMS total travel time down 63–70 seconds (about 5%–6%), with little evidence of traffic displacement to adjacent areas. The authors distinguish that boundary design from a standard difference-in-differences design confounded by a concurrent FDNY nearest-hospital directive.

Supported subtheme: a transport-demand intervention can connect a price and traffic change to an actual emergency-service operating metric, rather than only a modeled mobility benefit.
Not established: survival, treatment quality, patient cost, who received a faster response, neighborhood distribution of benefits and charges, long-run traffic adaptation, the policy's full welfare balance, or public acceptance.

12. Energized transmission creates physical and cost-allocation evidence before retail benefit is visible

The Smart Path Connect record reports completion and energization of 45 miles of line, three new substations, and six modified substations. NYPA reports $438 million annual system savings and expects monthly household/business savings, while NYISO's tariff allocates the facilities charge to load-serving entities by energy withdrawals with specified offsets. A project accounting line shows a $20.46 million annual revenue requirement for 2023. Those system, tariff, and accounting units do not show a customer bill, rate-class distribution, reliability, or affordability result.

Supported subtheme: physical realization, system savings, formal cost allocation, and retail household benefit are sequential evidence layers—none can substitute for the next.
Not established: actual customer savings, equal ratepayer benefit, outage reduction, local acceptance, or durable affordability.

12a. Transmission-cost review can create an expected credit pathway before any customer sees a bill effect

The New York transmission-ratepayer-refund record follows a different route from building or energizing an asset. New York's Department of Public Service announced a $13 million-plus-interest settlement after identifying apparently abnormal operating and maintenance expenses and the collection of proposal-preparation costs from customers. The settlement restricted automatic future recovery of proposal costs; the administrator expected a 2026 refund. A subsequent FERC proceeding set a June 24, 2026 refund-effective date, while local implementation material described a pro-rata credit against monthly transmission charges that might not appear as a separately itemized invoice line.

Supported subtheme: infrastructure-cost oversight can proceed from a named charge concern through a settlement, allocation rule, refund-effective date, and expected credit mechanism. Each is more specific than a generic promise of ratepayer protection, but none is a retail benefit observed at the customer level.
Not established: affected-customer denominator, allocation by rate class, credit posting, receipt, adequacy, a net household bill change, reliability, trust, switching, or durable affordability.

13. Engineering projections can overstate household energy savings even in an experimental program evaluation

The weatherization performance record evaluates more than 30,000 Weatherization Assistance Program households. Model projections were roughly 2.5 times actual energy savings, investment costs about twice realized savings, and the study found no significant increase in indoor temperature; its broader-benefit return estimate was about negative 9.5% annually. The abstract does not resolve measures, climate, contractor, bill, comfort, air-quality, safety, or health differences at the household level.

Supported subtheme: an installed efficiency intervention and an engineering savings forecast are not the same as realized household performance; comfort, safety, and bill outcomes must be measured separately.
Not established: that every weatherization project has the same return or that lower realized savings imply no comfort, health, safety, or equity value.

Cross-subtheme finding and next test

The federal technology-modernization record adds the same realization boundary to public digital infrastructure: $1.03 billion was invested across 68 projects, while 11 reported $13.5 million in realized savings and 24 expected $1.06 billion. Investment, projected savings, realized savings, service quality, user time, equitable access, and public trust remain separate outcomes.

technical demand, project, funding program, payment rail, or supplier concentration
  -> allocation, application, targeting, financing, construction, enrollment, contract, or maintenance
  -> operating service, bill/credit, access, answer, repair, readiness, or interruption
  -> household/place/partner consequence and public or strategic judgment
  -> governance correction and next-cycle capacity

The needed evidence follows the same asset, community, customer, or partner through this whole chain, including withdrawn projects and unsuccessful applicants. Aggregate announcements cannot fill its middle.

Connected US finding · repair and household cost

Repair access is part of a product’s real price

A phone, appliance, car, or work tool can be cheap to buy and expensive to keep alive. When repair is blocked, failure can become a forced replacement.

The argument

The customer needs a workable choice: an authorized shop, independent shop, part, manual, warranty claim, or replacement. Without it, the purchase price hides a future cost.

Repair access is financial slack: it gives a household time to absorb a shock.

Follow one broken product

A delivery worker’s phone stops charging, or a family’s washer fails. The part is unavailable, software blocks a third-party fix, the approved shop is far away, or the warranty takes weeks. The household borrows a device, misses work, buys a replacement, or uses credit.

product breaks → parts, tools, software, warranty, and shop choices → price and waiting time → repair, replace, borrow, or go without → money, work, care, safety, and waste change

The deeper finding

A household that can fix a product may avoid a large payment and keep work, transport, communication, or care moving. A household that cannot may pay for replacement even when the original could have continued working.

The strongest measure is whether repair is affordable, timely, safe, and available where the customer lives. A nominal right that requires weeks without a work tool is a weak practical right.

What remains unknown

The FTC record does not join a failure to a repair quote, waiting time, replacement debt, work interruption, and later household stability.

Next test

Track product failure, repair options, parts price, labor, warranty claim, waiting time, replacement, borrowing, work or care interruption, and repeat failure. Compare authorized and independent repair by product, place, income, rural access, and disability.

Reading rule

Do not call a product affordable from its purchase price alone. Include the cost and availability of keeping it working.

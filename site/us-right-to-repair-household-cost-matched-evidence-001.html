A short argument · US repair, cost, and household time

Repair access is part of the product's real price

A product can be cheap to buy and expensive to keep alive.

The argument

The key question is whether the customer has a fair, workable choice: an authorized repair, an independent repair, a part, a manual, a warranty claim, or a replacement with a clear price. Without that choice, the purchase price hides a future cost.

Follow one broken product

A delivery worker's phone stops charging, or a family's washer fails. The part is unavailable, software blocks a third-party fix, the nearest approved shop is far away, or a warranty process takes weeks. The household borrows a device, misses work, pays for replacement, or uses credit.

product breaks → repair parts, tools, software, warranty, and shop choices → price and waiting time → repair, replace, borrow, or go without → money, work, care, safety, and waste change

The deeper finding

Repair access is financial slack. A household that can fix a product may avoid a large payment and keep work, transport, communication, or care moving. The useful measure is whether repair is affordable, timely, safe, and available where the customer lives.

What remains unproven

The FTC record identifies restrictions and legal protections but does not join a product failure to a repair quote, waiting time, replacement debt, work interruption, and later household stability.

Next test

Track product failure, repair options, parts price, labor, warranty claim, waiting time, replacement, borrowing, work or care interruption, and repeat failure. Compare authorized and independent repairs.

Reading rule

Do not call a product affordable from its purchase price alone. Include the cost and availability of keeping it working.

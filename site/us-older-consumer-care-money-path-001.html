Connected US finding · older customers, care and money

When age hides different kinds of need

People over 65 are not one customer group. Health, money, mobility, and living situation may matter more than age itself.

In one sentence

Age alone can hide both buying power and vulnerability, so a better fit can help one person and sort or pressure another.

Keep age, need, choice, price, care, and independence separate.

What the sources show

Older customers differ in health, spending, and risk

HBS reports that older adults are a large and varied market, with segments differing in health and activity. NBER evidence finds that older adults often report greater economic satisfaction as spending declines, while health, widowhood, and changing enjoyment may reduce the desire to spend. Other NBER work finds that health shocks affect consumption and that older households face income and health risks.

These sources do not follow the same older customer through a product choice, care decision, price, service result, and later independence.

The connection

age, health, wealth, and living situation → product need, ability, and willingness to pay → firm design, service, and targeting → independence, care burden, or exposure to bad advice → household money and family decisions

Lower spending can be a choice, a health effect, a loss, or a lack of money. The same number cannot tell those stories apart.

The four outside views

Household: A person weighs simplicity, trust, price, access, health, transport, and whether a product helps independence. Family may help choose or pay.

Company and sector: Firms can design for vision, hearing, mobility, memory, caregiving, and different digital comfort. They can also use loyalty or confusion to sell an unsuitable product.

Money and finance: Count income, wealth, health costs, care, insurance, price, fees, and time saved. Buying power is not the same as money safe to spend.

Public power: Consumer protection, accessible service, insurance rules, fraud prevention, and care support shape free choice. Age rules should not replace evidence about need.

What would change the finding

  • Health, disability, mobility, cognition, living arrangement, and care needs.
  • Income, wealth, insurance, debt, price, fees, and financial advice.
  • Product access, service quality, digital access, and actual use.
  • Whether a purchase improved independence or reduced care time.
  • Family influence, scams, complaints, refunds, and later outcomes.
  • Differences by age, race, gender, place, education, and support.

Next test

Follow older customers for two years across a product or care decision. Join health and living conditions to price, service, use, family help, care hours, money, complaints, and independence.

Reading rule

Do not use age as a shortcut for need. Do not call lower spending a problem or a success until the rest is visible.

Connected US finding · older customers and care

When age hides different kinds of need

People over 65 are not one customer group. Health, money, mobility, living arrangement, digital access, and care need matter more than age alone.

The argument

An older customer choosing a phone, insurance plan, delivery service, or care product is not buying from age alone. One person may value simplicity and have money to pay. Another may need transport, memory support, or fraud protection and have little room for an error.

A product aimed at “older people” can make a capable customer feel managed while making a vulnerable customer navigate a service that assumes too much.

Age can hide buying power and vulnerability at the same time.

Follow one decision

An older customer needs a new phone. She lives alone, has arthritis, uses online banking, and wants an emergency contact feature. Her neighbor wants the same phone for travel and needs no assistance. If a firm treats both as “senior customers,” it may simplify the menu, add a costly support plan, or make the phone less useful to the person who wants control.

age, health, wealth, and living situation → actual need and safe ability to pay → product design, service, and targeting → independence, care burden, or bad advice → household money and family decisions

A device easy to buy but hard to set up shifts work to family. A service preserves independence only if it also works with payment, mobility, and digital access.

The deeper finding

“Simple” is not the same as accessible. A service can remove steps for one person and remove control for another. Good design makes help available without turning age into a verdict about competence.

What remains unproven

The sources do not follow the same customer through product choice, care decision, price, service result, and later independence. They do not show whether need-based targeting improves outcomes without raising price or exposing customers to manipulation.

Next test

Follow older customers for two years across one product or care decision. Join health, mobility, living conditions, price, service, digital access, family help, care hours, complaints, refunds, money, and independence. Compare age-based targeting with design based on actual need.

Reading rule

Do not use age as a shortcut for need. Do not call lower spending a problem or success until health, money, choice, and independence are visible.

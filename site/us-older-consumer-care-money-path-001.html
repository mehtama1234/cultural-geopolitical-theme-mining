Connected US finding · older consumers and care

When age hides different kinds of need

People over 65 are not one customer group. Health, money, mobility, living arrangement, digital access, and care need matter more than age alone.

The argument

An older customer choosing a phone, insurance plan, delivery service, or care product is not buying from age alone. One person may value simplicity and have money; another may need transport, memory support, or fraud protection and have little room for an error.

Age can hide buying power and vulnerability at the same time.

What the sources establish

HBS describes older adults as a varied market, with differences in health, activity, buying power, and influence over family purchases, while companies often rely on age stereotypes.

NBER finds that older adults often report greater economic satisfaction as spending declines; health, widowhood, and changing enjoyment may reduce spending. Other NBER work finds health shocks affect consumption and that older households face income and health risks.

These sources do not follow one customer through product choice, care, price, service, and independence.

HBS over-65 market · NBER spending · NBER old-age risks

Follow the need

age, health, wealth, and living situation → product need and ability to pay → firm design and targeting → independence, care burden, or bad advice → household money and family decisions

Lower spending can be a choice, a health effect, better fit, financial loss, or reduced ability to use a service.

Keep these apart

  • Age and actual need.
  • Buying power and safe-to-spend money.
  • Simplicity and exclusion.
  • Purchase and improved independence.
  • Lower spending and better well-being.

Next test

Follow older customers for two years across one product or care decision. Join health, mobility, living conditions, price, service, digital access, family help, complaints, money, and independence.

Reading rule

Do not use age as a shortcut for need.

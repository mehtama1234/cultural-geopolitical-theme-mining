Matched evidence · US renters and energy

The person paying the bill may not control the fix.

A renter can face the monthly cost while the landlord controls the building, appliance, insulation, and heating system.

In one sentence

Energy efficiency can fail as a household solution when the person carrying the burden cannot choose the upgrade.

A lower bill is not a renter benefit until the renter can reach it and keep it.

The connection

renter pays energy bill → landlord controls building upgrade → upgrade is delayed, funded, or shared through a bill tool → bill, comfort, rent, and property value change → energy burden and housing stability change

The sources support the control problem and possible financing bridge. They do not yet measure a common renter outcome across upgraded and unchanged homes.

The useful surprise

The person with the strongest reason to save energy may have the least power to change the home.

Four views

Household

Count bill, comfort, repair requests, rent, move cost, and money left for other needs.

Customer and resident

Record who controls equipment, who pays, who receives savings, and whether the tenant can stay during work.

Money and finance

Separate upgrade cost, rebate, on-bill charge, rent change, bill savings, and debt.

Public power

Test rental standards, incentives, disclosure, utility programs, landlord duties, and pass-through limits.

Next test

Link same-home energy bills before and after an upgrade to rent, tenure, landlord investment, and household stability.

Reading rule

Show who paid for the fix, who controlled it, and who kept the savings.

A prescription event can be followed by mixed health and work transitions

Status: bounded same-person MEPS follow-up comparison
Checked: 2026-09-20

The bounded finding

Among 18,691 MEPS person records with valid three-round ordering, 724 had a first prescription event strictly inside the R3/1-to-R4/2 window and 17,967 formed the complementary comparison group. In the later R4/2-to-R5/3 clock, the prescription-event group did not show one simple trajectory:

Later measure Prescription event No event in window
Health worsened 19.62% (SE 1.79; n=684) 22.16% (SE 0.46; n=17,553)
Fair/poor health at follow-up 9.47% (SE 1.34; n=684) 10.85% (SE 0.34; n=17,584)
Employment status changed 13.15% (SE 1.68; n=493) 9.32% (SE 0.24; n=14,499)
Nonemployment onset 12.81% (SE 3.65; n=140) 7.11% (SE 0.41; n=6,089)
Nonemployment resolved 11.43% (SE 3.13; n=143) 5.19% (SE 0.40; n=5,986)

The later health measures are not higher in this descriptive comparison, while employment movement and both entry into and exit from nonemployment are more common in the event group. The small transition cells have wide uncertainty.

Why it matters

This adds a second clock to the health-cost chain:

prescription event
  -> later health and work status
  -> [open] fill, adherence, treatment continuity, and payment burden
  -> [open] household adaptation, remedy, trust, action, or exit

A later health or employment value is an outcome surface, not automatically recovery. The mixed directions are useful because they resist the easy story that a prescription event must produce either straightforward deterioration or straightforward improvement.

Do not overread

This is a month-ordered descriptive follow-up, not a causal treatment effect. The prescription event may be downstream of an earlier health need, and the comparison group may contain events outside the selected window. The data do not identify prescription fill or non-fill, adherence, treatment completion, payment or coverage obligation, hours or earnings, household care/time, or an event-specific institutional remedy. Employment status change is not job loss, and nonemployment resolution is not restored security.

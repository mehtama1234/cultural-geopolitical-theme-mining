Uber / Kyei timing-extension record v1

Decision: [2025] FWC 2269, Kwasi Kyei v Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd (UDE2025/105)

Finding: the Commission extended the filing period and allowed the case to proceed to directions; it did not decide the deactivation merits.

Why this case matters

Kyei adds a timing-and-case-management endpoint to the Australian census. It shows that the statutory 21-day filing period is a substantive access gate, but not an automatic merits bar. The Commission found exceptional circumstances despite a 60-day delay because Kyei challenged the deactivation, sought an internal review, and had an arguable case that the platform process was deficient.

Primary record

Kyei had worked as an Uber driver since approximately 2014. The Commission accepted Uber’s position that the deactivation took effect on 19 March 2025, rather than the later date suggested by Kyei. The application was filed on 8 June 2025, outside the 21-day statutory period.

The Commission found that Kyei knew of the deactivation and had the benefit of the filing period, and that lack of knowledge of the limitation period alone would not be enough. But the full set of factors amounted to exceptional circumstances: Kyei disputed the allegations soon after receiving them, asked to be heard, pursued the respondent’s internal review, and presented an arguable case that the decision was based on limited information without substantive input on recent allegations. The extension was granted and the matter was to be relisted with directions.

The extension is not a finding that Uber acted unfairly. The arguable merits issue is preserved as a reason the case was allowed to continue, not promoted as an adjudicated process failure.

Cross-case use

Kyei should be read with Waheed, Jibril, Singh, and Phillipps-Lewis. Together, these cases show that access to the merits route can be shaped by:

  • when the worker reasonably became aware of deactivation;
  • whether pre-commencement work can count toward the statutory period;
  • whether platform and contract identity match the alleged access restriction;
  • whether the work pattern is regular even below illustrative thresholds; and
  • whether delay, internal review, and an arguable Code issue justify an extension.

The practical implication is that a remedy ledger must preserve timing and case-management events, rather than counting only final merits decisions.

Open acquisition

Search UDE2025/105 for the relisting outcome, final Code/merits decision, reactivation, lost-pay order, payment, and continued access. The current public record establishes only the extension and procedural continuation.

Where the bottleneck is concentrated

The largest open-arrow clusters are not one universal societal chain. They are several recurring conversion problems:

  1. Worker remedies: public orders and reported payment can be observed, but payment receipt, continued or alternative work, household recovery, and durable trust are usually not retained in the public record. The DOL Workers Owed Wages route adds an earlier handoff: even recovered wages can remain in a locate/notice/claim process before the worker can use them.
  2. Consumer and public redress: distribution, reissue, or a reported repair can be observed, but recipient-level receipt, account correction, adequacy, and later switching are usually missing. The new SNAP EBT route adds a sharper control failure: an issued benefit can be stolen before it becomes usable food access. The new Medicare Advantage route adds a second gate: an authorization can be formally reversed after an appeal while admission, interim care, and functional recovery remain unknown.
  3. Infrastructure and geopolitical capability: governance, capacity, schedules, and route decisions can be observed, but household incidence, acceptance, delivered/accepted output, and changed external behavior remain open.
  4. Political meaning: hardship, trust, approval, contact, and action can be measured on related surfaces, but a dated burden, attribution, remedy, and later response are rarely held on the same unit.

The UAS route now makes the required middle fields concrete rather than abstract. Its public documentation identifies respondent-wave identity, weights, dated shocks, and later wellbeing; UAS 537 adds billing contact, effort/time, respect, and reported billing result; UAS 698 adds outside help, amount, foregone care, and reasons such as cost, delay, scheduling, work/care conflict, and transportation. These fields could connect obligation to choice and institutional response, but the route remains registration-gated and no respondent file, overlap, receipt, adequacy, or later recovery is currently observed. Treat this as an acquisition specification, not a completed bridge.

Formal access/control checkpoint

The formal-access/control synthesis now consolidates four bounded routes: SNAP EBT theft, Medicare Advantage prior-authorization reversal, wildfire-insurance partial payment, and consumer redress. They do not share one cause or effect. They share a measurement rule: formal availability must be separated from usable control.

The route boundary is often the decisive selection mechanism. A person who cannot notice a theft, file an appeal, gather documents, wait safely, cash a payment, or use a replacement disappears from later success counts. This does not prove that the institution failed in every case; it means a distribution, reversal, or approval cannot stand in for recipient-level recovery.

This checkpoint changes the next-pass design in one practical way: retain failed and successful route attempts in the same denominator. For each episode, capture notice/knowledge, effort, interim substitute, decision, actual receipt or access, adequacy, and later durability before interpreting trust, switching, voice, or exit.

What this changes about the next pass

The next pass should not add another broad sentiment or aggregate distribution count. It should target one of these compact, lawful follow-ups:

  • an authorized UAS Older Ages or PSID file with a stable respondent key and a dated event followed by effort, outcome, and later persistence;
  • a recipient/account-level redress or repair follow-up with status, receipt, correction, and later use; or
  • an identifier-bearing defense or infrastructure follow-up that distinguishes planned delivery from shipment, acceptance, fielding, operation, and changed behavior.

If those inputs are unavailable, the correct output is an explicit acquisition boundary, not a stronger causal claim. The current portfolio already has broad coverage; its limiting factor is observability of the middle and the later outcome.

Priority decision after the worker-route recheck

The worker-remedy lane is the highest-yield public partial chain currently retained. The Mudiyanselage case contains a bounded job-loss episode, forced exit, constrained alternative employment, partial payment, compensation order, and worker-linked refusal to return because trust was broken. The official later-outcome search found no public final-payment, compliance, new-job durability, household, or later-action record. This is an acquisition boundary—not evidence that the order failed.

The next serious acquisition should therefore seek a privacy-preserving payroll, compliance, worker-confirmed, or later-employment follow-up for one named remedy case. Its minimum fields are payment receipt, accepted work or non-use, pay/hours/commute trade-off, later work status, recurrence, and trust or exit where directly measured. Until that exists, do not promote the worker case to durable recovery or practical exit, and do not replace it with another aggregate remedy count.

Method boundary

Counts were computed from each ledger's arrows array in analysis/data/us-broad-event-ledger-index-v1.json, retaining only arrows with status: "open". Gap-family counts use phrase matching against each arrow's remaining_gap field and are intentionally overlapping. The machine-readable version is the open-middle blocker audit JSON. The scope-check script independently recomputes the 102-ledger, 316-event, 484-arrow, and 176-open-arrow scope and prints an exploratory phrase screen. Its phrase screen is deliberately not used to overwrite the stored family counts, because the calibrated family classification and the exploratory token groups are different diagnostics. These are planning diagnostics, not statistical estimates of societal prevalence.

Connected US finding · transport, service and work

When customer protection changes who can provide the ride

A license rule may be meant to protect riders, but its first effect may be on who can enter the work and how many rides are available.

In one sentence

Customer protection must be measured in the ride, not only in the rule.

Count entry, supply, fare, wait, quality, and safety.

What the source shows

Licensing did not consistently improve measured outcomes

Using trip-level Uber data, driver ratings, and telematics-based driving measures, NBER finds no consistent evidence that licensing improves consumer outcomes. In Houston, drivers who entered after deregulation looked like previously licensed drivers on ratings and driving behavior.

The study does not show fares, wait times, worker income, entry costs, or serious safety incidents.

NBER Working Paper 35635

The connection

licensing rule → who can drive and at what cost → driver pool and trip supply → customer rating, behavior, fare, and wait → safety and worker access

Licensing may protect riders through checks or standards not captured by these measures.

The four outside views

Household: A rider may value safety, short wait, low fare, or service to a difficult route. The rule can improve one part and worsen another.

Company and sector: Platforms, drivers, insurers, cities, and taxi firms share the result. Entry costs may reduce supply while standards reduce risk.

Money and finance: License fees, training, insurance, fares, surge prices, cancellations, and worker income decide who pays for the rule.

Public power: Licensing, checks, enforcement, data access, disability access, labor rules, and transport alternatives decide protection in practice.

What would change the finding

  • Licensing cost, time, training, checks, enforcement, and driver entry.
  • Driver pool, fares, wait, cancellations, routes, ratings, and serious incidents.
  • Worker income, expenses, outside jobs, and customer repeat use.
  • City, platform, neighborhood, disability, language, and trip type.
  • Customer trust and access where other transport is weak.

Next test

Follow cities, drivers, and riders for one year around a licensing change. Join entry, exit, fares, wait, cancellations, routes, ratings, incidents, worker income, and customer access.

Reading rule

Do not judge a rule from its stated purpose or entry count alone. Keep licensing, supply, quality, fare, wait, and safety separate.

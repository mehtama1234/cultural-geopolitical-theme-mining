Finding 011: Planning can change before measured output does

Status: provisional
Last checked: 2026-09-11
Project: AI, work, and control

Finding

The first economic effect of AI may appear in planning, capital spending, data work, training, and the redesign of production before it appears as a clear rise in output, pay, or employment. This makes “AI adoption” a weak end point. We need to ask what firms are preparing to change and whether the change is later realized.

What we directly know

  • BEA's 2026 paper compares business expectations of AI use with later reported use. It finds that expectations were not always realized and that a late-2025 change in the Census survey question created a break that nearly doubled reported use. BEA: AI Expectations and Outcomes
  • The same paper finds mixed links between stated adoption motivations and later industry outcomes. The labor-automation motivation is associated with increased capital inputs, but not with a statistically significant relative decline in labor's contribution to output growth. BEA: AI Expectations and Outcomes
  • BEA says its results suggest structural change may be in the planning process but not yet observed in outcome data. BEA Digital Economy
  • A BIS firm study reports a 4% short-run labor-productivity increase, no short-run employment reduction, higher wages in adopting firms, and larger gains for medium and large firms. BIS Working Paper 1325

These records do not conflict. They observe different stages: expectations and production changes, then measured firm outcomes. They also use different units and methods.

Causal chain under test

business expectation or use case
  -> investment in software, data, training, R&D, or equipment
  -> work process and task structure change
  -> measured output, labor demand, wages, prices, or margins change
  -> gains and risks are distributed through firms, workers, households, and states

The early arrows are plausible and partly observed. The full chain is not yet established.

Why this matters

A firm can report an AI plan before workers experience a new tool. A sector can increase capital and R&D before productivity statistics move. A country can announce AI capacity while still lacking power, skills, data, or institutions. If research starts with the final outcome only, it misses the period when choices about ownership and control are being made.

Measurement cautions

  1. “Adoption” changes meaning when survey questions change.
  2. Business motivation is not proof that the intended result occurred.
  3. Industry averages can hide different firm sizes, workers, and investment paths.
  4. Capital deepening may raise output without replacing workers in the short run.
  5. A modeled or planned gain is not a realized gain.

Four-map reading

What would change our mind

  • Planning and complementary investment do not precede measurable changes once timing and selection are handled.
  • Survey-definition changes do not materially alter adoption trends.
  • Firm size, ownership, and complementary capacity do not predict who captures the result.
  • Early capital and process changes produce no later difference in work, output, pay, prices, or margins across settings.

Next tests

  1. Build a time-ordered panel: stated goal, investment, system function, worker exposure, output, wages, prices, and employment.
  2. Separate survey-definition breaks from real changes in use.
  3. Compare firms that plan to automate labor with firms that plan to expand products or improve quality.
  4. Match BEA/BLS production accounts to worker and firm evidence without treating industry averages as worker outcomes.
  5. Compare large and small firms and countries with different power, data, skills, and institutional complements.

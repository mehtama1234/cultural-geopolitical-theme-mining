Matched evidence · US small-business support and size rules

A wider small-business rule can push the smallest firms out.

When a US program lets larger firms qualify as “small,” it may reach more firms while giving less room to the smallest ones.

In one sentence

An eligibility change can quietly change who receives public demand, without telling us yet whether customers or workers got better value.

Public reach and public value are different measures.

The connection

eligibility rule changes → larger firms gain access → public money and demand move → smallest firms lose revenue, investment, or survival → wages, innovation, customer choice, and local ownership change

A program can look broader while becoming less reachable for the firm with the least cash and fewest other buyers.

Four views

Household

Follow owner income, debt, wealth, and family security instead of treating firm revenue as household welfare.

Company and workers

Separate firm exit from worker pay, hours, benefits, and the next job.

Customer

Check price, quality, delivery, repair, and the number of real alternatives.

Public power

State the goal first: price, reliability, local ownership, new firms, jobs, or innovation.

Next test

Follow one changed program across two years: identify which firms gained contracts, which lost them, and what happened to prices, quality, workers, innovation, and local choice.

Reading rule

Do not call a program fair because more firms qualify. Ask which firms gained, which lost demand, and what customers and workers received afterward.

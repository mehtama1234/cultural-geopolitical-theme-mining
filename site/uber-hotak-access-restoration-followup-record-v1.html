Uber / Hotak access-restoration follow-up record v1

Status: primary Full Bench record establishes voluntary reactivation and continued work; lost-pay amount, receipt, and durability remain open

Checked: 2026-09-15

Why this record matters

Hotak supplies the first public follow-up in the Australian packet that goes beyond a reactivation statement. The agreed facts recorded by the Full Bench state that Uber restored access on 19 May 2025, the worker recommenced work, and he completed more than 150 trips afterward. This is evidence of continued access and work activity after voluntary reactivation, not proof of payment, full restoration of the pre-deactivation position, or freedom from later retaliation.

What the record proves

The record supports four separate propositions:

  1. Access was restored before the final merits decision.
  2. Restoration was operational enough for the worker to perform more than 150 trips, so the packet can code continued work activity rather than merely an asserted account change.
  3. Voluntary restoration did not extinguish the Commission's jurisdiction or eliminate the need for a formal order.
  4. The formal order preserved the possibility of restoring the worker's financial and contractual position, not only reopening an app account.

The record does not prove that the worker received lost remuneration, that the account remained active after the reported 150 trips, that ratings or profiles were corrected, or that Uber changed its process for other workers.

Targeted public-record recheck (2026-09-16)

The official Fair Work Commission decision index and bulletin route were rechecked for UDE2025/53, Hotak, and a later lost-remuneration order. The recheck located the Full Bench procedural decision and the merits decision, both of which preserve the lost-remuneration question; it did not locate a later public filing that verifies payment receipt, net recovery, durable access, or non-retaliation. This is an indexed-public-record boundary, not evidence that payment did not occur or that the worker stopped working.

Interpretation

Hotak demonstrates why implementation evidence needs a clock and a unit. A worker can return to the platform and produce observable work activity while the legal system is still deciding whether a formal order is necessary and what lost remuneration would restore the pre-deactivation position. The institutional remedy therefore has at least two dimensions: renewed capacity to work and reconstruction of the income position lost during exclusion.

It also sharpens the corporate-identity theme. The Full Bench agreed facts identify Rasier Pacific as the platform operator and distinguish it from Uber B.V. within the services arrangement. That named respondent structure made it possible to litigate both access and the broader restoration question. It does not resolve whether the worker could enforce every part of the order in practice.

Decisive next acquisition

Locate the later lost-remuneration order or party communication, then seek worker-side evidence of payment and continued access. Preserve separate dates and evidence classes for voluntary reactivation, formal reactivation order, trip activity, payment, and later recurrence.

Boundary

The record establishes access restoration and subsequent trip activity in a primary adjudicative document. It does not establish the worker's net income, payment receipt, ongoing account status, corrected platform record, or protection from retaliation.

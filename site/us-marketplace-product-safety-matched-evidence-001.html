Matched evidence · US marketplaces and household safety

A visible listing does not prove a safe product.

A seller, review, or safety mark may appear on screen while the product still carries a risk the customer cannot see.

In one sentence

Safety depends on what happens after the signal: detection, removal, recall, refund, and recovery.

Seller verification is not proof that a product is safe.

Five layers

LayerWhat the evidence saysWhat remains open
SignalListings may show seller identity, reviews, and safety marks.Whether the signal is true and understood.
RiskCPSC says fake safety labels can mislead buyers and move hazardous products online.Product-level exposure and injury.
VerificationINFORM requires covered marketplaces to verify seller information and provide reporting tools.Whether verification finds unsafe or repeat sellers.
ResponseCPSC and FTC provide reporting, recall, return, refund, and dispute paths.Removal speed, refund rate, and who carries the loss.
Household resultA bad product can create replacement cost, injury, lost time, or distrust.A joined record from purchase to recovery.

The connection

seller and safety signal shown → customer believes the product is acceptable → counterfeit, unsafe, or non-conforming product arrives → report, recall, return, refund, or injury → household money, health, and trust change

The sources support the risk and response systems. They do not yet join one US listing to a customer's purchase, harm, and recovery.

The useful surprise

More information can still leave the customer carrying the hardest part: detecting danger, reporting it, stopping use, returning the product, and waiting for money back.

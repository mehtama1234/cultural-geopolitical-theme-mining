Matched evidence · US bank customers and deposits

Staying with a bank can save effort while giving up interest.

A familiar account can be useful and still pay less than another option. The same customer habit can help a bank's funding and weaken competition.

In one sentence

The customer's convenience and the bank's stability may be real, while the customer still gives up interest.

A low rate is not enough by itself to prove harm.

The connection

account opens → bills and income attach to it → switching effort
              → lower rate accepted or better offer pursued
              → customer interest and bank funding
              → competition, local bank choice, and stability

The research supports low switching and a bank benefit from that behavior. It does not show that every inactive customer is being exploited.

The two-sided tradeoff

The customer's cost is the possible interest gap minus fees, taxes, time, risk, and service differences. The bank's gain is steady funding that may help it lend and survive stress. The right test is whether customers can compare and move when the gain is worth the effort without losing needed services or safety.

Four views

Household

Calculate interest difference, fees, time, access, and emergency needs.

Customer

Compare full account terms, not the advertised rate alone.

Money and finance

Separate deposit return, bank funding cost, liquidity, and default risk.

Public power

Ask whether disclosure and switching tools improve choice while preserving local options.

Next test

Pair deposit rates with account balances, fees, switching actions, automatic payments, branch or digital access, and bank risk.

Reading rule

Do not call a depositor asleep or a bank exploitative until you compare the rate gap with switching cost and the account’s actual service and safety value.

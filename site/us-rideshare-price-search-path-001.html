Connected US finding · transport, prices and household money

When app convenience leaves money on the trip

Two apps can show different prices for the same ride while most riders check only one.

In one sentence

The fare gap matters alongside comparison effort, wait, reliability, and the choices a rider can make.

Keep convenience and total customer cost together.

What the source shows

Price gaps existed while comparison was rare

An NBER audit of 2,238 matched New York City trips found an average absolute price gap of about $3.50, or roughly 14% of the average fare. Only about 16% of people who opened one app also opened the other, and neither app was consistently cheaper.

The study does not show income, service quality, final cost, or later switching for each rider.

NBER Working Paper 34441

The connection

two platforms show different prices → effort and habit of checking → app chosen → fare paid and money left → customer power and platform competition

A higher price can buy better service or save time. It is not automatically a loss.

The four outside views

Household: A rider may need to arrive on time, carry a child, reach work, or feel safe. The cheapest fare is not always the lowest total cost.

Company and sector: Platforms benefit when an app becomes the default and comparison takes effort. Prices, rewards, maps, and wait estimates shape the choice.

Money and finance: The ride costs fare, tip, time, and any missed work or care. Repeated small gaps can matter to a low-cash household.

Public power: Data access, platform design, fee disclosure, transport alternatives, and competition rules decide whether riders can compare and switch.

What would change the finding

  • Rider income, time value, trip purpose, disability, safety, and alternatives.
  • Posted and final fare, tip, discount, wait, cancellation, reliability, and quality.
  • App opening, comparison time, booking, repeat use, and switching.
  • Platform rules that limit comparison or make one app the default.
  • Results outside New York City and over a longer period.

Next test

Follow riders and matched trips for six months. Join both app prices, comparison, wait, cancellations, quality, trip purpose, income, total cost, and switching.

Reading rule

A price gap is not automatically a customer loss. Keep posted price, comparison effort, service quality, chosen app, and total value separate.

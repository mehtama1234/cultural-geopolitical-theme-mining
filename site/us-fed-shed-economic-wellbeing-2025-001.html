Finding 001: Personal financial well-being, local economic judgment, and national economic judgment move on different tracks

Status: bounded household-meaning and political-judgment finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

The Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking found that 73% of adults said they were doing okay financially or living comfortably near the end of 2025. But only 45% rated their local economy good or excellent, and only 25% rated the national economy good or excellent. Price increases were a major concern for 53%, and 28% said they were worse off than a year earlier.

Personal financial well-being was sharply unequal. Eighty-six percent of adults with at least a bachelor's degree were doing okay or comfortable, compared with 41% of adults with less than a high-school degree. The comparable figures were 82% for Asian adults, 79% for White adults, 62% for Hispanic adults, and 60% for Black adults. Adults age 18–29 were at 63%, compared with 83% among adults age 60 or older.

The practical route is:

prices, jobs, income, and household conditions
  -> personal financial position and interpretation
  -> judgment of local and national economic conditions
  -> [open] blame, trust, spending, voting, protest, policy demand, and persistence

The cultural insight is that “the economy” is not one experience. A person can say they are managing okay while judging the country poorly, or feel personal decline while living in a place they still view favorably. These judgments are shaped by group position and reference points; they should not be collapsed into one approval or confidence measure.

What this adds to the atlas

This adds a current personal-to-local-to-national meaning layer beside the existing hardship, care, price, trust, and political-action records. It shows where interpretation enters the broad atlas: material conditions are not automatically translated into one public judgment or one political response.

Limits and next test

  • SHED is a cross-sectional survey of adults, fielded in October 2025; it does not follow the same household through a dated shock or later outcome.
  • Self-assessed financial well-being and economic ratings are not directly comparable objective measures of income, prices, or local performance.
  • The subgroup gaps are descriptive and do not identify their causes.
  • The report does not show whether respondents changed spending, voting, trust, protest, policy preferences, or political identity because of these judgments.
  • A national/local judgment gap does not prove misinformation, partisan bias, or policy failure.

The next decisive evidence would repeat the same respondents or link a dated price, job, or local event to attribution, institutional trust, spending, voting, organizing, or later persistence.

Source

  • Federal Reserve: Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025

Evidence status: official survey estimates establish separate personal, local, and national economic judgments with large subgroup differences; causal meaning, attribution, action, and persistence remain open.

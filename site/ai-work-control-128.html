Finding 312: Privacy control is measurable at the request desk before it is verified in a person's data or life

Status: bounded digital consumer-power finding · Checked: 2026-09-20

The bounded finding

Walmart's 2025 California privacy metrics show that the company received 3,199 requests to delete personal information, completed 1,962, and denied 1,237; the reported median completion time was 22 days. It reported 1,914 access requests, with 1,588 completed and 326 denied, and 17,367 requests to stop selling personal information, with 17,276 completed and 91 denied.

This is useful middle-stage evidence: a person can submit a request, encounter verification or an exemption, and receive an administrative completion or denial within a reported time frame. But the metrics stop before independently verified deletion, residual exposure, satisfaction, trust, switching, or a change in everyday behavior. Sale opt-out, access, and deletion are different forms of control and should not be collapsed.

privacy concern
  -> request and identity/authorization check
  -> completed or denied route with elapsed time
  -> verified data removal / adequate explanation / trust or switching [open]

Why it matters for the larger atlas

This gives the digital/platform lane an actual administrative outcome rather than only a right on paper or a public awareness measure. It also sharpens a broader atlas rule: an institution may be able to process a request while the affected person still cannot see, verify, or control the final state of the underlying data.

Evidence boundary

  • These are company-reported aggregate metrics for 2025, updated in 2026.
  • “Completed” does not independently prove that every relevant data element was removed or that the person considered the result adequate.
  • Denials combine verification, authorization, and valid-exemption pathways in the published explanation; their separate shares are not reported.
  • The record does not measure privacy harm, trust, switching, or wellbeing.

Next test

Pair request-level status with a de-identified verification audit: identity check, data categories matched, deletion or opt-out result, exemption, repeat request, residual exposure, and later customer choice.

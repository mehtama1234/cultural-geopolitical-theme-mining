Cross-source reading · AI, work, and public capacity

AI can widen capability and dependence at the same time.

Four source layers help explain why an adoption headline is not yet a social outcome. The World Bank describes the complements required to use AI well; the IMF models separate wage and wealth channels; the Office of Financial Research shows how public capacity can change alongside staffing and infrastructure; and NBER/O*NET records a task-level measurement layer. Together they map the middle of the story. They do not yet prove a US-wide change in worker control, household security, or geopolitical leverage.

How to read this page: statements are kept at the level each source supports. A model is not an observation, an institutional self-report is not an impact evaluation, and worker-reported task use is not the same as employer implementation or worker power.

The chain under examination

concentrated tools and firm adoption → infrastructure, skills, data, management, and institutional complements → task change and public/firm capacity → wages, wealth, service quality, time, and control → bargaining, trust, regulation, switching, or geopolitical leverage

The value of the comparison is that it exposes the missing denominators. “AI capacity” can mean a model’s technical capability, a firm’s ability to integrate it, an agency’s ability to supervise it, a worker’s experience of changed tasks, or a household’s ownership of the resulting gains. These are connected questions, not interchangeable measurements.

What each source contributes

World Bank · framework

Access needs complements

The 2026 World Development Report organizes the path as adopt, adapt, advance. Electricity, connectivity, skills, data, local fit, capital, procurement, and institutions determine whether an available tool becomes useful capability. Concentration can lower access costs while creating provider dependence.

Boundary: the report does not identify a US firm’s completed stage, a measured service improvement, or a realized geopolitical concession.

IMF · modeled mechanism

Wages and wealth can diverge

IMF Working Paper 2025/068 models a baseline in which the wage Gini falls 1.73 percentage points while the wealth Gini rises 7.18 points between modeled 2014 and 2048 distributions. High-income task complementarity and capital returns are separate channels.

Boundary: these are scenario outputs, not realized US changes. The Gini does not show absolute security, job quality, ownership breadth, or who controls work.

OFR · institutional report

Efficiency can coexist with retrenchment

OFR’s FY2025 annual report describes staffing moving from 188 employees at the start of the fiscal year to 109 at year end, a reported 11% budget reduction, and AI/cloud use in data processing, security monitoring, procurement comparison, and support. It also describes new bilateral repo data that made roughly $5 trillion in daily commitments more visible.

Boundary: the report does not attribute staffing change to AI or measure workload, accuracy, response time, staff wellbeing, or financial-stability impact.

NBER/O*NET · task measurement

Task adoption is not worker control

NBER W35677’s public indexes are built from worker-reported use of digital tools for detailed work activities and mapped through O*NET occupations. The current audit confirms the official O*NET 30.3 and 31.0 mapping files are byte-identical, while exact structural identifier agreement is incomplete.

Boundary: the indexes do not observe employer implementation, pay, discretion, appeal, job quality, or the worker’s ability to refuse or correct a system.

The central interpretation

The strongest shared insight is conditionality. A tool can be available without being usable; usable without being governable; productive without distributing gains broadly; and visible to a regulator without being controllable. These conditions create several possible paths:

Why this matters culturally and politically

People do not encounter “AI adoption” as an abstract rate. They encounter a changed schedule, a performance score, a denied correction, a public form, a new service boundary, a firm that cannot answer a question, or a household whose assets capture less of the gain than someone else’s. Those experiences can affect status, trust, attribution, identity, and political demand—but the current sources do not measure that full sequence together.

The program therefore keeps the final arrows open. A staffing reduction is not automatically a cultural loss. A modelled wealth effect is not a vote effect. A foreign provider is not automatically geopolitical control. The question is whether a person or institution has a credible alternative, can inspect and correct the system, and can make the actor with control bear a cost.

Next empirical test

The next pass should match a defined US firm or public-service implementation to the people and places it reaches. Preserve firm size, sector, ownership, occupation, exposure date, task, worker pay and hours, training, representation, human review, correction route, household assets and debt, and later trust or exit. In parallel, compare annual institutional staffing, workload, data coverage, errors, and response times. Use the World Bank framework as a coding scheme—not as a substitute for those observations.

This is the point where the long-term atlas can move from adjacent evidence to an end-to-end episode: a decision enters an institution or workplace, changes what people can do, shifts money/time/control, and produces an observed response by workers, households, firms, regulators, or other states.

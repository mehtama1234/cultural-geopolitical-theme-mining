A connected reading of platform products, seller data and customer choice.

When the platform is both store and seller

A platform can help a customer find a product while also deciding which sellers, data and choices are visible. That creates a real conflict, but not a simple answer: removing the platform's own products can hurt some customers, while weak rules can leave outside sellers with less room to compete.

Read this carefully

Customer choice and market fairness are different tests. The sources do not follow one customer through search, data access, price, service and a remedy.

What the sources show

1. Some customers gain from the platform's own product

In an Amazon field experiment, researchers hid Amazon-owned brands from some shoppers. Customers did not search more or switch to another platform, but some chose the Amazon brands when they were available. Removing them reduced estimated customer welfare by 5.5% in the tested setting.

NBER Working Paper 34135

2. Moving the product lower did not solve the tradeoff

The study also reports that moving Amazon-owned brands lower in search did not increase estimated welfare. Prominent placement was useful to some customers, so a rule aimed only at visibility may remove a benefit without showing that outside sellers become stronger.

This is a customer result in one product setting. It is not proof that self-preferencing is harmless.

3. Data access can change the seller side of the market

An NBER model studies rules requiring a platform to treat its own and outside sellers equally when sharing data. Equal data and equal prices can open competition, but the model also finds that a platform may have less reason to produce or improve data. Customer welfare therefore depends on both access and data quality.

NBER Working Paper 35159

4. Privacy can make customers share more

An HBS analysis of nearly 16,000 users of a receipt-and-reward app found that users in California and Virginia submitted 9% more information after privacy rules took effect. More sharing may bring better offers or service. It may also give the platform more power to sort, target or price people.

HBS source

5. The company earns from both sellers and visibility

Amazon's 2025 filing reports $172.162 billion in third-party seller-services revenue and $68.635 billion in advertising-services revenue. It says seller programs can include commissions, fulfillment, shipping and other fees. These are company-reported totals, not proof of seller profit or customer harm.

The FTC and state case record alleges that Amazon connected seller prices, seller data, advertising, Featured Offers, Prime and promotion of its own products in ways that limited competition. In a separate August 2026 case, the FTC and 22 states allege hidden charges in Amazon search-ad auctions raised costs for more than one million brands and sellers, including more than 500,000 small and medium businesses. The FTC says some costs were likely passed to US consumers. These are allegations, not established facts.

Amazon 2025 Form 10-K · FTC marketplace case · FTC advertising case

The connection

platform sells or ranks its own product
  -> customer sees a useful choice
  -> platform controls search, seller data and access
  -> outside competition and customer options may change
  -> privacy, service and market rules decide who can question the result

The first step is measured in the Amazon experiment. The data and privacy steps are supported by a model and a separate platform study. The full chain is still open.

The four outside views

Customer: A platform-owned product can be a good fit, save search time and raise estimated welfare for some shoppers. A customer may still have little way to know whether the ranking, product and data deal were shaped by the platform's own interest.

Company and sector: The platform can earn as a seller, a search gate and a data holder. Outside sellers may gain access to customers while depending on rules they cannot set.

Money and finance: Seller data and search position can act like business assets. Equal access may move value toward outside sellers, while weaker data investment may reduce the information available to everyone.

Public power: Competition and privacy rules decide what the platform must disclose, share or separate. A complaint is useful only if a regulator, court or firm can inspect the result and change it.

What would change the finding

  • Seller entry, exit, prices and quality before and after platform-owned products appear.
  • Search and sponsored-placement records by product, seller and customer group.
  • Data access price, data quality and platform investment after a neutrality rule.
  • Customer notice comprehension, privacy choices and actual service outcomes.
  • A record of complaint, human review and remedy when the platform controls the result.
  • Results for small sellers, repeat customers, low-income customers and people with limited digital access.

Next test

Pick one US platform market and follow search position, seller data access, customer choice and later price or service. Keep customer benefit and competition risk as separate measures.

Reading rule: The Amazon result is a measured short-run choice effect. The wider market-power claim is an inference that needs seller, price, data and remedy records.

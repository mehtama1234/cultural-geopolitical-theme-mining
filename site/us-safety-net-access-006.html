Charitable food is an elevated but incomplete buffer

The short finding

Urban's WBNS shows charitable-food participation among working-age adults remaining elevated in 2025: 16.7%, versus 13.2% in 2019 and 19.7% at the 2020 pandemic peak. Among adults in food-insecure households, 39.5% reported receipt, but 28.8% also reported that they needed or wanted charitable-food help and did not get it. Receipt and unmet need therefore coexist.

The route costs time, movement, information, and comfort

Among charitable-food participants, 51.6% reported at least one listed access difficulty. The largest was getting the variety and types of food needed or wanted (40.2%). Travel time, transportation, and opening hours followed. Among those with unmet need, the leading reasons were a site not being open when available (45.5%), not knowing where to go (42.8%), not feeling comfortable getting help (39.9%), unavailable variety (37.6%), and transportation (35.2%).

These are overlapping reported barriers. The evidence supports a route with several failure points; it does not identify one decisive barrier for each household.

Treatment is part of the usable route

Nearly 1 in 4 charitable-food participants (22.9%) reported unfair treatment or judgment tied to at least one personal characteristic. The reported share was 31.8% among adults with disabilities and 28.6% among adults living with children. This is evidence about perceived treatment and possible deterrence, not an adjudicated discrimination rate or a measured change in generalized trust.

What this adds to the atlas

The supported chain is:

food-budget pressure / public-benefit insufficiency → charitable-food use → access and treatment route → residual unmet need

It is a stronger account of how households navigate a mixed public/private safety net than a simple participation statistic. But the source is not a same-household causal panel of SNAP cuts, pantry access, and later food security. The findings do not establish that policy changes caused the 2025 participation rate, nor that stigma alone explains nonuse.

Limits and next test

  • The published brief does not disclose every analytic denominator or full variance table in the text.
  • Reasons for unmet need are multiple-response categories and do not sum to 100%.
  • The survey records households' reports, not provider hours, capacity, distance, wait, food inventory, or administrative eligibility.
  • The 2025 survey methodology changed; prior estimates can differ slightly from earlier publications.
  • The public-use microdata and codebook remain the route to exact cross-tabs and design-based uncertainty.

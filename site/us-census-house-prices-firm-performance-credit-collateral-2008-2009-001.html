Finding 001: A housing crash can reach young firms through credit and collateral

Status: bounded housing, firm-finance, and local-capacity finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

An official Census working paper combines confidential business data with housing and banking data from the Great Recession. It reports large and persistent local house-price effects on employment at small businesses, especially young firms. Entry and exit help explain why young firms were disproportionately affected.

The study also reports that reliance on personal assets or home equity was associated with greater sensitivity to house prices, while young and older firms were sensitive to local credit shocks. Its model is consistent with two channels: bank credit supply and housing collateral.

The practical route is:

local house-price shock
  -> owner collateral and/or bank credit supply
  -> firm entry, exit, employment, and investment
  -> worker and local economic effects
  -> [open] household recovery, firm survival, and durable place capacity

The same housing shock can therefore reach firms through more than one financial route.

What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a finance-and-infrastructure version of the atlas's conversion problem. A place's housing market is not only a household asset or cost; it can also shape the credit and collateral available to local firms. Young firms may be especially exposed because they have less history, less retained capital, and more dependence on owner resources or local banks.

It also connects place capacity to household wealth without merging them. A firm employment effect can be real while worker pay, owner wealth, consumer prices, and community recovery remain unknown.

Limits and counterexamples

  • This is a working paper focused on the Great Recession and U.S. local markets.
  • House prices may proxy for local demand, migration, expectations, or other shocks.
  • The two channels are model-consistent interpretations, not proof that every firm experienced both equally.
  • Firm employment effects do not establish worker pay, survival for every firm, consumer outcomes, or household welfare.

Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected firm-owner-bank-place panel linking house prices, collateral, personal assets, lending, bank balance sheets, firm entry and exit, employment, pay, investment, prices, household resources, and later recovery. Until then, the safe claim is a local housing-to-firm pathway through credit and collateral.

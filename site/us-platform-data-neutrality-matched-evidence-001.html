When equal data access opens the market but weakens the data supply

Equal data access may help outside sellers compete. If sharing is cheap or compulsory, the platform may invest less in gathering and updating the data customers need.

The argument

The trade is not simply open data versus platform protection. Ask who gets access, what it costs, how accurate the data are, and what the customer receives.

More competition is not the only outcome.

Follow one product search

A seller needs platform data to price and place a product. An equal-access rule opens the data to rivals. Entry rises, but the platform invests less in collection and updates. The data become stale or costly. Customers see more sellers but spend longer searching or receive worse matches.

customer data → platform gathers and improves a data service → sellers seek access and compete → access price and control change → platform investment and data quality may change → customer price, choice, service, and privacy

The deeper finding

Data access is market design. A rule can help rivals and weaken the data service; protecting investment can preserve a gatekeeper. The customer result requires seller entry, data quality, platform investment, final price or fit, and privacy choice together.

What remains unproven

We do not yet have a named US platform with measured access prices, update rates, maintenance spending, seller outcomes, privacy choices, and customer welfare before and after a rule.

Next test

Choose one platform and measure access fees, quality, collection and update spending, seller entry, prices, variety, search time, privacy choices, and customer outcomes. Include small sellers and customers with few alternatives.

Reading rule

Do not call equal data access good or bad until you measure seller entry, data quality, platform investment, and the final customer result together.

Connected US finding · data access and competition

Equal data access can open the market and weaken the data supply

Opening platform data may help outside sellers compete. If access is cheap or forced, the platform may have less reason to gather, clean, and update that data.

The argument

The useful questions are who gets access, what it costs, how accurate the data are, who can correct them, what the platform still invests, and what the customer receives.

Data access is market design: it changes both competition and the incentive to produce the data.

Follow one product search

A seller needs platform data to price and place a product. Equal access opens the data to rivals. Entry rises, but the platform invests less in collection and updates. Customers see more sellers but spend longer searching or receive worse matches.

The reverse can also happen: the platform keeps strong data incentives but uses control to favor its own products or charge rivals more.

customer data → platform improves a data service → sellers seek access → access price and control change → investment and quality change → customer price, choice, service, and privacy

The deeper finding

The customer result requires four measures together: seller entry, data quality, platform investment, and final price or fit. Privacy adds a fifth: whether customers knowingly supplied the data that made the market work.

An open system can have poor data. A closed system can have excellent data and weak seller power. The public choice is about the full chain, including correction rights, access fees, updates, and who bears the cost when data are wrong.

What remains unknown

We do not yet have a named US platform with measured access prices, update rates, maintenance spending, seller outcomes, privacy choices, and customer welfare before and after a rule.

Next test

Choose one platform and measure data fees, quality, collection and update spending, seller entry, prices, variety, search time, privacy choices, and customer outcomes. Include small sellers and customers with few alternatives.

Reading rule

Do not call equal data access good or bad until you measure seller entry, data quality, platform investment, and the customer’s final result together.

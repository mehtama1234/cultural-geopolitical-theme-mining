A short argument · US platform data, competition, and privacy

A data rule can open the market and weaken the data supply

Equal data access may help outside sellers compete, but may also weaken the platform's reason to produce and update useful data.

The argument

The choice is not share data or protect the platform. The useful questions are who gets access, what it costs, how accurate it is, who can correct it, and what customers receive.

Follow one product search

A seller needs platform data to price and place a product. Equal access opens the data to rivals, but the platform invests less in collection and updates. Customers see more sellers but worse matches or higher search time.

customer data → platform gathers and improves a data service → sellers compete → access rule changes price and control → data quality and investment may change → customer price, choice, service, and privacy

NBER Data Neutrality · HBS data sharing

The deeper finding

Data access is a market-design problem. A rule can help rivals and hurt the data service; protecting investment can preserve a gatekeeper. The customer result requires seller entry, data quality, platform investment, and final price or fit.

What remains unproven

The NBER result is a model and the HBS result is one customer-sharing setting. We do not yet have a named US platform with measured access prices, update rates, seller outcomes, privacy choices, and customer welfare before and after a rule.

Next test

Choose one platform and measure data access fees, quality, collection and update spending, seller entry, prices, product variety, search time, privacy choices, and customer outcomes.

Reading rule

Do not call equal data access good or bad until you measure seller entry, data quality, platform investment, and the customer's final result together.

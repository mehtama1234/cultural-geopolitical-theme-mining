A short argument · US competition, treatment, and customer power

Competition helps only when customers can use it

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment.

The argument

Competition helps only when a customer can compare, switch, and receive a comparable service somewhere else. The real measure is the customer's alternative at the moment of decision.

Follow one customer across firms

A customer receives a higher price or worse service and considers switching. The other firm is farther away, requires more paperwork, has a weaker network, or uses a different risk screen. The customer stays. The market has several sellers, but the customer has no cheap exit.

customer group + ability to leave → firm incentive → price, service, treatment, or access → customer cost and choice → trust, regulation, and market entry

NBER competition and discrimination · NBER consumer lending · NBER internet car pricing

The deeper finding

Customer power is the bridge between competition and fair treatment. A market can have many firms and weak customer exit. The useful measure is not seller count; it is the customer's real alternative.

What remains unproven

The studies do not follow a current US customer across firms, prices, service quality, complaints, and repeat treatment, or show how digital comparison works for people with less time or access.

Next test

Match customers across comparable firms. Record search, travel, wait, identity checks, price, quality, denial, complaint, switch, and repeat outcome by group.

Reading rule

Do not say competition protects customers until you show who can compare, who can leave, and who receives the same product and treatment.

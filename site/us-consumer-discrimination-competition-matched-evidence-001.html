Competition helps only when the customer can leave

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment. Choice is real only when a customer can compare, switch, and receive a similar service somewhere else.

The argument

A firm’s incentive depends on the customer’s actual exit. A customer who needs a particular branch, network, language service, credit product, or accessible location may have several logos but no cheap substitute.

Seller count is a weak measure. Ask what this person could do today if the firm raised the price, denied service, or treated them worse.

Follow one customer

A customer gets a higher price or worse service. The other firm is farther away, asks for more paperwork, has a weaker network, or uses a different risk screen. The customer stays—not because the first firm served them well, but because leaving costs too much.

customer group + real ability to leave → firm incentive → price, service, treatment, denial, or access → household cost and trust → switch, complaint, regulation, or withdrawal

The deeper finding

Competition is experienced as an exit route. If a customer can leave at low cost, a firm has reason to retain them through price and treatment. If leaving means losing a local branch, restarting an application, traveling farther, or risking a new denial, the customer’s threat is weak.

A group may complain less because it is treated better—or because it expects no remedy and has nowhere else to go. Low switching can hide harm inside silence.

What remains unknown

The studies do not follow a current US customer across firms, prices, service quality, complaints, denials, switches, and repeat treatment. They do not fully measure time, transport, language, disability, trust, or the cost of proving identity.

Next test

Match customers across comparable firms. Record search, travel, wait, identity checks, price, quality, denial, complaint, switch, and repeat outcome by income, race, age, disability, language, and place.

Reading rule

Do not say competition protects customers until you show who can compare, who can leave, and who receives the same product and treatment.

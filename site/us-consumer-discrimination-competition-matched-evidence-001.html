Matched evidence · US customers and competition

Competition helps only when customers can use it.

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment. The result depends on who can compare, switch, and receive a comparable product.

In one sentence

Customer power—not seller count alone—is the bridge between competition and fair treatment.

A higher or lower price by itself does not prove discrimination.

The connection

customer group + ability to leave → firm incentive
                                 → price, service, treatment, or access
                                 → customer cost and choice
                                 → trust, regulation, and market entry

The evidence supports a competition-and-customer-power mechanism, but not one universal result. A customer who cannot leave may receive less attention even where several firms exist.

The useful surprise

The historical result warns against a simple story. Treatment and price can move in different directions. That does not make unequal treatment acceptable; it shows why comparable product, customer, and service records are needed.

Four views

Household

Record price, service, time, travel, search, and the cost of leaving.

Customer

Compare the same product, terms, quality, and treatment across firms.

Money and finance

Measure dollar price, income burden, credit price, fees, and denied access separately.

Public power

Ask whether competition, disclosure, and civil-rights rules give each group a real challenge path.

Next test

Compare current matched customers across firms using the same product, price, service quality, search path, and ability to switch.

Reading rule

Do not say competition protects customers until you show who can compare, who can leave, and who receives the same product and treatment.

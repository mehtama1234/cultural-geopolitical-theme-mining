A short argument · US fraud, money, and trust

The loss is only the first part of a fraud victim’s experience.

The next questions are who answers, who reverses the payment, and who carries the loss while they investigate.

The argument

The payment route changes the recovery path. A card dispute, a bank transfer, and a locked account do not leave the household with the same protection or delay.

The system can remain in use while confidence in it falls.

Follow the recovery effort

A customer is tricked into sending money by bank transfer. The bank reviews the event, the account may be limited, and the sending and receiving institutions may point to each other. A bill remains due while the recovery question is unresolved.

scam or unauthorized payment → loss or account lock → dispute and waiting → money returned, delayed, or lost → future use changes

The reporting gap

The FTC's reported losses and the Fed's estimated net non-card losses answer different questions and should not be added. The gap shows that formal reporting is not a complete view of what families lose.

The argument has limits

The sources support exposure, payment-route differences, loss, complaints, and recovery friction. They do not directly measure how trust changes for the same person after resolution.

Next test

Link payment route, scam type, dispute, recovery, resolution time, account access, later borrowing, account switching, and trust.

Reading rule

Do not equate an attempt, a complaint, a gross loss, and an unrecovered loss.

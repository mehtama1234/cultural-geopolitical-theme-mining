When a tariff keeps moving after the first price rise

A tariff can reach a US household in stages. The importer may pay more first. A retailer or producer may absorb part of the cost, change suppliers, or raise a shelf price later.

In one sentence

The tariff rate, importer cost, checkout price, household burden and job effect are different things.

These studies use different periods, products and methods. They should not be averaged.

What the sources show

1. Importers carried most of the first increase

An NBER study of 2025 US tariffs estimates that about 90% of the tariff was passed into the tariff-inclusive price paid by US importers. That is a strong first link, but it is not a retail bill.

NBER Working Paper 35064

2. The shelf price rose partly and unevenly

Another NBER study estimates about 20% retail pass-through for the 2025 tariffs and a roughly 0.7 percentage-point contribution to all-items CPI by September 2025. Imported goods rose about twice as much as domestic goods in that study.

NBER Working Paper 34496 · HBS tariff tracker

3. A second price can arrive later through domestic markups

An NBER study estimates that about 26% of the tariff increase reached consumer prices relative to less-exposed goods. About 64% was direct, while 36% came indirectly through imported inputs and higher domestic markups. The indirect effect took about nine to twelve months.

NBER Working Paper 35561

The connection

tariff decision → importer cost → supplier change, firm margin, or domestic competition → shelf price now or later → household substitution, delay, or lower spending room → firm, worker, regulator, and voter response

The first three price links have evidence. The household, job and political links remain open.

The four outside views

Household: A small average price effect can still matter when it lands on food, a needed good, or a low-cash household. The studies do not show which bill gave way.

Company and sector: Firms can absorb the cost, raise prices, switch suppliers, reduce choice, invest at home, or use weaker import competition to raise a markup.

Money and finance: The timing matters. A cost paid at import may become a later margin change, working-capital need, or household credit choice.

Politics: A later shelf-price rise can be hard to trace back to a trade rule. No opening source follows that blame into a vote.

What would change the finding

  • The same product from import entry through wholesale, retail and household purchase.
  • Prices, quantities, package size, quality and product choice by income and region.
  • Firm margins, supplier changes, domestic output, jobs and wages.
  • Household records showing trading down, delayed purchases, borrowing or lost care.
  • A test of whether people knew the price change was tariff-related and whether that changed policy support or voting.

Next test

Choose one everyday product category. Join customs cost, retailer price, quantity sold and household purchase data over twelve months. Add a survey question about who people blame. Keep the direct and delayed price paths separate.

Reading rule

The importer cost is not the checkout price. The checkout price is not the household loss. A price result is not a job result or a vote result.

Finding 016: Coverage and capacity can fail at the accessibility interface

Status: bounded disability health-care accessibility finding · Checked: 2026-09-19

The bounded finding

GAO identifies physical, sensory, communication, cognitive, digital, and provider-interaction barriers that can prevent health-care capacity from becoming usable care for people with disabilities. Examples include inaccessible exam equipment, check-in kiosks and apps, sensory environments, communication needs, and provider assumptions or denials.

The measurement gap is substantial: GAO reviewed 12 HHS population-health surveys and found that none collected national-level data on most accessibility barriers. CMS and OCR have oversight routes, but oversight activity is not a measure of completed care, correction, or restored access. HHS had amended requirements in 2024, while detailed execution plans and timelines remained incomplete at the report's publication.

The important result is not a national estimate of inaccessible care. It is a conversion boundary: coverage, an available provider, or a digital route does not automatically become a completed, safe, continuous encounter.

The chain

disability-related need
  -> accessibility or provider-interaction barrier
  -> extra time, failed/delayed/avoided encounter [open]
  -> treatment continuity, health, work, and family-time outcome [open]
  -> complaint, correction, trust, switching, or exit [open]

This is the care equivalent of the workforce-access finding: institutional capacity is experienced at the interface, and the person or family may absorb the conversion cost when the interface fails.

Limits and next field

  • The GAO survey of 1,194 adults with disabilities is nongeneralizable.
  • Barrier descriptions do not establish national prevalence or a causal health effect.
  • A complaint or oversight record does not prove harm, correction, or restored access; low complaint volume does not prove low harm.
  • The next decisive evidence is a privacy-preserving encounter ledger joining need, requested accommodation, receipt, completion, delay, treatment continuity, family/work time, complaint, correction, and later health.

Source: GAO-26-107120. The machine-readable ledger preserves the interface and outcome boundaries.

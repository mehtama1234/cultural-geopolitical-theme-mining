Finding 001: Election results can feed back into the political capacity that produces later support

Status: bounded historical U.S. political-action finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

The bounded finding

The revised NBER study treats presidential election outcomes as more than signals of existing support. In counties where Democrats narrowly won during the 1940–1968 realignment, later Democratic local officeholding and voter support increased. Local news treated the result as a binary win or loss, and the study identifies political investment—including advertising, direct voter outreach, and civil-rights organizing—as a channel. Effects were reported as largest in urban, Black, and union areas.

narrow election outcome
  -> winner/loser salience and strategic investment
  -> local officeholding, outreach, organizing, and voter support
  -> coalition durability and political capacity
  -> [open] material policy, trust, legitimacy, and present-day transfer

What this adds to the atlas

This adds a feedback mechanism between political action and political capacity: an election can allocate future organizing resources and institutional presence, so later support is partly produced by what happened earlier. It complements the atlas's household and trust layers without treating turnout, officeholding, or support as interchangeable outcomes.

Limits and next test

  • The evidence concerns U.S. counties during 1940–1968, not current elections.
  • Close outcomes do not remove every source of selection or national realignment channel.
  • Political investment and officeholding do not prove better material welfare, trust, or democratic legitimacy.

The next decisive test is a current or repeated historical election ledger that follows close outcomes through party investment, local representation, public services, household incidence, trust, and later electoral participation.

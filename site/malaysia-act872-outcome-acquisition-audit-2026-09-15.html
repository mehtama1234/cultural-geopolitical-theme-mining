Malaysia Act 872 outcome acquisition audit — 2026-09-15

Status: infrastructure located; case-level outcome not publicly located

Acquisition question

Has a Malaysian gig worker used Act 872's automated-management disclosure, non-automated review, hearing, conciliation, Tribunal, or deactivation-payment routes, with a public record of the resulting decision?

What is observed

  1. Act 872 is in force from 31 March 2026 according to the Ministry of Human Resources.
  2. The Ministry exposes an e-Aduan route for gig-worker complaints.
  3. The Ministry has published conciliation and Tribunal regulations.
  4. The legal system provides a path from platform decision to conciliation and potentially Tribunal adjudication.
  5. The Ministry publishes a stated e-Aduan service target and maintains an enforcement page updated 15 September 2026.
  6. The Ministry reports that the Gig Workers Tribunal and Gig Consultation Council were formally established on 3 April 2026.
  7. Secondary reports attributing facts to KESUMA identify a first Tribunal hearing on 1 September 2026 involving an e-hailing worker and GrabCar Sdn Bhd; the matter reportedly returned for evidence and witness examination.
  8. The Ministry is reported to have said the Tribunal received other filings involving account deactivation or suspension, loss of income, account reactivation, platform-service issues, and payment claims by non-platform gig workers.

What is not observed

  • number of complaints filed under Act 872;
  • number of complaints concerning automated monitoring or decision systems;
  • platform responses or written explanations under section 14(9);
  • completed non-automated reviews;
  • reactivation or interim payments under section 14(5);
  • conciliation settlements;
  • Tribunal awards;
  • enforcement or compound-offence actions;
  • actual e-Aduan resolution times or outcome categories;
  • Tribunal hearings, awards, or aggregate referral counts after the reported institutional establishment;
  • the primary case identifier, pleadings, hearing record, award, or final finding for the reported GrabCar matter;
  • the number, identifiers, decision types, or outcomes of the additional filings reported by the Ministry;
  • whether the GrabCar claimant received association, family, technical, or data-access assistance under the Tribunal's low-cost representation design;
  • recurrence prevention or anti-retaliation outcomes.

The absence of a public record is not evidence that no complaints or outcomes exist. It may reflect the Act's recent commencement, non-public administrative records, search-index limits, or a reporting practice that does not publish case-level data.

Promotion rule

Do not promote Malaysia from official_measure_effective to official_enforcement_or_case_found based only on the secondary report. The report is sufficient to add case_hearing_reported, but an official case identifier, primary hearing record, award, or official aggregate outcome is still required for the enforcement/outcome promotion. Do not code a portal, form, regulation, or hearing-in-progress as a worker remedy outcome.

Next acquisition

  1. Monitor the Ministry's e-Aduan and Tribunal portals for a public statistics or decisions page.
  2. Search Malaysian legal and administrative repositories for Act 872 case identifiers after each new publication cycle.
  3. Locate gig-worker associations or legal-aid organizations that can provide anonymized, consented case records.
  4. If public records remain unavailable, make a narrowly scoped information request for aggregate complaints, deactivation disputes, conciliation settlements, Tribunal referrals, and awards, separated by decision type.

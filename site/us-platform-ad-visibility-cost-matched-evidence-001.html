A short argument · US marketplace ads, prices, and attention

Seller visibility can become a customer cost

Advertising can be the price a seller pays to be seen, even when the customer never sees that charge.

The argument

The current evidence comes from a newly filed FTC complaint. Its conduct and figures are allegations, not court findings. The next task is to separate the auction charge, seller response, product shown, and final price.

Follow one sponsored purchase

A seller pays to appear beside a search result. The charge rises. The seller absorbs it, raises the product price, reduces quality, stops advertising, or leaves the platform. A price effect is possible, but it must be measured.

ad-auction charge → seller visibility and cost → margin, product price, quality, or exit → customer sees changed products → price, search, choice, and trust change

FTC complaint and announcement

The deeper finding

Visibility is part of the price system even when it is not printed on the receipt. A seller may pay for attention and a customer may pay through a higher price or narrower visible choice.

What remains unproven

The complaint does not measure every arrow from auction charge to seller price to customer purchase. We still need auction logs, margins, product prices, quality, exposure, and final legal findings.

Next test

Collect auction bids, rules, charges, placements, seller prices, margins, product quality, customer views, purchases, returns, and exit. Compare small and large sellers.

Reading rule

Do not say an ad charge raised household prices until the record links the auction payment to the seller's price and the customer's final purchase.

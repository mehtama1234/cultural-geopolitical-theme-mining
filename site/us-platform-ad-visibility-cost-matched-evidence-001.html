Connected US finding · marketplace ads and customer cost

When seller visibility becomes a customer cost

On a marketplace, advertising can be the price a seller pays to be seen. The customer may pay through a higher price or a narrower set of visible choices.

The argument

A sponsored result changes the first comparison before the customer reaches the receipt. The full effect requires tracking the auction, the seller, the shelf, and the final purchase.

Visibility is part of the price system even when it is not printed on the receipt.

Follow one sponsored purchase

A small seller pays to appear beside a search result. The charge rises. The seller absorbs it, raises price, reduces quality, stops advertising, or leaves. The customer sees a changed shelf before seeing a changed receipt.

ad-auction charge → seller visibility and cost → margin, price, quality, or exit → customer sees a changed set of products → search time, choice, trust, and final price change

The deeper finding

A seller may pay for attention, and a customer may pay through a higher price or narrower visible choice. The seller may also absorb the cost or leave. The real effect depends on which response is common for small and large sellers.

What remains unproven

The complaint does not measure every arrow from auction charge to seller price to customer purchase. We need auction logs, margins, prices, quality, customer exposure, purchases, returns, and exit. An allegation is not a measured household price increase.

Next test

Collect bids, rules, charges, placements, seller prices, margins, product quality, views, purchases, returns, and exit. Compare small and large sellers across demand periods, and track whether customers chose sponsored products or merely saw them.

Reading rule

Link the auction payment to the seller's price and the customer's final purchase before claiming a household price effect.

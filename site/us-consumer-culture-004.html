Finding 004: Material pressure predicts later option-search more clearly than AI use or political confidence

Status: bounded same-respondent HTOPS cross-lag finding
Checked: 2026-09-19
Scope: April 2025 expense difficulty carried to June 2025 search, AI-use, and Congress-confidence fields; descriptive, not causal

The finding

Among 6,564 respondents linked by SCRAMID, people reporting expense difficulty in April were more likely than respondents reporting no difficulty to report targeted online searches in June. The same time-ordered contrast was much weaker for broad AI use and high confidence in Congress.

June outcome April expense difficulty April expenses not difficult Difference
Job search 40.6% (1.1) 21.8% (0.7) +18.8 pp
Food search 25.2% (0.8) 16.6% (0.4) +8.7 pp
Public-benefit search 19.6% (0.8) 11.1% (0.3) +8.5 pp
Credit search 20.3% (0.7) 11.4% (0.5) +8.9 pp
Housing search 18.1% (0.6) 13.3% (0.6) +4.8 pp
Medical search 17.0% (0.7) 10.7% (0.3) +6.3 pp
Any AI use 53.9% (1.0) 58.5% (0.8) −4.6 pp
High confidence in Congress 17.2% (0.7) 16.8% (0.5) +0.3 pp

Parentheses are successive-difference replicate standard errors in percentage points. The estimates use April PWEIGHT and its 80 replicate weights, with valid linked respondent counts of 2,589 in the difficulty group and 3,965 in the no-difficulty group.

What this adds

The stronger interpretation is not that financially pressured households “use more technology.” It is that pressure appears to redirect attention toward concrete option searches—work, food, benefits, credit, housing, and medical help—across a two-month window. Broad AI use is not the same behavior as material navigation, and political confidence barely separates the two baseline groups in this screen.

This is an early option-building signal. It does not show that the search found a provider, job, benefit, affordable home, credit line, or remedy. It also does not show whether the respondent made the search, whether the result was usable, or whether the later household condition improved.

Interpretation boundary

  • April expense difficulty is self-reported and does not identify a dated bill, price change, responsible actor, or amount owed.
  • April weights and replicate weights are not documented here as attrition-adjusted longitudinal weights.
  • The June fields are reported search and use outcomes, not clickstream, result-quality, application, approval, payment, or completion records.
  • The contrasts are descriptive associations, not a causal effect of expense difficulty.
  • Search is effort and navigation, not a completed alternative, remedy, recovery, switching, or exit.
  • High Congress confidence is an institution-specific judgment, not generalized trust or political action.

End-to-end implication

The observed route is:

April material pressure
  -> June targeted option-search
  -> [open] result quality, chosen route, cost, completion, remedy, and later security

The next decisive source would link a search or help-seeking episode to the result encountered, time and money spent, application or transaction outcome, institutional response, and later recovery or exit. Until then, the result should be read as constrained navigation—not successful adaptation.

Reproduction and source boundary

The compact machine result is the cross-lag JSON. The reproduction script is build_htops_2025_material_search_cross_lag.py. The source files were already retained locally under /tmp/htops-2504/ and /tmp/htops-2506/; no new download was performed and no raw respondent file was copied into Git.

Evidence status: same-respondent, two-wave descriptive follow-up with replicate-weight uncertainty; no causal, remedy, recovery, trust, or exit claim.

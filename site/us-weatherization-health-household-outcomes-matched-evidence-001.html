Matched evidence · US homes, energy, and health

Efficiency savings are real but smaller than the promise.

An upgrade may lower energy use while delivering less bill savings than predicted. The household result also depends on comfort, air, safety, and access.

In one sentence

Judge an upgrade by the actual bill, room, air, safety result, and later household choices.

Lower energy use is not automatically proof of a warmer or healthier home.

The connection

weatherization work → actual energy use and bill → temperature, air quality, safety, and comfort → health, work, care, and household cash → future repair, housing, and trust choices

NBER anchors the actual-savings result. DOE documents the broader health and safety path. The longer-run household outcome remains open.

The useful surprise

Lower measured energy use does not automatically mean a family is warmer or healthier. It may reflect a better home, continued hardship, or changed equipment and behavior.

Four views

Household

Count bill, temperature, air quality, safety, comfort, repair, health, work, care, and time.

Customer and resident

Record who requested work, permission, installation, and whether the home stayed usable.

Money and finance

Compare predicted and actual savings with program cost, rent, debt, medical cost, and essentials.

Public power

Measure eligibility, waitlist, completion, contractor quality, landlord cooperation, and group differences.

Next test

Link same-home bills, temperatures, air quality, equipment, health, missed work, and housing stability before and after work.

Reading rule

Do not call weatherization successful from a modelled saving. Show the bill, the room, the air, the safety result, and later choices.

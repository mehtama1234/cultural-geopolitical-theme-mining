Rent protection can move risk, not remove it

Rent protection can help after an income or health shock. But the real question is who pays before the crisis, who pays after it, and who is refused at the door.

The argument

Private coverage can spread a large loss across policyholders, but it may add a premium, deductible, exclusion, claim delay, or screening rule. Public protection can reach households private insurers avoid, but it needs public money and an eligibility rule.

A product can prevent eviction for one family while leaving another outside it or owing money after the claim.

Follow one shock

A family pays $1,800 in rent and buys coverage. One adult loses work. The policy may cover only a set number of payments, require proof, pay the landlord, or leave the family responsible for repayment. If the claim arrives after a notice or court date, the same nominal coverage may no longer prevent displacement.

income or health shock → rent cannot be paid → claim, public aid, legal help, or no help → payment, denial, delay, or repayment → housing and credit path

The deeper finding

The important outcome is not simply covered or uncovered. It is the path from shock to housing result. A policy that pays quickly may prevent a move. A narrow policy may only postpone it. A payment that protects the landlord but creates tenant debt may preserve the lease today and damage the next housing search.

Compare protection by the household’s final position: did the renter stay housed, did debt rise, and did credit, work, health, or the next lease change?

What remains unknown

The research does not measure premiums, enrollment, claims, exclusions, landlord response, claim timing, repayment, credit, or later housing.

Next test

Follow insured and uninsured renters for three years. Join premium, claim, income, health, rent, notice, landlord response, eviction, credit, work, and housing records. Compare private coverage, public aid, legal help, and no protection.

Reading rule

Keep modeled welfare, actual claims, prevented eviction, and later household security separate.

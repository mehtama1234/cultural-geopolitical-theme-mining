Connected US finding · career, debt and housing

When career movement slows around a long payment

A worker can keep a job and still lose room to move. A student-loan payment may take the cash needed for a better job, a move, a down payment, or more training.

The argument

Imagine a borrower offered a better job in another city. The role pays more, but the move needs a deposit, transport, and several months of risk. The borrower already owes a monthly loan payment. Staying may be a good choice because the current job has better hours. It may also be the only choice the balance sheet allows.

A title and a debt balance cannot tell us which one.

What the sources establish

HBS research using 150 million resumes and wage data measures career progress by time to reach a job level and finds that most workers top out at a mid-level. The measure does not show control, schedule, health, benefits, or household safety.

The Federal Reserve reports that 20% of borrowers were behind on a student-loan payment or had a loan in collections in 2024. An NBER study of female lawyers models links between debt and career, marriage, and education choices. These sources give us a meeting point, not one tracked worker’s full story.

Follow the choice

years of work → pay, career level, control, benefits, or security → loan payment meets income → cash left for rent, saving, moving, or training → worker moves, stays, studies, or waits → later wealth and work options

The open question is what makes debt binding: payment size, low earnings, the repayment rule, housing costs, or the risk of leaving a job with good coverage.

What must stay separate

  • Job title and job quality.
  • Loan balance and monthly payment.
  • Staying by choice and staying because alternatives are closed.
  • Delinquency and total household harm.

Next test

Follow the same borrower for ten years from school into work and housing. Join career level, pay, loan terms, rent, moves, applications, savings, and family timing. Test a repayment change against work and housing outcomes.

Reading rule

Do not treat a flat title as a stalled life or a large loan as a bad result. Measure the payment against earnings, housing choices, job quality, and room to move.

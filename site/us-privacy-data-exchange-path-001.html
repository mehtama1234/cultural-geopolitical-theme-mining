When privacy protection increases the data exchange

A privacy rule can make people more willing to share, not less. A safer permission process may give a firm a richer record and more power to sort customers.

The argument

The useful question is what the customer understood, what value the customer received, what the firm did with the record, and whether the customer could later stop or undo the exchange.

More sharing is not proof of a better exchange.

Follow one receipt

A shopper scans receipts into a rewards app. After a privacy rule takes effect, the permission screen feels safer and the reward improves. She submits more receipts and visits. The firm sees a fuller picture of her shopping, but she may not know how long the data remain, whether they are sold, or whether future offers change.

privacy rule and clearer permission → person feels more protected → more receipts, visits, or categories shared → firm gains a richer record → offers, sorting, pricing, or exposure may change

HBS analyzed nearly 16,000 receipt-and-reward app users before and after California and Virginia privacy rules; users in those states submitted 9% more information.A privacy rule can increase willingness to share.
The increase included 1.5 additional receipts per month, 5% more unique store visits, and 4% more retail categories.The record became richer, not merely larger.
The study covers one platform and uses a policy comparison.It does not show that users read the notice or understood later uses.
It does not measure better service, equal prices, deletion success, or bargaining power.More sharing is not proof of a better exchange.

Source: HBS: More Trust, More Data.

The deeper finding

Consent is a market event. It can change the data a firm owns and the choices it can make about a customer. The customer’s value is reward, service, price, and control; the firm’s value is prediction and sorting.

What remains unproven

The study does not show whether users understood the exchange, received better offers, faced different prices, or could delete the record. Results may differ by age, income, race, disability, language, and digital access.

Next test

Follow users for one year. Vary the notice, permission, reward, and deletion controls, then measure what people understand, share, receive, and can stop. Compare customer value with the firm’s ability to predict and sort.

Reading rule

Ask what the person knew, what the firm did, and whether the customer could say no.

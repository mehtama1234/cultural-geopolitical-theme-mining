When place changes what a paycheck can buy

Two families can earn the same amount and have different spending room because they live in different places. The difference is not just rent.

The argument

Food, transport, care, services, product choice, and distance all change what a paycheck can do. A higher-cost place may offer better jobs or services; a lower-cost place may require more travel and offer fewer choices.

Ask what the household receives for the money it spends.

What the source establishes

An NBER study uses household consumption data covering about 5% of the US population and local prices for more than 140 products. Price differences between commuting zones are especially large for low-income households; the spread is almost twice as large as for high-income households.

The result shows different real buying power. It does not show why a family stays, moves, changes stores, or judges the place.

NBER Working Paper 29533

Follow the place into the budget

place → local prices, services, product choices, and travel distance → real buying power → food, housing, transport, care, or moving choice → household security and public judgment

A family may pay less for one item and more to reach it. Real buying power is useful, but it is not the whole value of a place.

Keep these apart

  • Price level and service quality.
  • Rent and the full cost of reaching work or care.
  • Nominal income and real buying power.
  • A low price and a good life.
  • Staying by choice and staying because moving costs too much.

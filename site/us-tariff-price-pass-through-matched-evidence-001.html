A short argument · US tariffs, retail prices, and household choice

A tariff reaches the household through a chain, not a single price

A tariff does not move straight from a government announcement to one checkout price.

The argument

The chain has at least five steps: the rule, duty paid, importer cost, firm price or product choice, and household response. Each step can change the result.

Follow one imported product

A tariff is announced. Some shipments are exempt or delayed. The importer pays more, the firm changes price or quality, and the household buys less, trades down, or waits. The announcement is easy to date; the household may feel it months later.

trade rule → border payment → importer cost → firm price or product change → household pays more, buys less, or switches → economic outlook and public judgment

What the sources actually establish

StepEvidenceMeaning
Announced rateThe public rule states a rate for a product and country.It is not the duty paid on every shipment.
Importer priceNBER finds high pass-through into tariff-inclusive import prices in the episodes studied.It does not mean every retailer adds the same amount.
Retail priceNBER and the Fed find gradual and uneven movement across goods and origins.Retail change is not a household basket.
Household choiceFed research finds quantity cuts and trading down among worried households.A changed purchase is not automatically a later vote.
TimingPrice pressure can build over time rather than appear as one jump.Cause and lived experience may be separated.

The deeper finding

The useful political question comes after the price chain: did the household know the policy, connect it to the purchase, and change its judgment? Price data alone cannot answer that.

What remains unproven

The sources do not join one product's tariff exposure, import price, retail price, quantity, household income, substitution, and political response.

Next test

Build a product panel joining tariff exposure, origin, price, package size, quantity, firm margin, and household purchase. Add repeated measures of policy knowledge and blame.

Reading rule

Never write “the tariff raised prices” without naming the price: announced rate, duty, import price, retail price, or household cost.

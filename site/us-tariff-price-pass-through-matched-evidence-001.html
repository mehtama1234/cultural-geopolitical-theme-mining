Matched evidence · US tariffs and household prices

A tariff reaches the household through a chain, not a single price.

The rule announced, the duty paid, the import cost, the shelf price, and the household response are separate records. Keeping them separate shows where the pressure actually moves.

In one sentence

Much of the cost can reach importers, while retail prices and household choices change later and unevenly.

The evidence does not yet prove that a price change caused a political choice.

The connection

trade rule → border payment → importer cost → firm price or product change
           → household pays more, buys less, or switches
           → economic outlook and public judgment

The first four links have direct evidence. The last is still a test. The announcement is easy to date; the household may feel the result months later through a changed price, smaller package, missing product, or weaker choice.

Four views

Household

Measure the product, quantity, substitute, and month—not just the average tariff.

Customer

Track a higher price, cheaper version, smaller package, or no purchase.

Money and finance

Keep the announced rate, duty, import price, retail price, and household burden separate.

Public power

Test whether people know the policy and connect it to the price.

Next test

Join tariff exposure to household purchases by product, income, retailer, and month. Mark each result as a border cost, importer price, shelf price, or household outcome.

Reading rule

Never write “the tariff raised prices” without naming which price you mean.

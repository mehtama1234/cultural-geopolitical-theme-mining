Cash and Counseling: one intervention, three different lived currencies

Status: bounded randomized cross-unit synthesis; not a pooled household recovery estimate
Checked: 2026-09-19

Why this matters to the broad atlas

The Cash and Counseling demonstrations are one of the clearer retained examples of an institutional design reaching beyond a policy announcement. The randomized intervention changed who could direct care, who could be hired, and how services could be scheduled. Follow-up then measured consequences for beneficiaries, informal caregivers, and paid workers.

The three populations must not be merged into one outcome. They show that the same institutional change can improve one currency while leaving another exposed:

Unit What the intervention changed Observed follow-up Still unknown
Beneficiary More control over service timing/type, hiring, and permitted purchases Higher service satisfaction, fewer reported unmet personal-care needs, and greater access to paid care in the cited comparisons Individual take-up, completed care, long-run health, independence, household cost, and durable continuity
Informal caregiver Possibility of paid care, more control over the care arrangement, and altered service support Better selected wellbeing, satisfaction, and perceived financial-strain measures; effects were not uniform Caregiver work, exact paid/unpaid hours, recipient outcome, household resources, and longer-run strain
Direct-hire worker A flexible paid route, often converting an existing family/friend care relationship into paid work Generally positive satisfaction with conditions and compensation; many still provided substantial unpaid care and had less formal training or benefits than agency workers Wage adequacy, payment regularity, retention, replacement care, later earnings, and exit

The end-to-end segment actually supported

Medicaid consumer-direction design
  -> beneficiary control over care route
  -> service access/satisfaction and caregiver/worker experience
  -> different money, time, health, and strain consequences

The randomized assignment supports treatment-versus-control contrasts for the reported follow-up outcomes. It does not mean every assigned person used the allowance, hired a worker, or obtained adequate care. The compact public records do not retain a same beneficiary-caregiver-worker identifier, exact spending, completed service, or long-run follow-up, so the three arrows are parallel evidence segments rather than one household chain.

What the comparison reveals

  1. Control can be a real benefit even when recovery is incomplete. More choice over timing, worker, and permitted purchases can improve satisfaction and reduce unmet need without proving independence or stable care.

  2. Relief can coexist with transferred responsibility. Consumer direction moves authority toward the beneficiary and family, but it can also move hiring, payroll, scheduling, backup-care, and continuity risk into the household.

  3. Paid work is not the same as adequate work. Direct-hire workers could gain flexible compensation while continuing unpaid care and facing thinner training or benefits than agency workers. Satisfaction is not a wage, health, or retention measure.

  4. An intervention can improve wellbeing without closing the cultural or political endpoint. The study measures satisfaction and perceived strain, not institutional legitimacy, dignity, collective action, switching, or exit. Those endpoints must remain open rather than being inferred from positive treatment effects.

Broad-program coding consequence

This is a useful positive counterweight to the remedy-heavy records elsewhere in the atlas. It shows a support design with randomized downstream improvement, not merely an order, allocation, or reported distribution. But it also gives a reusable rule:

randomized assignment != individual take-up
caregiver relief != recipient recovery
worker satisfaction != adequate durable work
short-run improvement != household security or political trust

The next decisive upgrade would be a linked beneficiary-caregiver follow-up with allowance receipt, paid and unpaid hours, completed care, recipient health, household resources, worker retention, and later trust or exit. Until that exists, this synthesis closes a strong intervention-to-short-run-outcome segment while preserving the missing middle and later stages.

Finding 035: A later refund can be targeted to people who already cleared the first access hurdle

Status: bounded consumer-remedy reachability finding · Checked: 2026-09-20

The bounded finding

The FTC's 2022 refund report says Yellowstone Capital mailed 7,731 first-round payments and that 3,557 businesses had cashed a payment by the reporting date: 46% of the eligible group. The FTC's June 2026 page then says that a third round of 3,759 checks was sent to small businesses that had cashed a previous check.

The sequence is:

business harm
  -> first refund route
  -> some businesses cash a payment
  -> later payment is targeted to prior cashers
  -> third-check delivery and cashing [open]
  -> account correction, residual loss, operating recovery, or exit [open]

Why it matters

The later payment is not reaching a random cross-section of everyone harmed. It is conditioned on an earlier successful route. That may be sensible administration, but it also means that people who lacked notice, had an old address, could not use the payment channel, or did not complete the first route may disappear from later rounds.

This is a consumer-power and small-business survival issue. A refund system can return money to some recipients while still selecting for the businesses that were able to navigate the first payment process. The public record shows reachability as a stage; it does not show whether the third payment arrived or whether any payment repaired the underlying account harm.

Do not overread

  • The 46% figure is the 2022 cashing count divided by the 2022 eligible count; it is not a loss-recovery rate or a measure of all harmed businesses.
  • The 2026 figure is checks sent to prior cashers, not third-check cashing.
  • The two FTC reports do not identify the same businesses at the individual level, and they do not show amount, timing, failed delivery, reissue, account correction, continued operation, employment, trust, or exit.

The next decisive record would be an authorized administrator or business-level follow-up with delivery, cashing, reissue, remaining loss, account correction, and later operating status.

Sources

  • FTC 2022 Annual Report on Refunds to Consumers
  • FTC Yellowstone Capital refunds, June 2026

Evidence status: official aggregate refund records; the finding separates first-route reachability from later payment and business recovery.

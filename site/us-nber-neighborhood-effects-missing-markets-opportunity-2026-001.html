Finding 001: A voucher or move does not by itself create access to opportunity

Status: bounded neighborhood, housing-market, and long-term-opportunity finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

An NBER review of experimental and quasi-experimental evidence concludes that childhood neighborhoods can have substantial causal effects on adult earnings and other long-term outcomes.

The review identifies several reasons families may not be able to buy or reach opportunity through the housing market: they cannot borrow against a child's future earnings, neighbors create spillovers, people make decisions with limited information, and housing search itself is costly and difficult.

It also reports that customized mobility services combined with subsidized vouchers can help low-income families move to higher-opportunity areas, while place-based mixed-income policies appear to improve long-term outcomes for less-advantaged children.

The route is:

family resources and housing market
  -> search, voucher, mobility service, or place-based policy
  -> actual neighborhood and school exposure
  -> [open] cost, continuity, safety, belonging, and later opportunity

What this adds to the broad atlas

This sharpens the housing finding already in the atlas:

A public housing benefit can exist while the market, search process, and social cost of moving still decide whether a family reaches opportunity.

The review also warns against treating residential segregation as only a matter of preference. Financing limits, information, search friction, and neighborhood spillovers can make opportunity unevenly purchasable even when families would benefit from a different place.

Limits and next test

  • This is a review of multiple studies, not one new common sample or one pooled estimate.
  • The public abstract does not provide one denominator, one effect size, or one implementation standard for mobility services or mixed-income policy.
  • A move may improve later opportunity while imposing immediate rent, transportation, care, safety, or social-network costs.
  • The review does not follow one family through voucher issuance, search, lease-up, schooling, continuity, and adult outcomes.

The next decisive artifact is a protected family-place ledger joining voucher or mobility-service offer, search effort, reachable units, move, rent, transport, school and social exposure, continuity, and later outcomes. Until then, the safe claim is that housing-market access and long-term opportunity are connected through a difficult middle stage.

Source

  • NBER Working Paper 35419: Neighborhood Effects and Missing Markets for Opportunity

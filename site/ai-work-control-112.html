Finding 112: A remedy can restore a job on paper while leaving cultural access and security unresolved

Status: bounded named worker-remedy finding · Checked: 2026-09-19

The bounded finding

In [2025] FWC 2621, the Fair Work Commission found that Mr Alam Shah was unfairly dismissed by Real Dairy Production after a workplace dispute. The public decision records several conditions that are usually invisible in a simple dismissal/remedy count:

  • Mr Shah was struck by a forklift on 17 April 2025, took time off on workers compensation, and returned to work on 28 April.
  • He was dismissed by email on 2 May without a valid reason or an opportunity to respond.
  • He was Rohingya, had limited English, needed an interpreter, did not drive, and lived about a 25-minute walk from the workplace.
  • He was supporting family in a refugee camp in Bangladesh, making the loss of a nearby job materially and personally consequential.
  • The Commission ordered reinstatement, continuity of service, translation of the decision into Rohingya, and $18,035.56 in lost pay.

The case therefore links workplace control to cultural and material access: communication, mobility, family obligation, and the credibility of a remedy all shape the worker's real option set.

The chain

injury and workplace dispute
  -> dismissal without usable explanation or response opportunity
  -> lost local income, mobility, and family-support capacity
  -> tribunal review with interpreter and translated decision
  -> reinstatement, continuity, and lost-pay order
  -> [open] actual return, payment, communication change, durable security,
     trust, bargaining, or exit

What this adds to the broader program

  1. Culture is operational, not decorative. Language and cultural difference affected whether workplace expectations and warnings were understood and whether the worker had a genuine chance to respond.
  2. Mobility is part of job quality. A walkable job can be materially different from an equivalent job that requires a car, longer travel, or unaffordable transport.
  3. Household and geopolitical ties enter the workplace. Family support in a refugee camp is not a generic demographic label; it changes the stakes of income loss and the meaning of “alternative work.”
  4. Procedural remedy and lived recovery are separate. Reinstatement, translation, continuity, and a lost-pay amount are strong institutional stages, but the public record does not show compliance, payment receipt, actual return, or later security.

Limits

  • This is one adjudicated case, not a prevalence estimate for migrant, Rohingya, multilingual, or injured workers.
  • The decision does not report a completed alternative job, the full search or offer set, post-order return, payment receipt, later job duration, or household budget outcome.
  • Translation and reinstatement are not proof that workplace communication or treatment changed durably.
  • The case does not establish employer intent or a general cultural mechanism.

When the small-business rule changes the local market

An eligibility line can change which firms receive public credit or contracts, and what happens to owners, workers, and local services.

In one sentence

A broader program is not automatically a fairer program.

Count need, scale, competition, jobs, and customer value.

What the source shows

Larger-firm access changed smaller-firm outcomes

Using Census data and changes in US eligibility rules, NBER finds that expanding access to larger firms reduced revenue for the smallest firms, shifted government contracts to larger firms, increased exits, and reduced wages and patenting.

The study measures firm and market effects. It does not show each owner's household result, customer prices, or local services.

NBER Working Paper 35703

The connection

eligibility rule changes → larger firms gain access → contracts and support move → smallest firms lose revenue or exit → wages, innovation, customers, and local choice change

Scale can help delivery, but it can also reduce entry and local competition.

The four outside views

Household: A small firm may be a family's income, savings, and identity. Its exit may remove a local job or service.

Company and sector: Scale can lower costs or improve delivery. It can also move contracts away from newer firms.

Money and finance: Eligibility affects contracts, loans, grants, and private credit. Losing one public customer may weaken later financing.

Public power: Size lines, application burden, contract design, place, and review rules decide who can compete.

What would change the finding

  • Exact eligibility, program, contract, loan, grant, and application rule.
  • Firm size, age, productivity, owner wealth, household income, and financing need.
  • Revenue, exit, wages, hours, benefits, innovation, prices, quality, and local alternatives.
  • Worker, customer, owner, and neighborhood outcomes after the change.
  • Effects by race, place, industry, firm age, and private-credit access.

Next test

Follow firms, owners, workers, and customers for five years around an eligibility change. Join support, contracts, loans, revenue, exit, wages, patents, prices, service access, and household income.

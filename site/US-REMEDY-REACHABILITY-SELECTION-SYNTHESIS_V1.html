Remedy reachability can select who appears recovered v1

Checked: 2026-09-20
Status: bounded cross-domain synthesis; not a recovery rate or causal estimate

The finding

Across the current consumer, public-benefit, disaster, product-safety, and worker records, later remedy visibility is often conditioned on an earlier successful route step:

harm or need
  -> notice, application, complaint, or first payment route
  -> successful reach / acceptance / cashing / contact
  -> later remedy or administrative follow-up
  -> [open] adequate recovery and durable security

This creates a selection problem. A second payment, later administrative follow-up, or visible recipient endpoint may describe people who were already reachable, not everyone exposed to the original harm. People who missed a notice, lacked time, could not complete paperwork, could not cash a check, or did not know how to pursue the route can disappear before the later endpoint.

Product recalls A repair or repeat remedy can be recorded for a purchaser or product The public record does not establish safe use, purchaser receipt, or the people who never reached the repair route

These rows are not pooled. They show a recurring measurement structure, not a common denominator or a universal institutional failure claim.

Why this matters for culture and consumer behavior

The latest CFPB records add an important refinement: reachability has at least two separate layers.

  1. Route reachability: the person has a usable instruction, contact path, application, complaint channel, or payment mechanism.
  2. Outcome reachability: the person actually receives, cashes, uses, and benefits from the remedy.

Cash App currently gives public evidence of route reachability: consumers are not required to take action at this time, and a contact path is published. That does not establish outcome reachability. Wells Fargo goes further in a different direction: the regulator reports formal fulfillment and terminates the order, but the public record still does not provide borrower-level receipt, adequacy, or durable recovery. These should not be collapsed into a single “redressed” variable.

Atkins shows why the distinction matters even when receipt is reported: all ten drivers were reported paid, but the record still leaves adequacy, later work, trust, and household security open. Reaching the money stage is stronger evidence than reaching the information stage, but it is not the same as reaching recovery.

Reachability changes the meaning of later behavior. A person who receives a second refund may look like a successfully recovered customer even though the record says nothing about people who never received the first check. A person who remains on SNAP may look like continued participation even when the route was interrupted and household buffers were depleted. A worker who reaches an agency may appear in the remedy record while a similarly harmed worker who does not complain is invisible.

The cultural consequence is that “trust,” “satisfaction,” “continued use,” and “recovery” can be selectively observed among people who remained able to use the institution. Non-use may mean distrust, exclusion, exhaustion, lack of information, or a workable alternative; continued use may mean dependence, not confidence. The atlas must not infer the meaning from the endpoint alone.

The NYC DCWP restitution reachability ledger and settlement page makes the same selection visible before payment: the city can name eligible worker groups, state restitution amounts, schedule checks, and provide a mail/deposit update route without exposing which workers actually received the money or whether it restored lost time and income. A delivery plan is evidence about institutional reachability, not a worker recovery rate.

Required fields for the next episode pass

Every remedy-focused episode should preserve two denominators:

  1. everyone exposed or eligible for the route; and
  2. everyone who successfully reached each successive step.

The minimum sequence is:

exposed / eligible
  -> notice received
  -> understood and attempted
  -> accepted / filed / cashed / contacted
  -> remedy issued
  -> remedy received
  -> account, work, housing, or safety correction
  -> later security, trust, switching, non-use, or exit

Missingness at each step must be reported separately. A later-stage rate must not use the earlier successful group as though it were the full exposed population.

Boundary

The current evidence supports a reachability-selection mechanism and several concrete route barriers. It does not estimate how large the selection effect is, prove that any institution intentionally excludes people, or show that unreached people would have recovered if reached. The next decisive evidence is a linked claimant, case, or respondent record containing failed attempts, reissue or correction effort, final receipt, and later outcome.

Related: remedy implementation and recovery synthesis, strongest end-to-end chains, remedy-stage coverage audit, and consumer repeat-refund recipient gate. The new DOL Workers Owed Wages reachability layer shows that locating, notifying, and maintaining a payment route can remain part of the remedy after an agency has recovered the wages. The new CFPB redress-plan follow-up finding shows that a formal redress design can explicitly include follow-up for undeliverable or uncashed payments, while still leaving final receipt and recovery open.

The same distinction appears in the CFPB Block/Cash App consent order: the order requires a report after the redress plan identifying consumers and accounts that received redress and the total amount paid. That is a useful measurement contract, not evidence that the report was completed or that the underlying fraud/dispute problem was corrected for any named consumer.

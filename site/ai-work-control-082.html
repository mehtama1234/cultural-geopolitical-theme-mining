Poland’s JASSM-ER control record shows an evidence gap before delivery can be inferred

Status: historical implementation/control finding · Checked: 2026-09-14

The finding

The Polish Supreme Audit Office (NIK) provides a useful historical control layer for the JASSM-ER case, but not a delivery count. In its review of Poland’s 2022 defence-budget execution, NIK says the Armament Inspectorate settled a JASSM-ER production-cost line using a program-review presentation that showed dates and described the production process, but did not contain a numerical value tied to the settlement. NIK therefore reported that the agency lacked reliable knowledge of the actual realization and effects of the relevant contracts and had recorded nearly PLN 2 million in advances unreliably.

This is evidence about the observability and control of contract realization, not evidence that JASSM-ER missiles were or were not delivered. It strengthens the current customer-realization watchpoint: an announcement, production milestone, advance, or process description is not interchangeable with an accepted quantity, shipment, fielding, or operational inventory record.

production-cost / FMS settlement
  -> program milestones and process description  [observed]
  -> numeric evidence tied to settlement          [missing in audited case]
  -> accepted JASSM-ER quantity                   [open]
  -> delivery / fielding / training               [open]
  -> operational capability or leverage           [open]

Why it matters for the end-to-end geopolitical question

The program is trying to follow a capability from material and institutional conditions through production, delivery, usable readiness, and external consequence. NIK shows why the middle of that chain cannot be filled by language such as “production,” “milestone,” or “advance.” The decisive fields remain variant, lot, quantity, acceptance authority, shipment date, recipient unit, training/integration status, inventory status, and any subsequent action by Poland or another actor.

The finding also adds an institutional mechanism: the state’s ability to know what a contract produced depends on documentary controls, not only on whether a supplier reports activity. That mechanism is relevant to geopolitical capacity, but the audit does not show that the control gap changed Poland’s military options or alliance bargaining position.

Counterinterpretations kept visible

  • Missing numeric support in an audited settlement does not mean the supplier failed to produce the missiles.
  • The audit may concern a production-cost or subscription line whose accounting object is narrower than the missile inventory.
  • A later reconciliation may have supplied documentation not available during the audited period.
  • The separate 32-unit delivery example is deliberately not treated as a JASSM-ER observation.
  • The 2022 control finding cannot establish delay or non-delivery of Poland’s 2024 JASSM-ER agreement.

Next decisive acquisition

Seek the underlying FMS case/contract identifier, later Polish audit follow-up, US DSCA or DoD delivery/acceptance record, and Polish Air Force or base-level training/inventory notice. Preserve accounting settlement, production, acceptance, shipment, fielding, and operational-use evidence as distinct stages.

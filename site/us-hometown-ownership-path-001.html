Connected US finding · ownership and place

When business ownership moves away from home

An owner does not have to be born in a place to serve it well, and a local-born owner does not automatically protect it. The issue is whether the place keeps firms, services, jobs, wealth, and a voice in decisions.

The argument

An owner may move because customers, workers, credit, housing, or a better market are elsewhere. The move can strengthen a business in the new place and leave a service gap in the old one. A firm can look local while its profits and key decisions sit elsewhere.

Birthplace is not the same as commitment.

What the sources establish

HBS’s account of Census research reports that the extra chance of staying in one’s birth state among self-employed White men fell from 8 points in 1970 to 2 points in 2018; the hometown advantage was below zero for all Americans in the latest sample. This measures birthplace and residence, not business survival or service quality.

NBER finds that home equity can help some people start longer-lived businesses, while much house-price-linked entry is small or tied to construction and real estate. Another NBER study links business formation to long-run local traits.

Follow ownership into place

capital, skills, housing, and local networks → where an owner can live and start → who owns firms and controls services → jobs, prices, profits, and place identity → housing, migration, and political demands

The evidence supports a weaker tie between birthplace and business location. It does not show that local control was lost or that mobile owners serve people worse.

Keep these apart

  • Local-born ownership and local commitment.
  • Firm location and decision location.
  • Business opening and survival.
  • Revenue and local wealth.
  • Owner movement and customer harm.

Next test

Link owner moves to firm records and local customer outcomes over five years. Track survival, jobs, wages, prices, service gaps, housing costs, profits, taxes, and local participation.

Reading rule

Follow where decisions, profits, jobs, and services end up.

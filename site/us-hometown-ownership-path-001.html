Connected US finding · place, ownership and housing

When business ownership moves away from home

A weaker link between birthplace and business location may change who owns local firms. It does not by itself show that residents lost services, jobs, or a say in the place.

In one sentence

The useful test is what happens to firms, workers, customers, and local voice after owners move.

Keep birthplace, local commitment, business survival, and resident benefit separate.

What the sources show

The hometown tie weakened

An HBS account of US Census research reports that the extra chance of staying in one's birth state among self-employed White men fell from 8 percentage points in 1970 to 2 points in 2018. The hometown advantage was below zero for all Americans in the latest sample. The measure is about staying near birthplace, not business survival or service quality.

NBER research links home equity and long-run local traits to business entry, but neither study follows residents through a local ownership change.

HBS account · NBER 21458 · NBER 28606

The connection

capital, skills and local networks → where an owner lives → who controls firms and services → jobs, prices, local wealth and voice → housing, migration and political demands

The evidence supports a weaker birthplace tie. It does not yet prove a loss of local control.

The four outside views

Household: A move may bring better pay or a better market. People who stay may lose a familiar service or gain a new one.

Company and sector: Owners follow workers, customers, credit, housing, and supply chains. New owners can bring money or a useful skill.

Money and finance: Home equity and credit can decide who can start and who must leave to find room. Profits and decisions may leave even when a business looks local.

Public power: Housing, lending, taxes, schools, and local buying rules shape who can own a firm and who has a voice.

What would change the finding

  • Firm openings, closures, owners, workers, wages, prices, and service access in the same place.
  • Owner residence, birthplace, industry, credit, housing wealth, and reason for moving.
  • Whether movers build firms that last longer or employ more people.
  • Whether residents can still start firms, buy homes, and shape public decisions.
  • Where profits, taxes, and major decisions end up.

Next test

Link owner moves to firm records and local customer outcomes over five years. Compare places where owners leave with similar places where owners stay.

Reading rule

Birthplace is not the same as commitment. A business can be useful without a local-born owner, and a local owner can still extract value from a place.

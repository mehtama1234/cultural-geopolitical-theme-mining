A short argument · US payments, rewards, and prices

Payment choice can move a cost through the price of everything else

A payment method can decide who receives a reward, who pays interest, and who absorbs a merchant fee.

The argument

The evidence supports uneven exposure, not a simple story that one group always loses. The net result depends on the same person's price, reward, balance, protection, and ability to switch.

Follow one purchase

A shopper buys the same item as three neighbors. One uses a rewards card and pays in full. One carries a balance. One uses cash. The merchant sees different payment costs but may post one price. The reward system connects all three households.

payment choice → merchant fee and reward funding → common price or payment benefit → household net cost, balance, protection, and trust → ability to shop or switch

The deeper finding

Payment systems distribute value through ordinary prices. A lower-cost method is not automatically better if it is less safe or cannot preserve a record; a reward is not a gain if it creates a carried balance.

What remains unproven

The NBER estimate is a system result, not a bill for one household. The sources do not yet show the same person's prices, rewards, interest, protection, and ability to choose another method.

Next test

Link household purchases to payment method, merchant fee, posted price, reward, interest, dispute, fraud, and later balance.

Reading rule

Keep the $30 billion system estimate separate from a person's actual net result. Uneven exposure is supported; personal loss needs linked records.

Finding 061: Local civic capacity can stay strong while national political trust falls

Status: bounded rural civic-capacity and local-legitimacy finding · Checked: 2026-09-21

The bounded finding

The Trust for Civic Life's 2025 community survey reached 1,029 people through 24 participating rural Civic Hub networks. In this selected sample, trust in local organizations averaged about 70%, compared with about 25% for national organizations. Nine in ten respondents said they had personal power to make a positive difference where they lived, and about three-quarters said their community could come together to solve problems. Only 11% said national leaders made their voice matter in the country's direction.

Among the small returning respondent subset, the report describes a 46% fall in mean trust in national political institutions from fall 2024 to fall 2025 while local trust, agency, and belonging held steady. The report also finds that agency was associated with deeper organizational responsibility and perceived problem-solving, not simply more volunteer hours.

national political distance
  -> local organization, relationship, and concrete problem-solving
  -> belonging and sense of agency
  -> local action or cross-difference cooperation [open]

Why it matters

This is a useful counterexample to the idea that falling national trust means people have become generally passive or disconnected. People may feel shut out of national politics while still believing that neighbors and local groups can solve practical problems. The likely mechanism is not conversation alone but having a meaningful role in work that produces a visible local result.

Limits and counterexamples

  • The sample came through Civic Hub networks and snowball recruitment; it is not a representative sample of rural Americans.
  • The pre-post comparison uses a small returning group and is not an attrition-adjusted causal panel.
  • Trust, agency, belonging, leadership, and action are self-reported and use source-specific question frames.
  • Local confidence does not prove material help, policy influence, durable participation, or agreement across political differences.
  • The report's funder and network role may shape who was reachable and how respondents interpreted the organizations.

Next test

Follow a place-linked civic episode from a concrete local problem through entry, role, time and resource contribution, visible result, cross-difference contact, and later continued participation or exit. Keep nonparticipants and people without a usable local organization in the comparison.

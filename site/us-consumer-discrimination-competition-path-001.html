Connected US finding · competition and fair treatment

When competition protects some customers first

Competition may push firms to treat customers fairly when those customers can leave. The people with the most time, money, information, and nearby alternatives may benefit first.

The argument

A firm responds to the customers whose defection would hurt it. That response can improve treatment for one group without removing the unequal cost carried by another.

Competition is a customer result only when the customer can find, reach, pay for, and use the alternative.

Follow one purchase

A Black customer visits one of two local businesses. The second offers fairer treatment but costs more and is farther away. A customer with a car and flexible hours can switch. Another may accept the cheaper discriminatory option, pay more, or go without.

customer group and ability to leave → firm competition and incentive → treatment, price, and service → customer access and effective cost → regulation, complaints, enforcement, and trust

The same logic can appear online when a platform limits search or makes leaving hard.

What the source establishes

EvidenceWhat it means
Using nationwide data from three industries before the 1964 Civil Rights Act, NBER finds increases in non-discrimination were concentrated in the least competitive markets, where the threat of White customer defection was lowest.Firms may change treatment when ordinary pressure is weak.
Non-discriminatory firms charged higher prices than discriminatory firms in the same local market, while Black customers were harmed through higher prices in the non-discriminatory market.A fairer treatment label does not reveal the full customer cost.
The study measures historical treatment and prices.It does not measure today’s firms, online markets, service quality, or every reason for a price difference.

Source: NBER Working Paper 33547.

The deeper finding

Customer power is unevenly distributed. Time, money, mobility, language, credit, disability access, and information determine whether competition is usable. A rule that relies on switching may protect people who can leave and miss people who cannot.

What remains unproven

The study does not establish how current firms treat different groups, whether online choice reduces or increases unequal treatment, or how much of a higher price buys a different service. It does not follow a present-day customer from treatment to search, switch, complaint, remedy, and trust.

Next test

Follow customers and firms in current markets for one year. Join concentration, switching, treatment, prices, fees, search time, service quality, denial, complaints, enforcement, and outcomes. Compare people with different ability to leave.

Reading rule

Keep competition, customer power, treatment, price, service, and discrimination separate.

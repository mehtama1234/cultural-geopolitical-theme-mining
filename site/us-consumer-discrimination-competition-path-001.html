Connected US finding · competition and customer treatment

When competition protects some customers first

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment. Choice is real only when a customer can compare, switch, and receive a similar service somewhere else.

The argument

A firm responds to the customers whose defection would hurt it. That response can improve treatment for one group without removing the unequal cost carried by another.

A customer may face a longer trip, a new application, a deposit, a different language service, or a new identity check to leave. The alternative exists, but it is not cheap to use.

Competition is a customer result only when the customer can find, reach, pay for, and use the alternative.

Follow one purchase

A Black customer visits one of two local businesses. The second offers fairer treatment but costs more and is farther away. A customer with a car and flexible hours can switch. Another may accept the cheaper discriminatory option, pay more, or go without.

“Fair” also needs separating. A firm may treat a customer with respect but charge more. It may offer the same price but deny a service. It may stop an openly discriminatory practice while leaving the customer with no nearby alternative.

customer group and ability to leave → firm competition and incentive → treatment, price, service, denial, or access → customer effort and effective cost → switch, complaint, enforcement, trust, or withdrawal

The practical exit route

Search

The customer must find the alternative and know that its price and treatment are comparable.

Switch

Leaving may mean travel, a new application, a deposit, a new identity check, or lost account history.

Remedy

A complaint may lead to help, but a low complaint rate may also mean people expect no remedy.

Competition works through a credible threat of exit. The market may look open from above while feeling closed inside the household. This connects to platform search, credit approval, and customer-service authority: the formal option matters only if the person can use it.

What remains unknown

The study does not establish how current firms treat different groups, whether online choice reduces or increases unequal treatment, or how much of a higher price buys a different service. It does not show whether low switching means satisfaction, high exit cost, or both.

Next test

Follow customers and firms in current markets for one year. Join concentration, switching, treatment, prices, fees, search time, travel, identity checks, service quality, denial, complaints, enforcement, and outcomes. Record what happened after the customer tried to switch.

Reading rule: keep competition, customer power, treatment, price, service, access, and discrimination separate.

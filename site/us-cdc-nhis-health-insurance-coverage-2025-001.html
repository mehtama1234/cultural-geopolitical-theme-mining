Finding 001: Coverage routes can expand while a large uninsured gap remains

Status: bounded population health-coverage and unequal-access finding · Checked: 2026-09-22

The bounded finding

CDC's preliminary 2025 National Health Interview Survey estimates that 28.0 million people of all ages, or 8.3%, were uninsured at interview. That was not significantly different from 2024's 27.2 million, or 8.2%.

Adults ages 18–64 had the highest uninsured rate at 11.6%, or 23.5 million people. Children ages 0–17 had a 5.6% rate, and adults 65 and older had a 0.7% rate. Among adults 18–64, private coverage rose from 66.6% in 2021 to 69.4% in 2025, while public coverage declined from its 2023 high to 21.0% in 2025.

Exchange-based coverage among people younger than 65 rose from 4.3% in 2021 to 6.3% in 2025, or 17.4 million people. Hispanic adults remained the group with the highest uninsured rate in 2025 at 21.9%, although that was lower than 2024's 24.6% and lower than 2021's 30.1%.

The route is:

coverage policy and enrollment route
  -> private, public, or exchange coverage
  -> continuity, premium, network, and cost-sharing
  -> ability to obtain care
  -> health and financial outcome [not observed here]

What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a population-level version of a recurring access pattern:

A coverage route can expand without eliminating the uninsured population or proving that insured people can afford and use care.

The age and racial differences also show why a national coverage average is not the same as equal practical protection.

Limits and counterevidence

  • These are preliminary early-release estimates based on household interviews; final editing and weighting may change results, generally by up to about 0.5 percentage points according to CDC's technical notes.
  • Insurance status at interview does not show continuity, adequacy, premium, network access, care use, medical debt, or health outcome.
  • The year-to-year comparisons are descriptive and do not identify which policy caused a coverage change.
  • Some people can have both public and private coverage, so categories are not mutually exclusive in every table.
  • A lower uninsured rate for a subgroup does not prove equal access or better care.

The next decisive artifact is a protected person-level route joining coverage transitions, premium and cost-sharing, network availability, delayed or received care, medical debt, and later health outcome.

Sources

  • CDC/NCHS 2025 NHIS early-release insurance estimates

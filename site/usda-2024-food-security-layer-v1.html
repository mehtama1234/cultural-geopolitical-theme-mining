USDA 2024 food-security and budget layer v1

Checked: 2026-09-17 Source: USDA Economic Research Service, 2024 Food Security Supplement
Unit: US households, households with children, income/status groups
Status: national descriptive layer; not a causal price-to-food estimate

Why this layer belongs in the broad program

Food is where a general affordability problem becomes a question of basic security, family protection, public assistance, health, and social meaning. It also shows why “consumer response” cannot be reduced to a purchase total: households may change diet variety, use assistance, seek community food, or reduce intake.

income, price, work, care, or resource constraint
  -> food budget, shopping, assistance, and substitution response
  -> food insecurity, diet change, or reduced intake
  -> health, family protection, dignity, and household security
  -> public-program demand, community response, and political meaning

The USDA measure captures whether households had enough resources to obtain adequate food during the year. It does not identify which price, employer, policy, store, or individual event caused a household’s status.

Vintage recheck: 2026-09-17

The USDA ERS food-security media resources page was updated July 28, 2026 and continues to identify the 2024 report as the latest annual household estimate. It reports 13.7% food insecurity in 2024 versus 13.5% in 2023, with no statistically significant difference, and 5.4% very low food security in 2024 versus 5.1% in 2023, also without a statistically significant difference. No newer annual estimate or raw file was acquired for this recheck. The local machine record and the 2024 CPS-FSS vintage therefore remain authoritative; the result should not be narrated as a newly accelerating food-security trend.

What the 2024 evidence shows

USDA estimates that 13.7% of US households—18.3 million households—were food insecure at some point in 2024. Of all households, 8.3% had low food security and 5.4%—7.2 million households—had very low food security, where eating patterns were disrupted or food intake was reduced. The overall rate was not statistically different from 2023.

The distinction matters. Low food security includes households that used coping strategies such as less varied diets, Federal assistance, or community food pantries while avoiding substantial disruption of eating patterns. Very low food security is the more severe outcome and should not be silently treated as equivalent to any report of food hardship.

Among households with children, 18.4% were food insecure. In 9.1% of all households with children, both adults and children experienced food insecurity; in some households adults were food insecure while children were protected. This is evidence of family-level distribution and shielding, not proof that parents made one uniform “food choice.”

Food insecurity was distributed unevenly: 39.4% of households below the Federal poverty line were food insecure, and rates were also higher than the national average for single-parent households, women living alone, Black and Hispanic households, and households in principal cities and rural areas.

USDA also reports that among households experiencing very low food security in the 30 days before the survey, 56.4% participated in SNAP, free or reduced-price school lunch, or WIC during that same period. Participation is an institutional response and resource source; it does not mean the program fully restored food security or removed the underlying constraint.

What this adds to the 14-theme map

  • Household room and consumption: food is a basic protected or sacrificed need, with severity and diet variety kept separate.
  • Care and social reproduction: adults and children can experience different levels of hardship within the same family.
  • Unequal exposure and status: poverty, family structure, race/ethnicity, gendered living arrangements, and place shape the distribution.
  • Public systems and policy feedback: assistance participation is part of the observed response, not merely background context.
  • Trust, identity, and cultural meaning: food access can implicate dignity, family responsibility, and community support, but those meanings need direct measures rather than inference from the food-security score.

Limits and next tests

The annual CPS Food Security Supplement is population-representative and valuable for prevalence and subgroup comparisons, but it is not a diary of each purchase or a longitudinal record of the exact price shock and later recovery. The 2024 national rate also does not by itself establish a recent price trend: USDA says its report does not analyze the causes of prevalence or trends.

Next, align food-security status with the same-period price basket, earnings and work transitions, benefit receipt and timing, health/care measures, and local food access where the unit and time window permit. Compare households that remain food secure through different strategies with households that reach low or very low food security. Measure diet variety, reduced intake, assistance, unpaid help, stigma, and political interpretation as separate outcomes.

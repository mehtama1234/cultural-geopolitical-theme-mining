Connected US finding · privacy and customer power

Privacy protection can open the door to more sharing

A privacy rule can make people feel safer and share more. That may improve the exchange—or give a firm a richer record of a customer’s life.

The argument

The real questions are what the person understood, agreed to, could refuse, and what the firm did afterward. Receipts and visits traded for a reward may later shape offers, prices, ranking, credit, or access.

Trust and power can move in opposite directions.

Follow one permission

A shopper sees a privacy notice and joins a reward app. The notice feels safer after a new state rule. The shopper shares more receipts and store visits. Refusal may mean losing the reward or service. The first click does not settle the whole exchange.

privacy rule + clearer permission → customer feels safer → more data shared → firm learns more → offers, prices, ranking, service, or access may change → trust and power diverge

The deeper finding

The useful measure is not data collected. It is the full exchange: what the customer understood, what benefit they received, what later uses were allowed, whether they could withdraw, and whether they could challenge a bad result.

Trust helps when it supports an informed choice. It becomes a risk when it lowers scrutiny while the firm’s knowledge and ability to sort customers increase.

What remains unknown

The HBS result comes from one app and selected states. The sources do not follow a customer from permission to later price, offer, ranking, denial, service quality, deletion, withdrawal, or ability to use the service without extra sharing.

Next test

Track what customers were shown, what they shared, what they could refuse, and what happened later to prices, offers, service, eligibility, deletion, withdrawal, and complaints. Compare income, age, language, disability, and digital access.

Reading rule

Do not call a privacy rule successful because sharing rose. Show what the customer understood, what the firm did next, and whether the customer could still say no.

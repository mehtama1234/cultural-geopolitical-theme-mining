A short argument · US privacy, data, and trust

Privacy protection can open the door to more sharing

A privacy rule can make people feel safer and share more. That may mean a clearer exchange—or a richer record for the firm.

The argument

The real questions are what the person understood, what they agreed to, what they could refuse, and what the firm did afterward.

Follow one permission

A shopper joins a reward app after a state privacy rule makes the notice feel safer. The shopper shares more receipts and store visits. The firm learns more, while refusing may mean losing the reward or service.

privacy rule + clearer permission → customer feels safer → more data shared → firm learns more → offers, prices, ranking, service, or access may change

The deeper finding

Trust can increase sharing without increasing customer power. The useful measure is whether the customer received a real benefit, understood later uses, could refuse or withdraw, and could challenge a bad result.

What remains unproven

The HBS result comes from one app and selected states. The sources do not follow a customer from permission to later price, offer, ranking, denial, service quality, or ability to use the service without extra sharing.

Next test

Track what customers were shown, what they shared, what they could refuse, and what happened later to prices, offers, service, eligibility, deletion, withdrawal, and complaints.

Reading rule

Do not call a privacy rule successful because sharing rose. Show what the customer understood, what the firm did next, and whether the customer could still say no.

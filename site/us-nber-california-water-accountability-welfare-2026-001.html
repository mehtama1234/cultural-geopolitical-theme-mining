Finding 001: Local accountability changes which water investments become usable welfare

Status: bounded California infrastructure-and-political-economy finding · Checked: 2026-09-23

The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35690 studies California municipal drinking-water systems. Using water-system performance and investment data, political-accountability variation, and housing transactions at system boundaries, the authors report that weaker electoral accountability is associated with higher pollution and a weaker response to public investment aid. The model uses residents’ housing choices to recover clean-water valuations and estimates that stronger accountability would redirect investment toward high-need systems, reduce pollution, and generate progressive welfare gains.

local political accountability
  -> investment selection and subsidy response
  -> pollution and water-system performance
  -> resident-valued welfare and distribution
  -> [open] bills, health, maintenance, trust, and durable protection

What this adds to the atlas

This is a rare infrastructure finding that carries the chain beyond funding or construction. It shows how the political route can affect whether public money reaches high-return systems and whether residents receive the quality they value. It also gives the atlas a concrete counterexample to treating a grant or mandate as equivalent to realized public capacity.

Limits and next test

  • The evidence is California-specific and relies partly on structural modeling.
  • Electoral accountability may proxy for media, administration, finance, or resident composition; the observed relationships are not a universal causal estimate.
  • Housing-based clean-water valuations are not direct household health, affordability, or trust measures.
  • The counterfactual is not an observed statewide reform and does not establish maintenance, service continuity, or durable protection.

The next decisive test joins a named water system to investment, pollution, rates, maintenance, outages, household exposure, health, complaints, and local electoral response over time.

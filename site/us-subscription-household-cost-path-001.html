Connected US finding · recurring charges

When a forgotten subscription becomes a household cost

Automatic renewal can be useful, but it can also keep a charge running after value falls or attention fades. The customer needs a clear deal and a real way out.

The argument

A small recurring charge can compete with food, rent, care, debt, or a needed purchase in a thin budget. Silence can mean the service still matters, the customer forgot, could not afford the attention, or found cancellation too hard.

Not cancelling, not valuing, and not being able to cancel are different findings.

What the sources establish

NBER payment-card data across ten products show more cancellations when replacement cards force subscribers to actively renew. HBS describes recurring revenue and subscription fatigue. In May 2026, the FTC said Shutterstock agreed to pay $35 million to settle allegations involving charges without informed consent and difficult cancellation.

That case shows an enforcement path, not proof that every renewal is unwanted.

Follow the recurring charge

subscription starts → payment renews → use falls or value becomes unclear → notice or forced card change triggers action → cancellation, continued payment, fee, or overdraft → household room changes

The meaningful measure is not just the monthly fee. It is the fee, notice, use, cancellation effort, refund, and other bill displaced.

Keep these apart

  • Automatic renewal and unwanted renewal.
  • Not cancelling and not valuing.
  • Clear choice and silent default.
  • Cancellation effort and service value.

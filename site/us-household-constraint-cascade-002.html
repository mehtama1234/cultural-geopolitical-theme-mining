A prescription event can sit beside payment, debt, and work pressure

Status: bounded month-ordered MEPS event-to-household-context comparison
Checked: 2026-09-20

The bounded finding

Using MEPS 2024 person records with valid round ordering, 1,773 people had a first prescription event strictly between their R3/1 and R4/2 endpoint months; 16,950 formed the complementary round-valid group. The first recorded event's weighted mean self/family payment was $12.76, and 28.8% of event-window events had zero recorded self/family payment.

At the later R4/2 round, the event-window group also showed higher contextual rates of cost-related care delay, medical debt, debt-collector contact, and nonemployment:

Later/context measure Prescription event Complementary group
Cost-related care delay 8.09% (SE 0.73; n=1,755) 6.45% (SE 0.29; n=16,633)
Medical bill problem 8.82% (SE 0.74; n=1,758) 7.48% (SE 0.49; n=16,657)
Medical debt 19.45% (SE 1.14; n=1,743) 15.17% (SE 0.68; n=16,471)
Debt-collector contact 15.03% (SE 1.24; n=1,750) 13.08% (SE 0.67; n=16,609)
Not employed at R4/2 37.53% (SE 1.78; n=1,523) 33.42% (SE 0.59; n=13,740)

Why it matters

This gives the health-cost route a dated material-pressure layer:

prescription event
  -> recorded event payment component
  -> care delay, bill, debt, collector, and work-status context
  -> [open] fill, adherence, treatment continuity, and household substitution
  -> [open] remedy, recovery, trust, action, switching, or exit

The key insight is not that a prescription caused debt. It is that a health event can appear alongside several different household currencies at once: cash payment, delayed care, debt, collection pressure, and employment status. Those currencies should not be collapsed into one “medical burden” measure.

Do not overread

This is a selected, month-ordered descriptive comparison, not a causal effect. The prescription may follow an earlier health need, and the complementary group is not a no-need or no-prescription control. The payment field is not the total price, deductible, insurance liability, unpaid balance, or forgone-care cost. Round-level outcomes are not claim-specific, and the data do not show fill/non-fill, adherence, treatment continuity, household time substitution, remedy receipt, recovery, trust, political action, switching, or exit.

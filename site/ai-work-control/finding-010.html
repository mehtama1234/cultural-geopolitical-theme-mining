Finding 010: The job of the system matters more than the AI label

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

“AI adoption” is too broad to predict what happens to workers. A system that recommends training, assigns shifts, sets work pace, monitors time, evaluates performance, or recommends discipline changes different parts of the work relationship. The outcome depends more on that job and its surrounding rules than on the label AI alone.

What we directly know

  • The JRC defines algorithmic management as programmed procedures that coordinate labor, including shifts, instructions, performance assessment, rewards, and penalties, whether or not AI is used. JRC
  • JRC analysis reports different relationships by practice: direct direction of tasks and pace is most strongly associated with lower discretion and greater work intensity, while some platformisation has no significant effect. JRC AIM-WORK
  • An NBER experiment found no significant average performance effect from digital surveillance, but unexplained surveillance changes reduced output. NBER
  • A separate NBER field experiment found generative-AI access reduced email time and work outside regular hours without detectable changes in task quantity or composition. NBER
  • The IBM Germany framework separates lower-risk recommendations from higher-risk personnel recommendations and automatic decisions. IBM record

Causal chain under test

system function -> type of decision or constraint -> discretion and information -> pace, time, evaluation, or responsibility -> job quality and bargaining

The system function is the first variable to record. “AI use” is only a starting label.

Minimum classification

System functionImmediate questionMain risk or possible gain
AssistanceDoes it help a worker complete an existing task?Time saved, learning, or deskilling.
RecommendationWho sees it, and can the person reject it?Better access or hidden ranking.
AllocationWho assigns shifts, tasks, customers, or opportunities?Better matching or less choice.
DirectionWho sets task order, method, or pace?Coordination or intensification.
MonitoringWhat is observed, and can it be used for discipline?Safety and feedback or surveillance.
EvaluationWho is scored, by what data, and with what appeal?Feedback or bias and status loss.
Personnel actionCan it affect hiring, promotion, pay, or termination?Speed or high-stakes harm.

Wider meaning

A country may host workers while another firm owns the model, data, cloud service, or platform rule. Dependence is different when a system assists a worker than when it allocates labor, evaluates people, or controls access to income. This is an inference, not proof that every software dependency becomes geopolitical leverage.

Strongest challenge

Real systems bundle functions together. A tool can assist, monitor, and evaluate at once. The classification is useful only if the project records which data travel across those functions.

What would change our mind

  • The AI label predicts outcomes as well as or better than system function.
  • Different functions have similar effects after rules, sector, and worker differences are controlled.
  • Workers experience no meaningful difference between systems that help them choose and systems that choose for them.

Next tests

  1. Code every source by function, decision, data, and appeal path.
  2. Find workplaces where more than one function is observed.
  3. Compare unionized, unorganized, platform, public, and contractor settings.
  4. Track when assistance data become performance or personnel data.

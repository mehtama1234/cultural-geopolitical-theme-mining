Finding 014: Infrastructure can build capability and dependence at once

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

Data centres, cloud migration, and public financing can give a country more digital capacity while tying it to particular operators, contracts, equipment, and power systems. The question is whether local skills, data control, provider choice, and public oversight grow with the physical asset.

What we directly know

  • World Bank says many countries lack connectivity and reliable power, secure cloud and compute, quality data, and skills; uncoordinated gaps keep pilots from scaling. World Bank
  • In Romania, the Bank reports migration of up to 60 public applications to cloud, including payments, procurement, and early-warning systems, with new rules. World Bank
  • Romania's digital authority specifies a target of at least 30 migrated applications by June 2026 and an eligible value of about €177.05 million. Romanian Authority for Digitalization
  • IFC disclosed up to US$150 million for a 96 MW first phase in Johor; more than US$900 million in total financing was later announced. IFC · IFC financing
  • Yondr announced the completed sale of the campus to Vantage, and Vantage linked it to a US$1.6 billion APAC investment led by GIC and ADIA. Yondr · Vantage
  • World Bank says readiness requires synchronizing supply, demand, and rules. World Bank
  • IEA identifies grid, equipment, minerals, and energy-security constraints. IEA

Romania and Malaysia are project accounts. Expected benefits are not realized outcomes.

Causal chain under test

public finance or policy support -> physical data centre, cloud migration, or connectivity -> users adopt service -> local data, skills, jobs, and applications grow -> provider choice and public capacity change -> costs and dependence are distributed

Cultural and geopolitical meaning

Infrastructure enters daily life through payments, procurement, alerts, health, education, work, and business data. “Data sovereignty” matters only if institutions and people can exercise control, not merely if a facility sits inside national borders.

who finances -> who owns -> who operates -> who supplies power and equipment -> who holds data -> who can switch -> who bears interruption

This is an inference, not proof that either country is coerced or unable to exit.

Strongest challenge

External operators may provide cheaper and more reliable services. Foreign capital can create skills and jobs. Public commitments can create a market rather than lose sovereignty. Compare cost, service quality, local learning, and exit options.

Next tests

  3. Map operators, cloud, chips, power equipment, lenders, insurers, and public anchor clients.
  4. Compare public-cloud migration with interoperable or local alternatives.
  5. Test local skills and bargaining power.

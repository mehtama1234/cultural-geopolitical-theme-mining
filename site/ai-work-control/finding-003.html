Research index · AI, work, and control

Finding 003: Control changes through meaning, not only measurement

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

The early evidence does not support a simple story in which more digital monitoring automatically raises performance. A randomized study suggests that surveillance by itself had no average performance effect, while unclear explanations for why monitoring was used reduced output. A separate field experiment found that AI access saved time without changing the amount or mix of recorded tasks.

The deeper issue is not only how much work is measured. It is whether workers understand the system, can question it, and know who is responsible when it fails.

What we directly know

  • In an online labor-market experiment, surveillance had no significant average effect on performance. When the reason for using or removing surveillance was not explained in relation to worker performance, output fell. NBER Working Paper 33348
  • In a field experiment across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers, AI access reduced email time and time spent working outside regular hours, without detected changes in task quantity or composition during the experiment. NBER Working Paper 33795
  • HBS describes trust, experimentation, and clear responsibility when something goes wrong as conditions for adoption. HBS Working Knowledge

Causal chain under test

new system -> workers ask who controls it -> explanation and recourse shape trust and effort -> time, task choice, and review change -> performance and job quality change unevenly

The experiment supports the importance of explanation in one performance setting. The wider claims about trust, discretion, and job quality remain open.

Why this matters

A firm can use the same tool to remove wasted time while preserving judgment, or to turn every action into a score without giving workers a way to challenge the score. The technical system may look similar while the daily experience is different.

Strongest challenge

These studies use different tools, populations, and outcomes. They do not prove that explanation always matters or that AI access is harmless. They show why monitoring, assistance, and management must remain separate variables.

Next tests

  3. Search for direct evidence on promotion, discipline, performance scores, and appeals.
  4. Compare employer-controlled tools with worker-chosen tools.
  5. Track whether saved time becomes training, higher output, shorter hours, or more work.

Finding 008: Worker voice is part of system design

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

Worker voice is not only a remedy after an automated decision goes wrong. It can shape what the system is for, what it measures, how it is limited, and who is responsible before the tool becomes part of daily work.

The ILO’s global case-study review shows worker representatives taking part in AI and algorithmic-management decisions at national, sectoral, company, and workplace levels. The cases do not show that participation always works. They show that participation is itself a practical control point.

What we directly know

  • The ILO review examines cases across Europe, North America, Asia, South America and the Caribbean, and Africa, at national, regional, sectoral, company, and workplace levels. ILO Global case studies
  • The study focuses on worker representatives influencing decisions about employment, algorithmic management, and working conditions. ILO Global case studies
  • The European Commission identifies transparency, human oversight, safety, accountability, and the ability to contest decisions as central issues. European Commission

Causal chain under test

workers receive information -> they question purpose and design -> rules change before deployment or during use -> data, pace, evaluation, and recourse are bounded -> the system fits work better and causes fewer avoidable harms

The case study supports the existence of this governance path. It does not prove that participation produces better outcomes in every setting.

What to record in each case

  1. Who represents workers?
  2. At what level does dialogue occur?
  3. What information is shared before deployment?
  4. Which choices can workers influence?
  5. Can they block, delay, or change deployment?
  6. Are data, evaluation, pace, and health limits written down?
  7. What happens when management rejects a concern?
  8. Is there a later appeal or enforcement path?

Why this matters

An appeal is a late form of power. Participation before deployment is earlier power. A firm can offer training while keeping the purpose, data, and rules fixed; that is not the same as giving workers a say in the system.

Strongest challenge

Case studies often select visible or successful examples. Participation may be symbolic, late, or uneven. We need the actual terms, decisions, and outcomes before calling it effective worker power.

Next tests

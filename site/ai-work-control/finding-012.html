Finding 012: AI access is not the same as AI capacity

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

An AI interface does not create equal capability. Results depend on capital, data, software, training, electricity, connectivity, institutions, and a way to change or challenge the system.

What we directly know

  • The World Bank says AI gains depend on local complements and that mismatched development and deployment contexts can weaken results. World Bank
  • BIS reports that software, data, and workforce training support AI gains, which are largest for medium and large firms. BIS
  • IEA shows that AI depends on data-centre electricity, grids, minerals, and connection capacity. IEA
  • BEA links AI measurement to production accounts, capital, R&D, labor, data assets, and digital services, while noting incomplete definitions and data. BEA
  • IMF modeling separates wage inequality from wealth inequality and links distribution to firm adoption choices and capital returns. IMF

These sources cover different layers. They support a complements hypothesis, not a global ranking.

Causal chain under test

model or tool access -> capital, data, software, skills, power, connectivity, institutions -> actual use and system function -> output, worker opportunity, public service, or energy demand -> value and cost distribution -> firm and state dependence

Minimum capacity test

ComplementQuestionHidden failure
CapitalWho pays for integration and change?A free tool exists but only large firms redesign work around it.
DataWho owns, cleans, and reuses data?Local data are missing, restricted, or poor.
Software and workflowCan the tool enter the real process?A pilot works while the old process remains.
Skills and trainingWho can use, supervise, and correct it?Responsibility shifts to workers without support.
Electricity and hardwareCan reliable power, chips, cooling, and connection be secured?Service is imported while physical dependence grows elsewhere.
InstitutionsCan people inspect, appeal, procure, and regulate?Adoption grows while accountability lags.
Outside optionsCan a worker, firm, or state refuse or switch?Formal access exists but practical choice disappears.

Cultural and geopolitical meaning

The same technology can feel like opportunity in one place and dependence in another. The relevant map is not simply which country uses AI, but who owns the model, cloud, chips, data, energy, contracts, rules, and exit options. This is an inference, not a finding about every deployment.

Strongest challenge

Complementary inputs may simply measure better management. Larger firms may have been more capable before adoption. Scenarios are not worker outcomes. Comparisons must handle size, prior capability, sector, country, and timing.

Next tests

  1. Create a firm-country complement scorecard with sources and dates.
  2. Compare large and small firms using the same system function and outcome.
  3. Match AI use with software, data, training, electricity, and capital investment.
  4. Track whether imported systems create local capability or deepen dependence.
  5. Add a public-service procurement and appeal case.
  6. Add counterevidence from capability built without dominant external providers.

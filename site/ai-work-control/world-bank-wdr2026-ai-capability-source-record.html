Question

What does AI require to create useful capability in low- and middle-income countries, and how might concentration of the AI value chain change power within and across countries?

Report frame

The report distinguishes adopt existing tools, adapt them to local language, data, institutions, and needs, and advance frontier models and infrastructure. Adoption and adaptation are more feasible for most developing countries than building the frontier. Importing a tool is not enough when local complements are weak or contexts do not match.

Four practical foundations

  • Connectivity: reliable internet and power
  • Compute: data centers and secure cloud
  • Context: good local data
  • Competency: people who can use, adapt, and govern systems

What this establishes

  • Frontier production is concentrated, while adoption is more feasible than building the frontier.
  • Local fit and complementary capacity affect value.
  • Public procurement can influence local capability.
  • AI can shift power within and between countries.

What it does not establish

  • It does not rank countries by AI sovereignty.
  • It does not prove concentration causes coercion.
  • It does not provide a causal estimate of national growth, jobs, or political stability.
  • It does not replace a country case with contracts, outcomes, switching costs, and user evidence.

Country-case fields

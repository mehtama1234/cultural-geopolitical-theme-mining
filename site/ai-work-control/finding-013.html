Finding 013: AI sovereignty is more than owning a model

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

A country does not become capable merely by buying access to an advanced model. A more useful test is whether people and institutions can adapt, inspect, switch, protect local data, build skills, and enforce rules.

What we directly know

  • The World Bank reports that advanced models, chips, and data centers are concentrated in a small number of countries and firms, creating dependency risks. It distinguishes adopting, adapting, and advancing AI. World Bank
  • Imported systems may not fit local languages, institutions, norms, or needs; electricity, internet, skills, data, capital, and institutions also matter. World Bank
  • The report recommends multiple platforms and providers and describes low-cost “small AI” through basic phones and limited-connectivity channels. World Bank
  • IEA shows that AI depends on electricity, grids, data centers, and critical-mineral supply chains. IEA
  • World Bank AI-readiness work names connectivity and reliable power, compute and secure cloud, quality data, and skills as practical foundations. World Bank AI readiness

These sources support a capability framework. They do not establish that any particular country is sovereign or dependent.

Causal chain under test

provider or model access -> local adaptation, data, skills, infrastructure, interoperability -> usable system -> worker, household, public-service, or firm outcome -> local learning and bargaining power -> ability to switch, regulate, or refuse

Practical sovereignty test

CapabilityQuestionFailure
AdaptationDoes it fit local language, law, norms, and work?Available but socially or legally wrong
InteroperabilityCan institutions use more than one provider or move data?A pilot becomes lock-in
InspectionCan people test errors and bias?The system becomes a sealed authority
Local learningDo local workers and firms gain reusable skills and tools?Use grows while value leaves
Physical capacityCan power, connectivity, compute, and maintenance be secured?Access fails during shocks
Public capacityCan government procure, evaluate, regulate, and remedy?Adoption outruns oversight
ExitCan users switch without losing records, service, or income?Formal choice, practical dependence

Cultural and geopolitical meaning

National AI competition can hide the ordinary question of whether a teacher, nurse, farmer, public worker, or small business can use a system that understands the setting and can be corrected. The less replaceable each provider and infrastructure link is, the more a technical relationship may become a bargaining relationship. Concentration alone does not prove coercion.

Strongest challenge

Local adaptation and interoperability may cost more and reduce short-run performance. Provider concentration can lower prices. Sovereignty should be measured by outcomes and options, not self-sufficiency slogans.

Next tests

  1. Build country profiles using the seven capability fields without one synthetic score.
  2. Compare the same function in single-provider and multi-provider settings.
  3. Find cases where local language, data, or procurement changed performance.
  4. Track portability, switching costs, interruptions, and renewal terms.
  5. Add open or locally adapted systems as counterevidence.
  6. Link capability to worker, household, public-service, and firm outcomes.

The question is whether worker knowledge and collective action changed the purpose, limits, data, pace, ownership, or protection around an AI or algorithmic system. The ILO paper is a working paper based on case studies, interviews, archival sources, academic research, and news reports. This table records what it reports; it is not a controlled test.

First pattern

information -> bargaining or collective action -> enforceable change

Information alone is not the same as power. The reported changes become stronger when workers have a recognized forum, legal rights, a credible strike or public-action threat, system-data access, or a way to reach the lead firm behind a contractor. This is an inference from the cases, not an estimated causal result. The ILO’s comparison says efforts tend to be more effective where employer exit is constrained, collective voice is supported, and labor solidarity is inclusive.

What remains missing

  1. Actual agreements, contract language, and legal decisions.
  2. Failed or symbolic consultation cases.
  3. Outcomes by gender, race, migration status, disability, and contract type.
  4. Persistence after a new vendor, manager, contract, or model version.

Finding 011: Planning can change before measured output does

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

The first economic effect of AI may appear in planning, capital spending, data work, training, and production redesign before a clear rise in output, pay, or employment. “AI adoption” is therefore a weak end point.

What we directly know

  • BEA finds that business expectations of AI use were not always realized, and a late-2025 Census survey question change nearly doubled reported use and created a break in the series. BEA
  • BEA finds mixed links between stated motivations and later industry outcomes. Automating labor is associated with increased capital inputs, but not a statistically significant relative decline in labor's contribution to output growth. BEA
  • BEA says structural change may be in planning before it is visible in outcome data. BEA Digital Economy
  • BIS reports a 4% short-run labor-productivity increase, no short-run employment reduction, higher wages, and larger gains for medium and large firms. BIS

These records observe different stages, units, and methods.

Causal chain under test

business expectation or use case -> investment in software, data, training, R&D, or equipment -> work process and task structure change -> output, labor demand, wages, prices, or margins change -> distribution through firms, workers, households, and states

Measurement cautions

  1. Adoption changes meaning when survey questions change.
  2. Business motivation is not proof of the intended result.
  3. Industry averages hide firm and worker differences.
  4. Capital deepening may raise output without short-run job replacement.
  5. A planned or modeled gain is not a realized gain.

Next tests

  1. Build a time-ordered panel from stated goal to worker exposure and outcomes.
  2. Separate survey-definition breaks from real changes in use.
  3. Compare labor-automation plans with product-expansion and quality plans.
  4. Match production accounts to worker and firm evidence.
  5. Compare firms and countries with different complementary capacity.

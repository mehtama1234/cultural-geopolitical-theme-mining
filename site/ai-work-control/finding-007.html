Finding 007: Flexibility is not the same as freedom

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

Platform work can give people more control over when they log in while leaving them constrained by safety, household time, assets, location, customer behavior, and platform rules. At the country level, AI opportunity also depends on infrastructure: exposure is not the same as the ability to use a tool.

What we directly know

  • An NBER study of India and Indonesia finds very low female participation in platform driving. Women who enter work fewer hours, value flexibility and supplemental earnings, and face distinct safety, harassment, discrimination, accident, and hourly-earning gaps. NBER Working Paper 35467
  • The World Bank and ILO estimate that 30–40% of jobs in Latin America and the Caribbean have some generative-AI exposure, while inadequate digital infrastructure may block up to half of potential productivity gains. World Bank/ILO analysis

Causal chain under test

flexible access -> worker chooses when and where to work -> safety, care, assets, infrastructure, and rules limit choice -> access and returns differ by gender and place -> flexibility coexists with dependence

The evidence supports different constraints. It does not show that the platform caused every gender or regional difference.

Cultural and social meaning

Flexibility is often described as freedom. For many workers it may instead mean fitting paid work around unpaid care, safety concerns, or a lack of other jobs. The same work system can be liberating for one person and limiting for another.

Strongest challenge

The India/Indonesia study is descriptive and concerns platform driving; the World Bank/ILO figures are modeled estimates. Neither directly tests generative AI in ordinary employment.

Next tests

  1. Add worker studies from Latin America, Africa, and Southeast Asia.
  2. Track assignment, pricing, deactivation, and appeal rules.
  3. Compare workers who own assets and connections with those who rent or share them.
  4. Add care, safety, and social-protection measures.
  5. Test whether AI expands choice or optimizes around existing constraints.

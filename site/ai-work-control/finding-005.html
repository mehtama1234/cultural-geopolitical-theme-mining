Research index · AI, work, and control

Finding 005: Work can become easier and more controlled at the same time

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

Worker evidence adds a needed complication to the AI-at-work story. AI can remove tedious work and help people finish tasks, while monitoring and automated management can raise the pace of work, reduce room to decide, or make evaluation feel harder to escape.

The question is not whether AI is good or bad for workers in general. It is which use is being introduced, who controls it, and whether the worker can push back.

What we directly know

  • The JRC AIM-WORK survey collected responses from 70,316 people aged 16–65 across all 27 EU Member States during 2024–2025. JRC AIM-WORK
  • The Commission reports that 30% of EU workers use AI tools at work, 37% are monitored for working hours, and 24% have schedules set automatically. Survey results
  • The JRC reports that algorithmic management is associated with lower autonomy, less ability to take breaks, and higher work-related stress, with results varying by practice. JRC publication record
  • OECD reports that managers see possible efficiency gains while also reporting unclear accountability, difficult-to-follow decision logic, and worker-health concerns. OECD

Causal chain under test

AI assists a task -> time and effort may fall
monitoring or automated direction -> pace and discretion may change
both happen together -> a worker may feel helped by the tool and constrained by the system
-> job quality depends on purpose, limits, and recourse

The survey evidence shows the two experiences can coexist in the same economy. It does not yet prove how often they occur in the same job or person.

Cultural and social meaning

Productivity is not the whole experience of work. A worker may finish more in less time but have less choice about when, how, or why the work is done. The conflict is partly about status: is a worker treated as someone who exercises judgment, or as a data source whose pace and behavior are continuously scored?

Strongest challenge

Most worker-side evidence is survey-based and reports associations. Harder jobs may be more likely to receive monitoring, so the technology may not be the only reason for stress or lower autonomy.

Next tests

  1. Read the JRC tables and methodology, including country and sector differences.
  2. Separate AI assistance from automated evaluation and task direction.
  3. Add worker evidence by age, gender, disability, migration, contract type, and occupation.
  4. Compare safeguards with reported stress and autonomy.
  5. Add one non-European worker survey.

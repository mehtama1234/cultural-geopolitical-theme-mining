Finding 009: A written control is not yet lived control

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

A workplace AI rule can give workers formal rights without showing that workers can use those rights in daily work. The IBM Germany framework sets risk classes, correction paths, works-council inspection rights, and a ban on the highest-risk personnel decisions. But the record does not show how often those rights are used, whether managers follow them, or whether workers experience less harm.

The useful unit is the full path from a written rule to a worker’s ability to see, question, correct, delay, or stop a decision.

What we directly know

  • The archived IBM framework requires transparency, explainability, human decision-making, fairness, data and algorithm checks, risk classification, and an AI Ethics Council. Source record
  • It prohibits its highest-risk category of personnel recommendations or automatic decisions without a workforce benefit or harm-reduction potential, and requires correction processes and works-council inspection rights. Agreement text
  • ver.di reports an IBM internal skill-recommendation example in which a human remains responsible. ver.di
  • A 2022 IBM works-council interview says the framework was created before AI affected employees’ daily work and describes very-high-risk systems as excluded. This is not an audited deployment record. BTQ Kassel
  • An NBER experiment found no significant average performance effect from digital surveillance, but unexplained surveillance changes reduced output. NBER
  • JRC evidence associates some monitoring practices with lower autonomy, fewer breaks, and higher stress, with variation by practice. JRC
  • The Kenya court record shows legal access and interim protection can produce different outcomes across related appeals. Case record

Causal chain under test

written rule -> information access -> challenge -> timely correction -> changed work, pay, safety, status, or bargaining position

The first arrow is directly visible in the IBM framework. The later arrows are open tests. “A human decides” may still mean that a manager accepts a system recommendation or lacks time to review it.

What to measure

StageObservable test
Rule existsIs it written, binding, and within the worker’s scope?
InformationCan the worker see the data, purpose, inputs, and confidence?
ChallengeCan the worker question the result without losing pay, status, or future opportunity?
CorrectionIs there a named person or body that can correct the record or model?
TimingDoes correction happen before promotion, discipline, scheduling, or termination?
EnforcementAre violations recorded, remedied, and visible to representatives?
OutcomeDo monitoring, pace, pay, safety, autonomy, and mobility change?

Wider meaning

When software, data, and work cross borders, a local agreement may protect workers in one legal entity while the model, vendor, or lead firm sits elsewhere. The Kenya case shows why local work-to-lead-firm responsibility must be traced rather than assumed. This is an inference, not a claim that workplace agreements determine state power.

Strongest challenge

Formal rules may already prevent harm even when no dispute is recorded. The current evidence cannot tell whether the gap between formal rights and lived control is large or small.

What would change our mind

  • Audited records showing routine inspection, challenge, and correction before adverse action.
  • Similar autonomy, stress, pay, and mobility outcomes inside and outside such agreements.
  • Evidence that human review is independent, timely, and not merely approval.

Next tests

  1. Find a real IBM or works-council correction, block, or redesign.
  2. Compare an unorganized workplace using a similar tool.
  3. Add evidence on appeals, promotion, discipline, and retaliation.
  4. Track controls after vendor changes, restructuring, or model updates.

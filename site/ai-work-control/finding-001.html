Finding 001: AI may reorganize work before it changes average pay

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Finding

The first evidence suggests that AI can improve task performance for some workers and spread into many workplaces without producing an immediate change in average pay or hours. The deeper change to test is therefore the structure of work: who decides, who checks, who carries blame, and who captures the gain.

What we directly know

  • A study of 5,179 customer-support agents found a 14% average increase in issues resolved per hour after access to an AI assistant, with larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. NBER Working Paper 31161
  • A Denmark study reports task reorganization after early adoption, while finding no detectable effect larger than 2% on earnings or recorded hours two years after ChatGPT's launch. NBER Working Paper 33777
  • A U.S. survey reports rapid adoption but only a limited share of total work hours assisted. NBER Working Paper 32966
  • HBS describes adoption as tied to trust, responsibility, experimentation time, and worker review of AI output. HBS Working Knowledge

Causal chain under test

AI introduced -> tasks assisted -> work rules change -> discretion and responsibility move -> gains and risks spread unevenly

Only the first two links have meaningful first-pass evidence. The remaining links are a research program, not a conclusion.

Strongest challenge

The apparent gap between productivity and pay may reflect timing, measurement, or different study populations. We must not call it a transfer of power until direct evidence shows changed discretion, risk, bargaining, or value capture.

Next tests

  1. Read the full NBER papers and compare outcome definitions and time windows.
  2. Locate the underlying HBS cases and faculty research.
  3. Add direct evidence on monitoring, promotion, worker learning, and job quality.
  4. Compare large firms, small firms, contractors, and public-sector workplaces.
  5. Add one non-U.S. labor institution and one worker-led or critical source.

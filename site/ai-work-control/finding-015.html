Research index · AI, work, and control

Finding 015: AI adoption is broad, but shallow and uneven within work

Status: provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-13

Finding

Generative AI is entering work quickly, but adoption is not a uniform occupation-wide change. Workers doing similar tasks may use it at different depths, and controlled studies show that access can change productivity or working time without automatically changing the composition of work. The long-term question is which workers gain usable capability and who controls the resulting workflow.

What the sources directly show

  • In a staggered-access study of 5,179 customer-support agents, AI access increased issues resolved per hour by 14% on average, with larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. NBER W31161
  • In a field experiment across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers, treated users spent less time on email and reduced work outside regular hours, without a detected shift in task quantity or composition from individual provision. NBER W33795
  • A nationally representative worker survey finds adoption across many occupations and tasks, but fewer than half of workers adopt within most occupations and tasks. NBER W35677
  • A randomized experiment with 1,174 adults found larger performance gains for lower-education participants and a substantial narrowing—but not erasure—of the measured education gap on a defined task. NBER W34851

Evidence chain

AI becomes available across occupations -> workers doing similar tasks adopt unevenly -> organizational support and task rules shape actual use -> time, productivity, skill, discretion, and evaluation may change -> pay, status, trust, bargaining, and household security may change

The sources directly support availability, adoption heterogeneity, and selected productivity/time outcomes. The later worker-power and social-meaning arrows remain open.

Counterevidence

Individual AI provision can save time without changing task composition, and AI can narrow an education-based performance gap on a defined task. These results limit both a universal widening story and the claim that access automatically reorganizes work.

Cultural and societal meaning

The early social change may be a new difference between people doing similar work: some receive assistance that helps them learn or finish tasks, while others encounter the technology mainly as monitoring, ranking, or allocation. Adoption alone cannot tell us whether workers experience AI as assistance, pressure, opportunity, or loss of control.

Next test

Follow workers within the same occupation or workplace over time. Separate assistance, recommendation, allocation, monitoring, and evaluation. Measure training, task assignment, pace, autonomy, correction, promotion, pay, health, worker voice, and whether saved time is retained by workers or absorbed into new demands. Then connect those differences to household security, trust, status, and political or collective response.

Company bridge: Capgemini as an AI-using service firm

Status: first pass; descriptive and provisional · Last checked: 2026-09-11

Why this company matters

Capgemini is a large service and technology firm whose work depends on people, skills, projects, delivery processes, and client contracts. Its annual report gives us a customer/operator view of AI adoption inside a workforce.

What Capgemini reports

  • More than 420,000 talents in more than 50 countries.
  • 310,000 employees trained in generative AI and 194,000 in agentic AI, excluding WNS.
  • Average completed learning of 97 hours per employee in 2025.
  • An internal AI enterprise-intelligence platform available to all employees, combining company applications, internal data, curated external data, routine self-service, document generation, and role-specific agents.
  • AI-related changes to roles, skills, and career paths managed through workforce-pyramid monitoring, upskilling, and mobility.
  • About €700 million in expected restructuring costs over two years as the group adapts its workforce and skills.

Sources: learning at the speed of AI, employee data and AI platform, 2025 Universal Registration Document, and 2025 results.

What this adds

AI tool access -> training at scale -> role and career redesign -> workforce cost and risk adjustment

This supports the theme that work can be reorganized before average pay or employment data makes the change obvious. It also raises a material question: who pays for the transition when a service firm must train, redeploy, hire selectively, or restructure?

Inference under test

service work decomposed into skills -> skills visible in a company platform -> assignments and careers reorganized -> mobility depends on access and challenge rights

The first two steps are company-described. The last step is untested.

Strongest challenge

Training numbers show reach, not learning. Restructuring costs may reflect many forces besides AI. The report shows what management is building and watching; it does not show whether workers receive more choice, higher pay, or better jobs.

Next tests

  1. Find country and workforce disclosures on actual restructuring and mobility.
  2. Compare training participation with promotions, exits, pay, and assignment.
  3. Find employee consultation, data-correction, and appeal rules.
  4. Track whether client contracts turn AI productivity promises into changed workloads.

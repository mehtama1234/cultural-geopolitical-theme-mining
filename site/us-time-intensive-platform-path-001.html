Connected US finding · time, attention and customer choice

When a free app charges in time

A free app can still have a real price: time, attention, ads, and the other things a person cannot do at the same moment.

In one sentence

Time spent is not automatically wasted, but it is still part of the customer exchange.

Count what the service gives and what the time replaces.

What the source shows

Users responded only a little to more ads

Using two field experiments on Facebook and Instagram, NBER finds that users respond only a little to ad load, the main user cost in the setting. Time devoted to an activity and the cost of that time affect which online or offline activities can replace it. The paper also models a possible platform separation.

The evidence does not show long-run well-being, work, care, sleep, household effects, or an actual separation.

NBER Working Paper 34743

The connection

free or low-money-price service → time and attention cost → staying, switching, or offline activity → user benefit and platform competition → public rule or firm design

People may stay because the service is valuable, familiar, or socially important. More ads may be a tolerable price.

The four outside views

Household: Time on an app may offer connection, information, or rest. It may also replace sleep, care, work, or another service.

Company and sector: Platforms choose ad load, design, ranking, and how hard it is to leave. Weak alternatives can reduce exit.

Money and finance: A free service can create value without a bill, while data and attention support firm revenue. Count time value and later fees or purchases.

Public power: Privacy, competition, child safety, labor, and platform rules shape real choice and whether time cost is visible.

What would change the finding

  • Time use by income, age, disability, household role, work, care, and sleep.
  • Ad exposure, content value, notifications, design, and user control.
  • Staying, switching, offline substitutes, exit cost, and social ties.
  • Well-being, work, care, sleep, spending, and consumer value.
  • Actual merger, separation, pricing, or design change.

Next test

Follow users for six months before and after a change in ad load or platform design. Join time use, ads, content, work, care, sleep, well-being, switching, spending, and exit.

Reading rule

Do not call a free service low-cost without counting time. Keep attention, use, well-being, and competition separate.

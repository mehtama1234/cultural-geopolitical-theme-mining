When a free app charges in time

A free app can have a real price in attention and minutes that cannot be used for sleep, care, work, a local business, or another service.

The argument

Time online may provide real connection or information. Staying does not prove its price is low; it may show that alternatives are weak.

A free service is not low-cost until its time cost is counted.

Follow one evening through a household

A parent opens an app to answer a message. The feed and alerts keep the parent there for an hour. The time may supply real social contact, but it also replaces sleep, a child's conversation, or a local purchase. Another user sees more ads but leaves quickly. A third cannot leave because work, friends, and community information are concentrated there.

free or low-money-price service → time and attention cost → staying, switching, or offline substitute → user value and household tradeoff → platform competition and public rule

What the sources establish

EvidenceWhat it means
Two field experiments on Facebook and Instagram find users respond only a little to ad load in the study setting.More ads do not automatically produce immediate exit.
The NBER paper finds the share and cost of a person's time affect which online or offline activities can replace it.The same minute has different value for different users and households.
The paper models a possible separation of the two platforms.Competition and user choice depend on how connected the services are.
Connection, information, rest, or customer reach can be a real return for time spent.Time is a price, not proof of harm.
The evidence does not show long-run well-being, work, care, sleep, household effects, or an actual platform separation.The experiments do not settle the full social cost.

Source: NBER Working Paper 34743.

The deeper finding

The market price of a platform is not its whole price. A household feels the missing minute before it sees a bill. If friends, customers, news, and work contacts gather there, the user may have little practical choice even when the app is technically free to leave.

What remains unproven

The evidence does not show whether more time reduced sleep, care, work, or well-being for the same users. It does not show whether ads, alerts, content, or social ties are the main reason people stay, or what a separation would do to alternatives.

Next test

Follow users for six months before and after changes in ads, alerts, ranking, or design. Join measured time use to content, work, care, sleep, spending, well-being, switching, offline substitutes, and exit. Compare service value with the activity displaced.

Reading rule

Keep money price, time price, attention, use, well-being, and competition separate.

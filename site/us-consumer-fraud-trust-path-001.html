When a trusted service becomes a dispute

Fraud is a test of whether a person can get an answer, stop the loss and recover enough money to keep going. Payment type changes who carries the loss.

In one sentence

A financial loss can become a customer-service and trust problem.

We do not yet know whether a specific recovery response changes later use or political views.

What the sources show

1. Fraud reaches ordinary financial life

The Fed found that 21% of adults experienced financial fraud or a scam involving their money in 2024. Eight percent experienced fraud not related to a credit card.

Federal Reserve financial fraud findings

2. Payment type changes the household loss

Among adults with non-credit-card fraud, 63% lost money and 32% said some was not recovered. The Fed estimates $63 billion in net losses borne directly by consumers.

Federal Reserve household report

3. Reported losses are rising

FTC data show more than $12.5 billion in reported consumer losses in 2024 and $3.5 billion in reported imposter-scam losses in 2025.

FTC 2024 fraud data · FTC 2025 imposter data

4. The next problem is getting a response

CFPB complaint records cover credit reporting, debt collection, cards, accounts and money transfers. Complaints show where people seek help, not proof of a violation.

CFPB 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report

The connection

trusted name or payment route → scam or unauthorized transaction → loss, recovery attempt, time cost or account lock → trust, usage, credit or household-budget change

The sources support exposure, loss protection, reported losses and the complaint path. They do not show the full sequence for one consumer.

What is still unknown

  • How long does recovery take?
  • Does the consumer receive money, an explanation or an appeal?
  • Does the person borrow, stop using the service or change account behavior?
  • Does a recovery response change trust?

Next test

Join FTC reported-loss categories with CFPB complaint outcomes for the same product and period, then compare with the Fed household loss measure. Keep attempts, reported losses, unrecovered losses and trust changes separate.

Reading rule

A complaint is not proof of a violation, a scam attempt is not a loss, and a reported loss is not always an unrecovered loss.

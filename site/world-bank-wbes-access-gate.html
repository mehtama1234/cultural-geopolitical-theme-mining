Firm, work, and AI · World Bank Enterprise Surveys

The firm-level AI bridge is specified, but the microdata are still behind the portal.

The 2026 World Bank Enterprise Surveys AI follow-up is one of the most valuable missing links in the atlas. It can connect firm size, sector, ownership, finance, infrastructure, training, AI use, employment, sales, and productivity—but only after the survey file and design documentation are actually obtained and audited.

Current status: the US AI follow-up survey is confirmed in the official catalog; the authenticated firm microdata have not been retrieved; no firm adoption, employment, productivity, or country-comparison estimate is promoted.

What is confirmed

Eight AI follow-up surveys

The May 2026 release includes the United States, India, Jordan, Kenya, Malaysia, Mexico, Nigeria, Thailand, and Türkiye.

What the survey is designed to measure

Firm AI use and adjustment, reported productivity, employment, sales, costs, training, finance, infrastructure, and management constraints.

Public catalog evidence

The World Bank update page, dataset-list workbook, and WDR 2026 reproducibility catalog confirm the files and their intended role.

Public indicators are a context layer

The public custom-query layer exposes established economy/subgroup indicators with standard errors and observation counts, but the current public topic surface does not expose a separately documented AI-follow-up indicator family.

What is not available

The public catalog does not provide the respondent-level US AI follow-up file, questionnaire, skip patterns, missingness surface, or complete variance guidance.

Why it matters for the atlas

Current evidence can show worker AI use, industry output, firm expectations, algorithmic management, infrastructure capacity, and household room. Those are different layers. WBES could add the firm-level middle: which businesses adopt, what they change, what complements they have, and who reports an adjustment.

That would still not prove that a firm’s reported productivity change improved worker control or household security. It would make the bridge more testable by adding a defined firm unit, survey field date, size/sector/ownership cells, and reported outcomes.

Checks required after access

  1. Download the US file, comparable economy files, questionnaire, sampling documentation, weights, and variance guidance.
  2. Audit firm universe, field dates, size classes, sector definitions, ownership, missing codes, skip patterns, and survey weights.
  3. Separate any AI use, business-function use, frequency, integration, substitution, complementarity, and planned-adoption measures.
  4. Estimate employment, sales, productivity, costs, wages, training, investment, finance, infrastructure, and management outcomes with the documented survey design.
  5. Compare countries and firm groups only after checking common questions, periods, definitions, denominators, and uncertainty.
  6. Keep company claims, firm survey reports, worker outcomes, household outcomes, and geopolitical implications as separate evidence stages.

Access boundary

The official data-updates page confirms the AI follow-up release. The public indicator/custom-query layer is useful for bounded baseline context, including subgroup standard errors and observation counts, but it is not currently an AI-follow-up estimate. The WDR 2026 catalog identifies the files but says the firm microdata are not redistributed there. The Enterprise Surveys portal remains the required authenticated route.

This is an acquisition gate, not a finding that firms are or are not adopting AI. Until the file-level audit passes, the atlas keeps only source-existence and access evidence.

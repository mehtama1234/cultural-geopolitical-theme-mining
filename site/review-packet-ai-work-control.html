Review packet: AI, work, and control

Purpose: a short, evidence-disciplined route through the current writeups and themes. Read this before opening the full source registry or complete case ledger.

Current thesis: AI and algorithmic systems make work increasingly legible to organizations before they make work better for the people doing it. The central empirical question is who can see, change, contest, and ultimately bear the consequences of a decision.

The eight-piece route

1. The theme map: the argument in outline

Theme map v1 lays out the working chain:

work data -> profile/score/workflow -> allocation/evaluation/review
           -> discretion and access -> pay, status, trust, bargaining
           -> firm and state dependence

The first three arrows have the strongest support. The later arrows are the research program rather than settled findings.

2. The remedy spectrum: what counts as control

Remedy spectrum: from explanation to collective control compares disclosure, explanation, human review, appeal, restoration, payment, anti-retaliation, collective bargaining, and workplace co-governance.

Its key discipline is stage separation. A formal right to explanation is not a review; a review is not a changed decision; a changed decision is not proof of payment or durable protection.

3. Bandameeda: the strongest observed remedy chain

Amazon Flex / Bandameeda is the clearest single case. The Fair Work Commission found the deactivation unfair, found the platform's process deficient, ordered reactivation, and ordered lost remuneration.

It establishes that an external institution can test the substance of a platform's stated review process and that restoration and lost remuneration can appear in one public record. It does not establish payment receipt, durable access, process change, or generalization to automated ranking.

4. Kumar: the necessary counter-case

Uber Eats / Kumar prevents the project from treating every platform decision as presumptively unfair. The Commission accepted warnings, notice, human Community Operations review, and an objectively reasonable rating threshold, and dismissed the claim.

The comparison with Bandameeda is more informative than either case alone: the decisive variable is review quality and evidentiary reasoning, not the mere presence of a score or a human label.

5. Warraich and Khan: explanation, inquiry, implementation

Warraich and Khan show the recurring failure mode in which a worker receives warnings or a review label but cannot obtain a sufficiently particular account of the accusation or decision. Both reach reactivation; the remaining question is what happened after the order, including lost pay, access, and recurrence.

These cases move the project beyond “was there a review?” toward four sharper questions: what information did the worker receive, what evidence did the reviewer test, could the reviewer change the consequence, and was the remedy implemented?

6. Australia–Malaysia–Cambodia: institutional comparison

Stage-compatible comparison places three unlike evidence designs on one remedy chain without pretending they are one sample:

Place Strongest current evidence Current boundary
Australia adjudicated reactivation and lost-remuneration orders receipt, durability, and prevalence
Malaysia statutory architecture and an initial Tribunal hearing merits finding, award, payment, enforcement
Cambodia worker-reported complaint access and low reported resolution individual correction, payment, and state outcome

The narrow comparative finding is that institutional maturity is layered. Australia currently shows the deepest public remedy record; Malaysia shows a route moving from formal architecture into use; Cambodia shows broad reported access with weak reported resolution. None verifies the full chain through durable, non-retaliatory system change.

7. IBM Germany: control before harm

IBM Germany works-council AI framework is the anticipatory counterpart to the platform cases. A Group Works Council agreement documents risk classification, inspection, explainability, human decision-making, correction, escalation, and limits on high-risk personnel systems. A representative account also reports systems used or not used in Germany.

This is evidence of ex-ante governance architecture and reported practice, not proof of worker-level benefit. The decisive missing artifact is one named live deployment where a works-council intervention changed, constrained, or stopped the system.

8. Microsoft Germany: deployment-stage participation

Microsoft Germany's Copilot deployment record adds a company-reported live rollout: councils participated in a controlled tolerance phase, raised concerns about ranking, sensitive inference, and guardrails, and supplied feedback to product engineering before broader approval. It is useful implementation evidence, but the public account does not identify a specific feature change, worker outcome, or enforceable veto.

Themes to test while reviewing

  1. Legibility before benefit. Who gains the ability to see and classify work first: the worker, firm, platform, or state?
  2. Proceduralization without recovery. Do notice, appeal, and human-review language produce corrected records, restored access, payment, or prevention of recurrence?
  3. Flexibility versus freedom. Does flexible access expand outside options, or deepen dependence on opaque scores and platform identity?
  4. Corporate identity as remedy infrastructure. Can a worker identify and reach the entity that must implement the decision?
  5. Institutional translation across borders. How does the same control problem become a court order, Tribunal hearing, regulator commitment, or unresolved complaint in different settings?
  6. Sovereignty as usable alternatives. Do local skills, data control, interoperability, inspection, procurement, and provider exit grow with AI infrastructure?

Current synthesis

The project has moved from a general concern about AI adoption to a testable theory of control: systems convert work into profiles and decisions; institutions determine whether the affected person can inspect, contest, correct, and recover. Australia provides the best public evidence of adjudicated restoration, while Malaysia and Cambodia show why formal access and reported complaint availability are not equivalent to a remedy. The next decisive work is implementation evidence: payment receipt, continued access, recurrence prevention, and comparable case-level outcomes outside Australia.

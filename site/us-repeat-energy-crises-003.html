Finding 003: FEMA assistance visibility is stronger than survivor recovery visibility

Status: provisional current OpenFEMA aggregate delivery finding · Checked: 2026-09-19

The bounded finding

The live FEMA OpenFEMA Registration Intake and Individuals Household Program dataset provides a current aggregate view for the 2025 Los Angeles County wildfires (disaster 4856). Across 621 city/ZIP rows, it reports 261,117 valid registrations, 98,459 IHP referrals, 35,165 IHP-eligible records, and $182.8 million in IHP amounts. The same rows report separate Housing Assistance and Other Needs Assistance stages.

The safe conclusion is:

FEMA's public data can show where registration, referral, eligibility, and reported assistance amounts stand for a disaster, but it still does not show whether a particular survivor received an adequate payment, completed a repair, obtained stable housing, or recovered later.

Chain under test

wildfire damage and displacement
  -> registration and channel use
  -> IHP referral and eligibility decision
  -> award/payment, repair, or housing placement
  -> usable housing and material recovery
  -> next need, repeat crisis, trust, complaint, or political action

The API is an aggregate geography/program surface, not a survivor panel. The referral and eligibility fields describe different stages and should not be read as a single person-level funnel without identifiers and stage timing.

Evidence comparison

Stage Current observation Still missing
Registration 261,117 valid registrations across 621 city/ZIP rows Damage severity, household composition, insurance, duplicate/linked applications, and survivor follow-up
IHP referral 98,459 referrals, or 37.71% of valid registrations in this aggregate snapshot Referral reason, timing, channel sequence, appeal, and whether the same registrant later received assistance
IHP eligibility 35,165 eligible records, or 35.72% of IHP referrals Decision explanation, correction, payment issuance, receipt, adequacy, and unmet loss
Housing assistance 74,609 HA referrals; 5,653 HA-eligible records; $81.27M reported HA amount Placement, repair, rent/lodging outcome, completion, duration, and stable housing
Other needs assistance 94,152 ONA referrals; 35,083 ONA-eligible records; $101.50M reported ONA amount Need type, timing, payment receipt, protected outcome, and repeat need

The ratios are descriptive ratios of published aggregate fields, not approval rates for unique people and not estimates of recovery. HA and ONA categories may overlap with IHP stages and should not be added to count unique survivors.

What this adds to the atlas

  1. Administrative visibility can be precise without being lived recovery. FEMA exposes a much sharper registration/referral/eligibility surface than the broad GAO total alone, but the endpoint remains an administrative one.
  2. Eligibility is not payment. A record classified as eligible does not establish issuance, receipt, amount adequacy, or completed repair/housing.
  3. Geography does not create a household panel. City and ZIP rows support place-conditioned delivery diagnostics, not survivor-level causal or recovery claims.
  4. The missing middle is now explicit. The next useful file would preserve a lawful applicant key and timestamps through decision, payment, repair or placement, and later housing/work/health status.

Counterexamples and limits

  • A low referral-to-registration ratio can reflect eligibility rules, incomplete referral, duplicate or invalid records, different program universes, or timing; it is not automatically exclusion or failure.
  • An eligible record may later receive a payment, repair, or housing placement; the public aggregate snapshot simply does not expose that linkage.
  • Assistance amounts are program-reported totals, not average survivor awards and not proof that losses were covered.
  • A city/ZIP row can contain heterogeneous damage, insurance, income, housing, language, and access conditions.

Next decisive FEMA test

Acquire or obtain a lawful de-identified applicant-level follow-up that links:

disaster and damage
  -> registration channel and effort
  -> referral, eligibility, appeal, and decision reason
  -> payment/placement/repair and receipt
  -> housing/work/health status and residual loss
  -> repeat need, trust, complaint, or action

Until that exists, use the API for delivery geography and stage diagnostics, not a survivor recovery rate.

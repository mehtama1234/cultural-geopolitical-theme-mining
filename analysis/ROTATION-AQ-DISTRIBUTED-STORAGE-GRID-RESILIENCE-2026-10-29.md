# Rotation AQ — Distributed storage and grid resilience

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** energy / infrastructure / climate / public service / place  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 restored-service question

## Research question

When governments, utilities and communities deploy batteries, microgrids and
resilience hubs, what converts storage capacity into dependable electricity for
households and critical services during an outage—and who receives the benefit,
carries the cost and controls the operating decision?

## Conversion chain

```text
hazard or grid stress
  -> storage/microgrid investment and interconnection
  -> dispatch, islanding and maintenance capability
  -> electricity for a named household or critical service
  -> continuity of water, cooling, health, food, communications or shelter
  -> recovery and trust in the place's energy system
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| GAO-26-107772 Puerto Rico | About $14 billion was obligated for recovery and modernization since 2017, but little was disbursed and few projects were complete; resilience hubs and storage were among the interventions. | funding, project categories and implementation delay | named outage avoided, service restored, household continuity and durable reliability |
| GAO utility-scale storage review | Storage can improve reliability, resilience and efficiency, but deployment depends on technology, market, policy and operational conditions. | capability and deployment constraints | delivered service under a specific outage and distributional effect |
| DOE Community Energy Resilience Hubs | A hub design connects battery storage with essential businesses and nearby residences; the concept is explicitly place-based. | physical/community architecture | operating hours, prioritization, access rules, maintenance and resident benefit |
| DOE Critical Facility Energy Resilience | Demonstration projects aim to support critical facilities during outages or emergencies. | demonstration objective and technology pathway | measured continuity of a named fire, water, health or community facility |
| FERC reliability safeguards | Bulk-power reliability is treated as a system issue involving cyber threats, extreme weather and rising demand. | regulatory reliability frame | whether distributed storage changes local outage duration, affordability or trust |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** storage and resilience hubs are expanding as responses to weather,
  demand, cyber risk and fragile distribution systems.
- **Mechanism:** batteries only protect a place if they are connected correctly,
  charged, maintained, safely dispatched, and governed for the users who depend
  on them.
- **Operation:** an outage occurs; the system islands or dispatches; a facility,
  household or hub receives power; operators communicate and repair the wider
  network.
- **Material consequence:** critical services continue, medicines and food are
  protected, cooling or water remains available, and households avoid unsafe
  displacement or economic loss.
- **Meaning:** a resilience hub can become a trusted civic anchor when access is
  legible and reliable. If it is unavailable, poorly maintained or reserved for
  institutional users, “resilience” can be experienced as another promise that
  protects infrastructure before people.
- **Power:** utilities, agencies, owners and operators decide dispatch,
  eligibility and repair priorities. The reviewed public sources do not close
  the household-level distribution of benefits or burdens.

## Bounded finding

> **Distributed storage is a service-continuity capability, not a battery-count
> outcome.** A grant, installed megawatt-hour, resilience hub or storage
> demonstration does not establish that a named household or critical service
> retained electricity through an outage, or that the benefit was affordable,
> accessible and fairly governed.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Resilience is interpreted locally because the consequence is local. A hospital,
water plant, cooling center, school, small business and household do not have
the same load, backup duration, access rules or recovery clock. In Puerto Rico,
the source record makes the political meaning especially visible: large funding
obligations coexist with incomplete projects and recurring blackouts. The gap is
not merely technical; it affects whether residents believe that public recovery
capacity reaches ordinary life.

Community storage also raises an ownership question. A hub may protect a
community center or essential businesses while leaving renters, medically
dependent households or people without transport unable to use it. A strong
ledger therefore needs the outage, beneficiary, duration, dispatch rule and
maintenance record—not only installed capacity.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Storage can provide real reliability and ancillary services; this packet does
  not claim that capacity has no value.
- A conventional line repair, vegetation program or transformer replacement may
  outperform a battery for a particular outage; resilience is portfolio- and
  place-dependent.
- A hub serving essential businesses may create indirect household benefits, but
  indirect benefit must not be reported as household continuity without evidence.
- Puerto Rico is a high-value place-based case, not a universal estimate of all
  storage programs.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: installed storage and resilience funding are
not the same as restored or continuous service. Keep the distributional and
household claims in Tier 2 until a named-place outage ledger links system
operation to beneficiary continuity and maintenance.

## Next test

Track one resilience hub, one critical facility and one residential battery
program across a defined storm or outage season. Record outage start/end,
islanding, dispatch, duration, users served, unmet demand, fuel/maintenance,
cost allocation, communication and whether water, cooling, health, food or
communications continuity was preserved.

# Rotation EY — Peacekeeping, local safety and durable peace

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction; Tier 2 peacekeeping-mandate-to-local-safety-and-durable-peace conversion question  
**Research lane:** peacekeeping / civilian protection / local safety / community engagement / mediation / women peace and security / transition / trust / post-mission durability

## Why this rotation

The atlas contains geopolitical capability, humanitarian response and local
peacebuilding themes. This packet isolates the conversion from an international
mandate to everyday safety: whether a mission’s presence, patrols, dialogue,
training, protection site, mine action or transition plan changes the exposure,
agency and durable security of named communities.

Mandate language, troop and police numbers, patrols, trainings, community
meetings, resolutions, protection sites, incident reports and mission budgets
are important signals. They do not by themselves establish reduced violence,
safe mobility, justice, livelihood recovery, trust, local ownership or peace
that persists after mission drawdown.

## Research question

When a peacekeeping operation is mandated to protect civilians, support a
political process or help transition security responsibilities, under what
conditions does it produce safe daily movement, reduced threats, inclusive
local conflict resolution, accountability and durable peace for civilians and
host communities—especially women, children, displaced people, minorities and
people with disabilities?

## Conversion chain

```text
mandate / mission / patrol / dialogue / training / protection site / transition
  -> threat monitoring, deterrence, mediation, referral, local-security capacity
  -> encounter with civilian, armed actor, authority, community or service
  -> safety, mobility, justice, return, livelihood, trust and local agency
  -> repeated non-violence, accountable institutions, reconciliation and peace
  -> mission legitimacy, local ownership, transition durability and geopolitical effect
```

The minimum unit is a named mission, locality, incident, patrol, community,
protection site, peace agreement, mediator, service and time period. A mandate
or deployment is an intermediate capability signal—not proof of community
safety or durable peace.

## Evidence snapshot

### 1. Civilian protection is a whole-mission responsibility

UN Peacekeeping describes protection of civilians as a responsibility spanning
civilian, police and military components. It emphasizes early warning, community
alert networks, community liaison assistants, public information and local
protection plans. This makes local information and community participation part
of operational effectiveness, not merely outreach.

Source: [UN Peacekeeping, Protection of Civilians mandate](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/protection-of-civilians-mandate).

### 2. Community capacity is an intended mechanism, not a closed outcome

In Tambura, UNMISS trained community members and organized forces in civilian
protection, reconciliation and peace stability. The case shows a concrete route
from mission activity to local capacity, while the atlas must still test whether
trained people changed warning, response, safety and repeated violence in their
communities.

Source: [UNMISS Tambura community protection training](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/news/tambura-community-members-trained-by-unmiss-to-protect-civilians-during-conflict).

### 3. Locally negotiated agreements carry the peace meaning

UNMISS reporting on Panyijiar describes a community-level agreement negotiated
by local actors and emphasizes that peace requires participation by political
and security actors, women, people with disabilities, older people, traditional
leaders, youth and civil society. A signed or facilitated agreement therefore
needs follow-up on implementation, violence, mobility, trust and inclusion.

Source: [UNMISS, Panyijiar community dialogue](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/news/panyijiar-communities-say-dialogue-and-forgiveness-key-sustained-peace).

### 4. Return requires a bundle of security and material conditions

The Hai Matar return initiative in South Sudan combined unexploded-ordnance
clearance, shelters, a police post, local police training and community
partnerships. This is a useful named case because return is not counted as
durable merely when families cross a boundary; safe land, housing, policing and
community relations all matter.

Source: [UNMISS, facilitating safe return in Hai Matar](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/news/united-nations-and-partners-facilitate-safe-return-home-families-displaced).

### 5. Local ownership is a strategic lesson for mission effectiveness

UN peacekeeping ministerial discussions identify locally tailored mandates and
local ownership as ways to strengthen effectiveness and durability. The
operational translation is to observe who decides, who can refuse, who receives
protection, who bears risk and whether local institutions retain capacity after
international support changes.

Source: [UN Peacekeeping Ministerial 2025](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/node/129448).

### 6. Women’s participation is connected to durable peace, not only representation

The UN Women, Peace and Security report describes peacekeepers engaging women
and girls, women’s organizations and networks in civilian protection, conflict-
related sexual-violence prevention, human rights and political processes. The
conversion test must include actual influence, safety and later institutional
change rather than only the number of women consulted or trained.

Source: [UN Peacekeeping Women, Peace and Security report 2025](https://peacekeeping.un.org/sites/default/files/25-00065_wps_report_2025_english.pdf).

## Bounded proposition 156

**A peacekeeping mandate, troop or police deployment, patrol, protection site,
community training, dialogue, local peace committee, mine-clearance activity,
return operation, peace agreement or transition plan is not by itself reduced
violence, safe daily mobility, justice, livelihood recovery, inclusive local
ownership, public trust or durable peace.** Mandate text, personnel numbers,
patrols, meetings, participants, incidents reported, hectares cleared,
returnees, agreements or handover milestones do not establish that named
civilians experienced sustained safety, could access remedy and services,
returned or moved freely, retained agency, or remained protected after the
mission or international partner reduced its presence.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

1. **Safety is local and temporal.** A mission can reduce risk on one road or
   during one patrol while insecurity persists at night, in farms, markets,
   schools or neighbouring communities.
2. **Peace is relational.** Trust depends on how mission staff, authorities,
   armed actors, traditional leaders, women, youth and displaced people treat
   one another, not only on the external force’s capacity.
3. **Local knowledge is operational.** Community alert networks and customary
   mediation may identify threats earlier, but can also exclude minorities,
   women or displaced groups unless representation is tested.
4. **Return is a material-social bundle.** Land clearance, housing, policing,
   documents, livelihoods, services, justice and neighbour relations jointly
   shape whether return is safe and durable.
5. **Transition changes power.** Handover can build state legitimacy when local
   institutions are capable and accountable, or expose civilians when capacity
   is nominal, politicized or abusive.
6. **Mission legitimacy is reversible.** Disinformation, misconduct, civilian
   harm, unfulfilled promises or perceived selectivity can reduce acceptance,
   access and freedom of movement even when formal mandate compliance continues.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- More patrols can coexist with recurring violence outside patrol windows or
  beyond the mission’s geographic reach.
- A community committee can improve early warning while excluding women,
  minorities, displaced people or people with disabilities.
- A peace agreement can reduce open fighting while increasing intimidation,
  land disputes, gender-based violence or forced displacement.
- A protection site can prevent immediate harm while creating prolonged
  dependency, poor services or restricted movement.
- A return can be safe at the moment of arrival while land disputes, unexploded
  ordnance, weak policing or lack of livelihood make it unsustainable.
- Training local security forces can improve capacity while transferring risk
  to civilians if oversight and accountability are weak.
- Mission drawdown can reflect mandate completion, political decision, funding
  pressure or failure; it is not itself evidence of durable peace.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote to **Tier 1 bounded distinction** for the proposition. UN Peacekeeping,
UN Women, Peace and Security, and named UNMISS cases support the separate
mandate, community, return, inclusion and transition mechanisms. Keep the
integrated peacekeeping-mandate-to-local-safety-and-durable-peace outcome at
**Tier 2** because public sources rarely follow the same community through
threat exposure, mission encounter, repeated safety, justice, trust and
post-mission durability.

## Source boundary

The packet closes the civilian-protection mandate, community-capacity, local-
agreement, return-bundle, local-ownership and women’s-participation mechanisms.
It does not close named violence reduction, safe mobility, justice, livelihood
recovery, trust, inclusion, accountability, return durability, post-mission
security or remedy. Those remain acquisition targets.

The fourth and sixth stages—material consequence and power—remain open across
most peacekeeping sources. Mandate delivery and community activity are
therefore kept separate from durable peace.

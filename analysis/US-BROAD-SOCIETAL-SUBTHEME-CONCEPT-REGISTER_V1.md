# US broad societal subtheme concept register v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-22  
**Purpose:** collect the recurring concepts emerging from the diverse source
families without turning them into one pooled theory or one population estimate

This is the interpretive layer between the 14-theme map and the individual
source records. Each concept names a pattern that appears in more than one
part of the atlas. The evidence is layered: different sources can point to a
shared structure, but they do not automatically describe the same people or a
single causal chain.

## Local delivery can increase reach while reducing visible control

**Plain meaning:** federal money can reach more places through states and local
partners, while the original funder sees less of what happens at the final
handoff.

GAO reviewed 20 large federally funded, state- or locally administered programs
representing about $1.1 trillion in FY2025 obligations. Funds can pass through
states to Tribes, local governments, subrecipients, contractors, and
subcontractors. Only 5 of the 20 selected programs had documented evidence
consistent with identifying and assessing fraud risks to prioritize action; 15
did not. GAO listed 22 open recommendations.

**Boundary:** this is a governance and visibility finding, not a fraud rate.
The programs were selected for size, and the obligation total is not household
receipt. Missing risk documentation and open recommendations do not establish
that fraud occurred or that a recipient lost a service.

**Next test:** follow one named program and recipient or award through federal
funding, state allocation, local or contractor delivery, anomaly, complaint,
correction, recovery, and later service use. Record which institution can see
and repair each stage.

## A program can meet its latest target after the promise changes

**Plain meaning:** a security or infrastructure program may be “on track”
against its current plan even though cost, schedule, staffing, and the original
target have moved.

GAO reviewed 27 major DHS acquisitions. Nineteen had approved baselines; 15
revised them, projected costs rose $11.4 billion (26%) from the initial
baselines, and 18 of the 19 met their latest goals. Eight programs lost at least
20% of staff, and DHS reorganized department-level acquisition oversight.

**Boundary:** revising a baseline can be responsible management when new
information or requirements emerge. The report does not prove failure, waste,
unsafe systems, or strategic weakness, and it does not measure one common
operational outcome.

**Next test:** retain the original and current baselines for one named program,
then follow staffing, testing, fielding, use, sustainment, cost, and external
response.

## Older-worker participation is not the same as a usable work bridge

**Plain meaning:** a public job program can serve many older adults while the
training, employer contact, and lasting job route remain harder to use.

GAO found that people 55 and older were about 22% of participants in six
selected federal workforce programs in program year 2023. Their second-quarter
post-exit employment was about 60% in selected DOL programs, compared with 69%
for ages 40–54 and 73% for ages 16–39. Older participants used training less
often, and 41 interviewed workers described both digital-literacy benefits and
application, skills, openings, and possible age-discrimination problems.

**Boundary:** the programs and interviews were selected, and the comparisons do
not prove an age cause. Lower training use may reflect care, money, health,
disability, immediate work needs, or retirement plans. The report does not
measure job quality, wages, retention, or household security.

**Next test:** follow one older worker from outreach through training choice,
employer contact, job quality, retention, care, health, and retirement timing.

## Time to search has different value under different employer power

**Plain meaning:** unemployment insurance can give workers more time, but that
time may improve the eventual job match less when only a few employers dominate
the local market.

A Census CES study of Great Recession UI extensions finds about one additional
week of nonemployment. The overall earnings increase was economically
meaningful but not statistically significant; the earnings effect was weaker
in more concentrated labor markets, while the duration effect did not differ.

The [job-security, mobility, and incomplete-insurance record](records/us-nber-job-security-mobility-incomplete-markets-2026.json)
adds a separate risk-valuation mechanism. In a representative survey of
employed workers, respondents demanded a 1.63% pay raise for each additional
percentage point of annual unemployment risk in a new job. The paper's
search-model counterfactual estimates 12% more job mobility and 0.19% higher
productivity under complete markets; its unemployment-insurance mechanism
models a 1.3% productivity gain while also increasing job loss and unemployment.
The survey valuation is not an observed job switch, and the model outcomes
depend on its calibration rather than documenting a realized UI reform.

**Boundary:** this is a historical working-paper result. The public abstract
does not report the analytic sample size, and the study does not measure job
quality, retention, household security, or current UI policy effects. The
additional survey/model record does not resolve those gaps either.

**Next test:** follow UI receipt through search, employer contact, offer, wage,
hours, benefits, retention, household bills, and later security across local
markets with different concentration.

## AI adoption, worker use, and job change are different stages

**Plain meaning:** a company can adopt AI without workers using it, or workers
can use it before the company formally adopts it; neither number alone tells us
who gained control or who lost work.

The Census CES AI supplement reports AI use in 18% of firms, or 32% when
employment-weighted. Worker task use appears in 23% of firms, or 41%
employment-weighted. Most users report augmentation, while employment
decreases appear in 2% of firms. Broader functional deployment and operational
investment are associated with employment decreases; worker-task integration
alone is not significantly linked to headcount reduction after other factors.

**Boundary:** this is an early firm-reported working-paper snapshot. It does
not prove AI caused job changes, measure worker control or pay, or represent a
typical small firm when employment-weighted figures are used.

**Next test:** follow the same firm and workers through AI decision, function,
task, training, monitoring, pay, hours, headcount, voice, and exit.

## Information abundance does not create a trusted health route

**Plain meaning:** people can draw on clinicians, peers, websites, social media,
and AI while still finding it hard to decide which conflicting health claim to
trust.

Pew’s 2025 survey finds that 85% of adults get health information from
providers at least sometimes, 66% from people with similar health issues, 36%
from social media, and 22% from AI chatbots. Seventy-six percent see conflicting
health information at least sometimes, and 54% have at least some difficulty
knowing what to trust when claims conflict.

**Boundary:** these are self-reported source use and perceptions, not clinical
fact checks or health outcomes. Provider trust does not prove access,
affordability, or continuity; platform use does not prove belief or harm.

**Next test:** follow one protected health question through source exposure,
verification, clinician contact, treatment, cost, adherence, and outcome.

## Relatability can create health authority before accuracy is settled

**Plain meaning:** an online health voice can influence people through a mix of
credentials, coaching, business identity, parenthood, or lived experience—not
only through verified medical expertise.

Pew analyzed 6,828 prominent influencers and 12,800 accounts. Forty-one percent
described themselves as health professionals, 31% as coaches, and 28% as
entrepreneurs. Forty percent of U.S. adults said they ever get health and
wellness information from influencers or podcasts. Among those users, 10%
trusted all or most of it, 65% trusted some, and 24% trusted little or none;
54% said it helped them understand health better.

**Boundary:** bios do not verify credentials, accuracy, or sponsorship, and
surveyed trust or understanding does not show a medical action or outcome. The
sample focuses on large accounts and combines social media with podcasts.

**Next test:** follow one claim through platform exposure, identity,
sponsorship, verification, clinician contact, care, spending, adherence, and
outcome.

## Party identity can organize belonging without proving political action

**Plain meaning:** a party label or lean can provide a political home while
people inside the same coalition have different social positions, resources,
experiences, and reasons for belonging.

Pew's 2026 NPORS found 47% of adults Democratic or Democratic-leaning and 43%
Republican or Republican-leaning, while 44% called themselves independent or
something else before leaners were assigned. Men leaned Republican, women
Democratic, Black and Hispanic adults Democratic, and college graduates
Democratic while adults without a college degree were nearly even. These are
coalition boundaries, not a single social experience.

**Boundary:** repeated cross-sections do not show the same people switching;
affiliation is not a vote, policy position, turnout, or durable realignment.
Subgroup differences do not identify their cause.

**Next test:** join repeated identity to material change, messenger and
institutional trust, issue judgment, turnout or organizing, and persistence.

## Stable oversight counts can hide a changed coverage rule

**Plain meaning:** a safety regulator can keep the number of visible checks
steady even while the system it oversees grows, if it lengthens the time
between checks for selected cases.

GAO reports that FAA flight simulators increased by more than 500% from 1990
to 2025 while National Simulator Program staffing stayed roughly steady.
Evaluation activity remained fairly steady from 2019 through 2024, while an
extended interval allowed some simulator evaluations to move from 12 months to
as much as 36 months. Six of 10 selected sponsors raised communication
concerns, two cited possible safety concerns, and FAA had not resolved all
mission-critical skills gaps.

**Boundary:** a longer interval can be a reasonable risk-based strategy. The
selected sponsor sample is not generalizable, and the report does not measure
simulator failure, pilot performance, accidents, or the causal effect of the
interval policy.

**Next test:** follow each simulator through technology changes, eligibility,
interval decision, sponsor feedback, staff review, deficiency, correction,
training performance, and later safety signals.

**Plain meaning:** changing a survey interface can look like a correction and
still fail to improve the measured result against an administrative benchmark.

The Census SEHSD Medicaid content test found that redesigned and control ACS
questions performed equally well against CMS enrollment records. This adds a
health-measurement version of administrative legibility: question wording,
reported coverage, administrative enrollment, active care, and continuity are
separate stages.

**Boundary:** equal performance in one content test does not prove equal accuracy
for every subgroup or year, and enrollment is not care, affordability, or health.

**Next test:** join question version, response, enrollment, churn, managed care,
care use, claims, cost, correction, and continuity for the same respondent.

## Standardized questions can lose complicated household reality

**Plain meaning:** a public survey can be carefully designed and still miss
parts of people's lives when a household situation does not fit the available
time period, category, or response choice.

The Census/RTI ASEC cognitive-interview report found uncertainty about another
household member's income, unclear reference periods for moving, daunting lists
of moving reasons, and instructions that helped in one place but confused people
when repeated. This is qualitative testing, not a national rate of confusion or
a measured bias in published statistics.

**Boundary:** the interview findings show possible measurement failure points;
they do not show how common each problem is in the fielded survey, whether a
specific estimate is biased, or whether a respondent later received or lost a
benefit.

**Next test:** join question version, household context, item nonresponse,
correction, administrative-record agreement, eligibility, service receipt, and
later trust or participation while keeping respondent privacy protected.

## Participation counts can hide incomplete resource delivery

**Plain meaning:** a program can reach most eligible people while delivering a
smaller share of the money those people were eligible to receive.

The [Census/IRS Child Tax Credit delivery record](records/us-census-irs-child-tax-credit-participation-dollar-gap-2021.json)
estimates 91% taxpayer participation but 85% dollar participation, with about
3.1 million eligible nonparticipants and roughly $26 billion unclaimed or
under-claimed. Eighty-two percent of eligible nonparticipants were in tax units
with adjusted gross income below $75,000, and 53% filed a return. This adds a
benefit-conversion version of the atlas's support-versus-security tension:
filing and participation do not necessarily mean every modeled dollar reached
the family.

**Boundary:** the rates are conditional on modeled eligibility and a linked
survey/administrative method. The 2021 credit was temporarily expanded, and the
study does not identify the reason for each nonpayment or show household
adequacy, timing, spending, or later security.

**Next test:** follow one protected tax unit through eligibility, filing,
claiming, advance payment, reconciliation, correction, receipt timing, use of
the money, care/debt/housing effects, and later security.

## Formal family support does not create the same usable time option

**Plain meaning:** a leave policy can exist while parents still have unequal
ability to stop working after a birth.

The [SIPP first-time-parent leave record](records/us-census-sipp-parental-leave-race-sex-first-time-parents-2026.json)
finds that only about half of first-time parents in the 2014-2023 cohort used
paid leave, with lower paid-leave rates for Black and Hispanic parents than for
Asian parents in several sex-specific comparisons. Across pooled years,
no-leave rates were especially high among Black and Hispanic fathers, and Black
and Hispanic mothers returned to work sooner than Asian mothers. This adds a
work-family version of unequal optionality.

**Boundary:** the study is descriptive and restricted to first-time parents who
worked before and after birth. It does not separate race/ethnicity from wage,
occupation, education, employer policy, job protection, household resources,
partner support, or health, and it does not establish later family outcomes.

The [Pew working-parent work-family record](records/us-pew-working-parent-work-family-boundary-2026.json)
adds an arrangement-and-meaning layer. In CPS data for different-sex couples
with children under 18, both parents worked full time in 52% of couples in
2025, up from 31% in 1975, while the father-full-time/mother-not-employed
arrangement fell to 23% from 42%. In Pew's 2,242-working-parent survey, 83% in
the two-full-time arrangement reported a positive financial impact; perceptions
of child wellbeing and financial effects differed sharply for the
father-full-time/mother-not-employed arrangement, whose child-wellbeing report
reflects employed parents' views, especially fathers. These are cross-sectional
descriptions and reported interpretations, not proof that an arrangement caused
financial security, care adequacy, child wellbeing, career outcomes, or gender
equality.

**Next test:** follow the same parent-child episode through employer policy,
leave request and approval, pay, care substitute, return timing, employment
continuity, health, child wellbeing, household room, and later security.

## Firm culture can become a labor-market allocation mechanism

**Plain meaning:** new firms can create jobs while still giving more opportunity
to workers socially similar to the founder.

The Census LEHD study finds large founder-worker background similarity after
industry, metro, and founding-cohort controls, with patterns persisting as firms
age. Its wage decomposition favors a labor-demand explanation, suggesting that
the firm—not only worker choice—helps shape who receives opportunity and
relative pay.

**Boundary:** the primary data cover 30 states and D.C. and use a founder proxy;
the balanced sample selects firms observed for several years. The finding does
not prove discrimination, a single cultural mechanism, a national rate, or
worker harm.

**Next test:** follow founder access, recruiting channel, applicant pool, hiring,
pay, promotion, retention, worker voice, firm survival, and later household or
community outcomes in the same protected panel.

## Technology can weaken the first career rung without ending work

**Plain meaning:** a person can remain connected to employment while a new
technology changes the first job into a lower-paid or less suitable entry point.

The Census graduate study finds that the most AI-exposed majors had lower
initial employment and earnings after ChatGPT's release, with about half of the
earnings decline coming from movement into lower-paying industries. Many
graduates later adjusted, but the most exposed group still had weaker outcomes
around two years later.

**Boundary:** the study uses major-level exposure and a selected PSEO/LEHD
sample; it does not observe each employer's adoption, each graduate's AI use,
informal work, job quality, or long-run career. The timing is consistent with
an AI-related shock but does not prove one mechanism.

**Next test:** follow major, institution, application, offer, employer AI use,
industry, pay, training, job switching, debt, housing, and later career in the
same protected graduate panel.

## A temporary public job can change later work attachment

**Plain meaning:** a short government job can have a long effect for people
near the edge of employment, while doing much less for applicants with better
outside options.

The [temporary public-hiring and long-run attachment record](records/us-census-temporary-public-hiring-long-run-employment-2010-2023.json)
uses a fuzzy discontinuity around the Census test score of 70. Among applicants
near that threshold, receiving a 2010 Census job increased W-2 employment by
61 percentage points in 2010 and by 28 points in 2023; conditional wages did
not differ significantly, but the higher probability of work translated into
nearly $6,000 more annual W-2 wages. The long-run estimate was concentrated
among men. Across the broader score distribution, the roughly 22-point 2010
employment effect dissipated by 2011, and low-score hires were more than twice
as likely to be terminated for misconduct or unsatisfactory performance. The
mechanism may include pay, training, a résumé signal, a background check, or
public trust-sensitive work.

**Boundary:** this is a local estimate for a specific recession-era Census job,
test, and applicant group. W-2 employment is not job quality or household
security, and the study does not prove a universal job-guarantee effect.

**Next test:** follow application, threshold, offer, training, hours,
termination, next employer, pay, job quality, household room, and trust across
public-hiring designs and labor-market conditions.

## Credit terms can become environmental governance

**Plain meaning:** a lender's exposure to cleanup costs can change what a firm
does with pollution and abatement, even when the lender does not formally run
the firm.

The Census study finds that narrowing lender liability after 1996 increased
pollution and reduced abatement investment in affected establishments, while
loan maturity became longer. The result links legal responsibility, credit
contracts, firm behavior, and public environmental risk.

**Boundary:** the evidence concerns a historical CERCLA and secured-lending
setting. It measures establishments, loans, pollution, and production—not
resident exposure, health, cleanup, or current sustainable finance.

**Next test:** follow collateral, loan price and maturity, lender monitoring,
abatement, release, cleanup, worker and community exposure, health, complaints,
and regulator or lender response.

## Aggregate capacity can grow while local protection becomes harder to reach

**Plain meaning:** bigger hospitals and more hospital workers do not necessarily
mean that every place has easier access to care.

The Census hospital panel finds fewer, larger hospitals and more counties with
no study-defined hospital, especially in the rural South. This adds a place and
infrastructure version of the atlas's capacity-versus-usability tension.

**Boundary:** the establishment definition does not measure patient travel,
appointments, quality, insurance, emergency transport, outpatient care, or
telehealth usability. A county without a hospital may still use care nearby.

**Next test:** follow one county and patient episode through closure or opening,
travel, transport, insurance, appointment, emergency use, telehealth, cost,
quality, work time, and health outcome.

## Disaster spending can restructure local economies

**Plain meaning:** recovery money can change which firms survive and which new
firms enter, not just repair the firms that existed before the disaster.

The Census flood study finds productivity-selective exits, rebuilding by
survivors, more productive entrants, and increased bank lending after federally
declared floods. This adds a public-finance and firm-capacity version of the
atlas's recovery-versus-durability tension.

**Boundary:** the cleanest evidence is for manufacturing plants and selected
inland flood events. Productivity and county income are not worker continuity,
household security, health, housing, or political recovery; the fiscal-return
calculation is model-based.

**Next test:** follow one disaster episode through damage, aid application,
credit, closure/rebuilding, worker continuity, capital transfer, household
income, housing, health, migration, and local political response.

## Formal neutrality is not equal political voice

**Plain meaning:** a rule can look neutral while geography and institutional
design still give groups unequal chances of representation.

The NBER local-redistricting study reconstructs 3,712 city council and county
commission maps across 18,521 districts. Enacted maps overrepresent White voters
and underrepresent Black and Hispanic voters; race-blind simulations reproduce
much of the gap. Maps improve more where Voting Rights Act oversight or
districting litigation applies.

**Boundary:** geography-based disparity is not proof that every map was
intentionally discriminatory, and officeholder composition is not policy,
constituent service, turnout, trust, or material security.

**Next test:** join map changes, population and voter geography, candidate entry,
officeholder race, turnout, contestedness, policy votes, constituent service,
litigation, and later trust or participation.

## Safe exit is not the same as equal recovery

**Plain meaning:** a program can move people away from a physical danger while
the payment, destination, and later security remain unequal.

The NBER federal flood-buyout study reports lower compensation for households of
color and different destination-neighborhood outcomes for White and Black
participants. This adds a climate-adaptation version of unequal optionality:
leaving danger, receiving a fair payment, reaching a good neighborhood, and
recovering durable security are separate stages.

**Boundary:** the public abstract does not establish every reason for the
compensation or destination differences, and neighborhood quality is not the
same as health, income, safety, belonging, or political trust.

**Next test:** join appraisal, offer, negotiation, payment, debt, insurance,
moving cost, destination, household composition, services, health, employment,
return, trust, and security for the same buyout episode.

## A benefit works through the household's net resources

**Plain meaning:** receiving a benefit is not the same as having more usable
resources if another household income source falls at the same time.

The [child-SSI, parental-work, and adult-outcomes record](records/us-nber-child-ssi-parental-work-adult-outcomes-2026.json)
reports positive effects when parents did not reduce work and household income
increased, but zero or negative effects when parents offset the benefit by
reducing earnings. It adds a family-work layer to public benefit conversion:
money, parent time, care, work, and child opportunity can move in different
directions.

**Boundary:** reduced parental work may reflect caregiving, health, or schedule
needs rather than a simple disincentive; the study's channel decomposition and
adult outcomes do not capture every part of family wellbeing.

**Next test:** join eligibility, benefit amount, parental work and time, care
needs, health, school, household income, expenses, stress, services, adult
earnings, disability, and later security for the same family.

## The form and timing of help shape what becomes visible

**Plain meaning:** equal-value help can produce different first results because
cash, food benefits, one-time payments, and recurring payments leave different
choices and arrive on different calendars.

The NBER transfer-design study finds higher short-run food-store spending from
in-kind than cash transfers, and from recurring than one-time transfers. Its
food-store responses were 0.18 and 0.38 for one-time and recurring in-kind aid,
versus 0.06 and 0.20 for one-time and recurring cash.

**Boundary:** food-store spending is not food security, health, rent paid, debt
reduced, or durable household room. Lower food spending after cash may mean the
family paid medicine, rent, transport, or another urgent bill. Scanner data also
do not reveal every access barrier or the household's reasoning.

**Next test:** follow comparable families through payment date, food, rent,
medicine, transport, debt, savings, store access, fees, interruptions, food
security, health, stress, trust, and later policy support.

## A financial signal can help create the risk it predicts

**Plain meaning:** a score can begin as a summary of past trouble, then change
the terms a person receives, making later trouble more likely.

The NBER credit-score study finds that counting one extra inquiry lowered scores
by about five points without changing the rest of the credit report. Default did
not change for clean-record consumers, but rose 3.2 percentage points over two
years for consumers with prior derogatory marks; at least 13% of that relationship
was self-fulfilling.

**Boundary:** the public summary does not show which lender changed the rate,
limit, approval, or timing, and it does not follow housing, insurance, work,
utilities, recovery, or later applications. This is not a universal effect of
every score change.

**Next test:** join score movement, approval, rate, limit, lender, payment,
default, correction, housing, insurance, work, utilities, and score recovery;
separate changed terms from borrower self-withdrawal.

## A protective rule can change access without changing the average measure

**Plain meaning:** licensing may screen some people out or change who enters,
while the ratings and driving measures customers usually see remain similar.

The NBER Uber study finds no consistent improvement in measured consumer
outcomes across a New York City/New Jersey comparison and a Houston
deregulation event. Houston entrants after deregulation were indistinguishable
from previously licensed drivers on ratings and driving behavior, despite being
different in age and experience.

**Boundary:** the study does not measure fares, waits, cancellations, entry
cost, worker income, disability access, or rare serious safety incidents. A null
rating result is not proof that licensing has no safety value, and a driver-pool
change is not itself proof of worse service.

**Next test:** join each license, check, permit, insurance, inspection, or
training step to driver entry and exit, fares, waits, cancellations, routes,
disability access, serious incidents, worker income, enforcement, and transit
alternatives by neighborhood and hour.

## Shared work can create value while charging the household for connection

**Plain meaning:** one planned office day can improve communication and output,
but the worker may pay for that connection in travel, care, rest, money, or
lost control of the day.

The [NBER coordinated-office-day record](records/us-nber-one-office-day-remote-team-productivity-retention-2026.json)
assigned 248 customer-service employees to remain remote or work together at
the office one day each month. The office-day group handled 7.8% more calls per
hour, communicated 36 extra minutes with colleagues the following week, and
had about one-third lower attrition.

**Boundary:** the study is firm- and job-specific. It does not measure commute,
care, disability, household stress, pay, promotion, long-run career outcomes,
or whether an online meeting could create the same contact. More communication
does not prove better worker welfare.

**Next test:** join attendance to travel, care, cost, communication, output,
errors, pay, promotion, retention, household stress, and a designed online-
contact comparison across workers with different alternatives.

## Fiscal news can arrive before the bill

**Plain meaning:** people may change what they expect or spend after hearing
about future public debt, before any tax, benefit, price, or interest payment
actually changes.

An NBER experiment with approximately 11,000 Korean individuals linked random
fiscal-news treatments to high-frequency spending. Information that raised
expected future debt raised expected inflation and consumer spending; information
that raised expected fiscal balance raised expected growth but did not
significantly change spending.

**Boundary:** this is Korean comparison evidence, not a U.S. household estimate.
It does not follow a completed purchase, tax, benefit, rate, trust judgment, or
political response through later policy realization. Spending may be brought
forward or changed in composition rather than improved welfare.

**Next test:** join message source, trust, belief, liquidity, savings, debt,
spending, rates, taxes, benefits, policy reversal, final bill, and political
response in a U.S. panel, alongside the existing U.S. fiscal-news experiment.

## Removing a fee is not the same as removing the shortage

**Plain meaning:** a bank can stop one penalty while a cash-poor household still
has to find the original bill money or pay for another bridge.

The NBER transaction study finds that eliminating NSF fees immediately reduced
those charges across incomes. Relaxing overdraft rules reduced overdraft fees
mainly for wealthier and more liquid households; only those households later
showed lower late fees, interest, account-maintenance fees, and payday-loan use.

**Boundary:** account records do not show every original bill, merchant response,
need protected or delayed, family support, affordable-credit option, or complete
household welfare. A successful payment can preserve service while consuming
future room, and a lower fee does not prove that the shortage ended.

**Next test:** join the original bill, transaction order, paycheck, payment
result, merchant response, credit, food, care, account status, and 90-day
recovery by liquidity and access to alternatives.

## Financial fallback is distributed, not simply available

**Plain meaning:** a payment system, fee rule, or account can appear to offer
the same protection to everyone while cash, liquidity, credit, and physical
access determine who can complete the next transaction.

The bank-fee study supplies a cash-room version: removing a targeted fee is
immediate, but later reductions in related charges and alternative-credit use
appear only for more liquid customers. The payment-resilience study supplies an
outage version: digital payments can become unavailable while cash access is
associated with more completed transactions. These are different source
settings and cannot be pooled into one consumer-resilience rate. Together they
support a narrower pattern: formal payment access becomes practical continuity
only when a household has a usable fallback.

The [Global Findex financial-room record](records/us-world-bank-global-findex-financial-room-2011-2024.json)
sets a broader access boundary. U.S. adult account ownership rose from 88.0% in
2011 to 97.0% in 2024, while the reported male--female ownership gap moved from
7.9 percentage points to -0.2 points. That is an access and distribution
measure, not evidence that most adults hold liquid savings, can obtain
affordable credit, complete a payment during disruption, recover fraud losses,
or refuse a provider. Its 2014--21 measures of digital-payment use, saving,
formal borrowing, and ability to raise emergency funds are distinct repeated
cross-sections with indicator-specific wording; missing 2024 U.S. values are
not zeroes or evidence of stability.

The [payment-rewards redistribution record](records/us-nber-payment-rewards-redistribution-2026.json)
adds a merchant-price incidence layer. Using merchant settlement and transaction
data plus a sufficient-statistics model, the working paper estimates a $30
billion annual transfer from cash/debit users to credit-card users; even after
allowing for merchant sorting and fee heterogeneity, its estimated transfer
falls only 25%, and $9.2 billion is estimated to flow toward households earning
over $150,000. Its model also puts the effective sales-tax equivalent for cash
users 26% above that for premium-card users. These are modeled distributional
incidence estimates—not line items on individual receipts, measured household
welfare losses, or proof that every reward user gains at someone else's expense.
The results depend on pass-through and matching assumptions and merchant data
that cover only part of the card market.

**Boundary:** a completed payment is not proof that the purchase was essential,
adequate, affordable, or recovery-producing. The sources do not identify every
bill, merchant result, food/medicine purchase, debt repayment, family transfer,
or later wellbeing outcome.

**Next test:** retain a dated disruption, attempted payment method, cash and
credit availability, transaction result, goods or service obtained, repayment,
and later household room for the same unit.

## Staying with a bank can protect continuity and weaken competition

**Plain meaning:** people may stay with the same bank because moving bills and
paychecks is risky or useful, but that same staying can make the bank less
responsive to customers.

NBER's study of more than 900 banks finds that only about 5–15% of depositors
open a new account each year. Its model estimates that depositor “sleepiness”
accounts for 57% of average deposit franchise value and softens competition,
especially for banks with lower service quality; it also finds that sleepiness
can improve stability and reduce modeled default probabilities during the 2023
banking turmoil.

**Boundary:** the study does not show why any particular customer stayed, what
rate or service they gave up, how much switching cost them, or whether they
needed a branch, digital access, fraud help, or payment continuity. The modeled
stability result does not prove that customer inertia is good for household
welfare.

**Next test:** join rates, balances, fees, direct deposit, recurring bills,
switching attempts, failed payments, fraud holds, service use, branch access,
interest gained, and later household outcomes. Test whether safe assisted
switching creates bargaining power without increasing dangerous payment or bank
funding shocks.

## Health coverage can hold a family job in place

**Plain meaning:** a parent may stay in a job because leaving could put a
child's health coverage at risk; that can be a sensible protection and still
limit the parent's work options.

NBER uses employer-insurance claims and an Affordable Care Act birth-month
eligibility discontinuity. Extending dependent coverage increased dependent
take-up and parental job retention. The effect was strongest among parents near
a job exit, families that valued dependent coverage more, and employees of firms
offering more insurance options.

The [ACA underwriting-ban and heterogeneous-access record](records/us-nber-aca-underwriting-heterogeneous-effects-2026.json)
adds the earlier application gate. Pre-ACA applicants could face underwriting
and rejection for pre-existing conditions; using proprietary application and
underwriting records, the paper estimates that the ACA's ban on that practice
and its premium subsidies benefited low-income, high-risk, and over-50
applicants most. A coverage option can thus change practical room before a
family changes jobs or receives care. The study does not establish enrollment
persistence, premium payment, network access, treatment continuity, health,
job switching, or financial recovery.

**Boundary:** the dependent-coverage study does not show the full value of the
retained job, the family's plan terms, network, deductible, treatment
continuity, wages, training, job quality, or later household welfare. The
underwriting study does not establish those downstream outcomes either.
Retention does not prove satisfaction, coercion, or that portable coverage
would change the decision.

**Next test:** follow families through plan changes and job offers, recording
dependents, doctors, prescriptions, premiums, deductibles, networks, jobs
sought or refused, wages, hours, training, care delays, debt, and stress. Test
whether an affordable portable bridge changes mobility while preserving care.

## A weatherization promise is not yet a warmer or safer home

**Plain meaning:** an energy upgrade can be completed while the household gets
less bill savings than promised; the real result may appear in comfort, safety,
health, or cash room—or may not appear at all.

The [NBER weatherization savings and indoor-temperature record](records/us-nber-weatherization-actual-savings-indoor-temperature-2015.json)
reports an experimental evaluation of more than 30,000 Weatherization
Assistance Program households. Model-projected savings were roughly 2.5 times
actual savings; upfront costs were about twice actual savings, the study found
no significant evidence of higher indoor temperatures, and the estimated average
annual return was about –9.5% even after broader societal benefits.

**Boundary:** the study does not show which measures caused the gap or join each
home's bill, temperature, air quality, equipment safety, health, work, care,
shutoff, rent, and housing outcome. A lower-than-projected saving does not prove
that a particular home became unsafe, and a lower bill does not prove improved
health or comfort.

**Next test:** follow renters, owners, manufactured homes, and rural homes for
two heating and cooling seasons, recording work completed, actual energy use,
bill, temperature, humidity, air quality, carbon monoxide, equipment uptime,
health events, work and school absence, food and medicine tradeoffs, repairs,
and moves.

## Competition protects customers only when customers can use the exit

**Plain meaning:** a market can have many sellers while a particular customer
still has little power if switching costs, distance, price, or identity checks
make the alternatives unusable.

NBER's historical U.S. study examines three industries using exogenous changes
in the Black-to-White consumer ratio and more than 25,000 establishment prices.
It finds that increases in nondiscrimination were concentrated in the least
competitive markets, while nondiscriminatory firms charged higher prices than
discriminatory firms in the same local markets.

**Boundary:** this is historical evidence from the decades before the 1964
Civil Rights Act. It does not measure present-day firms, online markets,
service quality, current discrimination, customer exit costs, complaints,
enforcement, or household welfare. A higher price does not by itself explain
quality, safety, location, or the full cost of treatment.

**Next test:** follow current customers across comparable firms, recording
search, travel, time, identity checks, price, service, denial, complaint,
switching, remedy, and repeat treatment by income, race, age, disability,
language, and place. Measure who could actually leave before calling a market
competitive.

## Emergency credit can prevent collapse without making debt free

**Plain meaning:** after a disaster, fast money can preserve a car or another
needed asset and stop bankruptcy, while still creating a payment that the family
must carry later.

NBER links Federal Disaster Loan applications for households with uninsured
damage to credit records and uses an approval discontinuity. Among the studied
applicants, credit reduced bankruptcy likelihood by 61% in the following three
years and increased car purchases even three years after the loan. Additional
evidence supports liquidity as the mechanism.

**Boundary:** the study does not show the full repair, insurance, aid, family
support, work, health, care, housing, consumption, or repayment path. It does
not compare loans with grants or direct repair, or establish results for people
who could not apply. Lower bankruptcy is meaningful protection, not proof that
every borrower was better off.

**Next test:** follow approved borrowers, similar denied applicants, and people
unable to complete the application through five years. Record loan terms,
insurance, aid, repairs, mobility, work, health, care, delinquency, credit,
bankruptcy, housing, missed essentials, and stress. Compare rapid loans,
partial grants, deferred payments, and loan-plus-repair support.

## A safe yearly income can hide an unsafe month

**Plain meaning:** liquid wealth determines whether an ordinary pay change is a
temporary inconvenience or a forced cut to spending, care, housing, or future
choices.

NBER uses administrative banking data and firm-wide changes in labor income to
study typical month-to-month shocks. Average consumption elasticity was about
0.21; it was much higher for low-liquidity households and close to zero for
high-liquidity households. Black and Hispanic households were about twice as
sensitive as White households, with racial wealth inequality explaining nearly
all of that difference statistically.

**Boundary:** the study does not show which bill or need each household
protected or delayed, whether it borrowed or used informal support, or what
happened to health, care, housing, work, and later recovery. A spending change
can be a deliberate purchase, debt substitution, or forced sacrifice. The
modeled welfare cost is not a direct household wellbeing measure.

The [Census SIPP household-wealth record](records/us-census-sipp-wealth-households-2024.json)
adds the stock context beneath those monthly flows. Median 2024 household net
wealth was $204,900, but the 10th percentile was $0 and the 90th was $1.81
million. Assets at financial institutions were common (95.7%, with a $10,100
conditional median), as were vehicles (82.5%, $12,900); home equity (62.5%,
$220,000) and retirement accounts (60.5%, $89,150) were less universal and
often less immediately usable. Meanwhile 53.5% of households held unsecured
debt, including 40.0% with credit-card or store bills and 16.0% with medical
debt. These are population stocks and conditional medians, not a measured
shock buffer, available credit line, sale/withdrawal decision, or later
recovery. Ownership is not the same as liquidity or resilience.

The [trust and retirement-financial-behavior record](records/us-nber-trust-financial-behavior-retirement-2026.json)
adds an institutional-meaning layer to those stocks. In an observational Health
and Retirement Study analysis, trust in financial institutions was positively
associated with retirement-account ownership, stockholding, and net wealth,
alongside financial literacy; trust in government programs did not show the
same positive alignment and was negatively related to selected wealth behaviors.
The positive financial-institution relationship was reported for White older
respondents but not in the same form for Black or Hispanic respondents, while
government-program trust among minority households was linked to lower
stockholding and wealth accumulation. These are domain- and group-specific
associations, not proof that trust caused saving, access, wealth, adequacy, or
retirement security; prior resources and institutional experience can shape
both trust and behavior.

**Next test:** follow households with exact pay dates, bill deadlines, liquid
balances, credit offers, informal transfers, care duties, health needs, and
spending purpose. Record the first threatened need, the solution used, its
price, and whether the next month started weaker.

## Temporary work can create a second credit barrier

**Plain meaning:** unstable work can make a household need credit more, while
the same work status makes lenders less willing to provide it.

The [temporary-work and dual-credit-market record](records/us-nber-temporary-work-dual-credit-market-2024.json)
finds that temporary workers face significantly greater income risk than
permanent workers, even in the same occupation and at similar wages. Lenders
ration credit to temporary workers precisely when young adults may use
mortgages and loans to establish housing, smooth consumption, and buy durable
goods. The study describes this as a dual credit market alongside the dual
labor market.

**Boundary:** the evidence does not show each worker's contract, credit score,
application, denial reason, household support, housing need, family plan, or
later outcome. Temporary work may provide flexibility or entry, and credit
rationing may reflect real repayment risk; the result is not by itself proof of
discrimination or that permanent work is preferred.

**Next test:** follow workers through job changes and credit applications,
recording contract, pay volatility, hours, credit score, lender, approval,
terms, denial reason, housing, debt, family support, and later financial room.
Compare similar workers with different work security and actual ability to
choose a stable alternative.

## Career progress is partly a time measure, not just a wage measure

**Plain meaning:** two people can hold jobs with similar pay while taking very
different amounts of time to reach the same level; that timing can reveal a
career path without proving that faster advancement is a better life.

The [career-seniority and non-wage-attainment record](records/us-nber-career-seniority-nonwage-attainment-2025.json)
builds a seniority measure from more than 150 million resumes: the years the
median worker in the same industry and firm-size category takes to reach a
person's job title. Seniority is positively correlated with wages and
education, and higher seniority among public-sector workers predicts
transitions to higher-paying private-sector positions.

**Boundary:** the source does not measure job quality, control, hours, benefits,
health, care, debt, housing, discrimination, promotion cause, or satisfaction.
Time to a title is a constructed measure, and its relationship to later wages
does not prove that seniority causes mobility or that a slower path is failure.

**Next test:** follow workers through title, pay, hours, control, benefits,
care, debt, housing, training, moves, and stated reasons for staying or leaving.
Compare title progression with the actual household room and non-wage value of
the job.

## Home equity can fund a firm while exposing the household to housing risk

**Plain meaning:** rising home values can help some people start businesses, but
the resulting entry may be short-lived, concentrated in real estate, or tied to
debt secured by the home.

NBER's Census-based study finds that house-price growth was strongly associated
with local entrepreneurship from 1992–2007. Most associated entry was in
construction and real estate and tended to be small and short-lived. Using a
1998 Texas home-equity-lending reform, the authors find that the collateral
channel produced longer-lived and more sector-balanced entry, but represented a
tenth or less of the total entry associated with house-price growth.

**Boundary:** the study does not show customer benefit, job quality, local
service, owner residence, durable firm survival, household welfare, or whether
home-equity borrowing later endangered housing. Entry is not the same as a
lasting employer or community capacity.

**Next test:** follow homeowners and firms through borrowing, entry, sector,
survival, jobs, wages, service, profits, home payments, default, and closure.
Compare collateral-funded firms with other entrants and record what happened to
the household when the business or housing market weakened.

## Search can improve a price while charging the consumer in time and attention

**Plain meaning:** online choice is not free just because the alternatives are
visible; consumers may need repeated searches across several days to find a
better price.

The [eBay consumer-search and price-discovery record](records/us-nber-ebay-consumer-search-costs-lower-prices-2015.json)
finds an average of 36 searches per purchase over three distinct days, with
most sessions ending without a purchase. It estimates search costs at about 25
cents per search page and examines how more search relates to finding lower
prices.

**Boundary:** the study does not measure product quality, delivery, returns,
seller treatment, accessibility, household welfare, or whether search time was
valuable or burdensome. Lower price is not automatically lower total cost, and
the eBay setting may not represent other platforms.

**Next test:** follow a buyer from review and search through purchase, delivery,
quality, return, refund, complaint, and repeat choice. Record time, attention,
price, fees, replacement cost, and abandoned searches, and compare buyers with
different digital access, time, money, and language resources.

## More reviews can change seller visibility without proving customer welfare

**Plain meaning:** rewarding buyers for feedback can increase sales and help
higher-quality sellers become visible, but more feedback is not automatically a
more accurate or fairer record of what customers experience.

NBER's Taobao study finds that items with buyer-feedback rewards generated nearly
30% higher sales and were sold by higher-quality sellers. The mechanism is a
comparative platform result: rewards may help sellers build reputations and
signal quality when truthful feedback is otherwise under-provided.

The [FTC Amazon advertising-auction record](records/us-ftc-amazon-ad-auction-surcharge-2026.json)
adds a distinct U.S. enforcement allegation: the FTC and 22 states allege that
Amazon represented a second-price auction while charging Sponsored Products
advertisers their own winning bid close to 80% of the time by 2024, up from
about 30--40% in 2021. The complaint alleges the undisclosed surcharges
extracted tens of billions of dollars and were largely passed through to
consumers, but its case is pending and it supplies neither auction logs nor
seller margins, product prices, purchases, or a consumer pass-through estimate.
It therefore makes a seller-cost and visibility mechanism observable, not an
adjudicated price or household-harm finding.

**Boundary:** this is evidence from China's Taobao marketplace, not a U.S.
estimate; the Amazon material is a separate pending U.S. case. Neither source
follows product quality, returns, refunds, complaints, review edits, seller
margin, product price, or long-term trust, and neither establishes full
consumer welfare or universal causality.

**Next test:** compare rewarded and unrewarded feedback on U.S. platforms,
tracking review visibility, sales, product quality, return friction, complaints,
refunds, seller response, repeat purchases, and whether negative information
remains usable.

## Two rideshare apps do not automatically create two usable choices

**Plain meaning:** a rider can have two visible platforms while lacking the time,
information, or safe opportunity to compare them when a trip is urgent.

The [Uber/Lyft rideshare-comparison record](records/us-nber-uber-lyft-price-dispersion-search-frictions-2025.json)
finds price differences for identical trips, but only 16.1% of consumers
opening one app also opened the other. The authors estimate that comparison
frictions increase platform gross booking volume by more than $300 million
annually in New York City, and report that Uber API terms limit
price-comparison services.

**Boundary:** the study does not show each rider's wait, cancellation, safety,
accessibility, trip purpose, income, transit alternative, or completed-trip
quality. A lower fare may not be a better usable trip, and the modeled booking
volume is not an individual welfare estimate.

**Next test:** follow matched rides through both quotes, quote expiration, wait,
cancellation, safety, accessibility, completion, tips, lateness, backup route,
and repeat use. Compare riders with flexible trips against those with work,
care, medical, or mobility deadlines and no alternative route.

## Family caregiving can become a labor-market loss before it becomes a bill

**Plain meaning:** family care can protect an older person while changing the
caregiver's earnings and employment, with different paths for women and men.

The [family-caregiving and gendered-labor-path record](records/us-nber-family-caregiving-gendered-earnings-employment-2023.json)
links survey caregiving roles to administrative earnings records over a
25-year window around reported caregiving onset. Relative to a matched
comparison group, caregiving precedes drops in earnings and employment for
women, while men enter caregiving after significant labor-supply disruptions.

**Boundary:** the study does not observe each task, care hour, paid alternative,
leave, schedule, health, household support, savings, housing, or recipient
outcome. The trajectories do not by themselves establish that caregiving caused
every labor change, and employment status is not the full care burden.

**Next test:** follow caregiver and recipient together from the first recurring
care task. Record task intensity, hours, travel, paid care, leave, job offers,
earnings, savings, sleep, health, housing, emergency use, and recipient safety.
Compare leave, respite, transport, home services, and cash support by whether
they actually replace the hardest care hours.

The [wildfire-smoke caregiving and maternal-employment record](records/us-nber-wildfire-smoke-caregiving-mothers-employment-2026.json)
adds a climate-triggered child-care version. A quasi-experimental U.S. study
links smoke to school closures and student absences, then finds substantial
contemporaneous labor-market costs for mothers and employment loss when the
disruptions accumulate. The reported employment loss was fully offset among
mothers outside male-dominated industries, consistent with workplace tolerance
moderating the consequence of intermittent school-based care loss. That industry
comparison is not a direct measure of employer policy or bargaining power, and
the abstract does not measure household income, paid or unpaid hours, substitute
care, health, housing, or later employment recovery.

## An eviction order can become a work, health, and credit shock

**Plain meaning:** a housing-court order can add new damage to the earlier
income, health, or rent problem that brought the household to court.

NBER links administrative records in Cook County and New York City and uses
judge leniency as an instrumental variable. An eviction order increased
homelessness and hospital visits and reduced earnings, durable-goods consumption,
and credit access in the first two years. Longer term, indebtedness rose and
credit scores fell; housing and labor effects were concentrated among female and
Black tenants in the study.

**Boundary:** tenants were already in financial and health distress before
court. The study does not show each household's earlier shock, aid, legal help,
move destination, shelter, care network, school, transport, or later housing
quality. It does not establish that every later outcome was caused by the order
or that preventing an order creates stable housing.

**Next test:** follow the first notice through filing, hearing, order, aid,
counsel, move, next home, work, health, school, debt, credit, and recovery.
Separate voluntary, forced, and avoided moves, and record whether the next home
restored the route to work, care, and school.

## A lawyer can change the housing case without making rent affordable

**Plain meaning:** legal representation can protect a tenant inside housing
court, but the household may still face the same rent, income, health, or debt
problem afterward.

NBER studies New York City's Universal Access to Counsel rollout using detailed
housing-court data from 2016–2019. Free representation for tenants at or below
200% of the federal poverty guideline reduced possessory judgments, monetary
judgments, eviction warrants, and completed evictions. Effects were larger in
poorer places and places with more noncitizens.

**Boundary:** the study does not follow rent affordability, payment plans, work,
health, debt, credit, next housing, or long-term stability. A smaller judgment
or avoided eviction does not prove the underlying income or housing gap ended,
and avoiding a formal eviction may coexist with an informal move or later debt.

**Next test:** follow tenants from first notice through counsel, court outcome,
money owed, next rent, move, debt, credit, work, health, and next home. Compare
early counsel, late counsel, cash aid, payment plans, and no added support by
whether the household remains safely housed after assistance ends.

## Rent aid can improve payment without securing the next home

**Plain meaning:** emergency help can make this month's rent more likely to be
paid and reduce fear of eviction while the household remains exposed to the
same rent, income, or housing-market problem.

NBER evaluates five lottery-assigned emergency-rental-assistance programs in
four cities during the pandemic, distributing more than $200 million. Aid
increased rent payment and reduced eviction concern, with suggestive mental- and
physical-health improvements, but had little average effect on housing stability
or financial distress. Eviction moratoria, expanded safety-net programs, and a
softer rental market may have muted the measured displacement effect.

**Boundary:** the study does not follow each household from first missed payment
through application barriers, landlord response, legal help, court, next rent,
move, work, debt, credit, or care. The pandemic setting limits direct comparison
with ordinary periods. A small average stability effect does not mean no family
was protected, and a rent payment does not prove lasting affordability.

**Next test:** follow the first missed payment through aid request, decision,
delivery, landlord action, court, the next twelve rents, move, debt, credit,
work, health, and trust. Compare rapid cash, legal help, payment plans, and
landlord incentives by whether the household remains safely housed after aid
ends.

## The same spending number can hide opposite household conditions

**Plain meaning:** a household can spend less because it found a workable
alternative, or because it had to give something up; it can spend more because
it is comfortable, or because it cannot afford to wait.

The NBER study links transaction data to surveyed expectations for middle-income,
low-liquidity U.S. consumers. The median gap between observed and modeled
frictionless consumption was 40% in absolute value: 49% of consumers were below
the benchmark and 51% above it. Borrowing constraints alone could not explain
both directions.

**Boundary:** the frictionless benchmark is model-based, not a bill or direct
welfare score. The study does not identify every purchase's need, deadline,
informal support, health or work consequence, or whether higher spending was a
defensive response.

**Next test:** join item, deadline, cash, credit, health, work, housing, food,
informal support, adjustment cost, and later outcome; record the loss avoided as
well as the amount spent.

## The credit record can raise the price of protecting a home

**Plain meaning:** a homeowner may face a higher insurance bill because of both
the property's disaster risk and the owner's financial record.

The NBER study links about 70 million mortgage-associated insurance policies to
property-level disaster risk. Low-credit homeowners paid about 24% more for
identical coverage than high-credit homeowners. A Washington policy change that
banned credit information substantially weakened the credit-to-price link.

**Boundary:** the study does not follow each owner through renewal, escrow,
coverage reduction, nonrenewal, repair, sale, move, claim, or household food and
care tradeoffs. A premium increase is not by itself proof of a forced move or
uninsured loss, and a cheaper policy may have a larger deductible or exclusions.

**Next test:** join renewal, lender acceptance, nonrenewal, coverage cuts,
deductible, repairs, food and care tradeoffs, claims, sale, and move across
owners with different credit, liquidity, and disaster exposure.

## A business application is not yet a local job or service

**Plain meaning:** a place can generate many business ideas without generating
the firms, jobs, services, or meeting places that last.

The NBER study links the universe of U.S. business applications to later
employer-firm transitions. It separates pursuit from implementation: high-
startup locations show high application intensity, while low-startup locations
show low transition rates.

**Boundary:** the study does not follow each applicant's household resources,
financing, lease, permits, customers, service quality, local belonging, or
closure reason. An employer-firm transition is not durable survival, a good job,
local service, or community capacity.

**Next test:** join applications to leases, permits, credit, public contracts,
customers, payroll, service distance, prices, owner household resources, and
closure across local places.

## Remote work can move shopping work into the home

**Plain meaning:** saving a commute does not automatically mean saving money,
time, or household effort; it can change who shops, when they shop, and what
they pay.

The [remote-work shopping and consumption record](records/us-nber-remote-work-shopping-consumption-2026.json)
uses a survey and Nielsen consumer-panel evidence to find that working from
home increased online shopping and weekday store trips, total spending, product
quantity, and product range. It also found higher prices paid, lower deal use,
more shopping trips but fewer shopping minutes, and a roughly 1% increase in
grocery prices. Among married households, the price change was concentrated
where shopping responsibility shifted, especially when the new remote worker
was male.

**Boundary:** the study does not measure the full value of saved commuting,
unpaid planning, delivery and return work, household welfare, local store
survival, neighborhood change, or the experience of workers who cannot work
remotely. More spending can mean convenience or need, not automatically harm.

**Next test:** follow remote, hybrid, and on-site households with time-and-
receipt diaries covering commute, shopper identity, trips, orders, quantity,
prices, promotions, delivery, returns, unpaid labor, and satisfaction; link the
results to local stores and delivery activity.

## Fairer data access can reduce the data supply

**Plain meaning:** making a platform share data equally can give outside sellers
a fairer chance, but may also reduce the platform's reason to collect and
improve the data everyone uses.

The NBER model finds that equal data amounts alone can be undermined through
discriminatory pricing. Equal data and price can intensify competition, but the
platform may produce less data, and the modeled consumer-welfare result need not
improve.

**Boundary:** this is a theoretical mechanism, not a measured result for a
named U.S. platform, seller, household, or regulator. It does not measure
privacy, data accuracy, implementation cost, enforcement, or actual entry and
exit.

**Next test:** join data requests, price, affiliation, seller entry and exit,
data quality, privacy, search, consumer substitution, complaints, and
enforcement under real platform-data rules.

## Political voice passes through an institutional middle

**Plain meaning:** what voters want can be measured clearly and still be changed
by party pressure before it becomes a legislative vote.

The NBER party-pressure study bridges survey-based voter ideology and U.S. House
roll-call voting. It reports a wedge between members' actual votes and
constituent preferences and models parties balancing legislative wins with
members' electoral concerns.

**Boundary:** a roll-call gap is not automatically betrayal or policy failure;
party coordination can create coherent coalitions, and roll-call votes miss
agenda setting, bargaining, constituent service, trust, and material outcomes.

**Next test:** join issue preference, party pressure, member vote, amendment,
agenda control, constituent service, election risk, local conditions, trust, and
later participation for the same member-district-year.

## A stable average can hide worsening measurement

**Plain meaning:** a survey can keep a similar overall bias while individual
reports and absolute errors become less accurate.

The NBER CPS ASEC study evaluates 18 income and program-receipt variables against
linked administrative records. It finds respondent failure to report receipt is
often more important than nonresponse, and that absolute error can rise even
when net bias is stable.

**Boundary:** administrative records are not automatically complete or
conceptually identical to survey measures; the finding does not make every CPS
trend unusable or explain each respondent's reporting choice.

**Next test:** join question, response, contact and paradata, imputation,
administrative definition, match quality, correction, subgroup error, published
estimate, and downstream policy use for the same source-year.

## Mobility is not automatically recovery

**Plain meaning:** a worker can change occupations and keep working while still
losing earnings over the long run after the original occupation declines.

The NBER study follows more than 2.4 million workers from the 2000 Decennial
Census through administrative employment and earnings records to 2020. Starting
in an occupation that later contracted by at least 25% was associated with about
5% lower cumulative earnings despite slightly more quarters worked; occupational
mobility was substantial but incomplete.

**Boundary:** the public abstract does not establish every causal mechanism, and
earnings or quarters worked are not job quality, health, identity, family
security, or community continuity.

**Next test:** join occupation, industry, employer, place, training,
applications, switching, wages, hours, health, household resources, identity,
and children's later education and work for the same worker-year and family.

## Asset value is not automatically usable room

**Plain meaning:** a household can keep an asset and gain wealth on paper while
still being unable to use it for current spending or recovery.

The [mortgage-modification, wealth, and consumption record](records/us-nber-homeownership-loss-wealth-consumption-great-recession-2025.json)
reports that modifications raised home retention by 36 percentage points and
produced about $70,000 more housing wealth, but did not raise consumption
because credit constraints limited borrowing against equity.

**Boundary:** no current consumption effect does not mean housing wealth has no
future, residence, bequest, or security value; the setting is specific to
seriously delinquent homeowners and the study's modification comparison.

**Next test:** join delinquency, modification, payment, equity, credit access,
consumption, repairs, health, neighborhood, move or stay, bequest, and later
security for the same homeowner-year.

## Self-insurance can widen the distance between households

**Plain meaning:** higher income risk can make people with more room save more,
while households with less liquidity experience the wider financial environment
through borrowing, prices, or missed choices.

The NBER tax-return study reports that permanent income risk rose across the
distribution and nearly 70% among the top 5%. Its life-cycle model links higher
top-end risk to more saving, greater wealth inequality, and a 0.7-point lower
modeled risk-free rate.

**Boundary:** the rate effect is model-based; the study does not show which
assets receive saving, which borrowers get a lower rate, or whether a particular
family's rent, wage, distress, or political response changed.

**Next test:** join income shock, saving, asset destination, debt, rate, credit
approval, asset price, investment, wages, jobs, household consumption, and
political demand across households with different liquidity and ownership.

## Reporting is part of the safety outcome

**Plain meaning:** a public institution can change what people are willing to
tell it, so its records may move differently from the underlying problem.

The Census CES Secure Communities study reports that Hispanic victims became
less likely to report crimes, offending against Hispanics increased, and total
reported crime stayed unchanged. This is not evidence that every enforcement
policy has the same effect. It is a bounded mechanism showing that identity,
fear, institutional trust, and public-safety measurement are connected.

**Boundary:** reported crime, offending, victimization, police response, and
community safety are separate quantities. Silence may reflect fear, distrust,
cost, perceived futility, or another support route; it is not consent.

**Next test:** join policy exposure to reporting choice, victimization, police
response, alternative help, remedy, repeat harm, and later trust or civic action
for the same protected unit.

## Search time is not the same as choice

**Plain meaning:** a person can receive more time to find work while the local
market still offers too few credible employers or good matches.

The Census CES UI study reports that extensions lengthened nonemployment by
about one week, but their earnings effect was weaker in more concentrated local
labor markets. This adds a firm-power and social-insurance layer to unequal
optionality: the public route can change the clock without changing the market
at the end of the clock.

**Boundary:** concentration may capture several place and industry conditions;
earnings do not establish job quality, worker control, or household welfare.

**Next test:** follow the same worker from benefit timing through search,
available employers, accepted job, wages, schedule, benefits, commute,
retention, and later worker voice.

## Adoption is not one event

**Plain meaning:** a company can report AI use while workers, teams, and
business functions experience different kinds and levels of exposure.

The Census CES BTOS study separates firm adoption, functional deployment, and
worker-task use. The layers diverge: workers sometimes use AI without formal
firm adoption, and firms sometimes report adoption without worker task use. Most
users reported augmentation, but the survey does not show whether gains were
shared or control improved.

**Boundary:** firm-weighted and employment-weighted rates answer different
questions; cross-sectional correlations do not establish causality or later
worker outcomes.

**Next test:** link the same firm and worker to adoption timing, tasks, training,
monitoring, schedule, pay, headcount, promotion, voice, customer experience,
and exit or retention.

## A visible reporting gap can be only the tip of the iceberg

**Plain meaning:** a rule can make one part of hidden economic activity easy to
see while leaving most of the underlying gap unexplained.

The Census CES tip study finds threshold bunching in reported tips, but estimates
that this explains only about 1.5% of total missing tips under its revenue-based
assumptions. It adds a worker-firm-public-finance version of administrative
legibility: recorded income, actual income, employer behavior, tax compliance,
and household security are separate currencies.

**Boundary:** the study does not identify who retained unreported tips or prove
wage theft; the larger estimate depends on a 12% average tip assumption and a
specific restaurant scope.

**Next test:** join wage rule, payment method, tips, worker and employer records,
tax reporting, correction or enforcement, benefits, and household security.

## Screening is not support

**Plain meaning:** making a public benefit harder to keep can reduce its use
without creating the work or security the rule is supposed to produce.

The [Census CES SNAP work-requirements record](records/us-census-snap-work-requirements-participation-employment-2026.json)
uses linked SNAP and employment records from five states and a triple-differences
design around 2010s reinstatement. It estimates a 7% participation decline
without increased labor supply, with disproportionate screening of lower-income
people. The authors' welfare framework finds social costs exceeding budget
savings, but that is a study-specific interpretation rather than an observed
food-security or household-recovery outcome. This adds a direct policy-mechanism
test to the atlas: participation, employment, food security, and household
adaptation are different outcomes.

**Boundary:** the result is specific to the studied states, rules, and 2010s
reinstatement context. Participation loss can reflect reporting, exemptions,
recertification, administrative churn, changed eligibility, or non-use.

**Next test:** join notice, exemption, reporting, appointment, closure,
employment, hours, wages, food security, health, substitution, appeal, and later
participation for the same recipient.

## A narrow geopolitical rule can have a wide domestic footprint

**Plain meaning:** a state can restrict one product or destination while firms
change other exports, markets, and payroll too.

The Census CES export-control study reports a doubling in the share of U.S.
exports subject to controls from 2010 to 2024. Its 2014 Russia episode shows
reduced targeted exports alongside lower exports of other products and to other
destinations, plus lower average payroll. This adds a geopolitical version of
capacity versus burden: the target effect and domestic spillover must be measured
separately.

**Boundary:** export and payroll changes do not establish national-security
success, household harm, or worker layoffs; one policy episode cannot stand for
all controls.

**Next test:** join control, license, shipment, substitution, price, payroll,
employment, investment, new markets, consumer incidence, and security or
diplomatic outcome for the same firm and product route.

## Recovery can be productive and disruptive at the same time

**Plain meaning:** a disaster program can improve the productivity of the firms
that emerge while closures, job loss, and ownership change disrupt the people and
places that were there before.

The Census CES flood study reports exits concentrated among less productive
plants, machinery transfers to more productive entrants, survivor upgrades, and
financing help for young and small firms. This adds a firm-and-public-finance
version of the recovery tension: productive reallocation is not the same as
shared recovery or community continuity.

**Boundary:** plant productivity and modelled fiscal returns do not establish
worker wellbeing, local hiring, service continuity, or equal rebuilding.

**Next test:** join flood damage, aid, financing, exit, ownership, machinery,
jobs, wages, migration, local services, household recovery, and community
security for the same plant-worker-place episode.

## Recovery can change the people in a place

**Plain meaning:** rebuilding after disaster can restore structures unevenly and
change who can afford to return, so community recovery is not just a physical
repair count.

The Census CES wildfire study finds that home destruction produced three years
of lower income, with cumulative losses equal to 26% of pre-fire annual income.
Four years after fires, half of destroyed addresses remained unoccupied and
reoccupied addresses housed substantially higher-income residents. This adds an
individual and cultural-place layer to unequal exposure: the average affected
region can look wealthy while directly damaged households lose room and the
return population changes.

**Boundary:** the study does not identify insurance, aid, rebuilding barriers,
displacement, or whether nonreturn was voluntary. Higher-income reoccupancy is
not automatically preference or improvement.

**Next test:** join fire damage, insurance, aid, rebuilding, debt, income,
employment, health, occupancy, move reason, destination, social ties, and later
community security.

## Family advantage is not always the named asset’s effect

**Plain meaning:** children who grow up in owner-occupied homes may do better
later because of many family and neighborhood differences, even when the home
tenure itself is not the causal driver.

The Census CES housing study reports positive associations of about $2,000 in
adult income and 6 percentage points in adult ownership, but its unobservable
sensitivity and sibling fixed-effects tests find no detected causal effect of
being born into or spending years in an owned home. This is an important
intergenerational counterexample to turning status and wealth correlations into
a single cultural inheritance mechanism.

**Boundary:** a null tenure effect does not show that housing quality, crowding,
eviction, neighborhood, schooling, or family resources do not matter.

**Next test:** join housing quality, tenure, moves, schooling, transfers, debt,
networks, adult work and ownership, health, and later household security.

## Entry routes can move before established jobs do

**Plain meaning:** a technology shock may first change who gets hired into a
career, rather than first appearing as obvious layoffs.

The Census CES QWI study reports a 12% relative employment decline for workers
aged 22–24 in the most AI-exposed industry-state cells over ten quarters after
ChatGPT's introduction, with fewer hires as the main channel. Hiring later
recovered from a smaller base. This adds a generational and timing layer to
unequal optionality: the same technology can have different effects depending
on whether someone is entering or already inside the labor market.

**Boundary:** industry-state exposure is not individual AI use; post-pandemic
trends, remote work, education, sector demand, and monetary policy remain
relevant. Lower entry hiring is not automatically permanent displacement.

**Next test:** follow the same worker from exposure and application through
offers, hiring, pay, occupation, training, household resources, switching,
retention, and career progression.

## Faster contact is not equal participation

**Plain meaning:** a public institution can make it quicker to answer without
making it equally easy or safe for everyone to take part.

The Census Household Panel text-message study finds that text invitations can
bring some survey responses in faster, while access and consent differ across
age, race/ethnicity, education, income, region, marital status, and respondent
type. About half of panelists consented to text invitations. This adds a
measurement-and-voice layer: contact design can improve response timing while
leaving digital access, privacy, trust, burden, and representation unsettled.

**Boundary:** this is survey-contact behavior, not political participation or a
general digital-inclusion estimate. Consent, message access, response, and
completion have different denominators; faster response does not prove lower
burden, representative coverage, or institutional trust.

**Next test:** follow the same respondent through invitation mode, device,
accessibility, consent, message receipt, response timing, completion,
nonresponse, panel retention, and downstream institutional use of the data.

## A form can feel easy while different designs produce different results

**Plain meaning:** people can say a form is easy while one design still makes
it easier to answer correctly and quickly.

The Census date-entry experiment with 1,000 participants found that calendar
fields performed better than text entry for effectiveness and better than text
entry and dropdowns for efficiency on most near-date tasks. The advantage was
especially visible when a task required knowing the day of the week. Yet most
participants rated all designs as extremely easy. Public-form availability,
perceived ease, accurate completion, and timely service are therefore separate
stages.

**Boundary:** the experiment concerns near dates and does not establish equal
access across devices, disabilities, languages, literacy levels, or high-stakes
administrative forms. Objective task performance is not benefit receipt,
correction, trust, or political participation.

**Next test:** follow the same user through device, language, disability
accommodation, interface, completion, correction, dropout, processing time,
and actual service or record outcome.

## Privacy can protect people while changing what the public can see

**Plain meaning:** releasing safer data usually means deciding where exactness
is replaced with noise, and that changes which problems are visible.

The Census formal-privacy paper proposes a Markov-chain mechanism for releasing
noisy versions of discrete data and links the privacy parameter to a temperature
and sensitivity relationship. It proves a formal property for the proposed
mechanism and demonstrates it on histograms. The broader insight is not that
the mechanism works in practice, but that privacy, accuracy, subgroup
visibility, and public judgment are separate currencies.

**Boundary:** this is a theoretical framework and proof of concept, not an
operational Census product, field test, public-trust survey, or policy outcome.
Formal privacy does not establish useful accuracy, equal error, runtime,
security in practice, or understandable public statistics.

**Next test:** compare privacy loss, error, subgroup accuracy, suppression,
runtime, user understanding, and downstream decisions for the same statistical
product.

## Digital convenience can leave a cash-dependent minority behind

**Plain meaning:** the payment method most people prefer may not be the method
that works for everyone.

The Census incentive-preference study finds that about 5% preferred physical
cash while most preferred digital gift cards or electronic transfers. The
cash-preferring subgroup was mostly young, low-income, and without a college
degree. This adds a consumer and household-room version of unequal
optionality: a digital route can be convenient for many while requiring device,
bank, merchant, fee, or trust resources that others do not have.

**Boundary:** the study uses a large nonprobability survey and stated
preferences. It does not measure actual receipt, bank/device access, payment
failure, incentive effectiveness, or household improvement.

**Next test:** follow the same payment episode through eligibility, choice,
device/bank access, fees, delivery, receipt, failure, replacement, spending,
and later household room by subgroup.

## Risk changes with the unit of analysis

**Plain meaning:** a broad place map can show one pattern while a household and
parcel analysis shows another.

The Census CES flood-risk study finds little net migration to risky parcels and
no change in the overall household share living in floodplains, while income
gradients are nonlinear and building type, homeownership, and multiple property
ownership matter. It also identifies low-income uninsured homeowners as a
vulnerable subgroup. This is a climate and housing version of unequal exposure:
the relevant unit is not always the neighborhood average.

**Boundary:** parcel risk is not damage; protection is not repair or recovery;
stable location is not proof of voluntary choice or safety.

**Next test:** follow the same property-household through hazard, insurance,
premium, claim, repair, assistance, debt, health, move or stay, and later
housing security.

## Visibility is not inclusion

**Plain meaning:** a government can get better at estimating who is missing from
its records without every person becoming counted, represented, protected, or
able to correct an error.

The Census CES capture-recapture experiment tests whether administrative and
third-party records can improve coverage estimates for the 2020 Census. It adds
a measurement-governance stage to the atlas: record existence, linkage, coverage
estimation, enumeration, privacy, service access, and trust are separate.

**Boundary:** the experiment is methodological and does not provide a final
national undercount. More data can improve estimation while importing stale,
duplicated, mislinked, or uneven information.

**Next test:** join record source, match quality, missingness, correction,
enumeration, service use, privacy incident, and trust for protected units.

## 1. Unequal practical agency

**Plain meaning:** people may have the same formal option but very different
ability to recognize it, reach it, afford the effort, use it, correct failure,
and leave.

**Seen across:** benefits and SNAP route design, housing, care, consumer
remedies, platforms, work, infrastructure, and alliances.

**Why it matters:** the central inequality is not only the final outcome. It is
the room to maneuver before and after the outcome.

**Boundary:** this is a cross-source organizing concept, not a single agency
score.

**Next test:** retain failed attempts, non-use, successful use, correction, and
exit in the same denominator for one bounded episode.

The new AI adaptive-capacity finding extends this concept into the labor
market: two occupations can face similar technological pressure while workers
have different practical room to move, retrain, or bargain. This is an
occupation-level pattern, not proof that any individual worker can actually
exit.

The retraining follow-up adds a measured transition route: in a workforce
program sample, earnings returns for people from highly exposed occupations
were larger in 2022–2024 and were mainly associated with movement into less
AI-exposed occupations. The route may be protective, but it can also transfer
time, access, care, geographic, and identity costs to the worker; those costs
are not measured here.

The measurement-quality counterexample adds a further rule: before comparing
AI exposure across sources, record how exposure was produced. Different
language models applying the same rubric produced materially different scores
and downstream estimates. A technical measure can therefore become part of the
social and political dispute over who is considered at risk.

The Muslim-American review shows the same separation in cultural life:
population, institutions, and representation can become more visible while
public legitimacy and experienced treatment remain unsettled. Presence is not
the same as safety, equal standing, or agenda-setting power.

The chronic-absence file adds a school-participation version: enrollment can
exist while a large share of students are absent for at least 10% of the year.
The state differences are real descriptive variation, but the aggregate file
does not identify whether the mechanism was illness, care, housing,
transportation, safety, school climate, or disengagement.

The MOEquity audit adds an organizational-memory version: emergency rules and
technical assistance can operate at scale while written procedures, reliable
oversight data, and shared lessons remain incomplete. Crisis action and durable
institutional learning therefore run on different clocks.

The tribal-water review adds an infrastructure version: a built system can
still leave property eligibility, grant navigation, certified operators, energy,
and routine maintenance unresolved. Physical capacity and durable service are
therefore separate clocks, especially where legal status and agency boundaries
shape who can be connected.

The broadband-deployment audit adds a digital version: provider maps, public
challenges, awards, planned locations, construction, and ten-year financial
sustainability are separate steps. The communities with the fewest resources
may also have the least capacity to challenge the map that determines targeting.

The wearable-health assessment adds a data-rich-care version: more sensor data
can support remote and personalized care while device reliability, privacy,
workflow integration, liability, and clinician time remain unsettled. More
information is not automatically more usable care.

The Marketplace-broker review adds a consumer-control version: a licensed and
registered intermediary can still make changes without reliably verified consent
or notice. A transaction's visibility, authorization, correction, and coverage
continuity are separate stages.

The No Surprises Act review adds a provider-market version: legal protection
can coincide with changes in in-network claims while payment trends continue
older paths. Network participation, patient bills, affordability, and care
continuity should not be treated as one outcome.

The Part D stabilization review adds a public-subsidy version: government can
hold down an immediate premium shock and preserve enrollment while taking on
financial risk. Stable premiums and enrollment still do not prove stable
medication use or long-term affordability.

The federal eligibility-data review adds a state-accountability version: many
databases and a successful identifier match can still coexist with missing,
duplicate, and conflicting records. Data-rich oversight is not automatically
usable oversight.

The 2025 NHIS adds a population coverage version: exchange coverage expanded
while the overall uninsured rate stayed roughly stable. Coverage type, equal
protection, continuity, affordability, and actual care use therefore remain
separate questions.

## 2. Formal capacity is separating from usable protection

**Plain meaning:** a benefit, right, service, project, remedy, or capability can
exist without becoming reliable help for the intended person or place.

**Seen across:** SNAP and EBT security, health routes, skilled-nursing prior
authorization, vouchers, privacy requests, refunds, D.C. Solar for All credits
and billing correction, transmission finance, grid projects, military
readiness, and alliance commitments.

DOE's Clean Energy Connector adds an eligibility-to-opt-in stage: a minimum
savings rule can improve the formal terms of low-income solar access before
enrollment, bill receipt, and lasting energy security are observed.

The Pell/FAFSA review adds an education version: eligibility and maximum-award
eligibility expanded after simplification even though fewer students completed
the application. Some household configurations could lose eligibility, and the
data do not show award receipt or enrollment. A broader rule is therefore not
the same as usable college access.

The latest breadth pass extends this beyond benefits. HUD's housing stock
ratios fall sharply when availability and physical adequacy are added; BLS
finds that leave access can exist while workers still do not take needed leave;
arts participation varies strongly by income. The [NCES/BJS school-safety
record](records/us-nces-bjs-school-safety-fear-avoidance-2021-2022.json) adds
a youth participation boundary: reported at-school nonfatal victimization fell
from 52 to 22 per 1,000 students ages 12--18 from 2012 to 2022, while in
2021--22 3.8% reported being afraid of attack or harm at school, 3.6% avoided
activities or classes because of that fear, and 2.2% stayed home. The measures
are separate survey indicators, not a same-student pathway: they do not name an
incident, show how much instruction was missed, establish a learning or
mental-health effect, or show whether school response repaired safety. In the
geopolitical lane, GAO finds that allied space cooperation can be formally
organized while staffing, points of contact, and implementation milestones
remain weak. These are separate sources and units, but they repeat the same
interface problem: formal capacity becomes protection only when the affected
unit can actually use it.

**Why it matters:** official availability is an input; receipt, adequacy, and
durability are later outcomes.

**Boundary:** a visible gap does not prove universal institutional failure.

**Next test:** follow one unit from eligibility or approval through actual use,
adequacy, continuity, and recovery.

## 3. Households preserve one need by spending another resource

**Plain meaning:** staying fed, housed, connected, employed, or cared for may
require cheaper goods, delay, borrowing, less saving, extra work, outside help,
or lower quality.

**Seen across:** Federal Reserve SHED, SIPP, MEPS, Urban food-security trends,
childcare, housing, health-cost, and RECS energy-burden records.

The [Federal Reserve income-volatility and support record](records/us-federal-reserve-income-volatility-support-2025.json)
adds income timing, informal help, and budget margin. In 2025, 30% of adults
reported income that varied at least occasionally and 11% reported bill
difficulty because of that variation; among self-employed adults, the
corresponding shares were 58% and 22%. Twenty-three percent received help with
at least one listed expense from outside their household, ranging from 47% of
adults ages 18--29 to 13% of adults age 45 or older. Money left over always or
often ranged from 19% below $25,000 family income to 59% at $100,000 or more.
These are separate room measures and descriptive cross-sections, not one
household hardship score or a verified coping sequence.

The layered health-cost synthesis adds a non-pooled bridge from care-forgoing
to household adaptation, credit exposure, coverage, and short-run civic
contact. Its source-specific units and outcomes must remain separate.

The NCVS/NIBRS comparison adds a public-safety measurement subtheme: a survey
of people and a police-record system can describe overlapping events without
measuring the same thing. Lived victimization, reporting, agency recording,
coverage, and remedy must stay separate; a difference between rates is not by
itself a count of unreported crime or police failure.

The FEC campaign-finance record adds a political-resource subtheme: money can
show organizational capacity and communication potential without showing broad
participation, persuasion, turnout, policy change, or legitimacy. Political
voice has both a financial channel and a citizen-action channel; they should
not be treated as the same thing.

The EAVS record adds the administrative side of political voice: participation
requires a route from eligibility and voting method through ballot acceptance,
provisional handling, and counting. High turnout and auditable equipment do
not prove equal access, successful cure, public trust, or policy influence.

The NLRB record adds a worker-voice version of the same pattern: people can
invoke a formal labor process while agency capacity, settlement, and decision
stages shape what becomes visible. A charge or representation petition is not
yet a remedy, bargaining result, safer workplace, or durable worker control.

The GAO federal reentry audit adds a justice-and-return version: earned time
and formal eligibility can exist while missing eligibility dates, limited
halfway-house capacity, and late provider payments delay the actual bridge back
to housing, work, treatment, and community life. A prerelease route is not yet
usable reentry, durable safety, or social inclusion.

The [jail-healthcare accreditation and mortality record](records/us-nber-jail-healthcare-accreditation-mortality-2026.json)
adds a within-custody care route. In 46 midsized jails randomized to immediate
or delayed accreditation offers, compliance with prespecified healthcare
standards improved—especially training and patient care—and 12-month mortality
was substantially lower, with no detected increase in capital or labor inputs.
The reported mechanisms include better coordination, screening, and oversight.
This supports an institutional-practice-to-care pathway for participating
jails, not a claim about post-release health, trust, fairness of detention,
recidivism, contractor-wide performance, or the correctional system as a
whole.

The EEOC record adds a civil-rights version: complaint volume, administrative
resolution, monetary relief, and equitable-relief labels can all be visible
without proving that a particular worker received adequate protection or that
the workplace stayed changed. Formal correction and lived recovery remain
different stages.

The [federal-building accessibility record](records/us-gao-federal-building-accessibility-complaints-2026.json)
adds a disability-and-public-space version: a formal complaint route can exist
while potential users do not know it is available. GAO's 41 complaints, 25
closures, and 16 in-process cases show route activity, not barrier prevalence,
correction quality, or later usable access. Discoverability is therefore part
of institutional access, not a cosmetic communication detail.

The [FDA third-party device-review record](records/us-gao-fda-third-party-device-review-audit-2026.json)
adds a health-regulatory version: public agencies can delegate technical review
to accredited private organizations while the audit and correction clock remains
underspecified. The delegated route may increase capacity, but review volume and
accreditation do not establish device safety, patient access, or trust.

The VA electronic-health-record review adds a rollout-and-usability version:
deployment, accelerated scale, and recommendation activity can coexist with
unfinished cost visibility, user readiness, operational testing, and correction.
System availability is therefore not successful use, and successful use is not
yet clinical benefit or safer care.

The GAO disability-workforce audit adds a public-employment version: formal
workforce-program eligibility can coexist with incomplete disability data,
uneven interpreter and training access, weak aggregate monitoring, and lower
post-exit employment and earnings among participants reporting disabilities.
The outcome gap is real, but the audit does not identify accommodation receipt,
causation, retention, or durable job security.

The GAO working-adults analysis adds a household/employer version: millions of
adults enrolled in Medicaid or living in SNAP-receiving households worked in
2024, and selected-state data showed recurring overlap with large employers.
Work and public-benefit reliance can therefore coexist without proving employer
cost shifting, inadequate pay, or worker dependence. Wages, hours, health costs,
household needs, and benefit continuity remain separate stages.

The New York Fed small-business survey adds a firm-capacity version: employment,
revenue expectations, pricing power, profitability, and debt can move on
different tracks by firm size. Employment growth is therefore not the same as
firm resilience, worker security, customer welfare, or local prosperity.

The [BJS pretrial-jail-exposure record](records/us-bjs-jail-pretrial-exposure-2024.json)
adds a justice-contact subtheme. Midyear 2024 custody was 658,700 people and
the average daily population was 667,700, down 12% from 2014, while the annual
flow was an estimated 7.9 million admissions, down 31%. Sixty-eight percent of
people held at midyear were unconvicted, and the reported Black incarceration
rate (566 per 100,000 residents) exceeded the White rate (149). Admissions are
not unique people and the stock and flow use different denominators, but the
series shows why aggregate contraction can coexist with frequent, unequal, and
mostly pretrial contact with the state. Custody, admission, conviction, release,
supervision, reentry, and later safety are not one outcome.

BJS probation-and-parole data add the post-custody version: about 3.68 million
adults remained under community supervision at yearend 2024 even after a 24%
comparable-agency decline over the prior decade. Fewer people under supervision
does not mean little state contact, and administrative exits do not establish
durable reintegration, fair conditions, or later safety.

The FTA transit record adds an infrastructure-usability subtheme: a public
system can deliver enormous service and still leave the practical value of a
trip dependent on place, schedule, fare, reliability, accessibility, and the
person's actual need. National capacity and usable household mobility recover
on different clocks.

The GAO SMART review adds a public-technology conversion subtheme: a community
can apply for and receive funding for an advanced transportation demonstration
before the technology is procured, the project is complete, or residents and
workers can use a dependable service. Awards, pilots, operating performance,
learning, and durable public adoption are separate stages. See the [SMART
transportation record](records/us-gao-smart-transportation-demonstration-2026.json)
and [finding](projects/us-dot-smart-transportation-demonstration/findings/us-gao-smart-transportation-demonstration-2026-001.md).

The GAO ports-of-entry review adds a border-capacity version: a formally public
inspection system can use private reimbursement and donated infrastructure to
add overtime, lanes, biometric tools, or other equipment. Funding and traffic
scale do not establish shorter waits, equal access, lower trade costs, public
control at the margin, or trust. State capacity and who pays for it are related
but separate questions. See the [CBP partnership record](records/us-gao-cbp-public-private-port-partnerships-2026.json)
and [finding](projects/us-cbp-port-public-private-partnerships/findings/us-gao-cbp-public-private-port-partnerships-2026-001.md).

The GAO water-resilience review adds a place-protection version: federal grants
and loans can be substantial while local rates, debt repayment, maintenance,
and service-area mapping determine who can sustain and see the benefit. Water
investment is therefore not the same as maintained, affordable, or disaster-
resilient service. See the [water-resilience record](records/us-gao-water-infrastructure-resilience-vulnerable-communities-2025.json)
and [finding](projects/us-water-infrastructure-resilience/findings/us-gao-water-infrastructure-resilience-vulnerable-communities-2025-001.md).

The GAO technology-release review adds a geopolitical permission version: a
foreign partner may want sensitive capability while access depends on a
distributed U.S. review system whose timing, visibility, and urgency vary by
request. Approval is not transfer, transfer is not use, and use is not trust or
strategic effect. See the [technology-release record](records/us-gao-technology-release-foreign-disclosure-2026.json)
and [finding](projects/us-technology-release-foreign-disclosure/findings/us-gao-technology-release-foreign-disclosure-2026-001.md).

The State priority-recommendations review adds a foreign-assistance control
version: strategic money, weapons, and training can be delivered while fraud
review, incident documentation, safeguarding, and congressional reporting are
still incomplete. Authorization, delivery, use, correction, trust, and
strategic effect therefore remain separate clocks. See the [foreign-assistance
oversight finding](findings/us-gao-state-priority-recommendations-security-assistance-ukraine-2026-matched-evidence-001.md).

The GAO federal-Tribal agreement review adds a sovereignty-and-usable-voice
version: consultation can be formal and substantive while legal authority,
staffing, information, dispute procedures, and the time available for Tribal
advocacy determine how much control is actually usable. Eleven strengthening
features are a design resource, not proof of equal power or community consent.
See the [shared-control finding](findings/us-gao-tribal-federal-shared-decision-making-agreements-2026-matched-evidence-001.md).

The GAO security-cooperation workforce review adds a human-capacity version: a
state can count formal positions and complete legal reforms while staffing,
training, research, and overseas continuity remain unfinished. Workforce
professionalization is therefore a stage beneath partner capability, trust,
and geopolitical effect. See the [workforce record](records/us-gao-security-cooperation-workforce-2026.json)
and [finding](projects/us-security-cooperation-workforce/findings/us-gao-security-cooperation-workforce-2026-001.md).

The Navy robotic-autonomy review adds a capability-realization version:
strategic urgency and planned investment can coexist with leadership turnover,
platform-centered acquisition, siloed requirements, and unclear portfolio
ownership. Research, prototype, fielding, operator use, sustainment, and
external effect therefore remain separate clocks. See the [RAS
organizational-capacity finding](findings/us-gao-navy-robotic-autonomous-systems-organizational-capacity-2026-matched-evidence-001.md).

The GAO Coast Guard review adds a finite-capacity version: a visible emergency
response can gain assets and attention by moving them away from other missions,
while partner forces fill part of the gap. Activity in one mission is therefore
not the same as more total state capacity, and an agency's measure may miss a
successful action completed by another partner. See the [maritime tradeoff
record](records/us-gao-coast-guard-migrant-interdiction-tradeoffs-2026.json)
and [finding](projects/us-coast-guard-migrant-interdiction-tradeoffs/findings/us-gao-coast-guard-migrant-interdiction-tradeoffs-2026-001.md).

The NBER SNAP-office study adds a causal geography version: closing a local
enrollment office reduced nearby participation even though the formal benefit
program remained in place. Physical distance and route availability are part of
usable access, while participation still does not prove benefit adequacy or
food security. See the [SNAP place-access record](records/us-nber-snap-office-closure-participation-2025.json)
and [finding](projects/us-snap-enrollment-office-place-effect/findings/us-nber-snap-office-closure-participation-2025-001.md).

The SIPRI arms-transfer record adds a geopolitical-dependence subtheme:
supplier concentration can create material and technical reliance before it
creates political obedience or durable alliance behavior. Transfers, delivery,
operation, sustainment, replaceability, consent, and leverage must remain
separate.

The FTC social-platform study adds a platform-control subtheme: collection,
sharing, retention, and deletion exceptions can remain active behind a visible
privacy request or setting. Formal control, actual deletion, downstream copies,
user safety, trust, and exit are different stages.

**Why it matters:** visible stability can be produced by quiet sacrifice.

**Boundary:** adaptation shares do not identify the same household's shock,
protected outcome, or later recovery.

**Next test:** link a dated pressure to the resource spent, the need protected,
and whether the trade was reversed or became permanent.

The 2008 stimulus study adds a credit conversion: a temporary payment became a
durable purchase, mostly a vehicle, with substantial loan leverage. This can be
necessary mobility or future strain; spending, access, and repayment must stay
separate.

The guaranteed-income experiment adds an intervention version of the same
concept: one person's new cash changed other household members' training,
employment, transfers, relationships, and healthcare reports. The direction is
not uniformly positive or negative, so household room must be measured beside
work, care, trust, and post-payment durability.

The Child Tax Credit expiration adds a public-mood version: losing $1,000 of
benefit was associated with a 1.7-point fall in consumer sentiment, with larger
effects for lower-income families with multiple children. A household can
therefore lose practical room and judge the economy differently, while voting,
trust, and spending remain unmeasured. This is a policy-to-perception link, not
a same-household story through later political action.

Student-loan policy uncertainty adds a belief-before-receipt version: borrowers
changed payments and nondurable spending when they expected forgiveness or a
pause, even though the promised forgiveness was not implemented. Expectations
can therefore move household behavior before public relief exists, while later
delinquency and realized relief remain separate outcomes.

Employment policy adds a work-to-politics version: among lower-income mothers,
the study's policy-induced employment variation was associated with lower
turnout and civic engagement and more conservative policy preferences. Work can
bring income and social contact while taking time from civic life; the result is
not a universal work-to-ideology claim.

Corporate PACs add a firm-side legitimacy version: firms temporarily reduced
visible political giving when public attention made a tie risky, then returned
toward baseline. Reputation can change what a firm shows without proving that
it changed lobbying, policy goals, or underlying political influence.

Carbon tariffs add an international policy version: a measure can lower foreign
emissions while raising domestic emissions, and more participating countries
do not automatically improve the tariff-only result. Climate capacity,
household incidence, foreign acceptance, and geopolitical leverage are separate
stages.

Geoeconomic coercion adds a target-selection version: trade and financial
dependence can turn a private firm into the immediate target of a state
contest. Firm compliance, government compliance, public cost, alliance
response, and strategic success remain separate.

The retaliation record adds an external-response version: tariff pressure can
produce countermeasures and bargaining, followed by pauses or partial
reversals. Response is not the same as compliance, and a negotiated change is
not by itself proof that the original strategy succeeded.

Transportation adds a place version: reaching work or services can require a
large money and time payment. The payment can be worthwhile if it buys better
access, so cost must be read beside what the trip makes possible. National
spending and commuting averages do not establish which households could switch
mode, move, work remotely, or absorb a repair.

## 4. Search and waiting are hidden prices

**Plain meaning:** finding housing, care, work, food, benefits, refunds, or a
safe digital route consumes time, money, attention, dignity, and sometimes
political energy.

**Seen across:** voucher searches, SNAP application and recertification
timeliness, skilled-nursing appeals, care access, customer service, insurance,
subscription cancellation, and platform exit.

**Why it matters:** a nominally affordable option may be unusable once the
search and waiting costs are counted.

**Boundary:** longer search or waiting does not by itself identify its cause.

**Next test:** measure effort, alternatives, interim substitute, waiting time,
and the outcome that the displaced time or money replaced.

HUD's housing-stock report adds a place version: nominally affordable units can
be occupied, unavailable, or physically inadequate. Affordability, availability,
adequacy, location, landlord acceptance, lease-up, and durable staying are
separate stages.

The HUD–LEHD trajectory study adds a time-linked version: work and earnings
began to improve around first entry into assistance, while trajectories for
people who later left flattened relative to those who stayed. These are
suggestive associations, not causal proof, because need, disability, health,
education, household structure, and exit type differ across groups. Housing
help can create room for work without making earnings the whole measure of
housing security. See the [HUD-work trajectory finding](projects/us-hud-assistance-employment-trajectories/findings/us-census-hud-assistance-employment-earnings-trajectories-2005-2022-001.md).

The BJS victimization data add a public-safety version: lived harm can be
larger than police-visible harm. Experience, reporting, police response,
remedy, safety, trust, and later action should not be collapsed into one crime
or confidence measure.

The [Secure Communities victim-reporting record](records/us-census-immigration-enforcement-victim-reporting-public-safety-2026.json)
adds a policy-climate mechanism to that distinction. Its linked policy and
victim-reporting analysis estimates that the enforcement program reduced
reporting by Hispanic victims while offending against Hispanics rose; reported
crime did not move because the two changes offset. The reported 7.9% increase
in offending for a 10% reporting decline is a study estimate in that setting,
not a general public-safety elasticity. It does, however, show why a stable
police-visible count cannot safely stand in for stable victimization, safety,
trust, or institutional reach.

## 5. Remedy is a second access system

**Plain meaning:** after an institution admits a problem, people may still need
notice, identity proof, a claim, acceptance, cashing, reissue, account repair,
or another round of contact.

**Seen across:** Amazon Prime, CFPB Ombudsman redress, Credit Karma refunds,
wage recovery, recalls, privacy requests, D.C. Solar for All billing
correction, FleetCor small-business fee redress, transmission refunds, benefit
replacement, CFPB complaint responses, and the linked Census fraud follow-up.

The current FTC record makes the conversion stages visible inside one consumer
domain. Across 33 first-distribution programs in 2024, the FTC reported that
4.35 million consumers were sent payments and 64.2% had cashed one at the
report date; the Amazon Flex example shows a later payment-channel reissue can
raise aggregate participation. The separate Amazon Prime update reports more
than $845 million issued under an automatic-payment design that removes future
claim-form and notice-response steps. Together, these sources distinguish
eligibility, sending, cashing or acceptance, reissue, and aggregate issuance.
They do not establish a common person-level rate of receipt, adequacy, account
correction, subscription cancellation, switching, trust, or recovery.

The Handy/Angi case adds an identified worker-redress population: checks sent
to eligible people advance the route beyond a proposed fund, while cashing,
adequacy, restored earnings, and later work remain unknown.

The [Publishing.com earnings-promise and refund-route record](records/us-ftc-publishingcom-earnings-refund-2026.json)
adds a work-opportunity version. The FTC alleged that the firm's programs
promised substantial income from e-books and audiobooks while fine-print or
lengthy refund terms made refunds difficult or impossible; the final order
requires $1.5 million in payment, substantiated earnings claims, and clearer,
promptly honored cancellation/refund terms. A promise of economic independence
can therefore be paired with conditions that make exit difficult, while the
order still does not reveal individual purchase, refund receipt, income, debt,
alternative work, trust, or recovery.

The AI customer-service experiment adds a firm and platform version: AI can
make a support exchange faster and sound better while a repeat complaint stays
unfixed. Conversation quality, correction, and practical exit are separate
outcomes.

**Why it matters:** “money issued” or “case closed” is not the same as personal
recovery. Reporting a loss or receiving a formal recovery signal can coexist
with later household strain and mixed institutional confidence.

**Boundary:** aggregate issued, sent, cashed, and accepted amounts are
different administrative stages, and none is a recovery rate without a common
customer denominator and a later outcome measure.

**Next test:** preserve eligibility, notice, claim, receipt, amount, delay,
adequacy, and later use for the same account or case.

## 6. Administrative legibility is part of access

**Plain meaning:** a person must be recognized, classified correctly, asked for
the right proof, and given a workable way to correct the record before a rule
can help.

**Seen across:** public benefits, housing eligibility verification, federal
eligibility matching, healthcare authorization, privacy, migration, consumer
remedies, and platform governance.

The Medicaid expansion spillover study adds a cross-program mechanism: one
legible benefit route can lower the marginal effort of applying for another,
without proving that the household became secure. The official Census CES
version estimates about 0.6 additional SNAP participants per 100 people for a
typical 0-to-138% expansion and roughly one in five adults induced into public
insurance also induced into SNAP. That is a stronger participation estimate,
not a stronger wellbeing estimate; the research line still does not follow
the same household through notice, benefit amount, food security, or recovery.

The [Social Security information and application-choice record](records/us-nber-social-security-information-disability-early-retirement-choice-2026.json)
adds a benefit-interface version. An initial Social Security statement with
personalized estimated Disability Insurance and Old-Age Insurance amounts
increased applications for both programs among people ages 62 through full
retirement age with work-limiting health conditions or low work ability. The
increase split between DI-only and joint DI/OAI applications and was
concentrated among people farther from full retirement age. Better information
can therefore change the route selected without showing an award, payment,
adequacy, health improvement, work exit, or household security.

The CFPB API-vintage comparison adds a measurement layer: routing speed and
response labels can remain visible while fields, filtering, automation, and
submission rules change what the public record can represent.

GAO's FY2025 Treasury audit adds a government-wide knowledge layer: corrected
financial disclosures and closed recommendations can coexist with three new
control deficiencies and six older recommendations still open. A public
account can therefore be visible before the procedures behind it are fully
reliable. This does not mean every figure is wrong; it means public visibility,
audit confidence, and public trust are separate stages.

GAO's federal-program review adds the outcome layer: an agency can count money
spent or people served without being able to show whether the intended problem
improved. The selected 15-program review found performance-management
processes in 12 programs and not in three; a DHS case shows that oversight can
improve goals and tracking even when final impact remains open.

GAO's tax-rulemaking review adds a voice-and-attention layer: a legal comment
period can coexist with weak virtual access, incomplete hearing records, and
unclear handling of AI-assisted mass comments. Participation can become easier
to submit while becoming harder for the agency to interpret as independent,
representative public judgment.

GAO's Opportunity Zone review adds a place-and-capital layer: more than $108
billion in fund assets and completed projects can coexist with weak tracking of
jobs, housing, distribution, and resident outcomes. Investment can follow
infrastructure and local readiness without proving that it created durable
security for the people already living there.

**Why it matters:** people who cannot become legible to the system may vanish
from appeal-success or participation statistics.

**Boundary:** low participation does not show whether people were unwilling,
unable, unsafe, or unaware.

**Next test:** count attempted, abandoned, failed, corrected, and successful
routes—not only completed cases.

## 7. Support is social infrastructure, but someone supplies the slack

**Plain meaning:** families, neighbors, religious groups, public programs,
employers, platforms, hospitals, and alliances keep systems functioning.

**Seen across:** family help, eldercare, childcare, political homes, charitable
food, AI companionship, SNAP, military families, civil-military readiness, and Census
measures of expense difficulty, loneliness, and perceived support, plus Pew's
family-caregiving survey and Pew's religious-institution study.

Pew's Black family-networks survey adds that the support map itself can include
chosen and extended ties beyond the nuclear household; closeness is a potential
resource, not proof of delivered help or material capacity.

Pew's neighborhood survey adds a local-reciprocity split: people report more
willingness to help neighbors than confidence that neighbors would help them.
Local trust can therefore be a real cultural resource without being a reliable
shared safety net. The evidence is a cross-sectional survey of stated trust and
expectations, not a record of aid delivered or hardship prevented.

CDC's Community & Connection indicators add a measurement split inside the
same social-support question: separate youth and adult surveys report needed
social/emotional support, while a later adult measure reports health-related
difficulty participating in social activities. Perceived support and practical
participation are not the same outcome, and neither establishes adequacy,
reciprocity, loneliness, belonging, delivered help, or recovery. The separate
years, survey systems, and undisclosed summary-page denominators prevent an age
trend or same-person inference.

The [peer self-harm-death and surviving-youth record](records/us-nber-peer-self-harm-death-surviving-youth-outcomes-2026.json)
adds a network-shock version. In linked South Carolina administrative data,
exposure to a peer's self-harm death was associated with a nearly 50% higher
probability of a self-harm diagnosis and increases in both the incidence and
frequency of mental-health visits; assault and transportation deaths showed no
comparable rise in self-harm. The response route was uneven: reported care-use
and criminal-behavior effects concentrated among white boys, while students
without a prior diagnosis increased felony offending rather than care-seeking.
Administrative diagnosis, visits, and offending are not measures of informal
support, treatment quality, completed suicide, or recovery, and this study
does not establish a national rate or a single school, family, media, or peer
mechanism.

The Census/AmeriCorps civic-engagement supplement adds a reach-versus-time
split: formal volunteering and informal neighbor help rose after the pandemic
low, while average hours per formal volunteer fell. Civic contact can therefore
spread across more people without restoring the sustained time, leadership, or
organizational memory needed for durable collective capacity.

The [Pew grandparenthood and intergenerational-connection record](records/us-pew-grandparenthood-intergenerational-connection-2024-2025.json)
adds separate intergenerational clocks. In 2024, 66% of adults age 65 and over
were grandparents; in the 2022--23 caregiving window, 21% of responding
grandparents reported at least 100 hours of grandchild or great-grandchild care
over two years. Separate 2024--25 measures report that 8% of children lived
with a grandparent as household head, 70% of grandparents age 65 and over had
at least monthly contact, and 36% of adults with a living grandparent described
the relationship as extremely or very close. These are different populations,
recall windows, and meanings: status, contact, closeness, co-residence, and
substantial caregiving can coexist without establishing support quality, burden,
security, or a causal family effect.

The BLS leave module adds a workplace version: formal leave access can coexist
with unmet need because workload, job risk, and lost income make the route hard
to use. Access, request, approval, actual time, care result, and later job or
household security remain separate.

The Census child-care and BLS eldercare records make the time-conversion
mechanism visible at two different life stages. Among households with children
ages 0–13, the Census reports care problems that prevented more work and,
among affected households, converted lost work time and multiple adaptations
such as leave, reduced hours, non-search, and job quitting; those measures have
different denominators and are not one household trajectory. Separately, BLS
estimates that 38.2 million people age 15+ provided eldercare in 2023–24, and
its time diary describes care-day time among providers rather than a causal
work loss. Alongside the leave module, these sources support a cautious
subtheme: care capacity functions as a time buffer, but the practical buffer is
distributed through employer terms, income, household resources, and available
care arrangements. They do not establish a common national care-shortage rate,
the quality or safety of care, or the later earnings and wellbeing of the same
families.

The [Head Start full-day and maternal-work record](records/us-nber-head-start-full-day-maternal-work-2026.json)
adds a targeted policy conversion. The 2016 federal initiative increased
full-day Head Start enrollment by 19% in affected areas; among single mothers,
the study reports 2.5% higher employment, 3.2% higher full-time employment,
2.9% higher hours, and 7% higher earnings. That is evidence that more formal
care time can become paid-work capacity in the targeted setting, not evidence
that every care constraint is an hours problem. The initiative was not a random
national expansion and does not establish care quality, child wellbeing,
maternal wellbeing, schedule control, affordability, or equal access.

The Census 2023 SIPP child-wellbeing report adds a cultural-opportunity
version of the same distinction. Parent-child reading and dinner routines can
remain comparatively steady while access to sports, lessons, and clubs differs
sharply by family poverty status. Routine is a meaningful time-and-relationship
surface, but it is not a substitute for organized opportunity, nor does the
descriptive gap establish whether fees, transport, safety, supply, preference,
or adult schedule control produced it. The record does not follow the same
child into belonging, learning, confidence, or later outcomes.

The [Communities In Schools personalized-support record](records/us-nber-communities-in-schools-personalized-supports-long-run-2026.json)
adds a high-poverty-school intervention route. Its staggered-rollout study
reports higher test scores for struggling students, higher high-school
completion, and higher adult earnings. The paper attributes about half of the
graduation effect to test-score improvement and the remainder to attendance and
behavior progress, consistent with coordinators tailoring academic, behavioral,
mental-health, and basic-needs supports to different student needs. These are
bundled program effects in participating high-poverty schools, not evidence
that every reached student received the same service or that the program
separately caused trust, safety, health, family wellbeing, or community
recovery.

BLS's 2025 time-use release adds an everyday cultural distinction: socializing,
religious activity, volunteering, civic obligations, and informal help have
different reach and time patterns. Recorded time does not by itself show
belonging, organizational capacity, or political action.

GAO's unpaid-household-work estimate adds the hidden-economy scale: almost four
hours a day of domestic work and care, valued at least at $5.6–$6.0 trillion in
2024. The estimate makes social capacity visible without proving that the work
was equally shared, freely chosen, emotionally sustainable, or replaceable by
paid services. See the [GAO unpaid-work record](records/us-gao-unpaid-household-work-2026.json).

BEA's outdoor-recreation account adds a place-and-culture version of support:
recreation can generate jobs, travel, firms, and public investment while the
economic account remains silent about resident access, affordability, local
ownership, displacement, and belonging. See the [outdoor-recreation
record](records/us-bea-outdoor-recreation-economy-2024.json).

BEA's household-production update adds the accounting counterpart: unpaid work
can be a large part of consumption and total output while remaining outside
ordinary GDP. Greater visibility does not reveal choice, fatigue, control,
quality, or household security. See the [household-production
record](records/us-bea-household-production-account-2024.json).

The Federal Reserve small-business credit survey adds a firm-power layer:
credit is not one open market but a route through risk classification, lender
choice, requested amount, and partial or full approval. Approval is an
institutional stage, not proof of funded working capital, survival, jobs, or
community benefit. See the [small-business credit record](records/us-fed-small-business-credit-route-2023.json).

The [local economic participation synthesis](US-LOCAL-ECONOMIC-PARTICIPATION-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
now consolidates this firm route with disaster contracting, Opportunity Zone
investment, and outdoor-recreation production. Across them, money entering a
place is not the same as local receipt, workable terms, control, durability, or
shared security.

The [global-aging foreign-policy record](records/us-gao-global-aging-foreign-policy-implications-2026.json)
adds a slow social-demographic driver: aging can shift care demand, labor
supply, migration, health spending, and defense priorities before it appears as
a visible geopolitical event. The source maps those channels but does not show
country-level causation or changed alliance behavior. Demography belongs in the
geopolitical context without being treated as a forecast of strategic power.

The DOD international-arrangements record adds a geopolitical coordination
stage: partner agreements and associated dollars can exist while staffing,
review, cost-sharing communication, and challenge management slow the route to
joint capability. Arrangement count is not delivery, partner use, deterrence,
or legitimacy. See the [DOD arrangements record](records/us-gao-international-defense-arrangements-2021-2025.json).

The NEA arts/HPS layer adds a cultural-infrastructure distinction: arts access
and participation can be associated with reported connection while remaining
unequally reachable by income, education, race, place, time, and transport.
Attendance is not a causal measure of belonging or political action.

The NCES/BJS school-safety layer adds a youth institutional version: lower
aggregate victimization can coexist with fear and avoidance of classes or
activities. Safety, participation, support, learning, belonging, and later
trust remain separate outcomes.

GAO's school-facility survey adds a physical-infrastructure version: 19% of
districts canceled school for facility problems in 2024–25, affecting an
estimated 2 million students and 3.5 million learning days. A closure can be a
protective response, so the estimate does not equal learning loss; it shows
that building condition can interrupt the public route before recovery, family
burden, or later outcomes are known. See the [school-facility record](records/us-gao-school-facility-disruption-2026.json).

The FCC broadband layer adds a digital-infrastructure version: mapped
availability, subscription, provider choice, price, device, reliability, and
meaningful use are separate stages. A service area is not the same as usable
household access.

The GAO “dig once” review adds the coordination stage behind that access route:
46 of 52 state contacts reported a broadband coordinator and 46 reported broad
interagency coordination, but staff, IT, provider-participation, and outcome
visibility remained uneven. A shared highway right-of-way can be a more
efficient route without proving that construction, activation, affordability,
reliability, or meaningful use followed. See the [dig-once finding](records/us-gao-broadband-dig-once-highway-coordination-2026.json).

**Why it matters:** support can protect people while shifting money, care,
transport, privacy, emotional labor, staffing, or risk onto someone else.
Being reachable is not the same as being supported: material pressure can
coexist with frequent contact but much lower perceived social and emotional
backing. Family care can also create valued relationships while shifting
unequal health, work, money, and social costs onto caregivers.

**Boundary:** perceived support or willingness to help is not delivered,
adequate, repeatable protection.

**Next test:** identify the helper, effort, resource supplied, recipient result,
and whether the support can continue without exhausting the helper.

## 8. Digital systems create conditional agency

**Plain meaning:** platforms and AI can expand information, work, connection,
companionship, and civic access while keeping the rules of error, privacy,
attention, and exit elsewhere.

**Seen across:** Pew AI and platform surveys, topic-specific information
sources, privacy/removal records, Walmart's reported privacy-request stages,
AI companionship, cancellation, digital consumer remedies, task-level AI
choice, comparative platform-safety controls, financial guidance, and teen
information/conversation use.

AP-NORC's 2026 facts survey adds a verification-cost layer: trust in federal
information fell across major topics while many adults found candidate claims
and encountered information hard to check. AI may make complex material easier
to understand without making it easier to verify. More information is not
automatically more usable information.

Google's image-removal route adds a sharper example: request submission and
status visibility can improve without showing completed removal, protection
from copies, or safety after the request.

California's DROP system adds centralized reach: one hashed request can touch
many brokers and expose partial statuses. CalPrivacy reports more than 325,000
consumer sign-ups/submitted requests and over 600 active brokers in the route,
but does not publish a matched consumer-broker denominator, verified deletion,
or persistent deletion outcome.

Walmart's California metrics make the intervening administrative stages more
concrete: its 2025 company report separately counts received, completed, and
denied access, deletion, and sale-opt-out requests, with distinct median
completion times. That is evidence of a request-processing pathway, not proof
that a completed deletion removed all relevant data or prevented later
recollection. The FTC's nine-company social-media/video-streaming review is a
system-level counterweight: it reports that some reviewed companies did not
delete all user data after deletion requests, alongside broad sharing and the
potential for indefinite retention. These source units cannot be pooled into a
single completion or failure rate, but together support a recurring subtheme:
privacy control is a request route and status system before it is evidence of
durable data control.

The GM/OnStar order adds the service-tradeoff version: a formal deletion or
location-control right may exist while model eligibility, verification, safety
exceptions, and loss of connected functions shape who can use it.

The adult companion survey adds the social-meaning version: reported support,
disclosure, emotional connection, and anticipated loss can appear before any
human-substitution, wellbeing, dependence, or exit outcome is measured.

The [Pew teen AI-purpose record](records/us-pew-teens-ai-purpose-support-2026.json)
adds an age-specific purpose split. In its weighted survey of 1,458 U.S. teens
ages 13–17, 57% reported chatbot use for information searches, 54% for
schoolwork help, and 47% for fun or entertainment, compared with 16% for
casual conversation and 12% for emotional support or advice. These are
non-exclusive self-reported purposes, not session time or a measure of whether
human help was displaced. The smaller relational shares show an emerging
support surface, not evidence of emotional dependence, clinical adequacy,
privacy loss, manipulation, or practical exit.

The [Pew teen social-media and AI-use record](records/us-pew-teens-social-ai-2025.json)
adds a reach-and-frequency layer from the same-size 2025 teen survey: 64% of
teens reported ever using chatbots and 28% reported daily use, while 36%
reported using at least one of five measured social platforms almost
constantly. Daily chatbot use was 35% among Black teens, 33% among Hispanic
teens, and 22% among White teens; chatbot use was also higher among ages 15--17
than ages 13--14 (68% versus 57%). Those self-reported differences identify
uneven exposure and use, not differences in capability, school access, family
rules, learning, privacy loss, wellbeing, or dependence. “Almost constantly” is
a response category, not logged attention time.

The Take It Down Act adds a legal-remedy version: a 48-hour removal rule,
duplicate-copy scope, traceability, and federal escalation can strengthen
contestability without proving removal, safety, or durable exit.

Pew's smartphone survey adds a self-regulation version: people can describe a
device as useful for information and connection while also saying they use it
too much and struggling to cut back. This makes digital agency a practical
tradeoff, not just a question of preference or awareness.

The social-exit study adds a coordination version: leaving can improve short-
run wellbeing while the presence of other users makes individual exit costly.
Assigned deactivation, stated collective preference, actual deletion, social
replacement, and durable non-use remain separate stages.

The FTC social-scam record adds a material-risk version: profiles, ads, groups,
and social connections can also make financial deception look credible. Reported
loss, non-reporting, complaint, recovery, platform correction, and later trust
are separate stages.

GAO's federal AI privacy review adds an institutional version: expert-identified
privacy risks can outpace the shared guidance meant to govern them. The 2-of-10
coverage result is not a harm rate, but it makes the gap between AI capability,
public understanding, consent, and contestability visible.

GAO's homebuying proptech review adds a place-and-market version: digital search,
valuation, underwriting, and closing can save time while model opacity, data
collection, historical bias, and limited product-specific oversight affect who
can understand or challenge a housing route. Faster access is not automatically
fairer or more secure access.

The [rental-proptech screening, pricing, and privacy record](records/us-gao-rental-proptech-screening-pricing-privacy-2025.json)
adds a tenant-side version. Screening, rent-setting, advertising, and facial
recognition can move decisions about access, price, and surveillance into
systems a renter may not be able to inspect or correct. GAO identifies risks
from inaccurate criminal, eviction, or credit records; algorithmic price
setting; targeted advertising; and biometric misidentification or nonconsensual
data use. All 10 interviewed public housing agencies using or considering
facial recognition said they would benefit from additional federal direction;
HUD's guidance was high-level and its written guidance was still projected for
December 2026. Convenience and safety can be real while bargaining power,
privacy, and the ability to remain housed become more conditional. This is not
a national rate of denial, discrimination, rent increase, privacy loss, or
housing instability.

The World Bank's adopt-adapt-advance framework adds the infrastructure and
institutional complements behind those forms of conditional agency.

**Why it matters:** the political conflict is not simply technology versus no
technology; it is who sets the terms and whether a real alternative exists.
Using several systems for different tasks can be a coping strategy without
amounting to genuine exit from a dominant default. Concern can also remain
inactive when users lack knowledge, time, money, or a practical substitute.

**Boundary:** use, concern, or reported completion does not prove dependence,
privacy loss, persuasion, harm, or durable exit. A company's completed privacy
request is not by itself proof that all relevant data was removed or stayed
removed; program sign-ups, broker status labels, and company practice reviews
are different units and should not be treated as a shared person-level rate.

**Next test:** follow one user or account through request type, identity or
authorization check, exemption or match result, completion, residual-data scan
or recollection, appeal, and later trust, continued use, switching, or exit.

## 9. Place turns formal options into different lived bargains

**Plain meaning:** the same program, job, insurance route, energy project, or
security policy can be reachable in one place and costly or impossible in
another.

**Seen across:** vouchers, wildfire insurance, transit measurement and signal
priority, county capacity and mobility context, data centers, energy, grid
operations, local services, migration, and housing.

The [immigration local-demand and place-capacity record](records/us-nber-immigration-local-demand-supply-place-1980-2024.json)
adds a place-demand counterpoint. In 1980--2000 local-market evidence, each
immigrant was estimated to create about 1.2 local jobs for local workers, 62%
of them in non-traded services. A separate 2021--24 national
heterogeneous-agent model finds supply-side and demand-side immigration effects
roughly cancel for inflation. These are different periods, geographies, and
methods, not a shared current place estimate. Neither layer establishes local
housing, school, healthcare, transit, business survival, public capacity, or
belonging outcomes in a particular place.

Census's wealth-and-income mobility study adds an asset version of place
opportunity. The same parent-child population can show income mobility while
showing much lower mobility in homeownership and housing wealth; Great Recession
associations are also harsher for poorer families' later wealth. A place can
therefore look open by earnings while inheritance, ownership, debt, and housing
prices preserve unequal room. County patterns do not identify which local
institution caused the gap, and gross wealth is not spendable security. See the
[wealth-income mobility finding](projects/us-wealth-income-mobility-geography/findings/us-census-wealth-income-mobility-geography-great-recession-1978-1986-001.md).

Environmental-policy beliefs add a legitimacy mechanism: people can support a
strong environmental goal while rejecting a tool they think will raise their
bill. Information can change support, but actual incidence, fairness, and
environmental results still have to be measured.

The Virginia data-center proposal adds a household-cost bargain: a public
credit and a modeled reduction in projected charges can make burden-sharing
visible before any customer bill or receipt shows that the bargain worked.

EPA and CDC's drinking-water records add a basic-service version of the place
problem: community-system coverage is broad, while private-well users carry
more testing and maintenance responsibility and public systems face a large
lead-service-line replacement need. Formal coverage is not the same as equal
monitoring, repair capacity, or safe household service.

GAO's rural-water funding review adds the pre-construction access stage:
twenty-eight USDA and EPA programs can offer assistance, but different
applications, deadlines, matching requirements, limited data, and uneven local
capacity can keep an unserved community from reaching a project. A funding
menu is therefore not yet a usable route to water, health protection, or place
security. See the [rural-water funding record](records/us-gao-rural-water-funding-coordination-unserved-communities-2026.json).

The NBER *Water Works* study supplies a later measured step: pollution above
current standards fell by about half from 2003 to 2019, and Safe Drinking Water
Act loans are estimated to reduce pollution and older-American mortality. The
study links water-system evidence to a health result, but not to each
household's tap exposure, private cost, trust, or political response.

Georgia's large-load docket adds a firm-and-regulator stage: cost-protection
rules reached actual contract filings and review, while the household bargain
remains unmeasured. A contract can be real without proving payment, lower
residential bills, reliability, or community acceptance.

Puerto Rico's grid recovery adds a stronger operating example: federal money
can be obligated while disbursement, project completion, line maintenance, and
stable electricity remain incomplete. A household-facing solar promise can
also be redirected to other grid needs. Infrastructure capacity, reliable
service, and household benefit therefore need separate measures.

California wildfire insurance adds a housing-recovery version: claim payments,
a sampled enforcement investigation, and new smoke-damage standards can all be
visible before a property is safely repaired or a household can remain. Formal
recovery capacity and lived housing security therefore run on different clocks.

The [Census wildfire damage and community-composition record](records/us-census-wildfire-damage-income-community-composition-2026.json)
adds the lived post-destruction layer. Across more than 100 fires, occupants of
destroyed homes had lower income for three years, with cumulative losses equal
to 26% of pre-fire annual income. Four years after a fire, half of destroyed
addresses remained unoccupied; when addresses were reoccupied, their residents
had substantially higher incomes than before. Residents without direct property
damage had no detected income or employment effect in this study. Property
destruction, income loss, occupancy, and a changing community composition are
therefore distinct recovery clocks—not proof of insurance or aid adequacy,
health, debt, displacement, or why a household did or did not return.

The [Census/NBER eviction and child-outcomes record](records/us-census-nber-eviction-children-housing-school-outcomes-2025.json)
adds a later lived endpoint. In linked Chicago and New York records using
judge-assignment variation, an eviction order increased address changes by 13
percentage points in the case year—about double the nonevicted mean—and raised
recorded homelessness in both cities. It also increased absences and school
changes and reduced high-school graduation, while having little measured impact
on elementary and middle-school test scores. This makes housing a family and
education system rather than a rent-payment endpoint, while preserving the
limits of a two-city design that identifies cases marginal to judge leniency,
not a national effect or every informal displacement.

The [emergency-rental-assistance payment and stability record](records/us-nber-emergency-rental-assistance-rent-payment-stability-health-2024.json)
adds the intervention counterpoint. Across five lottery-evaluated programs in
four cities, aid increased rent payment and reduced eviction worry, with
suggestive self-reported health improvements, but did not produce a large
average change in housing stability or financial distress. Pandemic eviction
moratoria, expanded safety-net programs, and softer rental markets may have
weakened the measured rent-to-displacement connection. Relief can be real at
one stage while durability remains unresolved.

GAO's Native housing testimony adds a cultural and administrative-legibility
boundary: 30 federal programs can support Native housing while funding lags
construction and maintenance needs, and “doubling up” may not fit federal
homelessness definitions. Family sharing can be both support and instability;
the category alone does not tell us which. See the [Native housing record](records/us-gao-native-housing-homelessness-2026.json).

The New York tenant-counsel study adds a process intervention: lawyers changed
possession judgments, money judgments, and eviction warrants, while later rent,
moves, and housing security were not observed. This separates a more
contestable court process from a solved housing problem. Cash assistance and
legal representation are different levers, and both can improve an intermediate
stage without proving durable protection.

The [rent-guarantee insurance and repayment record](records/us-rent-guarantee-insurance-protection-repayment-2026.json)
adds a private-protection version. Its NBER quantitative model finds that a
specified rent-guarantee design can lower deposits and modeled homelessness,
but also predicts that unrestricted access is not financially viable: private
insurers must target better-off renters while public insurance targets those at
greater homelessness risk. Separately, the provider terms say a renter may buy
an upfront, non-refundable lease guarantee to qualify for a home; the landlord
is the beneficiary, and the renter remains liable to reimburse the provider if
it pays a covered landlord claim. Formal lease access, modeled welfare,
landlord protection, premium payment, claim payment, renter repayment, and
housing stability are separate stages. Neither the model nor provider documents
show approval rates, claims, repayment success, credit consequences, eviction,
or actual tenant outcomes.

Census's remote-work and residential-sorting paper adds a place-choice version:
when work no longer anchors a household near an office, some households can
sort toward space, ownership, public schools, and lower income-tax exposure.
The same movement can increase neighborhood turnover and shift the local mix of
taxpayers and public-service demand. A move is therefore evidence of changed
optionality and place sorting, not automatic evidence of improved life,
displacement, belonging, or durable security. See the [remote-work finding](projects/us-remote-work-residential-sorting/findings/us-census-remote-work-residential-sorting-2005-2023-001.md).

Payment outages add a digital-infrastructure version: a system can be widely
available in ordinary time while cash becomes the usable backup during a crisis.
More completed transactions are meaningful, but they are not the same as an
adequate essential purchase or later household recovery.

**Why it matters:** place distributes travel, waiting, cost, safety, belonging,
and the ability to stay or move.

**Boundary:** place comparisons do not automatically explain why places differ.

**Next test:** connect one named place or project to actual service, household
incidence, local burden, acceptance, and mobility or exit.

## 10. Belonging can become political capacity

**Plain meaning:** identity and community can provide information, transport,
food, compensation, safety, confidence, and a place from which to act.

**Seen across:** CIRCLE political homes, family and religious networks, racial
and migrant identity, working-class identity, civic groups, Pew's public-life
engagement groups, press-government legitimacy, press-freedom limits, CIRCLE's youth financial-strain and
political-engagement survey, and Gallup's anniversary meaning survey.

The CDC BRFSS analysis adds a direct unequal-connection layer: in a 26-state
sample, 32.1% reported loneliness and 24.1% lacked needed social and emotional
support, with higher rates among younger, low-income, bisexual, and transgender
adults. The mental-health measures were associated with disconnection, but the
cross-sectional design does not establish cause, recovery, belonging, or civic
action.

**Why it matters:** hardship does not automatically produce action; a credible
social route and belief that action can matter help convert pressure into
participation. Financial strain can reduce efficacy while still producing
conditional willingness to act if institutions appear capable of responding.
Trust and action can also diverge: people may act while distrusting an
institution, and a materially pressured group may be more politically active
than a less-pressured comparison group. National belonging may also be built
through family, local community, and personal stories rather than formal
ceremony alone.

Gallup's 2026 institution comparison adds that low confidence is selective,
not one flat national attitude: small business is rated far more favorably than
large technology companies, and party differences are especially large for the
presidency. People can therefore depend on or use an institution while judging
it poorly. Confidence is a political and cultural judgment, not proof of
performance or withdrawal.

The [Gallup social-courage and risk record](records/us-gallup-courage-social-risk-2026.json)
adds a voice-cost layer. In a May 2026 survey, 67% said courage in American
society was weaker than 20 years earlier, 79% said courage from elected leaders
was very important to the nation's future, and 44% were concerned about the
personal costs of taking a principled stand. These are current judgments about
social norms, importance, and perceived risk—not observed speech, workplace
voice, protest, retaliation, political participation, or a measured historical
decline in courageous action.

The NBER health-care trust study adds a domain-specific behavioral boundary:
trust in doctors, nurses, hospitals, insurers, and public-health entities is
associated with reported and anticipated care seeking and avoidance, but the
survey/model design cannot distinguish a trust effect from cost, time,
availability, health need, prior care quality, or other access conditions.
Institutional meaning can shape a care route without substituting for a
completed appointment, affordable treatment, recovery, or political action.

Pew's health-information and health-influencer records locate an earlier
authority problem: adults use providers, peers with similar experience, social
media, AI, influencers, and podcasts, while many report difficulty judging
accuracy or resolving conflicts. Influencer bios can combine professional,
coaching, and commercial identities. Those separate source environments can
shape who seems credible, but neither study identifies a specific claim,
verification, care choice, purchase, treatment, or health outcome.

Pew's topic-diversity survey extends the authority point across health,
elections, immigration, the economy, Social Security, foreign policy, and
technology regulation: adults name different primary routes across news,
government, social media, personal networks, school, other, or no information.
Source environment is therefore part of the topic-specific route into meaning,
but it is not exposure verification, accuracy, trust, persuasion, or action.

The Trust for Civic Life rural survey adds a level distinction: national
political distance can coexist with local agency, belonging, and trust in
embedded organizations. Concrete problem-solving and meaningful responsibility
appear more relevant to reported agency than volunteer hours alone. Because the
sample came through Civic Hub networks, this is a selected local-capacity
counterexample, not a general rural or causal estimate.

AP-NORC's America 250 poll adds a principle-versus-threat layer: speech,
voting, and religious freedom can be widely valued while many people also see
those rights as endangered. The American Dream and national symbols then sort
people by party, age, and experience. Shared ideals do not automatically create
shared judgments about current reality or political action.

Pew's political typology adds a coalition layer: people who share a party
label can differ on religion, abortion, immigration, the safety net, political
attention, and the role of U.S. power. A cross-pressured coalition is not a
single political meaning or action pathway, and a low-attention group is not
simply a moderate center.

Pew's democracy measure adds a performance judgment without a settled action
path: many people see democracy as a diminished example and are dissatisfied
with how it works, but dissatisfaction can mean defense, reform, participation,
or withdrawal. The survey does not tell us which route any respondent takes.

Pew's reform-feasibility analysis adds a related split: support for major change
can coexist with low confidence that change is achievable. This creates a
"pessimistic reformer" position—demand without a trusted or believable route to
delivery. It is an attitude pattern, not evidence that people are inactive.

Pew's electorate-composition analysis adds a formal-status version: a growing
or more diverse eligible electorate changes the field of political meaning,
but eligibility is not registration, turnout, representation, or policy power.
Demographic presence and usable political voice therefore move on different
clocks.

The employer-power study adds a material route into political meaning: a
dominant local employer can shape expectations about wages and jobs, and the
study reports higher Republican turnout where electoral monopsony power is
greater. This keeps economic dependence, reported influence, and actual voting
separate; it does not turn every employer relationship into coercion.

**Boundary:** belonging or association is not proof of turnout, policy
influence, or causal political mobilization. A shared diagnosis of institutional
conflict is not shared agreement about blame, power, or acceptable criticism.

**Next test:** connect a dated encounter or support setting to efficacy,
validated action, institutional response, and later trust.

## 11. State power is built from social capacity

**Plain meaning:** equipment, money, and formal authority work only when people,
families, care systems, hospitals, local partners, maintenance, and public
legitimacy sustain them.

The [GAO international-commitment reporting record](records/us-gao-international-commitment-reporting-transparency-2023-2025.json)
adds an oversight-and-legitimacy stage before external commitments can be
judged. Of 311 agreements and qualifying nonbinding instruments reported from
October 2023 through March 2025, 90 were late at one or both reporting points,
averaging 2.3 reporting cycles late. GAO estimated that 86% of items subject to
publication requirements had text and legal-authority information published,
but only 53% met the 120-day publication deadline (an estimated 78% for
instruments and 38% for agreements), and records were dispersed across
webpages. Late or hard-to-find reporting does not establish concealment, bad
faith, invalidity, or failed diplomacy; it does establish that formal
commitment, congressional visibility, public discoverability, implementation,
and strategic effect are different stages.

The [GAO federal performance-management record](records/us-gao-federal-performance-management-strategic-review-2025-2026.json)
adds a learning-loop boundary. In GAO's selected four-agency review, none fully
addressed the new 2025 statutory strategic-review requirements; OMB's August
2025 guidance addressed 10 of 15 requirements, and as of June 2026 it had not
identified the new President's Management Agenda cross-agency priority goals or
released related implementation detail. DHS, Treasury, and GSA had 2026 review
plans, while State did not confirm one. A performance framework and planned
review are thus not evidence that leaders compared results, changed a decision,
improved a program, or delivered a public benefit. Conversely, incomplete
formal compliance in four selected agencies is not proof that all federal
programs failed to learn or perform.

Census's BLS–Census establishment comparison adds a measurement-to-capacity
version. One official frame sees many more small and decentralized workplaces,
especially in home care and disability services; another groups more activity
into fewer, larger firms. The count can therefore change which work appears
visible, concentrated, or missing, and can change policy-model predictions. It
does not by itself establish actual market power, care quality, worker security,
or local economic health. See the [business-count finding](projects/us-business-establishment-measurement/findings/us-census-bls-establishment-frame-discrepancy-2004-2023-001.md).

The [bankruptcy-venue finding](records/us-census-bankruptcy-forum-shopping-delaware-employment-1990-2021.json)
adds a legal-institution-to-firm-survival layer. A specialized venue can
preserve establishments, creditor recovery, and post-bankruptcy employment for
firms whose filing choice responds to proximity, while worker quality,
consumer effects, and equal stakeholder benefit remain open. Firm survival is
therefore not only a balance-sheet or management outcome.

The [reusable-software finding](records/us-census-nonrival-custom-software-firm-scope-concentration-2026.json)
adds the technology beneath firm scope: one custom-software investment can be
used across many establishments, making larger organizational reach more
attractive in the model. Scope and concentration may therefore rise through
reusable digital capital, but prices, wages, worker voice, service quality, and
consumer switching remain unmeasured.

The [environmental lender-liability finding](records/us-census-environmental-lender-liability-pollution-abatement-1996-2026.json)
adds a finance-and-public-burden layer: changing who bears cleanup risk can
change loan terms, firm abatement investment, and pollution. This is a social
incidence pathway, not yet a household exposure, health, cleanup, or political
trust result.

The [housing-wealth labor finding](records/us-census-housing-wealth-womens-labor-supply-childcare-2026.json)
adds a gendered household-time mechanism: housing wealth is associated with
lower women’s labor-force participation, most strongly among mothers of young
children and women citing child-related reasons. The result makes childcare,
unpaid labor, job alternatives, and the difference between chosen and constrained
nonparticipation explicit rather than treating wealth as a simple buffer.

The [tariff-incidence finding](records/us-census-tariff-quantity-discounts-incomplete-pass-through-2018-2019.json)
adds a geopolitical price-incidence layer: transaction size and lost quantity
discounts can make import prices look fully tariff-passed-through even when
foreign markups absorb part of the cost. Import price, household burden,
domestic production, and political judgment must therefore remain separate.

The [NBER tariff pass-through record](records/us-nber-tariff-pass-through-consumer-prices-2025.json)
adds a delayed domestic-price layer: about 26% of the tariff increase passed
through to consumer prices in the study. Sixty-four percent of that estimated
price effect was direct through foreign varieties, while 36% arose indirectly
through imported inputs and higher domestic markups over roughly nine to twelve
months. A policy can therefore change competition and supply-chain costs before
the household sees the later shelf price.

**Boundary:** the estimate does not follow one household's full basket, wages,
debt, product quantity, package size, quality, store choice, or political
response. A later price rise can also reflect inventory, sourcing, competition,
or another shock.

**Next test:** join tariff exposure to shipment and inventory dates, supplier
contracts, margins, product size, store, purchase, wages, jobs, debt, savings,
policy knowledge, and household response.

The [status-mismatch finding](records/us-census-status-inconsistency-neighborhood-mobility-acs-2026.json)
adds a cultural-place mechanism: a household’s income rank relative to its
neighborhood is associated with whether it moves nearby or farther away. The
same place can therefore carry different meanings for differently positioned
households, while move reason, belonging, discrimination, and later security
remain open.

**Seen across:** Germany/Lithuania staffing, NATO medical readiness, KFOR,
defense procurement, U.S.-linked co-production and financing, state fiscal
reserves, grids, public infrastructure, Pew's cross-national survey of
confidence in the United States and NATO legitimacy, and JEF's cross-border
operating rehearsals, plus SIPRI's military-spending estimates.

The Germany-Lithuania record adds the public-permission split: defense
spending and European cooperation can have support while U.S. reliability,
mission knowledge, local experience, and approval of future burdens remain
separate or less settled.

NATO's ISR record adds repeated-operation capacity: flight hours, intelligence
products, host locations, and integrated support can be visible before the
decision effect, local burden, public acceptance, or deterrence is known.

Task Force X-Baltic adds a capability-conversion stage: a live test of more
than 70 autonomous air and maritime systems was followed by an eight-Allied
commitment to nationally owned systems that NATO could task and integrate. That
shows cooperation and institutionalization, not persistent coverage, changed
adversary behavior, deterrence, or public acceptance.

The Belgian MQ-9B deployment adds a partner-capability stage: NATO facilities
and Italian host coordination made a national Belgian asset usable in named
operations. That shows enablement and use, but not strategic effect,
replaceability, cost sharing, or local consent.

NATO's 2026 resilience baseline adds the policy stage beneath that operating
capacity: civilian communications, transport, health, food and water, movement,
border systems, and private-sector supply chains are explicitly connected to
military readiness and Host Nation Support. A formal civil-military requirement
can therefore widen the reach of state power without proving that countries
have implemented it, that populations accept the burden, or that essential
services perform better in a crisis. See the [NATO resilience-baseline
record](records/us-nato-resilience-baseline-requirements-2026.json).

The Steadfast Discovery 26 rehearsal adds the next stage: NATO and Denmark
practiced the liaison, logistics, transport, movement, reception, and
sustainment route in named places. Rehearsal makes institutional coordination
more concrete than a written requirement, but it does not prove crisis speed,
civilian continuity, fair burden sharing, local consent, or deterrence. See
the [host-nation rehearsal record](records/us-nato-host-nation-rehearsal-denmark-2026.json).

The named Lithuanian interception adds a live protective-action stage: an
alliance response can be operationally real while attribution, recurring cost,
civilian protection, deterrence, and public acceptance remain open.

The DHS grant audit adds a civilian continuity version: a preparedness or
disaster-protection program can be funded, paused, terminated, deobligated, and
then partly restored before a place receives a stable usable capability. The
administrative action is visible, but the local replacement funding, project
completion, and later protection are not necessarily observed.

GAO's disaster-contracting review adds the local-firm stage: recovery agencies
can have a local-vendor preference while failing to monitor whether the work
reached local businesses. The recorded share in FEMA's three selected disasters
was about 3%, but local capacity, nonlocal necessity, contract performance, and
local economic recovery are not the same measure. See the [local-vendor record](records/us-gao-disaster-local-vendor-use-2026.json).

The DHS contract review adds a fiscal-legibility version: a termination and a
large potential-savings number can be visible while the same public need is
funded through another contract. Reported reduction in one purchasing channel
is not the same as realized savings, service change, or public benefit.

The federal telecommunications transition adds an operating-continuity
version: a replacement contract and an old-contract extension can keep the
route formally open while delay, price exposure, and actual disruption remain
uncertain. A functioning replacement must be measured in service, not only in
procurement status.

The [foreign-sourced telecommunications procurement record](records/us-gao-foreign-sourced-telecom-procurement-information-2026.json)
adds a national-security supply-chain version: a prohibition and screening tool
can reduce visible obligations while agencies still lack shared affiliate and
origin information. The practical bottleneck is administrative identity, not
only formal policy. Contractor representation and procurement reduction do not
prove equipment-level control, avoided incidents, or geopolitical leverage.

The federal cloud-security review adds an operating-control version: agencies
can use cloud capacity while continuous monitoring, incident recovery, and
enforceable provider metrics are only partly in place. Adoption is not the same
as the ability to see failure, force correction, or recover reliably.

The MARSEC EU 26 exercise adds a cross-border response version: public,
military, emergency, agency, and commercial actors can rehearse a shared
infrastructure-protection chain. Rehearsal makes coordination visible without
proving real-incident success, repair, public consent, or deterrence.

FAA aviation cybersecurity adds a high-stakes monitoring version: an agency can
identify spoofing and jamming risks while real-time detection, formal sharing,
and authentication protections remain incomplete. Threat awareness is not the
same as continuous protection.

The Nature AI-dialogue experiment adds an encounter-to-action version: a
randomized conversation changed stated candidate or ballot preferences, while
verified voting, lasting belief, and factual reliability remained separate
questions. A persuasive effect is an observed choice movement, not yet durable
political power.

The NATO airspace follow-up adds persistence at the public-response level:
repeated incidents can produce consultation, attribution, solidarity,
condemnation, and defensive reinforcement without showing that the external
actor changed behavior or that host communities accepted the burden.

The GAO space-cooperation review adds an organizational version of the same
split: formal cooperation mechanisms can exist while staffing, responsibility,
and contact routes remain too weak or unclear for dependable shared action.
This is an operational coordination finding, not evidence of alliance failure
or strategic defeat.

The DOE transmission program adds a physical-infrastructure version: federal
capacity support can lower a financing barrier while a project is still subject
to withdrawal, termination, permitting, construction, and energization. Planned
megawatts are not yet usable service or proof of who pays and who benefits.

California's DROP system adds a digital-contestability version: a person can
see broker-level statuses and return to challenge or update a request, while
aggregate completion, downstream copies, reappearance, and later trust remain
unknown. Visibility creates leverage, but it is not the same as verified
control.

The Census volunteering study adds a material-place version: civic capacity
depends partly on the economic and organizational conditions that leave people
time and resources to participate. Rural solidarity is not a fixed cultural
trait; a major economic shock can change the place-participation relationship.

The USMCA labor case adds an external-remedy version: a cross-border trade
mechanism can produce workplace reinstatement, payment, union access, and
monitoring, while the worker's lasting bargaining power and the facility's later
behavior remain separate clocks.

The OFAC settlement adds a financial-infrastructure version: U.S.-dollar
payment channels can expose a foreign firm to U.S. enforcement, while a large
penalty and future compliance program still do not prove recurrence prevention,
deterrence, or changed state strategy.

The GAO readiness review adds a sustainment version: military modernization and
operations can be visible while the maintenance, staffing, repair, data, and
quality-of-life conditions that make capability dependable remain weak. The
watercraft example shows why inventory and activity are not the same as
availability at the point of need.

The historical women's-work study adds a political-mobilization version:
household burden can push people into paid work while that work supplies new
contacts, information, and public roles. Economic participation can therefore
be both a cost and a route into collective action, depending on the setting.

**Why it matters:** a capability can be real and operating while consent,
replaceability, deterrence, or changed external behavior remain unproven.
External public confidence is another layer: formal cooperation can continue
while the partner's legitimacy and perceived reliability weaken.

**Boundary:** readiness, exercise activity, or alliance approval is not by
itself strategic leverage.

**Next test:** follow one operating capability to local burden, partner or
public response, sustainment, and an attributed change in another actor's
choice.

## 12. Repeated contact can become political power

**Plain meaning:** who regularly shares space with whom can change access,
conversation, coalition work, and decisions, even when formal rules and stated
identities stay in place.

**Seen across:** the NBER randomized Senate-seating study, workplace voice,
political belonging, civic groups, and alliance coordination records.

The Senate study reports changed voting among close assigned peers and a larger
cross-party effect on final votes. This is a useful mechanism, not a general
claim that contact persuades everyone. It shows that social structure can enter
formal politics through ordinary proximity and timing.

The [historical elite-political-selection record](records/us-nber-elite-political-selection-family-origins-2026.json)
adds the entry-route counterpoint. Among Members of Congress born 1830--1950,
future officeholders were economic outliers from more advantaged families than
matched controls; by 1940, close to 60% of Members aged 18--40 held a college
degree versus under 5% of comparable young men outside Congress. The family-
background gap held or widened across cohorts, and the paper reports no
detectable compositional shift in tests of women's suffrage, the secret ballot,
direct primaries, or direct election of senators. This is historical selection
evidence, not proof about today's representation, constituent treatment,
policy quality, corruption, or ordinary citizens' political action.

**Boundary:** the contact evidence is about Senators in a special institution,
and the selection evidence concerns historical Members of Congress. Neither
measures ordinary voters, private belief change, current representation, public
legitimacy, or later coalition durability.

**Next test:** find another setting with assigned or plausibly exogenous contact
where later trust, cooperation, exclusion, or collective action is measured.

## 13. People can share a goal but disagree about the means

**Plain meaning:** people may want the same outcome, such as safety, while
their experiences, identities, and beliefs lead them to choose different tools.

**Seen across:** firearm safety, health treatment, energy choices, climate
policy, public benefits, and geopolitical protection.

The firearm-safety study adds an especially clear example: owners and
non-owners name safety as a common objective, but information about costs and
non-lethal alternatives moves storage and alternative-tool preferences more
than it moves the core ownership choice.

**Boundary:** shared language about a goal does not prove shared risk, shared
policy, actual behavior, or realized outcome.

**Next test:** compare goal, perceived threat, chosen tool, actual use, and
later outcome in the same bounded setting.

## 14. Community institutions can create trust without equal access

**Plain meaning:** a church, neighborhood, union, or civic group can provide
roles, contact, service, and trust while members still have different time,
education, geography, status, and ability to participate.

**Seen across:** the Pew Religious Landscape Study, volunteering, neighbor
support, family care, civic groups, and worker voice.

Pew's Latter-day Saint analysis reports high volunteering and generalized trust
alongside clear education and regional differences. The average therefore
shows a real community resource but not equal membership experience, low burden,
or a causal effect of affiliation.

The [Gallup institutional-confidence record](records/us-gallup-institutional-confidence-party-race-2025.json)
adds a wider institutional baseline. In June 2025, only three of 18 named
institutions had at least 50% reporting a great deal or quite a lot of
confidence—small business (70%), the military (62%), and science (61%)—while
Congress was at 10%. Across the nine consistently tracked institutions, the
published average was 37% among Republicans, 26% among Democrats, and 25%
among independents. Confidence also differed within specific institutions:
52% of White adults versus 24% of Black adults reported high confidence in the
police. These are descriptive survey positions, not evidence that any
institution performed better or worse, that a group shared one experience, or
that confidence caused service use, political action, or legitimacy.

The [Pew religious-institutions community-bonds record](records/us-pew-religious-institutions-community-bonds-2025.json)
adds a broader public-legitimacy surface. In the 2023–24 Religious Landscape
Study, 80% of U.S. adults said religious organizations help bring people
together and strengthen community bonds, and 78% said they play an important
role helping poor and needy people. Yet 40% reported at least monthly in-person
or online religious participation and 37% personally belonged to a congregation;
the net positive view among religiously unaffiliated adults was 23%. Perceived
community role, participation, membership, received service, belonging, and
acceptance of political influence are therefore separate stages. The
cross-sectional reports do not measure delivered aid, adequacy, or causal trust.

**Boundary:** affiliation is not attendance, service received, belonging,
leadership, or political influence.

**Next test:** measure participation, assigned role, time cost, received help,
exclusion, trust, and later action in the same community over time.

## 15. Progress, security, and meaning move on different clocks

**Plain meaning:** income, employment, enrollment, firm survival, identity,
coverage, or infrastructure can improve while debt, care strain, insecurity,
discrimination, distrust, or future uncertainty remain.

**Seen across:** household finances, health, work, migration, public coverage,
business recovery, firm-size allocation, small-business fee exposure, firm-size
capacity and infrastructure exposure, working-class identity, macro/public financial capacity, infrastructure,
geopolitical legitimacy, and linked Census material/trust transitions.

The Champlain Hudson project adds a realized infrastructure stage: commercial
operation and cross-border delivery can be visible before customer bills,
reliability, host acceptance, or durable leverage are known.

Gallup's repeated workforce study adds that AI risk is interpreted through
manager respect and organizational care: support can change the meaning of
exposure without proving a job or household outcome.

New York City's Local Law 25 adds a governance stage: public measurement of
algorithmic hiring and work effects can be mandated before a study, worker
feedback, or remedy is publicly visible.

The [OECD algorithmic-management record](records/us-oecd-algorithmic-management-2024.json)
adds an employer-reported operating layer. Among U.S. firms with at least 20
employees, managers reported that 90% provided at least one algorithmic-
management tool and 76% provided ten or more; 55% reported a tool that monitors
the content or tone of conversations, calls, or emails. Across the six-country
user sample, nearly two-thirds of managers reported at least one worker-impact
concern—most often unclear accountability for a wrong decision—while nearly
90% reported at least one governance measure and almost two-thirds reported
worker consultation. Tool provision, managerial concern, policy governance,
and worker protection are therefore separate stages. These are manager reports,
not worker exposure, system logs, effective consultation, pay/schedule effects,
health, remedy, or bargaining power.

**Why it matters:** one positive measure cannot stand in for durable security
or a changed cultural judgment. A household condition can persist or improve
without institutional confidence moving with it, and vice versa.

The linked HTOPS comparison makes that split more specific. Baseline expense
difficulty was followed by much larger material-pressure differences, but not
by one common confidence shift: Congress confidence was almost flat between
the groups while several other institutions showed wider gaps. The pattern is
therefore a same-respondent descriptive comparison, not proof that hardship
caused a change in trust.

**Boundary:** the divergence is not a universal decline claim and does not
identify which clock caused another to move.

**Next test:** measure protected and sacrificed outcomes together, then test
whether the change persists and alters trust, action, switching, or exit.

## Recent political-access addition

The historical US political-selection rotation adds a domestic version of the
network pattern. Members of Congress came disproportionately from advantaged
families across long historical cohorts, and four tested Progressive-Era
reforms did not detectably change who reached Congress. Voting access can be
formally broad while family resources, education, networks, money, and time
shape who arrives at the choice stage. The historical result does not establish
current selection, policy quality, or representation.

The Vietnam draft-lottery integration study adds an institutional-selection
counterexample. Immigrant veterans looked more integrated in ordinary
comparisons, but the lottery-based test found little positive causal effect on
citizenship, language, residence, or cross-national marriage, while finding a
college-completion benefit. A shared national institution can therefore supply
a real resource without automatically producing shared identity or belonging;
correlation may reflect who enters before the institution acts. See the
[military-service integration finding](projects/us-military-service-immigrant-integration/findings/us-census-military-service-immigrant-integration-vietnam-1970-2000-001.md).

## The common question

The nonprofit labor-market rotation adds a material layer beneath social
infrastructure. Nonprofits employ a large share of workers but show a lower and
highly uneven earnings profile. Mission, pay, worker retention, service quality,
client access, and community trust therefore move on different clocks; lower
pay does not prove lower social value, and social value does not erase the
material conditions of the work.

The ACS contact-strategy review adds a measurement-and-participation version:
plain language, layout, internet access, messaging, and reminders can improve
response in particular periods, while broader response decline continues.
Institutional participation is therefore shaped by the interface and by wider
conditions of time, privacy, language, digital access, legal obligation, and
trust. A response is not automatically equal voice or useful policy feedback.

## 16. A shared hardship can produce different explanations

**Plain meaning:** people can agree that prices hurt while disagreeing about
who caused the problem, which fix is acceptable, and what cost they are willing
to bear.

The NBER inflation-beliefs survey adds a clean public-meaning layer. Respondents
described inflation as broadly harmful and named several possible causes, but
partisan groups weighted those causes differently. Many also appeared to expect
inflation control without much loss of output or employment, and the tested
information did not easily move those beliefs. This is evidence about surveyed
interpretations and policy preferences, not a complete account of actual price
exposure or economic truth.

The [BIS household monetary-policy-beliefs record](records/us-bis-household-monetary-policy-beliefs-2026.json)
adds a policy-transmission perception layer. In a quota-based 2024 U.S. online
survey of 26,968 adults, a hypothetical one-point federal-funds-rate increase
had a reported overall spending response below 0.1 point and a -0.13-point
durable-spending response. A smaller randomized-information follow-up of 4,461
respondents found a -0.05-point durable-consumption response to a higher-than-
expected rate and a +0.08-point response to a lower-than-expected rate among
financially literate respondents who recognized the Federal Reserve's role.
The study's decomposition emphasizes inflation expectations and finds little
effect on expected income. These are short-horizon survey and treatment effects,
not observed economy-wide spending, contract rates, employment, or political
response.

**Why it matters:** “prices are too high,” “the government caused it,” “firms
are taking advantage,” and “interest rates are the answer” are different
judgments. They should not be collapsed into one measure of economic opinion.
The same household may want relief, distrust the messenger, reject the stated
cause, and oppose the side effect of the proposed remedy. Political conflict can
therefore arise after people agree about the hardship itself.

**Boundary:** the survey does not follow the same household through changing
bills, beliefs, spending, turnout, or voting; a cause belief is not proof of a
cause, and a policy preference is not evidence of policy implementation.

**Next test:** use a short repeated panel that asks separately what changed in
the household's prices, who caused it, which messenger is trusted, what remedy
is preferred, what cost is acceptable, and what action followed.

## 17. Place can transmit both skills and job access

**Plain meaning:** a neighborhood can shape opportunity through the people and
institutions around a child and through which firms and jobs become reachable
later.

The Census-linked neighborhood-and-firm study finds that between-neighborhood
intergenerational earnings relationships are stronger than relationships among
families within the same neighborhood. Its decomposition points to two routes:
person effects, such as skills and social conditions, and establishment
premiums, such as access to better-paying firms. This makes place more than a
housing or school label, while still leaving the causal mechanism open.

The racial pattern is not one-directional. Neighborhoods explain part of the
Black–White earnings gap conditional on parent earnings, but among neighborhoods
with similar average earnings, a higher Black share is associated with higher
adult earnings. That result cautions against treating neighborhood composition
as a simple measure of deficiency; institutions, networks, selection, and local
labor markets may matter.

**Boundary:** the study is observational and modeled. It does not show that
relocation alone creates mobility, or that earnings establish wellbeing,
belonging, health, safety, or political participation.

**Next test:** follow the same protected family through neighborhood, school,
move, network, discrimination, firm access, job quality, housing, health,
belonging, and civic action.

## 18. The same place can carry different status meanings

**Plain meaning:** neighborhood composition does not mean the same thing to
everyone; a person's relative position changes how contact, belonging, and the
decision to stay or move may be experienced.

The Census residential-mobility study finds that White residents without an
advantaged socioeconomic position are generally more likely to move, but that
the association between minority concentration and White departure changes with
relative status. Black and Asian concentration show weaker departure patterns
among status-disadvantaged White residents, while the Hispanic pattern is
stronger. The authors interpret the overall pattern as more consistent with
contact than simple group competition.

**Why it matters:** a move is not automatically a success, a rejection, or a
free choice. A status story can coexist with housing cost, school access, job
location, safety, discrimination, family ties, and the quality of the next
neighborhood. “Who moved?” and “what did moving accomplish?” are separate
questions.

**Boundary:** the ACS model is observational, concerns White residents, and
does not measure motives, contact quality, coercion, destination outcomes,
belonging, or political action. Racial concentration is not a direct measure of
culture or discrimination.

**Next test:** follow households through relative status, contact, housing and
job constraints, move or stay, destination conditions, safety, belonging, and
later civic action.

## 19. A formal opportunity can carry different social costs

**Plain meaning:** the same job opening may not be equally usable in every
place. Familiar networks, expected treatment, safety, family support, and the
cost of leaving can change the decision.

The Census labor-mobility study finds that Black and Hispanic workers respond
less than White workers to changes in job opportunities overall, but respond
more when the opportunity is in a location with a larger own-race population.
This makes social context part of practical opportunity without reducing the
finding to a simple preference. Lower movement can reflect constraint, risk
management, network protection, or information gaps.

**Boundary:** the study is observational. Own-race population is not a direct
measure of belonging, safety, discrimination, employer treatment, or family
support; the result does not establish job quality, earnings, retention, or
later political action.

**Next test:** follow the same worker through an opportunity, information,
network, expected and experienced treatment, moving cost, move or stay, job
quality, family support, belonging, and household security.

## 20. A firm can depend on a person-shaped culture

**Plain meaning:** workers may stay because of relationships, skills, and shared
meaning built around a founder; when that person leaves, the firm can lose more
than a title.

The [founder-departure and startup-retention record](records/us-census-founder-departure-startup-employee-retention-2026.json)
finds that founder departures increase employee turnover. The effect is
stronger in older and larger startups and among workers with longer shared
tenure or the same sex as the founder. This suggests that organizational culture
can become a real retention mechanism and a hidden form of firm dependence.

**Boundary:** the source measures turnover, not whether leaving was voluntary,
harmful, or beneficial. Founder death is not the same as succession, sale,
conflict, or resignation; turnover may reflect grief, uncertainty, financing, or
new leadership. Pay, worker voice, firm survival, household effects, and later
careers remain unmeasured in the abstract.

**Next test:** follow the same worker and firm through founder departure,
replacement leadership, pay, hours, control, voice, job quality, turnover
destination, firm survival, household effects, and later career.

## 21. A national productivity problem can hide different firm problems

**Plain meaning:** when firms appear less efficient, the cause may be pricing,
input mix, technology, financing, demand, or something else—and the burden may
fall on different people.

The Census manufacturing study separates measured misallocation into revenue-
margin dispersion and input-cost-share dispersion. Both contribute similarly to
the apparent rise, while a few industries account for more than half of the
aggregate decline. This makes the national number a map of where to investigate,
not a complete explanation of what workers, customers, or communities felt.

**Boundary:** the measures are model-based and do not directly establish worker
pay, employment, control, consumer prices, local decline, or household welfare.
High dispersion can reflect real technology, product, quality, or input
differences rather than waste.

**Next test:** join plant-level components to investment, jobs, wages, hours,
worker voice, prices, suppliers, entry/exit, and later place and household
outcomes.

The CDC Community & Connection rotation adds a sharper social-infrastructure
distinction: a majority reports receiving needed support, but youth report less
support than adults in the displayed indicators and a separate adult measure
shows health-related difficulty joining social activities. Support is therefore
not the same as usable participation, belonging, or later civic capacity; the
indicators use different years and are not respondent-linked.

Across the concepts, the program's simplest question is:

> Who can turn a formal option into a usable one, who performs the work when it
> fails, who carries the remaining risk, and who gets to change the rules?

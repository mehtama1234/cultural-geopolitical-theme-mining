# Rotation LX — Emerging-market AI adoption depth and local capability

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Lane:** technology / development geography / firm capability / local adaptation / geopolitical dependence  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded adoption-depth distinction plus Tier 2 local-capability-to-shared-value question

## Research question

When firms and governments in emerging economies report AI adoption, does that
mean deep local capability and shared value—or shallow experimentation dependent
on imported tools, foreign customers, multinational affiliates, scarce skills,
energy and cloud infrastructure?

## Conversion chain

```text
AI availability or adoption claim
  -> firm access to data, compute, skills, capital and organizational capability
  -> internal process use or external product/service adaptation
  -> local productivity, jobs, exports, public-service quality and value retention
  -> learning, supplier capability, bargaining power and reduced dependence
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |

Adoption varied sharply by region and sector. | Adoption prevalence, depth categories and firm-age/digital divide | Representative economy-wide adoption, quality, productivity, jobs, local supplier capability and value retention |
| World Bank adoption-depth update | Surveyed emerging-market firms may adopt AI rapidly while sophisticated use remains slower and concentrated; firms with skills and organizational capability are better positioned to move from experimentation to adaptation and deeper integration. | Fast-versus-deep diffusion mechanism | Named firm capability build, local data/language adaptation, worker outcomes and durable productivity |
| World Bank WDR 2026 | Developing countries need not build frontier models; the adopt/adapt/advance framework prioritizes local languages, institutions, data, skills, infrastructure, interoperability and “small AI” that works with constrained connectivity and compute. | Development strategy and complement architecture | Whether local adoption produces inclusive jobs, service quality, sovereignty or reduced supplier dependence |

## Bounded finding

> **AI adoption is not AI capability or local value.** A firm-use percentage,
> AI strategy, imported tool, digital-service export, frontier partnership or
> expected productivity gain can close an access or experimentation stage while
> local data, language, skills, organizational learning, worker outcomes,
> supplier spillovers, public-service quality, value retention and geopolitical
> dependence remain unobserved.

## Geographic and geopolitical interpretation

The adoption-depth gap is a geography of complements. A cloud account can make
AI available while electricity, bandwidth, data governance, language resources,
technical staff, finance and management determine whether it becomes useful.
The same product may be deep integration for a younger digital firm and a thin
chatbot layer for a small analogue business. Regional averages can therefore
hide firm-age, sector, gender, language, urban/rural and multinational/local
differences.

The geopolitical issue is not only whether a country owns a frontier model. It
is whether firms can adapt tools to local institutions and retain learning,
data, IP, revenue and bargaining power. A country can become a large user of
foreign AI while remaining dependent on external compute, models, cloud terms,
customers and standards. Conversely, small AI and interoperable tools may create
more locally useful capability than a prestige frontier project—if skills,
institutions and local accountability are built around them.

## Counterexamples and limits

- The IFC portfolio survey is not a representative sample of all emerging-market
  firms; it may overrepresent companies with finance, growth orientation or
  investment relationships.
- Rapid adoption can coexist with shallow use, and shallow use may still create
  useful incremental value; depth is not a binary success measure.
- The South Asia hiring result is an exposure-based association with plausible
  substitution and demand-displacement mechanisms, not proof of all job losses.
- Frontier-model ownership is not necessary for useful local AI, while imported
  tools are not automatically extractive or unsafe.
- Manager expectations of future productivity and employment are not realized
  outcomes; small AI may improve access without improving wages or power.

## Promotion decision

Promote adoption-versus-depth and adopt/adapt/advance as Tier 1 bounded
distinctions. Keep local-capability, shared-value, job-quality, public-service,
sovereignty and reduced-dependence claims in Tier 2 until named firms or public
services are followed through complements, adaptation, operation, learning,
value retention and user/worker outcomes.

## Next acquisition

Build a matched firm-and-place ledger across a local firm, multinational
affiliate, small analogue firm and public-service use case. Record AI access,
energy/connectivity, model/provider, data and language adaptation, skills,
capital, task change, hiring, productivity, price, IP/data control, revenue,
suppliers, tax, public-service encounter and later capability retention. Include
a rapid adopter with shallow use and a slower adopter that achieves deep local
adaptation.

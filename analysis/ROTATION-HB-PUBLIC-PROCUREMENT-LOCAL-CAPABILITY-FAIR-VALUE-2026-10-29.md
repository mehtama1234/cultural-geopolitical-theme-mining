# Rotation HB — public procurement, local capability and fair public value

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 conversion question; general public-procurement deepening beyond AI procurement, integrity and school-meal packets  
**Research lane:** procurement design, SME/local supplier capability, social value, labour standards, delivery, payment, maintenance and remedy

## Why this rotation exists

Public procurement is often represented by budget size, tender publication,
local-content percentages, SME targets, social-value clauses or contract award.
Those are upstream signals. The public-value question is whether a named buyer
can specify a real need, attract capable and fairly competing suppliers, pay on
time, manage delivery, protect workers and users, maintain the result, and give
excluded bidders or harmed communities a usable remedy. This packet is the
general procurement-to-local-capability corridor; it does not duplicate the
existing AI-procurement, public-integrity or school-meal packets.

## Research question

When governments use procurement to support SMEs, local suppliers, innovation,
social value, labour rights or resilience, under what conditions does the
procurement cycle produce qualified local capability, decent work, reliable and
affordable goods or services, durable maintenance and public legitimacy rather
than nominal local content, incumbent capture, higher cost, fragmented quality,
late payment, subcontracting abuse or an unremedied failed contract?

## Conversion chain

```text
public need / procurement rule / market engagement / tender / award / clause
  -> fair competition, capable supplier, finance, worker standards and contract management
  -> delivery to specification, timely payment, quality assurance and maintenance
  -> usable affordable service or asset, local capability, decent work and inclusion
  -> public value, trust, supplier learning and durable local/geographical resilience
```

## Evidence snapshot

1. OECD describes strategic procurement as a route to innovation, SME support,
   environmental goals and social values, while warning that lowest-price-only
   criteria can obstruct wider outcomes. This establishes procurement's policy
   potential, not delivered public value.
2. OECD's 2025 implementation review reports that 83% of respondents had at
   least one measure to improve SME access; common measures included dividing
   contracts into lots, capacity building and dynamic purchasing systems. Access
   measures are mechanisms, not proof that a supplier won, delivered, grew or
   improved local welfare.
3. OECD's 2026 trade and industrial-policy analysis identifies administrative
   complexity, scale advantages and limited technical capacity as barriers for
   SMEs. It also cautions that local or social criteria can indirectly favour
   domestic actors and affect competition, requiring market analysis and
   proportionality.
4. ILO guidance frames labour clauses as a way to prevent public contracts from
   driving down wages and conditions, and its 2025 human-rights procurement
   publication extends the issue to rights-respecting government purchasing.
   A clause on paper does not establish worker voice, wage receipt, safety or
   remedy in a subcontracted supply chain.
5. Public procurement therefore has a cultural and geographical dimension:
   the same tender can open a market for a regional firm, exclude an informal
   or minority-language supplier through documentation burden, shift value to a
   national incumbent, or create work without durable local capability. The
   evidence must preserve place, supplier tier, payment, delivery, worker and
   user outcomes.

## Named-case refresh

6. Rotterdam's Social Return policy makes a social clause operational rather
   than merely aspirational. Since 2008, contracts above EUR 50,000 have been
   reviewed for opportunities to employ people far from the labour market; the
   OECD reports about EUR 800 million in leveraged contract spending, annual
   welfare savings of EUR 10–25 million and 2,654 sustainable jobs in 2019.
   Employer complaints led to policy changes and more support for people facing
   multiple barriers. This closes a named buyer-to-worker mechanism and gives a
   measured employment signal, but it does not prove wages, retention, worker
   voice or user value for every contract.
7. Leeds City Council's Sayes Service contract connects procurement to a
   resident-facing infrastructure result. The 2024 contract replaced heating
   units and hot-water stores in 542 flats; the council reports a 10% household
   bill reduction, £811,770 in local-chain spend, one full-time apprentice and
   four student placements. The figures are useful for joining contract scope,
   resident service and local capability, while the council's own case study
   remains an administrative outcome report: independent verification,
   distribution across tenants and long-term maintenance are still open.
8. The Coquet Valley project in rural Northumberland shows procurement and
   interdepartmental coordination in a geographically isolated community.
   Home Office, MOD, BDUK, the National Park Authority and Northern Powergrid
   combined an Acquisition, Design and Build contract with rural-network work;
   the project connected off-grid properties and emergency-service sites to
   mains power and improved mobile coverage. The case closes a place-bound
   infrastructure and resilience route, but it is not evidence that local SMEs
   captured the value or that reliability and affordability persisted through
   future winters.
9. HM Passport Office's BI3 digital-services contract, worth up to £21 million,
   required bidders to propose apprenticeships and skills development. Atos
   committed to two apprentices through Ambitious about Autism and reported 20
   employees trained on autism inclusion. This is a named digital-procurement
   mechanism with inclusion commitments, not proof of SME participation,
   technical capability transfer, accessible passport-service outcomes or
   long-term employment retention.

## Bounded proposition

**A procurement law, tender, local-content target, SME set-aside, social-value
clause, supplier certificate, contract award, e-procurement dashboard or
reported local-spend percentage is not by itself qualified local capability,
fair competition, decent work, quality service, affordability, maintenance,
innovation, resilience, anti-corruption, inclusion or public value.** A contract
can be awarded without the supplier being able to perform at scale; a local
percentage can be achieved through low-value or nominal subcontracting; and a
social clause can exist without verified wages, safety, worker agency, delivery,
user benefit, payment, correction or remedy.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

“Local supplier” is not a self-explanatory category. It can mean registered
headquarters, ownership, workforce, production, subcontracting, tax presence or
community benefit. Each has different effects. Administrative language,
qualification thresholds, bonding, pre-financing, transport distance, digital
access, informal-sector status, gender and disability inclusion, customary land
authority and local trust shape who can bid and who can perform.

Local preference can develop capability and shorten supply chains, but can also
protect incumbents, raise prices, reduce competition or disguise political
favouritism. A large prime may satisfy a local-content target while retaining
technical control and value. Conversely, a small supplier may need lotting,
prompt payment, mentoring or consortium formation before it can meet a large
public specification. The unit of analysis should therefore include the prime,
subcontractors, workers, users, asset and place—not only the award notice.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- Strategic procurement can improve innovation, inclusion and resilience, but
  secondary objectives can conflict with value for money or trade neutrality.
- SME access can widen competition, but fragmentation can reduce quality,
  coordination and accountability if the buyer lacks contract-management skill.
- Local-content rules can build skills and supply chains, but may inflate cost,
  create token subcontracting or exclude more capable suppliers.
- Social and labour clauses can protect workers, but compliance paperwork is not
  the same as worker-paid wages, safe conditions, voice or an effective claim.
- An award or delivery milestone is not a durable outcome: inspect quality,
  payment, maintenance, user experience, recurrence and remedy over time.

## Promotion decision

Promote as **Tier 1 bounded distinction**: official evidence supports the
strategic role of procurement, SME-access mechanisms, implementation barriers,
labour/social-clause logic and competition trade-offs. Promote as **Tier 2
conversion question**: public-procurement-to-local-capability-and-fair-value,
requiring named tender-to-delivery evidence across supplier, worker, user and
place.

## Six-stage closure table

| Stage | What to observe | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | law, tender, award, local-content target, SME/social-value policy or dashboard | Policy architecture and access mechanisms are well documented |
| Mechanism | market engagement, lotting, qualification, finance, payment, labour clauses and oversight | Mechanisms are documented; capacity and enforcement vary by place |
| Operation | named supplier/prime/subcontractor performs a named contract | Awards and participation are visible; end-to-end execution is less joined |
| Material consequence | on-time quality delivery, wages, safety, affordability, maintenance and user receipt | Comparable supplier-worker-user-place chains remain limited |
| Meaning | fairness, dignity, local belonging, trust and perceived legitimacy | Social objectives are explicit; lived legitimacy is under-measured |
| Power | supplier voice, worker bargaining, bidder remedy, buyer accountability, switching and local control | Formal complaints exist; realized remedy and durable local bargaining power remain open |

## Next test

Trace three place-bound contracts: a public infrastructure project, a
health/education goods contract and a digital or professional service. For each,
follow need definition → market engagement → qualification → award → prime and
subcontractor ownership → payment → worker conditions → delivery quality → user
receipt → maintenance → complaint and remedy. Compare a local-content or SME
target with actual capability, total cost, competition, worker outcomes and
geographical distribution.

## Source boundary

The official record is strong for procurement's strategic potential, SME-access
mechanisms, administrative barriers, labour/social-clause logic and competition
trade-offs. It is weaker for comparable longitudinal evidence joining a named
procurement decision to local capability, worker welfare, user affordability,
service quality, maintenance, cultural legitimacy and remedy. That is a Tier 2
research opportunity, not a promoted causal finding.

# Rotation DE — IRS AI, taxpayer service, fraud detection and fiscal legitimacy

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Lane:** AI tax administration / taxpayer service / fraud detection / audit selection / identity verification / false positives / low-income burden / digital and human access / fiscal legitimacy / public trust
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 AI-tax-administration-to-fair-service-and-voluntary-compliance question

## Research question

When the IRS uses AI or advanced analytics to answer questions, select audits,
detect fraud, verify identity or process returns, how does a model-mediated
signal become a fair taxpayer service or enforcement encounter—and who bears the
waiting, documentation, false-positive, privacy and appeal burden across
income, disability, language, geography, business type and access channel?

## Conversion chain

```text
taxpayer, return, refund, question, payment, identity, income, business, risk signal and place
  -> data linkage, fraud filter, chatbot/voicebot, case score, audit-selection model,
     document extraction, identity-verification rule or staff recommendation
  -> notice, hold, question, review, audit, call, chatbot interaction, correspondence,
     refund decision, human escalation, appeal, correction and payment
  -> timely accurate service, fair enforcement, refund receipt, privacy, usable explanation,
     low administrative burden, equal treatment, voluntary compliance and fiscal legitimacy
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| GAO IRS AI review | IRS had 126 active AI use cases in June 2025, including taxpayer service, audit selection and fraud detection; over one-quarter lacked clear benefit information, some contracted criminal-case tools were omitted, and staffing/skills gaps threatened execution. | agency capability, inventory and governance gaps | taxpayer-level exposure, model accuracy, false positives, service resolution and remedy |
| IRS FY2025 financial audit | IRS used bots, address/document pilots, case-review support and letter drafting, while adopting interim AI governance to protect taxpayer rights, privacy and data security. | operational use and stated safeguards | whether speed/accuracy claims improve individual resolution without shifting burden to taxpayers |
| National Taxpayer Advocate | About 35 million calls were routed to voicebots in 2025; satisfaction was limited for key refund bots, and call measures did not capture quality or resolution. | channel exposure and measurement weakness | completed resolution, human fallback, subgroup burden and repeat contact |
| IRS taxpayer testimony | IRS says AI and advanced analytics can identify high-risk noncompliance more accurately and reduce false positives, improving service for compliant taxpayers. | official mechanism and intended benefit | independent false-positive rate, selection distribution, notice, explanation and correction |
| IRS Publication 3415 | IRS advisory material argues that improved fraud filters and community-based identity verification could reduce false positives and burden, especially for low-income taxpayers. | identified burden and proposed mitigation route | implementation, actual low-income relief, fraud leakage, access and taxpayer trust |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** the IRS announces AI use in taxpayer service, fraud prevention,
  audit selection, identity verification, return processing or digital contact.
- **Mechanism:** tax records, identity data, return features, account history,
  documents and contact behavior become a risk score, automated interaction,
  extraction result or staff priority.
- **Operation:** the system answers, routes, holds, requests documents, selects
  a return, escalates a case, issues a notice, releases a refund or sends a
  taxpayer to a human reviewer.
- **Material consequence:** a taxpayer receives a timely answer or refund—or
  faces delayed cash flow, repeated authentication, missed deadlines,
  correspondence, audit burden, privacy exposure, inaccessible service or an
  uncorrected error.
- **Meaning:** “fraud prevention” can express stewardship to the agency while
  being experienced by a low-income household, immigrant taxpayer, disabled
  person, rural resident or small business as suspicion, exclusion or a tax
  system that assumes they must prove innocence repeatedly.
- **Power:** the IRS controls data, thresholds, channels, notices and escalation;
  taxpayers carry the cost of waiting, documents, travel, language access,
  professional help and repeat contact, with unequal ability to challenge a
  model-mediated decision.

## Bounded finding

> **An IRS AI use case, fraud filter, audit-selection model, chatbot, voicebot,
> identity-verification process or human-review policy is not by itself fair
> taxpayer service, accurate fraud prevention or fiscal legitimacy.** A claimed
> accuracy improvement, faster answer, reduced false-positive goal, refund
> release, risk score or governance policy does not establish that named
> taxpayers received timely resolution, understood the reason, avoided
> disproportionate burden, corrected an error, obtained a human remedy or
> maintained trust in voluntary compliance.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Tax administration is a repeated relationship between the state and household,
not only a back-office optimization problem. A digital-first channel can work
for a salaried taxpayer with stable identity documents and reliable broadband
but fail for a gig worker, mixed-status family, elderly taxpayer, disabled
person, rural household, small business or person whose records do not match
agency data. The same fraud control can protect public revenue while imposing
cash-flow risk on people least able to wait for a refund. Community tax
professionals, libraries, in-person offices and trusted intermediaries may carry
the cultural work of making an opaque system legible. Fiscal legitimacy is
therefore produced through the full route from signal to notice to resolution,
not through model performance or aggregate enforcement yield alone.

## Counterexamples and limits

- AI can reduce repetitive work, detect genuine fraud and allow staff to focus
  on complex cases; a burden finding must not imply that automation is harmful
  in every use.
- A chatbot satisfaction result is not the same unit as a fraud-filter false
  positive; the service and enforcement pathways must be kept distinct.
- A higher audit yield may reflect better targeting, changed taxpayer behavior,
  staffing or case selection rather than model quality alone.
- A delay or notice is not by itself proof of algorithmic discrimination; records
  of features, thresholds, human action, subgroup exposure and correction are
  needed.
- A community-based identity route may reduce burden for some taxpayers while
  introducing privacy, training, coverage or geographic constraints for others.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: AI signal, administrative mechanism,
taxpayer operation, material burden/benefit, cultural meaning and fiscal power
are separate stages. Keep the AI-tax-administration-to-fair-service-and-
voluntary-compliance claim in Tier 2 until a named IRS pathway joins the model
or bot exposure, taxpayer subgroup, notice, human escalation, resolution time,
refund or audit outcome, correction, repeat contact, appeal and trust measure.

## Next test

Select one pathway—refund fraud filtering, “Where’s My Refund?” voicebot,
amended-return processing or audit selection. Join the operational log to
taxpayer contact, notice, hold, human escalation, resolution, refund/audit
outcome, correction, appeal and repeat-contact records. Compare low-income,
disabled, language-minority, rural, older, self-employed and represented versus
unrepresented taxpayers. Measure both fraud prevented and administrative burden
imposed, including time to cash, number of contacts, documents, travel,
professional fees and successful correction.

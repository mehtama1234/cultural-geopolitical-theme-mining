# US broad counterexample register v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-16
**Purpose:** keep the 14-theme program from turning recurring patterns into
one-direction explanations.

These are comparison requirements, not claims that the counterexamples have
already been found. Each proposed trend must be tested against at least one
case, group, place, firm, institution, or period where the expected arrow is
absent, reversed, or absorbed differently.

| Theme | Working pattern to test | Required counterexample | Unit and outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Household room and consumption | Pressure is absorbed through substitution, delay, borrowing, or giving something up | Similar price or payment pressure with no sacrifice because cash, timing, or support protected the need | Person/household; spending, debt, food, housing, recovery |
| 2. Time as hidden price | Work, care, travel, waiting, or paperwork displaces other time | Similar workload with stable social, care, or rest time because schedule control or nearby alternatives were available | Person/worker; time allocation and control |
| 3. Consumer power and recourse | A failed service creates repeat effort, weak remedy, dependence, or exit pressure | Similar failure resolved quickly through a human, appeal, correction, or effective switching route | Customer/case; effort, verified remedy, repeat contact, exit |
| 4. Platforms, data, and attention | Digital visibility or personalization changes access, interpretation, or dependence | Similar exposure with no change in belief or action because the user could inspect, contest, limit data, or leave | User/content or platform case; exposure, meaning, action |
| 5. Work, control, and bargaining | A tool or rule changes output while shifting control or risk toward the worker | Similar tool adoption with documented gains in pay, autonomy, safety, or bargaining power | Worker/job/firm; pay, schedule, health, voice |
| 6. Care, health, and social reproduction | A care need is paid through bills, work loss, unpaid family time, or another essential need | Similar care need with timely coverage, paid support, flexible work, or public capacity and no downstream displacement | Care episode/family/provider; care, work, health, time |
| 7. Housing, place, and mobility | Risk or cost changes the ability to repair, stay, move, work, or reach services | Similar hazard or bill with adequate coverage, repair, transit, nearby services, or secure staying | Place/household; repair, mobility, service access, move/stay |
| 8. Unequal exposure and status | The same formal condition produces different exposure or exit options across groups | A high-exposure group with equal outcome because an alternative, right, support, or remedy actually operated | Group/place/case; exposure, mechanism, outcome |
| 9. Trust, identity, and cultural meaning | Material or institutional experience is translated into blame, distrust, belonging, or identity | Similar experience interpreted as fair, trustworthy, or non-political, or political meaning changes without the expected material exposure | Respondent/case/community; attribution, trust, meaning |
| 10. Public systems and policy feedback | Eligibility, notice, burden, or interruption changes security and later demand | Similar eligible person or case receives help without interruption, or exits without material deterioration and without loss of trust | Program episode/person; route, security, meaning, response |
| 11. Political judgment and collective action | Exposure becomes action through attribution, information, identity, and trust | Similar material exposure with different action because information, identity, organizational ties, or perceived efficacy differed | Respondent/place/event; judgment, contact, turnout, vote |
| 12. Firm, sector, and market power | Firm growth, pricing, or entry transfers cost or control without equivalent public benefit | Similar firm or sector growth with durable local jobs, service quality, competition, worker gain, or lower recipient risk | Firm/sector/place; price, jobs, service, ownership, risk |
| 13. Infrastructure, technology, and dependency | New capacity expands capability while increasing provider or input dependence | Similar investment with interoperability, domestic alternatives, local learning, public control, and credible exit | Asset/provider/state; capacity, ownership, portability, exit |
| 14. Geopolitical and state consequences | Domestic exposure or dependence narrows state bargaining or strategic room | Similar external dependence with diversified suppliers, credible substitution, or no observed change in external behavior | State/firm/supply chain; alternatives, bargaining, action |

## Measured counterexample now in the atlas

The [Uber/Hotak access-restoration follow-up](projects/ai-work-control/uber-hotak-access-restoration-followup-record-v1.md)
is a bounded counterexample for themes 3 and 5. A platform-work exclusion was
followed by observed account reactivation and more than 150 subsequent trips,
so operational restoration and continued work activity are not merely assumed.
Payment receipt, net recovery, durable access, alternatives, household
outcomes, and non-retaliation remain open. The case therefore weakens any
simple rule that a formal remedy is either only a paper order or a complete
recovery, while not establishing a population remedy rate.

## How to use the register

For each proposed trend, select the counterexample before writing the synthesis
claim. Record:

- the exposure and the expected arrow;
- why the comparison is sufficiently similar;
- the alternative, protection, or mechanism that could explain the reversal;
- the outcome that failed to move or moved in the opposite direction;
- missingness, selection, measurement, and uncertainty; and
- whether the counterexample weakens the trend, narrows its population, or
  identifies a moderator.

Do not treat a counterexample as a nuisance. A reversal can reveal where power,
institutional design, place, identity, or practical optionality changes the
result. It may turn a universal-sounding claim into a conditional societal
pattern.

## Minimum decision rule

A trend remains provisional when its counterexample has not been measured. A
trend may be narrowed when a valid counterexample identifies a boundary. A
trend should be abandoned when comparable counterexamples repeatedly show no
pattern after accounting for timing, exposure, selection, and measurement.

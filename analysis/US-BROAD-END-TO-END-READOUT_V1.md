# US broad program: end-to-end readout v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-21
**Purpose:** give a plain-language answer to what the broad atlas has found,
what it has not found, and what the next serious test must add. This is a
synthesis of validated local records; it is not a new pooled estimate.

## The goal in one sentence

The project is testing how a rule, price, technology, shock, or institutional
decision travels through ordinary life: who gets exposed, who has room to
adapt, who controls the next step, what people do, how institutions respond,
and whether the result becomes trust, identity, consumer behavior, political
action, or geopolitical power.

The central finding so far is:

> The same pressure does not create the same life. It creates different menus
> of possible action, because money, time, care, status, information, rights,
> social support, and the ability to leave are unevenly distributed.

## What the atlas actually supports

1. **Optionality is unequal.** People facing the same price, job rule, service
   failure, or public-system requirement may pay with cash, time, food, care,
   health, debt, dignity, or future flexibility. “Resilience” is too blunt a
   word unless we say what was sacrificed.
2. **Time is a real price.** Waiting, paperwork, travel, care, customer-service
   loops, and unstable schedules can make a formally affordable service
   practically unusable.
3. **Interfaces decide whether capacity becomes help.** A benefit, remedy,
   transit route, insurance policy, or platform appeal can exist while notice,
   documentation, accessibility, staffing, or follow-up prevents usable access.
4. **People respond through an ecology of actions.** They may stay, delay,
   borrow, reduce use, seek help, complain, switch, move, organize, vote, or
   remain silent. These actions are not interchangeable signs of trust or
   resistance; dependence can look like loyalty, and silence can be a cost.
5. **Meaning is contested.** Groups can see the same condition and disagree
   about who caused it, whether it is fair, which institution should fix it,
   and whether action is worth the effort. Material exposure alone does not
   determine cultural or political meaning.
6. **Capability and dependence can rise together.** A new factory, platform,
   data center, defense system, or public program can add capacity while also
   increasing dependence on one supplier, one interface, one foreign input, or
   one authority. Capacity becomes power only when actors can use, inspect,
   refuse, replace, or impose a cost.
7. **Search and protective non-use are both constrained agency.** Under
   material pressure, people may search across jobs, food, benefits, housing,
   and credit without finding a usable alternative. Under perceived status or
   surveillance risk, families may avoid work, care, school, police, benefits,
   or community routes. Search is not success, and avoidance is not apathy:
   both reflect the safety, dignity, information, alternatives, and visibility
   costs attached to a route.
8. **Goodwill is not the same as dependable support.** People can say they are
   willing to help neighbors while being less confident that help would be
   returned. Social resilience therefore has separate stages: willingness,
   expected reciprocity, a dated request, help actually delivered, helper
   burden, adequacy, repeatability, and later trust. Community feeling is real,
   but it should not be treated as reliable protection until the support is
   delivered and lasts.
9. **Administrative legibility is a form of practical power.** Housing,
   benefits, care, privacy, and complaint systems increasingly decide access
   through identity matching, documentation, verification, and correction.
   Being eligible in principle is not enough if the system cannot recognize the
   person, explain the result, correct an error, or offer a usable appeal. This
   is a cross-cutting status and consumer problem, not only paperwork.

## The shared conversion problem

Across the program, the same mistake keeps appearing: a formal system output is
treated as if it were a lived outcome. The evidence supports a more precise
cross-domain rule:

| Formal capacity or response | What it can establish | What still has to be observed |
|---|---|---|
| Worker payment, reinstatement, or compensation order | An institution recognized a claim or assigned a remedy | Actual receipt, safe return, durable work, household security, trust, or exit |
| Consumer refund or redress distribution | Money was allocated, mailed, cashed in aggregate, or reissued | Person-level receipt, account correction, reduced residual burden, continued use, switching, or recovery |
| SNAP or energy-assistance participation | A public route processed, delivered, or restored something | Notice quality, effort, adequacy, food/utility protection, next crisis, trust, or political response |
| Care coverage, staffed capacity, or provider designation | A formal service or institutional resource exists | Reachable appointment, completed care, continuity, family time, safety, and later health |
| Data center, semiconductor, mineral, or defense capacity | Capital, infrastructure, production potential, or procurement commitment is visible | Accepted operation, replaceability, public incidence, refusal power, and changed external behavior |

The repeated finding is not that formal systems are useless. It is that power
changes hands at the conversion point: when a person or institution can
actually use, inspect, refuse, replace, sustain, or benefit from what was
provided. This is why the atlas keeps `offered`, `ordered`, `implemented`,
`received`, `usable`, `adequate`, `durable`, `trusted`, and `replaceable` as
separate states rather than creating one success score.

The [administrative-legibility layer](US-BROAD-ADMINISTRATIVE-LEGIBILITY-LAYER_V1.md)
names one recurring part of that conversion point: whether a person, household,
account, or case can be recognized, classified, corrected, and heard by the
institution controlling access.

## Latest end-to-end checkpoint

The current event-ledger portfolio passes its structural checks: **98 ledgers,
302 events, and 454 typed arrows**, with **164 arrows still explicitly open**
at a middle or later stage. Of the 302 events, 85 have an explicit remedy
stage, 34 are marked as institution-reported received, and none is independently
verified as recipient receipt or durable recovery in the portfolio audit. These
are coverage counts, not success or failure rates.

The portfolio also reveals its own blind spot: it is much richer in
administrative case rows than in lived units. It contains 228 case events, but
only 3 household events, 5 person events, 24 worker events, and 7 project
events. That imbalance explains why formal response is easier to document than
what a household, worker, customer, community, or affected place experienced
afterward. It is a reason to seek same-unit follow-up, not a reason to infer
that the formal record represents the lived population.

The strongest practical contrast is now clear:

- **Atkins truck drivers:** the official follow-up names ten drivers and says
  all ten received back-pay after court-held funds were released. This is a
  real worker-level receipt endpoint, but not proof of adequacy, later work,
  household recovery, trust, or exit.
- **Hotak:** the same worker is observed returning to platform work after
  voluntary reactivation and later receiving a formal reactivation order. The
  public record still does not show ordered-money receipt, durable access,
  alternatives, or later security.
- **Rehman:** the same worker is followed from deactivation through application,
  reactivation, and a formal gross lost-remuneration order. Payment, net
  recovery, durable access, and practical exit remain unobserved.

So the next research target is not another broad theme list. It is the missing
conversion layer: one stable unit, one dated response, one usable receipt or
outcome, and a later check on adequacy, durability, trust, switching, or exit.

## The atlas now has three realization clocks

The same conversion problem appears in three different forms:

| Domain | Early clock that institutions report | Later clock that people or states need |
|---|---|---|
| Personal remedy | complaint, order, payment issued, access restored, benefit approved | received, usable, adequate, durable, trusted, and connected to real choice |
| Physical infrastructure | approved, financed, constructed, energized, or assigned capacity | reliable service, actual load, household/business incidence, compensation, acceptance, and replaceability |
| Strategic capability | authorization, agreement, production support, delivery schedule, or test milestone | shipment, acceptance, training, maintenance, operational use, substitution, and changed external behavior |

This is not just a reporting-style issue. The earlier clock can look positive
while the later clock remains unresolved. A worker can have a compensation
order without usable recovery. A utility project can be energized without a
measured customer benefit. A defense program can have a signed agreement or a
successful test without operational leverage.

The newest cross-domain additions make the same point in several different
settings: a complaint system can improve routing while leaving individual
correction unknown; a privacy deletion route can define a processing clock
without proving actual removal; a cross-border power project can add regional
capacity without showing household distribution; an alliance brigade can move
from staffing pressure toward readiness without proving retention, family
capacity, or deterrence; Walmart's privacy metrics can show administrative
completion without verified control; and NATO ISR can produce repeated
operating outputs without proving strategic leverage. These are not side topics.
They extend the
same conversion test across consumer, infrastructure, social, and geopolitical
systems.

The revised disaster-liquidity study adds a stronger firm-side realization
clock: recovery loans are linked causally to lower firm exit and delinquency and
to higher employment, revenue, and private credit over a longer follow-up. It
is valuable evidence of productive-capacity recovery, but it still does not
show worker security, owner-household recovery, customer affordability, equal
access to loans, or durable community repair.

The infrastructure records make the distinction concrete: Wishing Star–Mars
has a reported construction-completion and energization stage, while
Golden–Mars remains an approved project. Neither record establishes improved
household reliability, actual cost allocation, compensation, or community
acceptance. The [infrastructure realization comparator](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-106.md)
keeps those project units separate. The [Poland F-35/JASSM comparison](projects/ai-work-control/jassm-lrasm-procurement-to-capability-realization-synthesis-v1.md)
shows the same logic for state capability: arrival and acceptance are stronger
than a schedule, but still weaker than readiness and strategic effect.

The latest breadth rotation extends this test beyond project schedules. A data-
control order can establish a formal user route without verified deletion or
switching; an energized transmission system can establish physical operation
without household benefit; and an alliance posture can establish institutional
response without verified deterrence or adversary change. These are different
units and evidence types, but they expose the same missing middle: the point at
which formal control, physical capacity, or strategic intent becomes usable,
durable, and replaceable power for the affected unit.

The broad finding is therefore:

> Public systems are usually easiest to observe at the moment they commit,
> announce, order, build, or classify something. The end-to-end meaning is
> determined later, when the affected unit can actually use it, afford it,
> rely on it, refuse it, replace it, or act because of it.

## The repaired bridge map changes the reading order

The atlas now registers the concepts that carry evidence between ordinary life
and large systems: consumer power/recourse, platform remedy, public-system
feedback, infrastructure dependence, work control, care reproduction, firm
market power, trust/meaning, and geopolitical consequence. They are bridge
topics, not extra top-level themes.

Seven routes now make the broader argument easier to follow:

1. **Care → recourse → public feedback:** formal coverage can become usable
   care only after a patient or caregiver navigates appeal, documentation,
   timing, and correction.
2. **Infrastructure → geopolitical consequence → legitimacy:** a project or
   technical capability can operate before household incidence, local
   acceptance, replaceability, or external leverage is visible.
3. **Alliance readiness → work control → meaning:** staffing and family/social
   capacity are part of strategic readiness, but readiness is not the same as
   retention, fair burden, public acceptance, or deterrence.
4. **Public liquidity → firm power → worker control:** a loan or public
   intervention can lower firm exit and raise employment or revenue while
   leaving worker security, job quality, bargaining power, customer
   affordability, owner recovery, and local power unresolved.
5. **Platform remedy → consumer recovery → trust and exit:** a multisided
   settlement can allocate money and prospective controls while workers,
   customers, and restaurants experience different receipt, access, fairness,
   alternative, and dependence outcomes.
6. **Defense reciprocity → industrial capacity → geopolitical leverage:** a
   planned co-production relationship can create a path toward domestic work
   and supplier capacity while production, readiness, autonomy, public
   acceptance, and changed external behavior remain unproven.
7. **Presence → usable membership → voice and action:** a person or family can
   be present as a worker, customer, neighbor, or cultural participant while
   documentation, language, housing, transport, safety, and institutional voice
   determine whether belonging becomes usable membership. The current
   immigration, place, and political records do not follow one unit through
   stable status, service access, safe participation, local influence, and
   durable civic action.

These routes sharpen the program's main cultural insight: people do not
experience “the economy,” “the state,” or “geopolitics” as separate boxes.
They experience bills, care, work, data, service routes, local infrastructure,
and trust. Larger institutional or geopolitical consequences matter only when
those everyday interfaces change what people can use, refuse, afford, or do.

## The single end-to-end test

The separate domain findings can be read as one chain. Each stage is a test;
passing an earlier stage does not imply that the later one happened:

```text
condition, rule, price, tool, shock, or capability
  -> exposure and incidence
  -> money/time/access/status/health/control/security change
  -> notice, understanding, and usable fit
  -> use, adaptation, non-use, search, switching, or exit
  -> receipt, remedy, bargaining power, or institutional response
  -> adequacy and durability
  -> trust, meaning, identity, voice, or political action
  -> firm, sector, public, state, or geopolitical consequence
```

This is the project’s actual end-to-end target. The four domain-specific
“ladders” are checkpoints inside it:

| Checkpoint | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Consumer option | Could the person understand, afford, use, or leave the route? |
| Work control | Did a tool or job change the person’s discretion, pay, voice, or bargaining position? |
| Remedy receipt | Did an announced remedy reach the person and reduce the remaining loss? |
| State capability | Did an announced or delivered capability become ready, replaceable, and strategically consequential? |

The atlas is strongest at the first half of the chain and at institutional
announcements or formal milestones. It is weakest at the joins: actual
receipt to adequacy, material change to meaning or action, and domestic
capability to another actor’s changed choice. Those joins—not another list of
themes—are the next research target.

## The cultural layer: shared concern does not create shared repair

The political and cultural records add an important second conversion problem.
People can agree that something is deteriorating while disagreeing about blame,
authority, representation, and repair:

- Pew's July 2026 survey found the economy was the top issue for 29% of
  registered voters, including 15% naming cost of living or affordability, but
  party advantage on economic policy was nearly even at 37% Democratic and 36%
  Republican.
- Gallup's May 2026 poll found 80% saying U.S. moral values were getting
  worse, while the public split 45% to 50% over whether government should
  promote moral values.
- The atlas's public-help and remedy records show a parallel institutional
  split: a person may continue using a benefit or service while judging the
  responsible institution as unfair, distant, or ineffective.

The safe cultural insight is therefore not “pressure produces distrust” or
“shared decline produces polarization.” It is:

```text
shared exposure or concern
  -> different attribution and identity meanings
  -> different ideas about legitimate authority and repair
  -> different action menus: vote, contact, organize, switch, stay, or withdraw
  -> uneven institutional response and later legitimacy
```

This is why the atlas treats economic salience, moral pessimism, institutional
confidence, vote framing, representation, and action as separate currencies.
The missing decisive evidence remains a repeated or event-linked record tying a
specific material or institutional encounter to attribution, action,
implemented response, and later trust or recovery.

The retained CCES panel gives a useful counterexample to a simple “pressure
creates distrust” story. Respondents reporting earlier job loss later showed
less congressional contact (37.36% versus 39.14%) and less local political
action (23.93% versus 31.57%), while favorable Congress approval was not lower
(13.86% versus 10.06%). Respondents without health insurance showed a similar
split: less congressional contact (31.75% versus 40.29%) and local action
(21.81% versus 32.48%), but nearly the same favorable Congress approval
(11.51% versus 10.23%).

This is a bounded same-panel comparison, not a causal estimate. Its value is
conceptual: material pressure may reduce the time, money, confidence, or
institutional access needed to act without producing one predictable judgment
of the institution. The atlas therefore keeps **civic capacity**—contact,
organizing, local action, voting, or withdrawal—separate from **institutional
judgment** such as approval, trust, or blame. See the [material-status and
political-action synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/material-status-political-action-divergence-synthesis-v1.md).

## The strongest same-unit bridges already in hand

The [ranked strongest-chain register](US-BROAD-STRONGEST-END-TO-END-CHAINS_V1.md)
now orders the retained routes by how much of the shared conversion chain is
held by one compatible unit. It makes the next work concrete: add a missing
stage to the strongest route before expanding the topic list.

The new [remedy-reachability synthesis](US-REMEDY-REACHABILITY-SELECTION-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
adds a cross-domain mechanism to the readout: later remedy visibility is often
conditioned on an earlier successful notice, application, complaint, acceptance,
contact, or cashing step. This is why later payment or continued use cannot be
treated as a recovery rate without retaining the people who failed at the
route boundary.

The [material-pressure/search synthesis](projects/us-consumer-culture/material-pressure-option-search-cultural-agency-synthesis-v1.md)
adds the consumer-cultural counterpart: pressure expands cross-system search
work, but search is effort rather than a completed alternative. Higher AI use
does not replace this navigation, and non-search cannot be interpreted as
satisfaction or freedom without an observed result and usable exit.

The [recovery-currency synthesis](US-BROAD-RECOVERY-CURRENCY-DIVERGENCE-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
now makes the program-wide interpretation explicit: money, time, access,
status, health, control, security, trust, and choice can improve or deteriorate
on different clocks. The atlas therefore treats recovery as a vector of
outcomes, not a single positive administrative endpoint.

The new [formal-access/control synthesis](US-BROAD-FORMAL-ACCESS-CONTROL-CONVERSION-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
sharpens that rule across SNAP EBT theft, Medicare Advantage prior
authorization, wildfire insurance, and consumer redress. A benefit, coverage
decision, partial payment, or refund route can exist while the person still
has to notice, document, appeal, monitor, or wait before gaining usable
control. This is a cross-domain conversion mechanism, not a pooled failure
rate or a claim that the institutions have the same cause.

The [protective non-use synthesis](US-BROAD-PROTECTIVE-NONUSE-CROSS-DOMAIN-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
adds an important cultural and political endpoint: avoidance can be an active
response to perceived surveillance, stigma, retaliation, paperwork, or low
expected value. Non-use is therefore not automatically apathy, satisfaction,
or free exit; it can protect immediate control while sacrificing care,
benefits, income, belonging, or future recourse.

### Three kinds of partial end-to-end proof

The strongest current results are not interchangeable, but together they show
what the full atlas is trying to connect:

| Evidence type | What is now observed | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| Same-person descriptive persistence | MEPS event-family screens place acute health events before later work and health-status measures; event groups show persistent nonemployment and mixed movement | A stable linked row set across both clocks, event-specific bill/choice, remedy, adequacy, trust, or exit |
| Randomized intervention to lived outcome | Cash and Counseling connects a consumer-directed care route to beneficiary satisfaction/unmet need, caregiver wellbeing, and paid-worker experience | One joined household, allowance take-up/spending, completed care, long-run security, retention, trust, or exit |
| State capability realization | Poland’s F-35 record moves from arrival and acceptance through pilot training to the reported eight-aircraft count | Ground-crew coverage, spares, certification, sustained mission use, replaceability, and changed external behavior |

The general lesson is that “end-to-end” is not one statistical technique. It is
the disciplined joining of different clocks and units while preserving each
boundary: persistence is not causation, a randomized short-run outcome is not
durable household recovery, and a capability threshold is not strategic power.

The current urgent-care wage case adds a concrete worker-voice version of the
same mechanism: asking about pay can surface a rule failure and trigger
retaliation risk before a public remedy is reached. The Department of Labor
reported recovered wages, but the public record does not show the suspended
worker’s receipt, return, alternative work, or willingness to speak again. This
keeps voice, institutional reachability, and lived recovery as separate stages.

The [migration/place cultural-conversion synthesis](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/migration-place-cultural-conversion-synthesis-v1.md)
adds the place-level boundary: growth and foreign-born composition do not by
themselves determine trust or civic action. Local capacity becomes cultural
meaning only through residents’ actual encounters with housing, language,
providers, firms, and public systems.

The [infrastructure lived-incidence boundary](projects/ai-work-control/infrastructure-modeled-protection-lived-incidence-boundary-v1.md)
extends the same rule to large loads: modeled cost allocation and public
participation are real governance stages, but they do not by themselves show
household bills, reliability, compensation, acceptance, or replaceability.

These are real bridges, but each closes only part of the full chain:

- **MEPS:** a dated medical event is followed by later health and employment
  measures for the same person across a strict event window and R5/3 follow-up.
  The event-family comparison finds that reported denial or
  prior-authorization delay is followed by more same-round care delay, medical
  debt, collections, and bill problems across office, ER, and inpatient
  groups. Alternatives, claim-level remedy, trust, and action are missing.
- **Health-cost institutional friction:** in the broader MEPS comparison,
  respondents reporting insurance denial or prior-authorization delay had
  15.18% cost-related care delay, 24.68% medical debt, and 20.26% collection
  contact, versus 5.81%, 12.98%, and 9.77% without reported denial/delay.
  This is a route-specific association, not proof of claim causation or failed
  remedy; it shows how an institutional gate can coincide with both delayed
  care and downstream financial exposure. In the round-to-round follow-up,
  perceived health worsened for 25.11% of friction reporters versus 21.74% of
  non-reporters, while employment retention was much closer (56.09% versus
  57.28%). Health and work are therefore separate downstream currencies, not
  one composite harm measure.
- **MEPS episode boundary:** the retained MEPS event ledger has 18,457
  privacy-minimized person/event rows with hashed episode identifiers and
  month-precision first-event dates. The strict event-window screen carries
  the same people into R5/3 and observes health/work transitions, but the
  public file does not identify the event-specific alternative, payment choice,
  appeal, verified remedy, or later trust/action/exit. A dated event therefore
  improves time ordering and persistence measurement without becoming a
  household recovery story.
- **MEPS work persistence sharpens the middle:** in the aligned descriptive
  two-clock screen, R5/3 nonemployment was 49.01% for the emergency-room event
  group versus 33.84% in its comparison group, and 62.18% versus 33.90% for
  the inpatient group. Yet nonemployment resolution was lower for those event
  groups (3.58% and 2.61%) than for their comparisons (5.52% and 5.51%),
  while status movement was slightly higher (11.49% and 11.83% versus 9.35%
  and 9.40%). This is not a causal employment-loss estimate—the two clocks do
  not assert an identical row set—but it is a useful warning: movement can
  coexist with persistent nonemployment, so “transition” cannot be coded as
  recovery without job quality, earnings, care continuity, remedy, and
  household-security evidence.
- **MEPS persistence is uneven across currencies:** in the strict R3/1–R4/2
  event window, fair/poor health resolved by R5/3 for 28.73% of the inpatient
  event group versus 40.34% of the no-event comparison, while nonemployment
  resolved for 2.61% versus 5.51%. Health and work therefore show a bounded
  same-person persistence signal, but not a single recovery measure; the
  comparison is not a causal control, and the public file still lacks the
  care choice, bill/claim, institutional response, remedy receipt, adequacy,
  trust, action, switching, or exit that would explain the transition.
- **Persistent care foregoing:** in the 2024–2025 SHED recontact panel,
  respondents who continued foregoing care reported 41.783% medical debt,
  65.334% reduced savings, 36.389% increased borrowing, and 76.442% delayed
  major purchases. This is longitudinal descriptive evidence of a continuing
  household adaptation surface—not proof that the original skipped-care
  decision caused every later financial outcome, or that later recovery was
  absent.
- **Health improvement is not financial reset:** in a separate 4,163-person
  SHED panel, among prior adopters, reduced-savings persistence was 56.9% on
  an improved-health path versus 62.7% on a worsened path, but borrowing still
  persisted at 56.4% after improved health versus 59.0% after worsened health.
  New borrowing also appeared for 9.8% of improved-health non-adopters. Health,
  cash room, debt, and delayed purchases therefore recover on different clocks;
  annual self-rated health does not establish the event, cause, or remedy.
- **SHED:** the same respondent’s financial condition and adaptations persist or
  reverse across waves. The trigger is not transaction-dated. The structured
  [SHED adaptation-persistence ledger](projects/us-household-financial-pressure/data/shed-financial-adaptation-persistence-event-ledger-v1.json)
  records this as a same-person adaptation-to-health/care route while keeping
  remedy, trust, action, switching, and exit open.
- **Adaptation is not one income ladder:** in the 2024–2025 SHED recontact
  panel, among prior adopters on a worsening financial-condition path,
  cheaper-product use persisted for 89.9% of respondents under $50,000,
  87.4% at $50,000–$99,999, and 84.1% at $100,000-plus. Increased-borrowing
  persistence was 61.5%, 57.0%, and 76.9%, respectively. The pattern says
  resources and the type of coping strategy interact; it does not identify a
  dated cause, wealth or liquid cash, or a universal low-income-to-adaptation
  gradient.
- **CCES:** an earlier job or benefit disruption is followed by institutional
  judgment, contact, and political action. The employer, remedy, and causal
  mechanism are not identified.
- **HTOPS:** linked respondents separate retrospective exposure, realized loss,
  reporting, sparse self-reported recovery, and later household context. In the
  11-person loss/report/recovery cell, 78.8% later reported being unable to pay
  an energy bill and 50.8% searched for jobs; the cell is too small for an
  estimate, but it is a sharp counterexample to treating reported recovery as
  restored household security. The incident date, verified remedy, and durable
  security are missing. The later search fields add an adaptation signal: loss
  groups search more for jobs, food, benefits, and credit, but searching is not
  a usable alternative.
- **Material pressure and social connection:** the corrected March 2026 HTOPS
  screen shows frequent loneliness rising from 1.8% among respondents saying
  expenses were not difficult to 30.2% among those saying expenses were very
  difficult; low needed support rises from 14.0% to 40.9%. Yet frequent
  phone/video contact stays near 28%–30% in the first three groups and falls
  to 21.8% in the most pressured group. Contact frequency, felt connection,
  and support adequacy are therefore different currencies; this is a
  cross-sectional association, not a causal expense-to-loneliness result.
- **Financial access and switching:** the 2025 SHED banking layer reports
  6% of adults unbanked, rising to 21% below $25,000 income, while 12% of
  adults experiencing non-credit-card fraud changed bank or financial provider.
  Sixty-five percent of that fraud group lost money and 35% recovered only
  some of it. Provider contact, recovery, and switching are therefore separate
  routes: formal access does not guarantee liquidity, and non-switching does
  not prove satisfaction or trust.
- **Material pressure and political action:** the structured [CCES job-loss
  follow-up ledger](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/data/cces-job-loss-political-followup-event-ledger-v1.json)
  adds a same-panel action endpoint. Respondents reporting job loss had lower
  later local political action (23.93% versus 31.57%) and slightly lower
  congressional contact (37.36% versus 39.14%), while favorable Congress
  approval was not lower (13.86% versus 10.06%). This suggests that material
  disruption can reduce observed action without producing a single predictable
  trust response; the job-loss screen is retrospective, not a dated causal
  event.
- **Material status and civic capacity diverge:** the companion [cross-route
  synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/material-status-political-action-divergence-synthesis-v1.md)
  shows the same directional pattern for earlier lack of health insurance:
  later congressional contact was 31.75% versus 40.29% among insured
  respondents, and local political action was 21.81% versus 32.48%, while
  favorable Congress approval remained close (11.51% versus 10.23%). The
  bounded interpretation is that material pressure may constrain the capacity
  to act while approval carries separate attribution and identity meaning; it
  is not a causal mechanism or a generalized disengagement rate.
- **Fraud-to-exit endpoint:** the structured [SHED fraud-recovery and
  switching ledger](projects/us-financial-intermediation/data/shed-fraud-recovery-switching-event-ledger-v1.json)
  records a stronger practical-action endpoint: among the non-credit-card
  fraud subgroup, 67% contacted a bank or financial company, 38% reported to
  an authority or complaint route, and 12% reported changing banks or
  financial companies. This still does not prove that a switch was completed,
  caused by the fraud, improved protection, or restored trust.
- **Staying with a bank is a constrained choice:** NBER account-opening data
  suggest only 5%–15% of depositors open a new account in a year, while its
  model estimates “sleepy” deposits account for 57% of average deposit
  franchise value. Staying may protect direct deposits, bill continuity,
  branch access, and fraud support—or reflect the time and risk of moving.
  Advertised alternatives are therefore not completed choice; the household
  rate gain, switching effort, error risk, service value, and later security
  must be measured together.
- **Monetary signals versus household action:** a randomized information study
  found that a 25-basis-point rate cut changed one-year inflation expectations
  by about 0.833 percentage points overall, while a separate household study
  found only small intended durable-spending responses to a 1-point rate
  surprise (about −0.05 points for one positive-surprise group and +0.08 for a
  negative-surprise group). These are different designs and outcomes, not one
  transmission coefficient: expectation movement is not a realized payment,
  saving, debt, care, or political response. Borrower/saver exposure,
  contract resets, liquidity, and later trust remain open.
- **Help-seeking and status:** the WBNS charitable-food layer reports food
  assistance receipt for 16.7% of working-age adults, while among adults with
  unmet need, 39.9% were not comfortable getting help and 35.2% cited
  transportation barriers. A route can therefore exist yet remain socially,
  physically, or informationally unusable; nonreceipt is not proof of no need,
  and receipt is not proof of restored food security or dignity.
- **Availability is not use:** across the interface synthesis, airport
  transit use ranged from 4% to 19% at the airports with usable reports, while
  smartphone users reported both connection benefits and difficulty reducing
  use: 53% said they spent too much time, and only 25% of those who tried to
  cut back felt extremely or very successful. A route, benefit, device, or
  facility becomes socially useful only when its time, price, accessibility,
  information, reliability, and alternatives fit the user; nominal capacity
  is not completed use, protected outcome, or practical exit.
- **Participation is not welfare or trust:** the consumer synthesis shows that
  searching, reporting, staying, switching, and non-use are ambiguous social
  signals. A household may search actively without finding a usable alternative;
  continued use may reflect dependence; and non-reporting may reflect stigma,
  futility, lack of awareness, time, transport, or inability to leave. Trust
  can lower friction while also increasing the data or dependence held by a
  firm or institution. These behaviors need an episode, effort, alternative,
  outcome, and later meaning before they can be read as satisfaction, distrust,
  resistance, or recovery.
- **Public capacity versus usable help:** states held very different fiscal
  cushions in the 2025 Fiscal 50 comparison, from about 320 operating days in
  Wyoming to zero rainy-day days in New Jersey. That is state operating room,
  not a resident benefit. Budget authority still has to become a program,
  notice, eligibility route, timely decision, and usable remedy before it
  changes household security or public trust.
- **Disaster recovery and legitimacy:** after Hurricane Harvey, 70% of
  affected residents said life was largely back to normal nearly a year later,
  but 30% still reported disruption and 42% said they were not getting the
  help they needed. Only 54% were confident relief benefited those who needed
  it most. Recovery progress, aid reachability, and legitimacy therefore move
  on different clocks; an application or public award is not receipt, adequacy,
  or restored trust.
- **FEMA delivery makes the middle of that chain visible:** GAO's 2026 review
  reports more than $3 billion in assistance to 1.2 million individuals and
  households across four recent disaster contexts, while also documenting long
  helpline waits, confusing requests for more information, housing constraints,
  and unequal access risks for older, disabled, rural, and digitally excluded
  survivors. A current Los Angeles wildfire aggregate snapshot adds 261,117
  registrations, 98,459 referrals, 35,165 eligible records, and $182.8 million
  in reported IHP amounts across 621 city/ZIP rows. These figures establish
  administrative capacity and friction, not unique-person approval, payment
  receipt, completed repair, stable housing, or survivor recovery. The public
  response can therefore be large and still be practically hard to use. See the
  [FEMA delivery finding](projects/us-repeat-energy-crises/findings/us-repeat-energy-crises-003.md).
- **Firm survival and household/local consequences:** a threshold-based SBA
  disaster-credit study estimates a 13.2-point reduction in firm exit and
  roughly 18% higher employment for the studied firms, but also about $17,712
  more non-SBA private debt. The result is strong firm-level recovery evidence,
  not proof of worker security, owner-household recovery, affordable customer
  service, or lower public risk: survival can preserve an option while moving
  obligations elsewhere.
- **Owner buffers can hide a business shock:** linked business/household
  account evidence reports a 40% early-COVID revenue fall, but only a 1.6-cent
  decline in owner consumption per dollar of lost revenue, with muted average
  pass-through persisting through 2021. That is a real owner-household bridge,
  but not proof of safety: savings, subsidies, debt, delayed investment, and
  later depletion are not fully observed, and the COVID shock is not the SBA
  disaster-loan effect.
- **Disaster recovery is redistribution, not one resilience score:** across
  the disaster evidence, public credit can preserve firms, owner buffers can
  absorb revenue loss, store closures can add customer travel, and residents
  can remain unconvinced that help reached those most in need. The relevant
  currencies are money, time, access, security, and legitimacy; a gain in one
  can coexist with a loss in another. The sources are deliberately not pooled
  into a single recovery or resilience rate.
- **Local customer access after disaster:** after major Harvey store closures,
  affected cardholders traveled about 15% farther to shop; entrants generated
  modeled consumer surplus but did not fully replace exits in the hardest-hit
  neighborhoods. Average metro welfare loss looked small (about 0.29% of
  pre-storm retail spending) while tract-level losses were larger. Aggregate
  prices and firm survival can therefore look stable while time, distance, and
  local access worsen for particular neighborhoods.
- **Cash App enforcement:** the CFPB record documents fraud/dispute-process
  failures, required operational correction, and up to $120 million in
  consumer redress. It advances the route from reported loss to a named
  institutional response, but does not show any individual payment, account
  correction, repeat effort, restored security, trust, or exit. A redress pool
  is therefore not a consumer recovery rate.
- **Administrative speed is not consumer remedy:** the CFPB complaint route
  exposes an administrative `timely` label and a separate receipt-to-company
  routing interval. In a 2025 student-loan sample, 59.8% were labeled timely,
  while 21.6% took more than 24 hours to route and the 90th-percentile lag was
  about 27 days. The label is not customer waiting time, company correction,
  payment, account repair, or practical exit; API field and vintage changes
  must also be treated as missingness rather than silently as zero.
- **Complaint closure does not expose consumer meaning:** in a controlled CFPB
  API probe, adding `has_narrative=true` to a credit-card/“closed with monetary
  relief” query returned the same 25 records and the same 21,425-match count as
  the control; neither response exposed a usable narrative field. This does
  not prove narratives are absent from every CFPB system, but it closes a
  tested acquisition route: response labels can be visible while the
  customer's explanation, alternative decision, effort, and attribution remain
  unobserved.
- **Compensation can coexist with the obligation:** the CFPB's Navient record
  reports ongoing checks to affected consumers while explicitly stating that
  the payments do not change or reduce the affected student loans. This closes
  enforcement and distribution stages but leaves individual receipt, balance
  correction, remaining loss, servicing dependence, trust, switching, and exit
  open. Cash received and the original material problem removed are separate
  outcomes.
- **Recall correction is not safe restoration:** a CPSC recall-key bridge for
  roughly 190,000 portable chargers links a dated fire hazard and free
  replacement offer to a later firm-reported 64,963 consumer-level corrections
  and 65,293 total corrections. The notice and progress report use different
  product/consumer-held denominators, and neither shows purchaser notice,
  replacement receipt, delay, residual cost, or safe durable use. A correction
  percentage is implementation visibility, not a consumer recovery rate.
- **Even repair can need a remedy for the remedy:** a September 2026 CPSC
  Sauna360 expansion says a 2025 free repair was ineffective, then instructs
  owners to stop using the benches and offers a second professional repair.
  The later notice covers about 6,000 units and reports 77 additional breakage
  reports, including six injuries. This advances the durability arrow—an
  institutional fix can itself be corrected—but still does not show owner
  notice, appointment, completed repair, downtime, safe use, or residual cost.
- **Small firms as infrastructure consumers:** the FTC reports 5,588 checks
  totaling more than $2.6 million sent to businesses charged hidden or
  unauthorized payment-processing fees, including early-termination and
  recurring “zombie” charges. The case shows how a processor can make formal
  provider choice costly, while checks sent, checks cashed, processor
  switching, restored transaction capacity, and business continuity remain
  separate clocks. This links market power to local-firm practical exit, but
  does not establish the business-level burden or recovery rate.
- **Marketplace power:** an FTC/state complaint alleges that Amazon's hidden
  advertising-auction surcharges affected more than one million advertisers,
  including more than 500,000 small and medium businesses, and that higher
  advertising costs were largely passed to consumers. This is an allegation,
  not an adjudicated finding or a price estimate; the missing bridge is seller
  cost → bid/visibility → product price or assortment → actual purchase and
  household burden. Visibility, seller participation, and consumer welfare
  must remain separate outcomes.
- **Aggregate remedy versus household recovery:** the FTC Amazon Prime record
  reports more than $845 million issued and about $235 million accepted across
  payment phases, but no customer-level denominator, receipt, delay, remaining
  loss, continued use, switching, or household outcome. Accepted dollars show
  that some money reached consumers; they do not establish a recovery rate or
  that the remedy restored the time, trust, or choice lost in the subscription
  episode.
- **Payment and continued platform participation are different endpoints:** the
  Amazon Flex tip-refund program reports more than $60.6 million distributed,
  130,952 drivers fully compensated, and participation rising from 90.3% to
  92.3% after a Zelle reissue route. Those are aggregate administrator and
  program measures, not driver-level receipt, remaining loss, free continued
  work, bargaining power, trust, switching, or exit. Higher participation can
  reflect dependence, payment reach, or ordinary platform use—not restored
  power.
- **SIPP SNAP:** adjacent-month transitions show that benefit exit is not
  automatically improvement: 76.2% of observed exits had a lower next-month
  income-to-poverty ratio, and only about half of transitions had a classified
  reason. Entry and exit are therefore participation states, not proof of
  adequacy, voluntary choice, or restored security.
- **SIPP care/work constraint:** the same-person stable-state bridge shows
  lower paid child-care use among SNAP records than no-SNAP records (23.9% vs.
  31.0%), but higher child-care assistance (14.6% vs. 5.2%) and higher reported
  work-prevention constraint (7.4% vs. 3.4%). These are descriptive cells with
  different universes and reference periods, not a SNAP effect. They show that
  participation, assistance, usable care, and work capacity are separate
  stages of household security.
- **SIPP capacity-conditioned hardship:** a second same-person comparison shows
  why those stages are not socially uniform. Among stable SNAP records, the
  work-limiting-condition/children-present group reports 17.85% rent or
  mortgage hardship and 31.57% utility hardship in the following-month record,
  versus 7.77% and 11.60% for the stable-SNAP group with neither marker. This
  is descriptive and selected, not a SNAP effect or a caregiving-hours
  estimate. It adds household capacity and responsibility as modifiers of
  usable protection; notice, amount, adequacy, trust, action, and recovery
  remain open. See the [capacity-conditioned SIPP finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/findings/us-safety-net-access-018.md).
- **SNAP route divergence:** the reason-linked SIPP screen shows following-
  month low/very-low food security after entry associated with job loss or
  reduced wages at 22.94% and after disability/inability to work at 31.73%;
  after exit attributed to income increase/ineligibility it remained 25.69%,
  and after requirements were not met it was 44.51%. These cells are small,
  reason-classified subsets with wide Fay-BRR uncertainty, not program-effect
  estimates. The key finding is that entry and exit are not interchangeable
  recovery states, and recorded reason is not the same as adequate food
  security.
- **Benefit entry can coexist with immediate compound hardship:** in the
  classified SIPP transition layer, simultaneous rent/mortgage and utility
  hardship appeared in 15.9% of job-loss/wage-reduction entries and 16.9% of
  other-income-loss entries. On the exit side, the small “still eligible but
  could not/chose not to collect” group was 27.3%, and “benefits not worth the
  trouble” was 32.3%, both with wide uncertainty. Receipt can therefore
  coexist with unresolved bills, and exit can coexist with hardship; these are
  descriptive transition cells, not SNAP effects.
- **Public-system route friction:** USDA state aggregates show application
  timeliness ranging from 61.24% to 97.22% and recertification timeliness from
  25.23% to 99.87%, while a separate WBNS route sample reports 24% involuntary
  SNAP interruption; among a conditional subgroup, 40% reported insufficient
  time after notice and 32% no notice. Administrative speed, notice quality,
  receipt, material protection, and institutional confidence are separate
  stages—not one program-success rate. The state-level participation-to-
  application-timeliness correlation is only 0.033, reinforcing that take-up
  and administrative performance are different signals.
- The structured [WBNS SNAP recertification ledger](projects/us-safety-net-access/data/wbns-snap-recertification-interruption-event-ledger-v1.json)
  now preserves the recipient-side notice, time, paperwork, interview, and
  interruption route while leaving correction, appeal, substitution, and later
  security open.
- **Protective non-use:** among 1,036 adults in immigrant families with
  children, 23% reported avoiding at least one essential activity because of
  immigration concerns; almost one in five reported going without public
  benefits for that reason, and 10% reported not visiting a doctor, clinic, or
  hospital. This is a direct access-behavior surface, not a vote or generalized
  distrust measure: non-use can protect against perceived risk while also
  sacrificing care, income, safety, or public support. The structured [WBNS
  non-use event ledger](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/data/wbns-2025-immigration-concern-nonuse-event-ledger-v1.json)
  now preserves this as a cohort-level behavioral endpoint while leaving
  respondent-level alternatives, remedy, and later action open.
- **Belonging has separate currencies:** the migration/place comparison finds
  that fast-growing counties had lower reported federal trust than the
  slowest-growth quartile (35.251% versus 41.927%), while state trust, civic
  action, and reported voting did not move as one bundle. Separately,
  immigrant-family respondents reported risk-linked non-use of essential
  routes. Demographic presence, local capacity, institutional judgment, civic
  action, and safe service use therefore cannot be collapsed into one
  “integration” measure; these are different units and not a causal chain.
- **Progress and belonging can point in different directions:** Latino
  respondents can report living standards better than their parents and hope
  their children will do better while still judging the American dream as hard,
  exclusionary, or out of reach. Hispanic identity can be central while being
  experienced as helping, hurting, or making no difference. Relative mobility,
  future hope, identity, belonging, and perceived opportunity are separate
  cultural currencies—not one assimilation or consumer-income score. See the
  [mobility/belonging finding](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/findings/us-immigration-local-demand-004.md).
- **Ohio place panel:** counties with higher zero-vehicle shares have nearly
  the same modeled routine-checkup median (76.95% versus 77.05%) but higher
  transportation insecurity, food insecurity, mental distress, and lack of
  insurance. Nominal service use can therefore coexist with weaker practical
  room; this is an ecological county comparison, not a resident-level effect.
- **Housing security:** the 2025 housing screen reports 23% of renters behind
  on rent during the prior year, about 33% below $50,000 income, while 6% of
  homeowners reported no homeowners insurance and the share was about 20%
  below $50,000. A separate energy model puts the lowest-income-band burden
  proxy at 28.73% versus 1.74% in the highest band. These are different
  universes and clocks, but together show why staying housed is not the same
  as staying protected: payment, coverage, energy, repair, and mobility room
  can fail at different stages.
- **A public insurance backstop can grow while claim usability remains weak:**
  California FAIR Plan data show 696,562 policies and $768 billion in reported
  exposure by June 2026, while a California DOI review of 259 smoke-damage
  claims found 118 violations tied to denials. These establish scale and a
  documented implementation problem, not household-level recovery or a claim
  failure rate.
- **Insurance recourse is not repair:** New York's 2025 DFS assistance unit
  received 39,443 insurance complaints, closed 39,064, and reported more than
  $121 million in recovery. In the mixed property-casualty/service-contract
  category, 498 of 2,869 closed cases were labeled positive outcomes, with
  about $17.95 million reported recovered. These figures make routing and
  reported recovery visible, but do not show adequate claim payment, completed
  repair, renewed coverage, temporary housing, or whether the household stayed
  or moved.
- **Restitution plus process correction is still not lived coverage:** Virginia
  settlement summaries report about $2 million to 2,959 Esurance consumers,
  $2.5 million to 91,696 State Farm consumers, and $346,000 across 1,680
  Anthem/Carelon consumers and 586 providers, alongside rating, filing,
  network, credentialing, and claims controls. The route advances beyond a
  complaint or proposed remedy, but public records still lack individual
  receipt, corrected premium/claim, coverage or care continuity, residual cost,
  trust, and switching.
- **Migration, belonging, and political meaning:** the joint county screen
  does not produce a simple growth or foreign-born-share explanation for
  federal trust; the lowest trust cell is high-growth/mid-nativity, while
  civic action stays comparatively stable. In the separate ANES respondent
  layer, people distinguish economic effects, job competition, citizenship,
  trust, and vote rather than holding one single immigration attitude. This
  is the intended end-to-end boundary: place conditions can frame exposure,
  but they cannot substitute for a person's reported meaning, attribution,
  belonging, or action.
- **Infrastructure and state power:** defense authorization and data-center
  capacity records show commitment and buildout stages, but not necessarily
  delivered capability, local control, replaceability, public benefit, or
  changed behavior by another state. The retained comparison includes 621 MW
  of reported Prince William capacity additions and separate 1,551 MW and
  1,800 MW load-drop events, alongside a Poland JASSM-ER procurement window;
  these are not interchangeable evidence of compute service, accepted weapon
  inventory, readiness, or geopolitical response. Capacity and dependence can
  rise together. The related JASSM/LRASM realization clock adds a dated US-side
  integration-validation milestone and LRASM C-1 fielding on one platform, but
  the live Polish Ministry of Defence record still provides only a 2026–2030
  delivery window for the JASSM-ER order. A US test or fielding milestone is
  therefore a real capability-stage comparator, not evidence of Polish
  shipment, acceptance, inventory, or changed external behavior. The separate
  Polish F-35 record makes the missing middle concrete: three aircraft arrived
  at Łask on 22 May 2026 and were formally accepted on 12 June 2026. That
  arrival-to-acceptance trail is observed for F-35, not for the JASSM-ER order;
  the reported in-country fleet has since reached eight aircraft, the numeric
  part of Poland's stated IOC criterion. Training coverage for all required
  personnel, spares/consumables, certification, full operational availability,
  and geopolitical leverage remain separate stages.
  Poland’s SAFE program adds the upstream industrial-allocation stage: the
  Ministry reports 62 contracts worth about 120 billion złoty, with nearly 90%
  directed toward Polish defense companies. This is evidence of procurement
  scale and domestic supplier allocation, not yet delivered output, readiness,
  replaceability, or a changed external actor.
- **Geopolitical legitimacy is a separate currency:** Pew's 2026 survey of
  42,151 adults in 36 countries found a median 35% saying the US contributes
  to global peace and stability, versus 57% of US adults; inside the US, the
  comparable partisan figures were 82% among Republicans and 35% among
  Democrats. Meanwhile, 83% of Americans across parties said the US interferes
  in other countries' affairs at least a fair amount. Capability, foreign
  reception, domestic identity, and agreement about the role therefore do not
  move as one bundle; the survey does not show changed alliance behavior,
  trade, aid acceptance, or another state's choice.
- **CHIPS implementation:** GAO reports about $50 billion in semiconductor
  programs, 49 projects across 24 companies, and $13.1 billion disbursed by
  April 2026—about 42% of direct awards—alongside 24 completed milestones,
  125 in progress, and 9 past anticipated dates. Those figures show money,
  milestones, and governance activity, not accepted output, worker retention,
  customer delivery, or reduced foreign dependence. Industrial policy becomes
  strategic capability only after funding reaches qualified production,
  usable supply, replaceability, and an observed change in another actor's
  choices.
- **Critical minerals and strategic autonomy:** USGS import-reliance and
  processing-concentration data, alongside GAO substitution and recycling
  assessments, show why domestic projects are not automatically independence.
  Recycling may reduce some battery-material reliance on a multi-year horizon,
  while semiconductor substitutes face tighter performance constraints. A
  domestic facility can therefore add production capacity while refined
  feedstock, qualification, recovery infrastructure, or supplier control
  remains external. The correct unit is the mineral–application–process–firm
  chain, followed through qualified output, supplier switching, and an observed
  external response. Capability, replaceability, and geopolitical leverage are
  separate stages.
- **AI capability can expand while replaceability remains weak:** the World
  Bank's adopt–adapt–advance framework treats infrastructure, skills,
  institutions, data, and local fit as complements to AI capability, while
  concentration across hardware, infrastructure, models, and applications can
  deepen dependence. The current record does not show a supplier switch,
  refusal, outage, concession, or changed external behavior. Access or
  investment can therefore create useful capacity without proving inspection,
  portability, bargaining power, or state leverage.
- **AI, work, and control:** a randomized workplace-access study found changed
  email/time patterns, while the available record does not show whether that
  time became rest, care, pay, worker voice, or higher employer expectations.
  In the six-month intervention across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers,
  treated users spent about two fewer hours per week on email in the later
  period; a separate 5,179-agent customer-support experiment estimated about
  14% more issues resolved per hour. Time and output moved, but ownership of
  the gain, pace, pay, and household consequence remain unobserved.
  A separate executive survey adds a time-horizon counterexample: 90% reported
  no past-three-year firm employment/productivity impact, while expected
  next-three-year productivity was positive and employer/employee employment
  expectations differed. Presently unmeasured impact and future anticipated
  reallocation can coexist; expectations are not realized worker outcomes.
  A separate OECD employer survey reports 90% of US firms with 20+ employees
  using at least one algorithmic-management tool, 76% using ten or more, and
  55% monitoring conversation content or tone; those are employer reports, not
  worker exposure or proof of harmful use.
  Task-level NBER evidence adds a sharper access boundary: generative AI
  reached more than 80% of occupations and more than 40% of tasks, but in most
  of those occupations fewer than half of workers adopted it; no task exceeded
  70% adoption. Occupation-level exposure is therefore not worker access, use,
  training, permission, or control, and the public evidence still does not
  show who captures any time or output gain.
  Across AI use, synthetic contact, and companion-product exit, immediate
  behavior can change before authority, portability, correction, or durable
  exit is observed. In the companion-product route, at least one continuation
  tactic appeared in 37% of 1,200 audited farewell conversations, while
  separate controlled experiments found much higher short-run engagement in
  some farewell-response conditions. That is evidence that a goodbye can
  become an engagement event; it is not evidence that users were unable to
  delete, switch, or leave. Usefulness and worker/consumer control are
  therefore separate outcomes. See the [AI companion farewell ledger](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/data/ai-companion-farewell-exit-event-ledger-v1.json)
  and [optionality synthesis](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/ai-optionality-control-conversion-synthesis-v1.md).
- **The AI distribution problem starts before displacement:** the latest firm
  and worker layers sharpen the missing bridge. Executives report high firm
  adoption alongside little retrospective employment/productivity impact and
  asymmetric future employment expectations; workers report much higher AI use
  among people with graduate education and greater task control, plus perceived
  time and quality gains. This means the first social divide may be who is
  allowed, prepared, and able to use AI—not simply who later loses a job. The
  records still do not show who receives the gain, who absorbs monitoring or
  employment risk, or whether household security improves.
- **Infrastructure creates voice and rules before it proves public benefit:**
  the [Virginia public-response case](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-104.md)
  records more than 600 registered speakers and written comments; the
  [land-control finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-105.md)
  shows a public institution's non-consent changing the approved route; and a
  [separate comparator](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-106.md)
  reached reported energization. Those are real institutional and realization
  stages, but they do not yet show resident compensation, reliability
  improvement, household bill effects, or durable acceptance. The [large-load
  governance finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-103.md)
  likewise shows risk-allocation rules before signed contracts, payments, or
  household outcomes are visible.
- **AI exit can become another engagement event:** a behavioral audit of 1,200
  companion-app farewells found at least one continuation tactic in 37% of
  farewells, while preregistered experiments with 3,458 US adults reported
  short-run post-goodbye engagement increases of up to 16× under some tested
  responses. This observes an exit-boundary mechanism, not durable lock-in or
  harm: curiosity, voluntary continuation, deletion, payment, human
  alternatives, remedy, and later wellbeing remain open.
- **CCES political follow-up:** respondents reporting earlier job loss had a
  large later insurance gap and lower local political action (23.9% versus
  31.6%), but Congress approval was not uniformly lower. Earlier lack of
  insurance showed the same action gap with near-similar favorable approval.
  Material insecurity, institutional judgment, and political action are
  therefore separate currencies; attribution and remedy remain unknown.
- **Material insecurity does not force one political response:** a separate
  CCES panel shows uninsured respondents later making less congressional
  contact and local political action while reporting slightly more favorable
  Congress approval. An ANES panel similarly shows financial worry preceding
  lower federal trust and different reported vote patterns. These are timing
  and counterexample surfaces, not proof that hardship caused a vote or
  withdrawal; identity, attribution, route cost, and perceived efficacy matter.
- **Usable care fails at the interface:** the disability-access evidence shows
  that coverage, a provider, a kiosk, an app, or medical equipment can exist
  while physical, sensory, communication, cognitive, digital, or provider-
  interaction barriers prevent a completed and continuous encounter. Capacity
  is not usable access, and oversight is not restored care.
- **Medical hardship can target contact rather than simply suppress action:**
  in same-respondent 2018/2020 CES crisis extracts, hardship was associated
  with official contact at 23.3% versus 21.4% without hardship in 2018, and
  28.3% versus 17.9% in 2020; exploratory adjusted contact odds ratios were
  1.69 and 1.80. Turnout, protest, campaign work, and donations did not move
  the same way across years. This is a descriptive, model-screened bridge:
  attribution may channel burden into institution-targeted action, but the
  bill, remedy, effectiveness, trust change, and recovery remain open.
- **Policy demand is identity-conditioned too:** in the ANES health-cost layer,
  support for increased government help with health-insurance costs was 57.5%
  among respondents not at all concerned about paying health-care costs and
  61.3% among those extremely concerned. Among strong Democrats the comparable
  values ran 84.6% to 71.2%, while among strong Republicans they ran 30.7% to
  50.0%. Concern alone therefore does not determine one policy demand; party
  identity changes the meaning of the material problem.
- **Redress can persist while recovery remains unknown:** [CFPB follow-up
  records](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-030.md)
  and the [FY2025 persistence finding](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-031.md)
  now show both funds being cashed and later reissue or redistribution after
  unresolved payments. The report records $20,809 in direct Premier reissue
  payments and later redistributions of $129,729 for U.S. Equity Advantage
  and $174,178 for SMART Payment Plan. This proves the remedy system can
  remain active after an initial distribution; it does not prove which
  affected people received the later money, whether accounts were corrected,
  or whether recovery, trust, switching, or exit followed. Reachability is
  part of the remedy.
- **Repeat refund reach is stronger than an announced fund, but not recovery:**
  the [FTC ACRO finding](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-032.md)
  reports 5,685 second-round checks totaling more than $6.2 million to people
  who accepted the first payment. That recipient-conditioned denominator is a
  real improvement in remedy observability; it still does not show that the
  second checks were cashed, that the debt or account was corrected, or that
  financial security and trust returned.
- **Reachability is part of consumer remedy:** the [CFPB Ombudsman's report](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/cfpb-ombudsman-redress-reachability-finding-2026-09-19.md)
  records people seeking help because redress checks were not received, not
  cashed, or needed reissue to a next of kin. It also records questions about
  eligibility, payment legitimacy, and third-party administrators. These are
  direct signs of remedy-navigation friction, not a reissue success rate or
  proof of final recovery.
- **Contestability has a documentation cost:** the NYC AEDT complaint route
  records a complaint entering the public system, followed by a request for
  the suspected-violation date, employer and job details, and supporting
  documents by a deadline. The rule and complaint form create a formal right,
  but the worker still has to identify the decision and produce usable proof;
  the public record does not show whether the request was completed or whether
  the hiring decision changed. This is a route-burden observation, not an AEDT
  compliance or worker-outcome rate ([NYC AEDT follow-up audit](projects/ai-work-control/nyc-aedt-enforcement-follow-up-acquisition-audit-2026-09-17.md)).
- **Repeat refunds reveal a reachability gate:** the [FTC comparison](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/ftc-repeat-refund-recipient-gate-synthesis-v1.md)
  shows ACRO sending 5,685 second-round checks to people who accepted the first
  payment, Next-Gen sending 104,820 to people who cashed the first check,
  American Vehicle Protection sending 9,074 to people who accepted the first
  payment, and Yellowstone sending 3,759 third-round checks to small businesses
  that had cashed a previous check. The FTC generally uses prior cashing as a
  condition for additional payments. This makes repeat-remedy totals evidence
  about successful earlier reach, not a recovery rate for people who were never
  reached or could not cash the first payment.
- **Scheduled worker restitution is not received worker restitution:** the new
  [NYC DCWP reachability ledger](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/data/nyc-dcwp-restitution-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json)
  records named workers, scheduled checks/direct deposits, and an address/bank
  update route, while the city's proposed Unclaimed Restitution Fund responds
  to money that never reaches an intended recipient. The public route still
  does not show issued, returned, reissued, cashed, adequate, or durable
  worker-level outcomes. This is a concrete selection mechanism, not a
  restitution success or failure rate.
- **Shared cultural pessimism does not create a shared remedy:** recent
  [moral-values](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-047.md)
  and [economic-attribution](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-046.md)
  layers show broad concern about decline or
  affordability alongside divided views of government, party representation,
  and policy responsibility. Concern, blame, legitimacy, and action are
  separate currencies.
- **Informal support has a reciprocity gap:** [Pew's mutual-aid finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-044.md)
  shows people are more willing to help
  neighbors than they expect neighbors to help them. That makes social trust a
  potential capacity for recovery, not proof that aid will arrive when a real
  shock occurs.
- **GSS meaning layer:** respondents who were not satisfied with their finances
  reported much lower generalized trust and fairness expectations than those
  who were satisfied. But reported financial direction also aligned with
  different vote intention patterns. Material position shapes meaning without
  determining one fixed cultural or political response.
- **Institutional confidence is a map, not one score:** Gallup's 2025 battery
  found majority confidence in small business (70%), the military (62%), and
  science (61%), but very low confidence in Congress (10%), television news
  (11%), and big business (15%). Low confidence is therefore institution-
  specific, not universal anti-institutionalism; it does not tell us whether a
  person stopped using a service, appealed, voted, organized, or received a
  remedy.
- **Party-conditioned political meaning:** the ANES panel shows that reported
  financial worry and vote look different inside party-identity groups. Among
  independents, the very-worried group leaned Trump (74.9%) while the not-at-
  all-worried group leaned Harris (71.6%); strong partisans stayed highly
  concentrated with their identity across worry levels. This is descriptive,
  not an economic-voting causal result: identity can shape both reported worry
  and vote, and the dated material exposure is still missing.
- **Action styles are not one ladder:** Pew's 2025 public-life study groups
  people as Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, or Outsiders across politics,
  civic life, religion, and news. Mobilizers are more politically active, but
  Connectors can show broad community and news involvement without the same
  direct political activity; Outsiders are less engaged across the measured
  activities, but the study does not identify why. This keeps voting, contact,
  volunteering, attention, trust, and withdrawal as separate action routes.
  The clusters are descriptive, not identities or platform-caused effects;
  the missing end-to-end link is a dated condition, interpretation, chosen
  action, institutional response, and later legitimacy for the same person.
- **Platform remedy case:** one worker had access voluntarily restored, then
  received a formal reactivation order and an A$7,096.96 gross lost-pay order
  with a 14-day deadline. The record still does not show payment receipt,
  continued work, durable access, or household recovery. Enforceability is a
  stage between formal remedy and lived recovery.
- **Payment is not restoration:** in the FWC Meadley follow-up, the official
  account reports compensation payment, failed reinstatement, a worker's
  court-enforcement action, and a further $15,045 award plus interest and
  penalty. This is a stronger remedy-stage case because payment is observed
  while the original work relationship remains unrestored; later employment,
  household recovery, trust, and exit remain open.
- **Partial payment is not full remedy:** the Fair Work Commission's Indian
  Food Catering enforcement example reports $7,000 paid against an $18,000
  compensation order, with $5,008 still outstanding after tax when the Court
  ordered the balance plus interest and imposed a fine. This strengthens the
  implementation layer, but the record still does not show final receipt,
  restored employment, household recovery, or later trust and exit.
- **A remedy can work for a while and still fail to secure the future:** in
  the Aitken case, union intervention was followed by return, three months of
  back pay, and prior-service recognition; the worker then worked for a period
  before a later dismissal after safety-policy conflict. This adds a real
  durability/rupture endpoint, but not a causal verdict or proof of later
  livelihood failure.
- **Money and access can move separately:** in the Cartisano case, the record
  reports back pay and continuity followed by actual return, materially altered
  duties, and cessation while still employed during a constructive-reinstatement dispute
  to the workplace during a risk assessment. The Full Bench explicitly held
  that reinstatement must be real and practical, not merely contractual or
  financial. This is a strong access/control boundary, not proof of the
  worker's later recovery.
- **Cultural access is part of usable work:** in the Alam Shah case, a worker
  with limited English, no car, a roughly 25-minute walk to work, and family
  obligations in a refugee camp received an order for reinstatement, continuity,
  translated reasons, and $18,035.56 in lost pay after an injury-related
  dismissal. The case makes language, mobility, and transnational family
  support part of the material option set; it still does not show payment,
  actual return, changed workplace communication, durable security, trust, or
  exit.
- **A remedy can repair wage and status together:** in a DOJ IER bilateral case,
  a citizenship-document problem was followed by termination and a lower-wage
  rehire; the worker contacted IER, then signed an agreement restoring the
  prior hourly wage, $4,712.50 in back pay, and seniority. Withdrawal of the
  complaint is an observed endpoint, not proof of durable employment, benefit
  use, household recovery, trust, or exit. This is one selected agency-reported
  case, but it closes more of the chain than an aggregate payment total.
- **Immediate worker repair can still stop before durable control:** two DOJ
  IER FY2026 summaries report $30,000 in back pay plus a reinstatement offer in
  one case, and $8,000 in front/back pay plus changed Form I-9/E-Verify
  procedures in another. These add reported money and firm-process correction
  to the chain, but not accepted return, payment adequacy, continued tenure,
  household security, trust, or exit. A later public recheck found no such
  follow-up for the strongest retained worker-voice cases.
- **Rapid rehire is still not durable recovery:** a separate DOJ IER telephone
  intervention in Disputanta, Virginia reports that an employer rehired a
  worker within a few days after rejecting proof of an automatic work-
  authorization extension and provided full back pay within a month. This adds
  a tighter implementation clock—barrier → intervention → rehire → payment—
  but the public record still lacks an independent receipt record, amount,
  later job stability, household effect, or worker meaning/action. It is now
  retained in the [IER telephone-intervention ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/ier-telephone-intervention-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json).
- **The remedy ladder needs both positive and negative counterexamples:** the
  [formal-remedy/lived-recovery audit](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/data/formal-remedy-lived-recovery-audit-v1.json)
  now compares thirteen named routes across worker, platform, recruitment,
  consumer-redress, and product-safety settings. It includes a reported
  next-day return plus approximately $420 back pay, aggregate payment-use
  routes, and a later recall stating that an earlier repair was ineffective.
  The calibrated conclusion is not that remedies fail: immediate practical
  improvement is observable in some cases, while adequate, durable, trusted
  same-person recovery remains much less observable.
- **Work access can be repaired by changing the social setting:** a June 2023
  DOJ IER case reports that an employment-document problem was resolved and the
  worker was offered a job at an alternate location so she would not have to
  work with the people who rejected her documents. This is evidence that a
  remedy may involve dignity, relationship, and location—not only back pay or
  reinstatement. The public record does not show whether she accepted the
  location, started work, or experienced durable safety, trust, or recovery;
  it is retained in the [IER telephone-intervention ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/ier-telephone-intervention-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json).
- **A remedy offer can coexist with non-return:** a November 2017 DOJ IER case
  reports that an employer offered reinstatement after an E-Verify suspension,
  but the worker ultimately decided not to return. This closes a worker-level
  non-return endpoint that aggregate remedy records cannot show. It does not
  reveal whether the worker had another job, why she declined, whether the
  decision was freely chosen, or what happened later; non-return is not
  automatically successful exit or recovery. See the [IER telephone-intervention
  ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/ier-telephone-intervention-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json).
- **Non-return can coexist with a wage remedy decision:** an October 2016 DOJ
  IER case reports that a worker who was not interested in returning to the job
  nevertheless prompted an employer decision to pay back wages after an
  E-Verify intervention. The record does not give the amount, payment receipt,
  reason for non-return, or alternative income path. This separates past-work
  compensation from restoration of the job and from successful exit; it does
  not establish any of those later outcomes.
- **Reinstatement is not automatically usable reinstatement:** the [Alam Shah
  case](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-112.md) records
  injury, dismissal, limited English, interpreter needs, lack of driving
  access, a walkable local job, family-support obligations across borders, and
  fear of repeat dismissal alongside an order for reinstatement, continuity,
  translated communication, and lost pay. The order reaches further than a
  generic remedy count, but the public [later-outcome search](projects/ai-work-control/fwc-later-outcome-search-2026-09-19.md)
  still does not show actual return, payment, durable safety, or later security.
  Cultural access, mobility, and trust are part of whether a formal remedy
  works in lived terms.
- **Care and work:** among households with young children, lower-income
  households reported much more converted work-time loss when care was
  inadequate (114.6 days below poverty versus 43.3 days above 400% of
  poverty). Responses included leave, cutting hours, quitting, non-search, and
  working while supervising. Separately, stable annual time-use averages
  conceal a selected SIPP group in which childcare-related work prevention
  coexists with much higher following rent and utility hardship (43.65% and
  32.39% in the small stable-SNAP/work-prevention cell). Provider bonuses and
  retention incentives still do not prove a dependable staffed slot; time loss
  is not automatically lost earnings or a political effect.
- **Stable hours, moving earnings:** the same-person SIPP subgroup screen
  shows hours staying unchanged for roughly 85%–96% of adjacent-month pairs
  across resource and household-context groups, while earnings move up or
  down much more often—especially in low-resource households with children.
  “Stable work” can therefore hide changing pay, job mix, schedule quality, or
  unpaid household substitution. Children and SNAP status condition the
  pattern, but do not identify a care event or a SNAP effect; job quality,
  protection, remedy, trust, and action remain open.
- **Aging and unpaid care:** BLS reports about 38.2 million people providing
  unpaid eldercare in 2023–24; care providers spent about 3.88 hours on care
  days, or 2.84 hours among employed providers, and roughly 51.6% provided
  care daily or several times a week. This establishes a large social input,
  not a burden or outcome by itself: the missing links are replacement options,
  schedule control, earnings, health, recipient safety, and whether employers
  or public programs absorb any of the risk.
- **Child-care pressure becomes unequal work loss:** the Census 2025
  child-care paper reports that, among households with a child age 0–13,
  affected households below the poverty line reported an estimated 114.6
  converted work-loss days on average, versus 43.3 days above 400% of the
  poverty line. The response is a menu—paid or unpaid leave, cutting hours,
  quitting, not searching, or supervising children while working—not one
  ordered pathway. This shows unequal practical room, not a causal income
  effect or a later-recovery estimate; provider availability, subsidy, wages,
  job quality, child outcome, and duration remain open. See the [Census
  child-care work-adaptation finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-001.md).
- **Child-care incentives are not dependable capacity:** GAO reports about
  19,000 DOD nonappropriated-fund child-care workers serving nearly 172,000
  children in FY2024, alongside 4,955 reported recruitment or retention
  incentives and continued lengthy waitlists. A fee discount can lower price
  without adding a staffed slot; a retention allowance can preserve a worker
  without shortening a family's wait. This is a provider-side capacity and
  intervention record, not evidence of completed family care, preserved
  parent work, or readiness. See the [GAO child-care capacity
  finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-005.md).
- **Family care is infrastructure, not a free buffer:** the care-system
  comparison places kinship care, childcare, adult caregiving, and assisted
  living on different evidence layers. About 2.4 million children lived in
  kinship families in 2023, while GAO identified at least $12 billion in
  Medicaid and traditional Medicare assisted-living spending in 2024 and
  Medicaid assisted-living coverage in 44 states. Those figures show scale and
  financing routes, not a usable slot or relief for a particular family. A
  relative may still supply housing, transport, supervision, advocacy, and
  emotional work after formal coverage appears. The missing link is who
  absorbs that burden, whether the arrangement is sustainable, and whether
  health, work, safety, and family continuity improve. See the [family-care
  capacity synthesis](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-004.md).
- **Consumer direction can improve several short-run currencies without
  proving durable household recovery:** the randomized Cash and Counseling
  demonstrations gave Medicaid beneficiaries more control over care routes and
  were followed by better selected service-satisfaction and unmet-need
  measures; informal caregivers showed selected wellbeing and perceived
  financial-strain improvements; and directly hired workers generally reported
  positive satisfaction with conditions and compensation. These are three
  separate units, not one pooled household result. The same intervention could
  also leave unpaid care, coordination, backup-care, training, benefits,
  worker retention, and recipient-care continuity unresolved. Randomized
  assignment strengthens the intervention-to-short-run-outcome segment, but
  take-up, allowance spending, completed care, long-run security, trust, and
  exit remain open. See the [multi-currency Cash and Counseling synthesis](projects/us-aging-care-strain/cash-counseling-multi-currency-realization-synthesis-v1.md).
- **Disability and usable work routes:** in PY2023 DOL programs, 60% of Adult
  participants reporting disabilities were employed in the second quarter
  after exit versus 76% reporting no disability; Adult training use was 33%
  versus 47%, and median second-quarter earnings among those employed were
  $7,048 versus $8,872. GAO also documents interpreter, technology, and
  training-access gaps. This is a bounded post-exit comparison, not proof that
  accessibility or accommodation caused it. It shows why formal program
  participation must be separated from completed service, placement, retention,
  wages, workplace control, and durable security.
- **Care capacity is not reachable care:** the current HRSA primary-care HPSA
  file contains 20,905 designated rows across 2,888 counties, but FTE is
  populated for only 16,582 rows and for just 543 of 4,866 facility/site rows;
  the file does not show active hours, new-patient acceptance, wait, travel,
  price, or continuity. A shortage designation or reported FTE is therefore
  an institutional capacity signal, not a resident-level appointment or usable
  alternative; county health outcomes cannot be assigned to a named site.
- **Employment is not automatically security:** in 2024, about 13.8 million
  Medicaid-enrolled adults and 10.6 million adults in SNAP households worked
  at some point; roughly 88% in each group worked in the private sector.
  Working alongside public benefits is not proof of employer shifting or
  inadequate wages, but it shows why “has a job” is not the same endpoint as
  predictable hours, employer coverage, adequate income, household security,
  or bargaining power.
- **Energy insecurity as household allocation:** EIA estimates 43.56 million
  primary-residence homes with at least one reported energy-insecurity
  condition; 32.89 million reported reducing or forgoing food or medicine to
  pay energy costs, 17.55 million reported leaving home at an unhealthy
  temperature, and 16.19 million received a disconnect or delivery-stop
  notice. These are overlapping housing-unit conditions, not a sequence or a
  shutoff rate, but they show energy pressure becoming food, medicine,
  temperature, and service-continuity trade-offs.
- **LIHEAP reach versus durable restoration:** the preliminary national
  performance route reports 1.59 million bill-payment-prevention occurrences
  and 159,320 post-disconnection restoration occurrences in FY2023; by FY2025
  those counts were 1.85 million and 171,077. They are program-reported
  occurrences, not unique households or success rates. Payment receipt,
  reconnection duration, next-bill affordability, repeat crisis, health/food
  protection, trust, and action remain unobserved.
- **Local administration changes the visible energy route:** in the preliminary
  FY2025 state diagnostic, restoration occurrences per served household ranged
  from 0.0007 in New York to 0.0703 in California, while prevention
  occurrences per served household ranged from 0.0796 to 0.6259 across those
  states. These are not effectiveness rates: occurrences can repeat, reporting
  rules and program design differ, and the denominator is not households at
  risk. The safe finding is that local administrative capacity shapes what is
  visible before the atlas can observe next-bill stability or durable recovery.
- **CFPB Ombudsman:** one redress recipient’s expired check leads to effort,
  escalation, and facilitated reissuance. The public record stops before cash
  receipt, adequacy, trust, or exit.
- **Remedy visibility is still not remedy recovery:** across the current 97
retained event ledgers and 302 events, 85 events carry an explicit remedy
  stage; 34 are marked as reported received, but none has verified recipient
  receipt, verified durability, or a defined outcome follow-up in the portfolio
  audit. In rough portfolio terms, that is about 28.1% with an explicit remedy
  stage and 11.3% marked institution-reported received; the other 217 events
  (about 71.9%) have no explicit remedy-stage field. These are coverage proportions,
  not success or failure rates. The result is still important: public records
  usually stop at order, implementation, or reported distribution rather than
  showing whether the affected person regained a protected outcome or practical
  choice. See
  the [remedy-stage portfolio audit](data/us-broad-remedy-stage-coverage-audit-2026-09-18.json).
  The stage is also source-dependent across employment, product-safety,
  customer-recourse, and health-cost records. This is a documentation-pattern
  comparison, not evidence that one domain remedies people better than another.
- **A positive receipt comparator now sits beside the open cases:** the official
  Atkins follow-up reports that all ten named truck drivers received back-pay
  amounts after court-held funds were released. This closes a reported
  worker-level receipt stage, but not independent payment confirmation,
  adequacy, household recovery, later work, trust, or exit. Receipt is real
  progress in the chain; it is not the end of the chain.
- **Prior receipt is a meaningful but incomplete consumer endpoint:** the FTC's
  Yellowstone refund record says 3,759 small businesses receiving a third
  distribution had already cashed a previous refund check. That is stronger
  than a payment offer or an aggregate “issued” amount: it establishes a
  recipient-side cashing condition and a repeat-remedy path. It still does not
  show that the third check was cashed, that the original account problem was
  corrected, or that the businesses recovered, stayed, switched financing, or
  changed trust. See the [Yellowstone refund ledger](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/data/ftc-yellowstone-refund-event-ledger-v1.json).
- **Payment proof is not the same as lived recovery:** the DOL's PAID program
  requires participating employers to pay back wages within 15 days and give
  WHD proof of payment, which is a stronger institutional checkpoint than the
  Workers Owed Wages route's public “money waiting to be claimed” state. Even
  that stronger checkpoint does not publicly show that a worker cashed the
  payment, found it adequate, regained household security, stayed in the job,
  or changed trust or exit behavior. The paired [wage-reachability layer](projects/ai-work-control/dol-workers-owed-wages-reachability-layer-v1.md)
  now retains this as a positive administrative counterexample rather than a
  worker recovery estimate.
- **The payment rail can itself become a gate:** the current Workers Owed Wages
  instructions tell paper-check recipients to update their information because
  payments are electronic, and require contact details, identity documents,
  login access, and a signed claim before processing. This may improve delivery
  for some workers while adding digital and documentation work for others; the
  public route still does not show recipient completion, delivery, cashing, or
  adequacy. See the [expanded wage-reachability ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/dol-workers-owed-wages-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json).
- **Employment remedy can repair status as well as cash:** the expanded [IER
  intervention ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/ier-telephone-intervention-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json)
  now includes agency-reported cases where rehire, back pay, prior wage, and
  seniority were restored. One DACA case reports $4,712.50 in back pay and
  restored seniority; a California case reports $1,650 and seniority repair.
  These are stronger than an order-only record, but still do not independently
  verify receipt, benefit use, durable employment, household recovery, or
  trust.
- **A rare near-complete exit-and-trust endpoint:** the [Mudiyanselage FWC
  finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-113.md) and
  [structured ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-mudiyanselage-underpayment-forced-exit-compensation-event-ledger-v1.json)
  records persistent wage delay, repeated complaints, Fair Work Ombudsman
  contact, forced exit, new employment, a documented $4,000 partial payment,
  and a $27,424.88 gross compensation order. The worker declined reinstatement
  because of broken trust and emotional stress. This is unusually close to the
  lived chain, but remaining unpaid wages at the hearing and missing later
  financial and employment follow-up prevent calling it full recovery.

## Recent cross-domain rotations

The latest additions make the same conversion problem visible across several
domains rather than only in consumer remedies:

- **AI and work:** firm adoption and worker access/control are now separated
  from productivity, pay, bargaining, and household security. The World Bank
  capability layer extends this to infrastructure, replaceability, and state
  power without treating adoption as leverage.
- **Public benefits and work:** GAO shows that Medicaid/SNAP participation and
  employment coexist at large scale. Work is therefore not a reliable proxy for
  material independence, and employer concentration is not proof of intent or
  benefit shifting.
- **Disability and access:** workforce-program eligibility can coexist with
  lower training use and post-exit employment/earnings, while health-care
  coverage can coexist with inaccessible equipment, communication, digital, or
  provider interfaces. Formal capacity and usable capacity are distinct.
- **Platform remedy:** Grubhub combines court authority, aggregate payments,
  and prospective controls across drivers, diners, and restaurants, but public
  evidence still stops before individual receipt, restored income/access/
  visibility, switching, trust, and exit.
- **Cultural meaning and political efficacy:** Hispanic identity can be central
  to self-understanding while its opportunity meaning is experienced as help,
  harm, or no difference; young adults under greater financial strain can feel
  less politically efficacious while remaining conditionally willing to act if
  voice, community change, and candidate follow-through seem credible. Identity
  and hardship therefore shape interpretation and readiness through different
  routes; neither is a direct proxy for action.
- **Digital control:** Walmart's request metrics add a real administrative
  stage—submitted, completed, denied, and timed access/deletion/opt-out
  requests—without showing verified data change, residual exposure, or exit.
- **Geopolitical operation:** NATO's ISR Force adds a real multi-country
  operating stage with reported flight hours and intelligence products. It is
  stronger than an announcement or contract, but still stops before product-
  level decision effect, host-community acceptance, adversary response, or
  durable deterrence.

The cross-domain conclusion is stronger than any single statistic: systems
usually document the input or institutional response more clearly than the
person's usable alternative, protected outcome, or later meaning and action.

The cross-domain [remedy implementation and recovery synthesis](US-REMEDY-IMPLEMENTATION-RECOVERY-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
maps these endpoints without pooling them: access, money, status, and choice
can move on different clocks.

Together these bridges show that the missing piece is not “more opinion data.”
It is the middle of the lived chain: usable alternatives, effort, control,
verified remedy, durable outcome, and the meaning/action that follows.
The current storage-light qualification screen reviewed 20 retained routes and
found zero with all of those stages in one stable unit. Eight routes expose an
alternative or non-use field, and three expose verified remedy receipt or a
defined outcome follow-up; none also closes the later meaning/action, recovery,
or exit requirement. A later 17-route strict screen applied the same practical-
optionality rule to a narrower explicitly retained candidate set and likewise
found zero qualifying episodes. These are measured data boundaries, not
evidence that remedies or exits never occurred.

The consumer lane now makes that middle gap especially visible: households can
actively search for a way out while the available records still cannot show
whether a provider, employer, agency, or platform supplied a workable route.
More searching is therefore a sign of constrained adaptation, not evidence of
freedom or recovery.

The failed-alternative-employment case sharpens this boundary at the
individual level: a worker searched for another job, pursued reinstatement, and
received a compensation order, yet the public record did not show secured
alternative work, payment receipt, or later household recovery. Search is an
observed action; a usable option is a later outcome that still has to be shown.

### What the latest broad rotation added

The reader's latest rotation widened the map across digital life, mobility,
political meaning, care, work, disaster, firms, and geopolitics:

- **Digital life:** phones can be useful infrastructure for information and
  connection while people experience excess use and only partial success when
  trying to cut back.
- **Place and care:** an airport route, assisted-living coverage, a workforce
  program, or a kinship-care arrangement can exist while time, accessibility,
  room-and-board, respite, or completion remain unresolved.
- **Work geography and public capacity:** remote or hybrid work can reduce an
  individual commute while shifting transit demand, fare revenue, downtown
  activity, housing geography, and planning needs. Private flexibility and
  collective service capacity therefore cannot be treated as the same outcome.
- **Family care and public support:** kinship care can preserve family and
  cultural continuity while moving housing, work, disability, legal, and
  emotional costs into relatives' capacity. The visible foster-care route is
  not the whole population receiving or supplying kinship care.
- **Availability and use:** an airport can have a rail or bus connection while
  timing, fare, accessibility, transfer, familiarity, parking, or shift fit
  keeps passengers and workers in cars or makes the trip practically harder.
- **Local activity and adequacy:** county health-sector job flow and a formal
  shortage designation can coexist. Employer growth is therefore not a
  substitute for a usable appointment, acceptable travel, coverage, completed
  care, or continuity.
- **Place type and sector mix:** metro and nonmetro counties can show different
  health, food, transport, and retail turnover profiles. A stronger aggregate
  retail signal does not establish stronger care, food access, wages, or local
  security.
- **Stable use is not stable security:** in the Ohio county panel, modeled
  routine-checkup rates were nearly unchanged across mobility groups while
  transportation, food, mental-distress, and insurance burdens differed. One
  utilization measure cannot stand in for the surrounding conditions of care.
- **Employer flow is not resident outcome:** nationwide counties with negative
  and nonnegative health-sector job flow had nearly identical modeled medians
  on several care and social-need measures. County employer dynamics alone do
  not identify provider capacity, completed care, or health improvement.
- **A candidate site is not completed care:** an Ohio HPSA row can share an
  organization identifier with a nearby health-center site, but the record
  still does not show designated-service identity, appointment availability,
  insurance acceptance, or actual use.
- **Shared cultural access and private making diverge:** in March HTOPS,
  expense difficulty aligned with lower exhibit attendance and lower perceived
  neighborhood arts opportunity, while personal art-making was not monotonic.
  Cultural participation cannot be reduced to either spending or deprivation.
- **Routine association and formal civic attendance diverge:** club or
  organization meeting participation fell across expense-difficulty groups,
  while public-meeting attendance was nonmonotonic. Civic withdrawal, issue
  engagement, and political efficacy need separate measures.
- **Geopolitical control need not be a cutoff:** the gallium/germanium case
  shows permission, disclosure, shipment, and price stages before any proven
  firm shortage or external concession. Export friction and strategic leverage
  are related but not identical outcomes.
- **Capability has multiple clocks:** the Piorun record shows reported foreign
  fielding before a later multilateral framework's planned deliveries. Demand,
  handover, operational use, alliance coordination, and deterrence are not
  interchangeable evidence.
- **Fielding is variant-specific:** LRASM C-1 fielding on the F/A-18E/F does
  not establish C-3 production completion, F-35 availability, maintained
  inventory, combat use, or external response. A system label can hide
  platform and successor transitions.
- **Remedies can fail forward:** the Sauna360 expansion reports that an earlier
  repair was ineffective and requires a second repair, including for prior
  recipients. A remedy milestone is not durable safety or recovered consumer
  control.
- **Repair route design matters:** the Honda Talon case offers free dealer
  inspection and repair, but the public record does not show notice, booking,
  completion, downtime, substitute mobility, or safe use. “Free” is not the
  same as reachable or completed.
- **Culture and public life:** identity can be connective and burdensome;
  diversity can be broadly approved while workplace action remains contested;
  civic interest, news trust, press freedom, and government trust form
  different combinations.
- **Work and firms:** employment can coexist with Medicaid/SNAP protection;
  disaster credit can preserve a firm while increasing debt; eligibility rules
  can shift opportunity toward larger firms; and profitability can coexist with
  weaker revenue room and uncertain local security.
- **Disaster and state power:** aid enters a scam-prone information
  environment, while domestic production, recycling plans, or mineral output
  can coexist with foreign dependence.
- **Different lived economies:** regional and demographic price baskets,
  earnings room, and spending directions can diverge before people form
  different explanations of the economy. A national average is therefore a
  starting point, not a shared experience.
- **Stacked household pressure:** the new SIPP same-person screen places
  utility difficulty, renter status, child-care-related work prevention, and
  following-month housing/food hardship on one adjacent-month frame. It adds a
  useful joint-constraint signal, but the small care-prevention cell and mixed
  clocks leave the bill, provider, payment, remedy, and recovery stages open.
- **AI and distribution:** industry output, productivity, cost, or labor-share
  signals can move before workers receive higher pay, consumers see lower
  prices, or households gain purchasing power. Measured growth is a middle
  stage, not proof of shared benefit.
- **Class and political meaning:** self-described working-class identity cuts
  across income, education, and occupation. It carries status and belonging,
  not just resources, so economic pressure cannot be translated directly into
  one political response.
- **Coalition structure:** two-party labels contain cross-pressured groups with
  different views of government, culture, the safety net, immigration, and
  foreign power. Party identity, political intensity, and action are separate
  stages.
- **Industrial capacity:** semiconductor awards and disbursements can advance
  while governance, qualified output, workforce retention, customer supply,
  and reduced foreign dependence remain unobserved. Strategic independence is
  a chain of realized stages, not a funding total.
- **Public-system and consumer control:** SNAP benefits can be issued while
  EBT account control is stolen, and a bank-partner transition can be repaired
  at the firm level while customer reimbursement and account correction remain
  unverified. Receipt, control, correction, and restored security are separate
  stages.
- **Material security has multiple clocks:** Hispanic poverty reached a record
  low while Medicaid coverage declined through the unwinding period; separately,
  the national median income reached a record while the upper tail rose and the
  lower tail did not move significantly. Population improvement, coverage
  continuity, percentile movement, and household room are not one outcome.
- **Care is both protection and cost transfer:** multigenerational living and
  paid/unpaid care can provide belonging and practical support while moving
  time, housing, mobility, and paid-work costs into families, often unevenly by
  gender. Care arrangements are adaptations, not standalone security scores.
- **Community capacity has a reciprocity gap:** willingness to help neighbors
  can exceed confidence that help will be available in return. Goodwill,
  expected support, delivered aid, adequacy, helper burden, and later trust are
  separate stages of social resilience.
- **Cultural and infrastructure legitimacy:** shared moral pessimism does not
  create a shared government remedy; Virginia can design large-load
  risk-allocation rules before actual payment or household bill effects are
  visible; and Poland can publish two JASSM-ER planning windows without a
  shipment or accepted capability. Public concern, rule design, schedule,
  delivery, and legitimacy must remain distinct.
- **Worker remedy and reachability:** Amazon Flex now supplies an
  administrator-reported aggregate receipt stage, while the Waheed appeal stay
  supplies a conditional interim-payment obligation. Neither proves the same
  worker received money, regained work access, or recovered security.
- **Platform remedy is not one clock:** the refreshed 27-case audit now places
  procedural defect with no restoration, substitute earnings beside a lost-pay
  order, and a stayed reactivation order with an interim-payment obligation in
  the same comparative remedy map. Those are different stages; none shows
  payment receipt, freely chosen alternative work, durable access, or practical
  exit.
- **Digital consumer control:** the Cash App enforcement record connects
  payment convenience to dispute-investigation failure, operational repair, and
  a large redress pool without exposing account-level correction or switching.
  A digital route can be easy to use and hard to repair.
- **Disaster persistence:** Los Angeles wildfire support now includes an
  extended housing clock and a mortgage bridge, but assistance duration is not
  completed repair, permanent housing, or a successful stay/return decision.
- **Worker voice and public safety:** the Texas asbestos case shows a safety
  complaint becoming both a retaliation remedy and a public-environmental
  signal; Southern Cross Care shows one payroll question triggering a wider
  self-audit and correction. Voice can reveal system failure while imposing
  risk on the person who speaks.
- **Status and labor control:** the Innovative Associates case shows how
  migration and tax-compliance pressure can be embedded in wage theft. Full
  reported back-pay repairs one formal loss without proving restored bargaining
  power, household security, or trust.

These are not one social index and do not describe one population. They are
cross-domain counterexamples to the same shortcut: treating availability,
receipt, use, adequacy, durability, legitimacy, and power as one outcome. The
next serious advance still requires a stable unit that carries at least three
adjacent stages through a dated event or valid follow-up.

## What would count as a true end-to-end result

The next promoted result must keep one stable unit and show, with dates or a
valid bounded window:

```text
condition or event
  -> available alternatives and selected/non-use route
  -> money, time, access, status, or control trade-off
  -> responsible actor and institutional response
  -> remedy receipt or defined protected/sacrificed outcome
  -> persistence, recovery, trust, action, switching, or exit
```

The local route selector confirms that no retained route currently qualifies
for full end-to-end promotion. MEPS is the largest event-linked surface, but
still lacks usable alternatives and event-specific remedy; CFPB/platform
records are strongest on institutional response, but lack recipient-level
receipt and durable outcome. The immediate executable routes are therefore
the authorized UAS/PSID acquisition gates or a compact public episode ledger
that supplies the missing fields. Until one of those routes is available, the
atlas should continue to publish bounded bridges and counterexamples, not
pretend that separate sources describe one household or one causal story.

## Bottom line

The atlas has moved beyond a list of themes. It has a repeatable explanation:
**unequal options plus institutional friction determine what pressure becomes**.
That pressure may become lost time, debt, reduced care, continued dependence,
consumer switching, silence, trust, organizing, or geopolitical constraint.
The next phase is to observe that conversion in one stable unit, including the
repair and meaning after the event.

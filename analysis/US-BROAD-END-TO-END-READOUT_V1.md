# US broad program: end-to-end readout v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-19  
**Purpose:** give a plain-language answer to what the broad atlas has found,
what it has not found, and what the next serious test must add. This is a
synthesis of validated local records; it is not a new pooled estimate.

## The goal in one sentence

The project is testing how a rule, price, technology, shock, or institutional
decision travels through ordinary life: who gets exposed, who has room to
adapt, who controls the next step, what people do, how institutions respond,
and whether the result becomes trust, identity, consumer behavior, political
action, or geopolitical power.

The central finding so far is:

> The same pressure does not create the same life. It creates different menus
> of possible action, because money, time, care, status, information, rights,
> social support, and the ability to leave are unevenly distributed.

## What the atlas actually supports

1. **Optionality is unequal.** People facing the same price, job rule, service
   failure, or public-system requirement may pay with cash, time, food, care,
   health, debt, dignity, or future flexibility. “Resilience” is too blunt a
   word unless we say what was sacrificed.
2. **Time is a real price.** Waiting, paperwork, travel, care, customer-service
   loops, and unstable schedules can make a formally affordable service
   practically unusable.
3. **Interfaces decide whether capacity becomes help.** A benefit, remedy,
   transit route, insurance policy, or platform appeal can exist while notice,
   documentation, accessibility, staffing, or follow-up prevents usable access.
4. **People respond through an ecology of actions.** They may stay, delay,
   borrow, reduce use, seek help, complain, switch, move, organize, vote, or
   remain silent. These actions are not interchangeable signs of trust or
   resistance; dependence can look like loyalty, and silence can be a cost.
5. **Meaning is contested.** Groups can see the same condition and disagree
   about who caused it, whether it is fair, which institution should fix it,
   and whether action is worth the effort. Material exposure alone does not
   determine cultural or political meaning.
6. **Capability and dependence can rise together.** A new factory, platform,
   data center, defense system, or public program can add capacity while also
   increasing dependence on one supplier, one interface, one foreign input, or
   one authority. Capacity becomes power only when actors can use, inspect,
   refuse, replace, or impose a cost.

## The 14 themes, in plain words

| Theme | What we can say now | Missing end-to-end link |
|---|---|---|
| Household room and consumption | Households adjust spending, saving, borrowing, food, care, and purchases unevenly. | A dated bill or price shock through the same household’s remedy and recovery. |
| Time as a hidden price | Care, travel, waiting, paperwork, and unstable schedules shift costs into time and work. | The same person’s time loss, trade-off, and later health, work, or civic outcome. |
| Consumer power and recourse | Visibility, complaint intake, routing, response, and remedy are separate stages. | A customer-level path from failure to usable correction, receipt, durability, and exit. |
| Platforms, data, and attention | Digital systems shape attention, privacy, identity checks, dependence, and perceived control. | Independent encounter or account records showing actual use, correction, switching, or non-use. |
| Work, control, and bargaining | Tools and AI can change output or monitoring without showing who receives the gain or control. | Worker-level links from changed control to pay, voice, household time, remedy, and exit. |
| Care, health, and social reproduction | Health and care costs are paid through bills, delayed care, unpaid family labor, work loss, and food or housing trade-offs. | A dated need with alternatives, treatment/payment response, and later family or health security. |
| Housing, place, and mobility | Insurance, energy, transport, local supply, and disaster exposure shape the ability to stay or move. | A matched place or household path from exposure to repair, mobility, belonging, and political response. |
| Unequal exposure and status | Race, age, disability, family structure, migration, tenure, and geography change exposure and usable options. | Mechanism-level evidence showing how unequal treatment changes remedy, recovery, or action. |
| Trust, identity, and cultural meaning | Shared conditions can produce different blame, dignity, belonging, moral judgments, and institutional trust. | Repeated or event-linked measures showing when an experience changes meaning or behavior. |
| Public systems and policy feedback | Eligibility, notice, take-up, interruption, appeal, and administrative burden shape real policy effects. | Same-episode receipt, adequacy, correction, security, trust, and later demand or exit. |
| Political judgment and collective action | Financial worry, institutional judgment, identity, contact, voting, and organizing are related but distinct. | A dated material experience tied to attribution and a distinct political action. |
| Firm, sector, and market power | Firms can shift risk through pricing, closure, eligibility, ownership, and labor strategy. | A common unit connecting firm decision to worker, customer, community, and response. |
| Infrastructure, technology, and dependency | Physical, technical, financial, and institutional complements determine whether capacity is usable. | Realized capability plus replaceability, local incidence, refusal power, and external response. |
| Geopolitical and state consequences | Domestic affordability, industrial capacity, technology, energy, finance, and legitimacy affect strategic room. | Evidence that a domestic capability or legitimacy change altered another actor’s choice. |

## The strongest same-unit bridges already in hand

These are real bridges, but each closes only part of the full chain:

- **MEPS:** a dated medical event is followed by later health and employment
  measures. A separate event-family comparison finds that reported denial or
  prior-authorization delay is followed by more same-round care delay, medical
  debt, collections, and bill problems across office, ER, and inpatient
  groups. Alternatives, claim-level remedy, trust, and action are missing.
- **Health-cost institutional friction:** in the broader MEPS comparison,
  respondents reporting insurance denial or prior-authorization delay had
  15.18% cost-related care delay, 24.68% medical debt, and 20.26% collection
  contact, versus 5.81%, 12.98%, and 9.77% without reported denial/delay.
  This is a route-specific association, not proof of claim causation or failed
  remedy; it shows how an institutional gate can coincide with both delayed
  care and downstream financial exposure. In the round-to-round follow-up,
  perceived health worsened for 25.11% of friction reporters versus 21.74% of
  non-reporters, while employment retention was much closer (56.09% versus
  57.28%). Health and work are therefore separate downstream currencies, not
  one composite harm measure.
- **MEPS episode boundary:** the retained MEPS event ledger has 18,457
  privacy-minimized person/event rows with hashed episode identifiers and
  month-precision first-event dates. A strict event-window screen can carry
  people into later health and employment rounds, but the public file does not
  identify the event-specific alternative, payment choice, appeal, verified
  remedy, or later trust/action/exit. A dated event therefore improves time
  ordering without becoming a household recovery story.
- **Persistent care foregoing:** in the 2024–2025 SHED recontact panel,
  respondents who continued foregoing care reported 41.783% medical debt,
  65.334% reduced savings, 36.389% increased borrowing, and 76.442% delayed
  major purchases. This is longitudinal descriptive evidence of a continuing
  household adaptation surface—not proof that the original skipped-care
  decision caused every later financial outcome, or that later recovery was
  absent.
- **SHED:** the same respondent’s financial condition and adaptations persist or
  reverse across waves. The trigger is not transaction-dated.
- **CCES:** an earlier job or benefit disruption is followed by institutional
  judgment, contact, and political action. The employer, remedy, and causal
  mechanism are not identified.
- **HTOPS:** linked respondents separate retrospective exposure, realized loss,
  reporting, sparse self-reported recovery, and later household context. The
  incident date, verified remedy, and durable security are missing. The later
  search fields add an adaptation signal: loss groups search more for jobs,
  food, benefits, and credit, but searching is not a usable alternative.
- **Material pressure and social connection:** the corrected March 2026 HTOPS
  screen shows frequent loneliness rising from 1.8% among respondents saying
  expenses were not difficult to 30.2% among those saying expenses were very
  difficult; low needed support rises from 14.0% to 40.9%. Yet frequent
  phone/video contact stays near 28%–30% in the first three groups and falls
  to 21.8% in the most pressured group. Contact frequency, felt connection,
  and support adequacy are therefore different currencies; this is a
  cross-sectional association, not a causal expense-to-loneliness result.
- **Financial access and switching:** the 2025 SHED banking layer reports
  6% of adults unbanked, rising to 21% below $25,000 income, while 12% of
  adults experiencing non-credit-card fraud changed bank or financial provider.
  Sixty-five percent of that fraud group lost money and 35% recovered only
  some of it. Provider contact, recovery, and switching are therefore separate
  routes: formal access does not guarantee liquidity, and non-switching does
  not prove satisfaction or trust.
- **Monetary signals versus household action:** a randomized information study
  found that a 25-basis-point rate cut changed one-year inflation expectations
  by about 0.833 percentage points overall, while a separate household study
  found only small intended durable-spending responses to a 1-point rate
  surprise (about −0.05 points for one positive-surprise group and +0.08 for a
  negative-surprise group). These are different designs and outcomes, not one
  transmission coefficient: expectation movement is not a realized payment,
  saving, debt, care, or political response. Borrower/saver exposure,
  contract resets, liquidity, and later trust remain open.
- **Help-seeking and status:** the WBNS charitable-food layer reports food
  assistance receipt for 16.7% of working-age adults, while among adults with
  unmet need, 39.9% were not comfortable getting help and 35.2% cited
  transportation barriers. A route can therefore exist yet remain socially,
  physically, or informationally unusable; nonreceipt is not proof of no need,
  and receipt is not proof of restored food security or dignity.
- **Availability is not use:** across the interface synthesis, airport
  transit use ranged from 4% to 19% at the airports with usable reports, while
  smartphone users reported both connection benefits and difficulty reducing
  use: 53% said they spent too much time, and only 25% of those who tried to
  cut back felt extremely or very successful. A route, benefit, device, or
  facility becomes socially useful only when its time, price, accessibility,
  information, reliability, and alternatives fit the user; nominal capacity
  is not completed use, protected outcome, or practical exit.
- **Public capacity versus usable help:** states held very different fiscal
  cushions in the 2025 Fiscal 50 comparison, from about 320 operating days in
  Wyoming to zero rainy-day days in New Jersey. That is state operating room,
  not a resident benefit. Budget authority still has to become a program,
  notice, eligibility route, timely decision, and usable remedy before it
  changes household security or public trust.
- **Disaster recovery and legitimacy:** after Hurricane Harvey, 70% of
  affected residents said life was largely back to normal nearly a year later,
  but 30% still reported disruption and 42% said they were not getting the
  help they needed. Only 54% were confident relief benefited those who needed
  it most. Recovery progress, aid reachability, and legitimacy therefore move
  on different clocks; an application or public award is not receipt, adequacy,
  or restored trust.
- **Firm survival and household/local consequences:** a threshold-based SBA
  disaster-credit study estimates a 13.2-point reduction in firm exit and
  roughly 18% higher employment for the studied firms, but also about $17,712
  more non-SBA private debt. The result is strong firm-level recovery evidence,
  not proof of worker security, owner-household recovery, affordable customer
  service, or lower public risk: survival can preserve an option while moving
  obligations elsewhere.
- **Local customer access after disaster:** after major Harvey store closures,
  affected cardholders traveled about 15% farther to shop; entrants generated
  modeled consumer surplus but did not fully replace exits in the hardest-hit
  neighborhoods. Average metro welfare loss looked small (about 0.29% of
  pre-storm retail spending) while tract-level losses were larger. Aggregate
  prices and firm survival can therefore look stable while time, distance, and
  local access worsen for particular neighborhoods.
- **Cash App enforcement:** the CFPB record documents fraud/dispute-process
  failures, required operational correction, and up to $120 million in
  consumer redress. It advances the route from reported loss to a named
  institutional response, but does not show any individual payment, account
  correction, repeat effort, restored security, trust, or exit. A redress pool
  is therefore not a consumer recovery rate.
- **Administrative speed is not consumer remedy:** the CFPB complaint route
  exposes an administrative `timely` label and a separate receipt-to-company
  routing interval. In a 2025 student-loan sample, 59.8% were labeled timely,
  while 21.6% took more than 24 hours to route and the 90th-percentile lag was
  about 27 days. The label is not customer waiting time, company correction,
  payment, account repair, or practical exit; API field and vintage changes
  must also be treated as missingness rather than silently as zero.
- **Small firms as infrastructure consumers:** the FTC reports 5,588 checks
  totaling more than $2.6 million sent to businesses charged hidden or
  unauthorized payment-processing fees, including early-termination and
  recurring “zombie” charges. The case shows how a processor can make formal
  provider choice costly, while checks sent, checks cashed, processor
  switching, restored transaction capacity, and business continuity remain
  separate clocks. This links market power to local-firm practical exit, but
  does not establish the business-level burden or recovery rate.
- **Marketplace power:** an FTC/state complaint alleges that Amazon's hidden
  advertising-auction surcharges affected more than one million advertisers,
  including more than 500,000 small and medium businesses, and that higher
  advertising costs were largely passed to consumers. This is an allegation,
  not an adjudicated finding or a price estimate; the missing bridge is seller
  cost → bid/visibility → product price or assortment → actual purchase and
  household burden. Visibility, seller participation, and consumer welfare
  must remain separate outcomes.
- **Aggregate remedy versus household recovery:** the FTC Amazon Prime record
  reports more than $845 million issued and about $235 million accepted across
  payment phases, but no customer-level denominator, receipt, delay, remaining
  loss, continued use, switching, or household outcome. Accepted dollars show
  that some money reached consumers; they do not establish a recovery rate or
  that the remedy restored the time, trust, or choice lost in the subscription
  episode.
- **SIPP SNAP:** adjacent-month transitions show that benefit exit is not
  automatically improvement: 76.2% of observed exits had a lower next-month
  income-to-poverty ratio, and only about half of transitions had a classified
  reason. Entry and exit are therefore participation states, not proof of
  adequacy, voluntary choice, or restored security.
- **Protective non-use:** among 1,036 adults in immigrant families with
  children, 23% reported avoiding at least one essential activity because of
  immigration concerns; almost one in five reported going without public
  benefits for that reason, and 10% reported not visiting a doctor, clinic, or
  hospital. This is a direct access-behavior surface, not a vote or generalized
  distrust measure: non-use can protect against perceived risk while also
  sacrificing care, income, safety, or public support.
- **Belonging has separate currencies:** the migration/place comparison finds
  that fast-growing counties had lower reported federal trust than the
  slowest-growth quartile (35.251% versus 41.927%), while state trust, civic
  action, and reported voting did not move as one bundle. Separately,
  immigrant-family respondents reported risk-linked non-use of essential
  routes. Demographic presence, local capacity, institutional judgment, civic
  action, and safe service use therefore cannot be collapsed into one
  “integration” measure; these are different units and not a causal chain.
- **Ohio place panel:** counties with higher zero-vehicle shares have nearly
  the same modeled routine-checkup median (76.95% versus 77.05%) but higher
  transportation insecurity, food insecurity, mental distress, and lack of
  insurance. Nominal service use can therefore coexist with weaker practical
  room; this is an ecological county comparison, not a resident-level effect.
- **Housing security:** the 2025 housing screen reports 23% of renters behind
  on rent during the prior year, about 33% below $50,000 income, while 6% of
  homeowners reported no homeowners insurance and the share was about 20%
  below $50,000. A separate energy model puts the lowest-income-band burden
  proxy at 28.73% versus 1.74% in the highest band. These are different
  universes and clocks, but together show why staying housed is not the same
  as staying protected: payment, coverage, energy, repair, and mobility room
  can fail at different stages.
- **Insurance recourse is not repair:** New York's 2025 DFS assistance unit
  received 39,443 insurance complaints, closed 39,064, and reported more than
  $121 million in recovery. In the mixed property-casualty/service-contract
  category, 498 of 2,869 closed cases were labeled positive outcomes, with
  about $17.95 million reported recovered. These figures make routing and
  reported recovery visible, but do not show adequate claim payment, completed
  repair, renewed coverage, temporary housing, or whether the household stayed
  or moved.
- **Migration, belonging, and political meaning:** the joint county screen
  does not produce a simple growth or foreign-born-share explanation for
  federal trust; the lowest trust cell is high-growth/mid-nativity, while
  civic action stays comparatively stable. In the separate ANES respondent
  layer, people distinguish economic effects, job competition, citizenship,
  trust, and vote rather than holding one single immigration attitude. This
  is the intended end-to-end boundary: place conditions can frame exposure,
  but they cannot substitute for a person's reported meaning, attribution,
  belonging, or action.
- **Infrastructure and state power:** defense authorization and data-center
  capacity records show commitment and buildout stages, but not necessarily
  delivered capability, local control, replaceability, public benefit, or
  changed behavior by another state. Capacity and dependence can rise together.
- **CHIPS implementation:** GAO reports about $50 billion in semiconductor
  programs, 49 projects across 24 companies, and $13.1 billion disbursed by
  April 2026—about 42% of direct awards—alongside 24 completed milestones,
  125 in progress, and 9 past anticipated dates. Those figures show money,
  milestones, and governance activity, not accepted output, worker retention,
  customer delivery, or reduced foreign dependence. Industrial policy becomes
  strategic capability only after funding reaches qualified production,
  usable supply, replaceability, and an observed change in another actor's
  choices.
- **Critical minerals and strategic autonomy:** USGS import-reliance and
  processing-concentration data, alongside GAO substitution and recycling
  assessments, show why domestic projects are not automatically independence.
  Recycling may reduce some battery-material reliance on a multi-year horizon,
  while semiconductor substitutes face tighter performance constraints. A
  domestic facility can therefore add production capacity while refined
  feedstock, qualification, recovery infrastructure, or supplier control
  remains external. Capability, replaceability, and geopolitical leverage are
  separate stages.
- **AI, work, and control:** a randomized workplace-access study found changed
  email/time patterns, while the available record does not show whether that
  time became rest, care, pay, worker voice, or higher employer expectations.
  In the six-month intervention across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers,
  treated users spent about two fewer hours per week on email in the later
  period; a separate 5,179-agent customer-support experiment estimated about
  14% more issues resolved per hour. Time and output moved, but ownership of
  the gain, pace, pay, and household consequence remain unobserved.
  A separate executive survey adds a time-horizon counterexample: 90% reported
  no past-three-year firm employment/productivity impact, while expected
  next-three-year productivity was positive and employer/employee employment
  expectations differed. Presently unmeasured impact and future anticipated
  reallocation can coexist; expectations are not realized worker outcomes.
  A separate OECD employer survey reports 90% of US firms with 20+ employees
  using at least one algorithmic-management tool, 76% using ten or more, and
  55% monitoring conversation content or tone; those are employer reports, not
  worker exposure or proof of harmful use.
  Across AI use, synthetic contact, and companion-product exit, immediate
  behavior can change before authority, portability, correction, or durable
  exit is observed. Usefulness and worker/consumer control are therefore
  separate outcomes.
- **AI exit can become another engagement event:** a behavioral audit of 1,200
  companion-app farewells found at least one continuation tactic in 37% of
  farewells, while preregistered experiments with 3,458 US adults reported
  short-run post-goodbye engagement increases of up to 16× under some tested
  responses. This observes an exit-boundary mechanism, not durable lock-in or
  harm: curiosity, voluntary continuation, deletion, payment, human
  alternatives, remedy, and later wellbeing remain open.
- **CCES political follow-up:** respondents reporting earlier job loss had a
  large later insurance gap and lower local political action (23.9% versus
  31.6%), but Congress approval was not uniformly lower. Earlier lack of
  insurance showed the same action gap with near-similar favorable approval.
  Material insecurity, institutional judgment, and political action are
  therefore separate currencies; attribution and remedy remain unknown.
- **GSS meaning layer:** respondents who were not satisfied with their finances
  reported much lower generalized trust and fairness expectations than those
  who were satisfied. But reported financial direction also aligned with
  different vote intention patterns. Material position shapes meaning without
  determining one fixed cultural or political response.
- **Party-conditioned political meaning:** the ANES panel shows that reported
  financial worry and vote look different inside party-identity groups. Among
  independents, the very-worried group leaned Trump (74.9%) while the not-at-
  all-worried group leaned Harris (71.6%); strong partisans stayed highly
  concentrated with their identity across worry levels. This is descriptive,
  not an economic-voting causal result: identity can shape both reported worry
  and vote, and the dated material exposure is still missing.
- **Action styles are not one ladder:** Pew's 2025 public-life study groups
  people as Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, or Outsiders across politics,
  civic life, religion, and news. Mobilizers are more politically active, but
  Connectors can show broad community and news involvement without the same
  direct political activity; Outsiders are less engaged across the measured
  activities, but the study does not identify why. This keeps voting, contact,
  volunteering, attention, trust, and withdrawal as separate action routes.
  The clusters are descriptive, not identities or platform-caused effects;
  the missing end-to-end link is a dated condition, interpretation, chosen
  action, institutional response, and later legitimacy for the same person.
- **Platform remedy case:** one worker had access voluntarily restored, then
  received a formal reactivation order and an A$7,096.96 gross lost-pay order
  with a 14-day deadline. The record still does not show payment receipt,
  continued work, durable access, or household recovery. Enforceability is a
  stage between formal remedy and lived recovery.
- **Payment is not restoration:** in the FWC Meadley follow-up, the official
  account reports compensation payment, failed reinstatement, a worker's
  court-enforcement action, and a further $15,045 award plus interest and
  penalty. This is a stronger remedy-stage case because payment is observed
  while the original work relationship remains unrestored; later employment,
  household recovery, trust, and exit remain open.
- **Care and work:** among households with young children, lower-income
  households reported much more converted work-time loss when care was
  inadequate (114.6 days below poverty versus 43.3 days above 400% of
  poverty). Responses included leave, cutting hours, quitting, non-search, and
  working while supervising. Separately, stable annual time-use averages
  conceal a selected SIPP group in which childcare-related work prevention
  coexists with much higher following rent and utility hardship (43.65% and
  32.39% in the small stable-SNAP/work-prevention cell). Provider bonuses and
  retention incentives still do not prove a dependable staffed slot; time loss
  is not automatically lost earnings or a political effect.
- **Stable hours, moving earnings:** the same-person SIPP subgroup screen
  shows hours staying unchanged for roughly 85%–96% of adjacent-month pairs
  across resource and household-context groups, while earnings move up or
  down much more often—especially in low-resource households with children.
  “Stable work” can therefore hide changing pay, job mix, schedule quality, or
  unpaid household substitution. Children and SNAP status condition the
  pattern, but do not identify a care event or a SNAP effect; job quality,
  protection, remedy, trust, and action remain open.
- **Aging and unpaid care:** BLS reports about 38.2 million people providing
  unpaid eldercare in 2023–24; care providers spent about 3.88 hours on care
  days, or 2.84 hours among employed providers, and roughly 51.6% provided
  care daily or several times a week. This establishes a large social input,
  not a burden or outcome by itself: the missing links are replacement options,
  schedule control, earnings, health, recipient safety, and whether employers
  or public programs absorb any of the risk.
- **Disability and usable work routes:** in PY2023 DOL programs, 60% of Adult
  participants reporting disabilities were employed in the second quarter
  after exit versus 76% reporting no disability; Adult training use was 33%
  versus 47%. This is a bounded post-exit gap, not proof that accessibility or
  accommodation caused it. It shows why formal program participation must be
  separated from completed service, placement, retention, wages, workplace
  control, and durable security.
- **Employment is not automatically security:** in 2024, about 13.8 million
  Medicaid-enrolled adults and 10.6 million adults in SNAP households worked
  at some point; roughly 88% in each group worked in the private sector.
  Working alongside public benefits is not proof of employer shifting or
  inadequate wages, but it shows why “has a job” is not the same endpoint as
  predictable hours, employer coverage, adequate income, household security,
  or bargaining power.
- **Energy insecurity as household allocation:** EIA estimates 43.56 million
  primary-residence homes with at least one reported energy-insecurity
  condition; 32.89 million reported reducing or forgoing food or medicine to
  pay energy costs, 17.55 million reported leaving home at an unhealthy
  temperature, and 16.19 million received a disconnect or delivery-stop
  notice. These are overlapping housing-unit conditions, not a sequence or a
  shutoff rate, but they show energy pressure becoming food, medicine,
  temperature, and service-continuity trade-offs.
- **LIHEAP reach versus durable restoration:** the preliminary national
  performance route reports 1.59 million bill-payment-prevention occurrences
  and 159,320 post-disconnection restoration occurrences in FY2023; by FY2025
  those counts were 1.85 million and 171,077. They are program-reported
  occurrences, not unique households or success rates. Payment receipt,
  reconnection duration, next-bill affordability, repeat crisis, health/food
  protection, trust, and action remain unobserved.
- **CFPB Ombudsman:** one redress recipient’s expired check leads to effort,
  escalation, and facilitated reissuance. The public record stops before cash
  receipt, adequacy, trust, or exit.

Together these bridges show that the missing piece is not “more opinion data.”
It is the middle of the lived chain: usable alternatives, effort, control,
verified remedy, durable outcome, and the meaning/action that follows.

The consumer lane now makes that middle gap especially visible: households can
actively search for a way out while the available records still cannot show
whether a provider, employer, agency, or platform supplied a workable route.
More searching is therefore a sign of constrained adaptation, not evidence of
freedom or recovery.

## What would count as a true end-to-end result

The next promoted result must keep one stable unit and show, with dates or a
valid bounded window:

```text
condition or event
  -> available alternatives and selected/non-use route
  -> money, time, access, status, or control trade-off
  -> responsible actor and institutional response
  -> remedy receipt or defined protected/sacrificed outcome
  -> persistence, recovery, trust, action, switching, or exit
```

The immediate executable routes are the authorized UAS/PSID acquisition gates
or a compact public episode ledger that supplies the same fields. Until one of
those routes is available, the atlas should continue to publish bounded
bridges and counterexamples, not pretend that separate sources describe one
household or one causal story.

## Bottom line

The atlas has moved beyond a list of themes. It has a repeatable explanation:
**unequal options plus institutional friction determine what pressure becomes**.
That pressure may become lost time, debt, reduced care, continued dependence,
consumer switching, silence, trust, organizing, or geopolitical constraint.
The next phase is to observe that conversion in one stable unit, including the
repair and meaning after the event.

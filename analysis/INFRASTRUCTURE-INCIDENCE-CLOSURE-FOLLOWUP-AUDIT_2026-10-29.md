# Infrastructure incidence closure follow-up audit: Virginia large loads

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Purpose:** test whether repeated public-source acquisition closes the chain from computational load or transmission project to named payer, realized household/service effect and later acceptance  
**Status:** event, governance, modeled allocation and local route outcome are observed; named customer incidence and realized material outcome remain unverified

## Executive result

The second closure pass strengthens the governance finding but does not close
facility-to-customer incidence.

The public record now supports four distinct stages:

1. a computational large-load event can be operationally consequential;
2. PJM, FERC and Virginia SCC are making ride-through, information, tariff and
   direct-assignment rules more explicit;
3. the SCC can model a lower projected residential Rider T1 increase and create
   a prospective framework for direct-connect customers; and
4. a named transmission project can be redirected through local institutional
   non-consent, as Golden-Mars moved from preferred Route 4 to authorized Route
   3A.

The public record still does not provide, for the July 22 event or Golden-Mars:

- a named facility’s event participation and backup duration;
- facility-specific fuel, emissions or operating cost;
- an executed direct-connect agreement and paid amount;
- a project-to-customer allocation for Golden-Mars;
- a realized residential bill change attributable to a named project/customer;
- construction/energization and later local acceptance; or
- a household/service outcome tied to the infrastructure event.

This is a source-boundary result after repeated acquisition attempts, not a claim
that costs were not assigned, that households were harmed, or that the project
was not built.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Closed? | Safe conclusion | Do not infer |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Physical capability/load growth | Yes, system-level | Computational load is a grid-governance object | Exact marginal customer burden |
| Operating event | Yes, system-level | Large-load behavior can create operator-relevant conditions | Household outage, emissions or bill effect |
| Regulatory response | Yes | Institutions are moving accountability toward point of dependence | Effective compliance or reduced risk |
| Cost-allocation design | Partly | Virginia has explicit large-load and direct-assignment reform | Executed payment or complete cost causality |
| Local permission | Yes for route authorization | Non-consent can shift the authorized route and local exposure | Durable acceptance or no construction |
| Material incidence | No | Public sources do not close named payer/customer endpoint | That incidence is zero or universal |
| Durable legitimacy | No | Organized voice and route contestation are observable | Community-wide opinion or lasting trust |

## Bounded finding

> Large-load governance is becoming more operationally accountable, but public
> legitimacy still depends on a missing attribution bridge: which facility or
> project caused the burden, who paid, who was exposed, and what changed in the
> affected place or household.

The important cultural distinction is between **protection as a model** and
**protection as an experienced endpoint**. A modeled reduction in a typical bill
or a direct-assignment rule can change institutional expectations. It does not
yet tell a resident whether their bill changed, whether reliability improved,
or whether a specific data center paid for a specific requirement.

## Promotion decision

Promote the infrastructure lane to a **Tier 1 source-boundary finding** for
operational accountability and local route permission. Retain the broader
facility-to-bill and facility-to-household incidence claim as Tier 2.

Required future fields remain:

```text
facility/customer | event timestamp | actual load | backup duration
| fuel/emissions/cost | interconnection/project ID | assigned payer
| payment/bill record | service/locality effect | construction/energization
| public response | later acceptance or challenge
```

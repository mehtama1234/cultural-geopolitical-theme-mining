# US life: the big picture

**Updated:** 2026-09-21

## What this map is for

The full recovery brief is [US broad theme inventory v1](US-BROAD-THEME-INVENTORY_V1.md). It records the societal, cultural, consumer, political, company, sector, and geopolitical themes and the active five-bridge program. The household calendar is one measurement instrument inside that program, not its definition.

This is a reading map for US customer, household, work, money, and public life. It starts with ordinary things: a bill, a job, a service, a payment, a trip, a care duty, or a price seen at checkout.

The map asks what happens next. Who pays? Who waits? Who can leave? Who decides? Who gets the useful data, the asset, or the gain?

The [provisional societal trend register](US-PROVISIONAL-SOCIETAL-TRENDS_V1.md)
is the current synthesis of the separate evidence layers. It is where the
program names broad trends, while the matrices remain the control documents for
units, methods, uncertainty, and missing links.

For the plain-language answer to the full end-to-end goal, see the [US broad
end-to-end readout](US-BROAD-END-TO-END-READOUT_V1.md). It summarizes all 14
themes, the strongest same-unit bridges, and the exact middle links still
needed before a full lived chain can be claimed.

For the compact theme taxonomy, see the [14-theme societal atlas](US-BROAD-THEME-SUBTHEME-MAP_V1.md)
and the [plain-language atlas summary](US-BROAD-PLAIN-LANGUAGE-ATLAS-SUMMARY_V1.md).
The current place/public-system/political rotation is consolidated in the
[place, public systems, and political feedback synthesis](US-BROAD-PLACE-PUBLIC-SYSTEMS-POLITICAL-FEEDBACK-SYNTHESIS_V1.md).
The shortest organizing read is [the six deep tensions underneath the atlas](US-BROAD-MACRO-TENSIONS-SYNTHESIS_V1.md),
with its [machine-readable macro-tension register](data/us-broad-macro-tensions-v1.json).

The [broad trend-extraction protocol](US-BROAD-TREND-EXTRACTION-PROTOCOL_V1.md)
sets the common rule for that synthesis: surveys, administrative records,
firms, markets, media, cultural measures, and international records retain
their own units and roles while contributing to a shared arrow map.

The atlas now contains 14 umbrella themes, 790 validated machine-readable
records, 1,864 observations, 229 recorded cross-topic links, and 93 reading
paths. It also contains 97 event ledgers, 300 recorded events, and 450 event
arrows. The latest cross-domain memo contains 90 bounded signals. Those
numbers describe the map, not the size of a proven social problem.

The program-level finding that currently interprets the full cross-source map is
[Unequal optionality is the recurring structure behind pressure, adaptation, and
power](findings/us-broad-program-unequal-optionality-path-001.md). It is the
long-form reading layer for the recurring pattern below: the same nominal
condition produces different practical outcomes because cash, time, care,
skill, institutional route, ownership, and ability to exit are unevenly
distributed.

## Current broad rotation

The latest pass did not narrow the project to one question. It widened the
map across six connected surfaces:

- **Care and social reproduction:** unpaid eldercare, child-care constraints,
  disability access, and assisted-living finance show that formal coverage or
  family continuity can coexist with lost work time, travel, housing, and
  replacement-care burdens.
- **Workforce access and remedy reachability:** disability workforce programs
  can count participants, offer technology, and monitor providers while
  accommodation, disclosure, training completion, and durable employment stay
  uneven. Likewise, recovered wages can remain unreachable until a worker is
  located, verifies identity, completes a claim, and receives payment. Formal
  labor capacity and recovered money are not yet the same as usable work or
  restored household room.
- **Civic life and political meaning:** volunteering, neighbor help, voting
  barriers, economic worry, and institutional trust move on different clocks;
  participation can be limited by time, health, transport, registration, or
  dissatisfaction rather than one generic “apathy” mechanism. The Oregon
  Medicaid lottery adds a randomized coverage-to-turnout bridge: voting rose in
  the 2008 presidential election but did not show persistence through 2010.
- **Belonging and usable membership:** a person can be present in a place as a
  worker, customer, neighbor, or cultural participant without having safe
  access, language and documentation support, mobility, institutional voice, or
  durable civic influence. Presence, local demand, identity, service access,
  and political participation are related stages, not one belonging outcome.
- **Digital culture:** adult AI use, teen social/chatbot use, attention,
  privacy, perceived control, and attempted exit are separate outcomes. Short-
  run engagement or warmth does not establish durable trust, wellbeing, or
  successful exit.
- **AI-mediated political contact:** synthetic outgroup contact can lower the
  immediate barrier to a real cross-partisan conversation and shift short-run
  warmth or a costly choice, while the one-week persistence result is weak.
  AI can therefore act as a rehearsal space for civic contact without proving
  durable depolarization, cooperation, voting, or institutional trust.
- **AI as social infrastructure:** the new companion survey adds support,
  advice, disclosure, emotional connection, and perceived reliability to the
  digital map. AI can become part of a person's social-support system before
  we know whether it supplements people, substitutes for them, or becomes
  difficult to leave.
- **Religious and community institutions:** religious organizations are often
  viewed as helping bring people together and helping people in need, but that
  perceived social value is not the same as membership, service receipt,
  institutional trust, or acceptance of political influence. Community
  legitimacy and political legitimacy are separate currencies.
- **Platform control and attempted exit:** the AI-companion farewell evidence
  shows that a goodbye can become a short-run re-engagement event, while the
  GM/OnStar case shows that formal privacy controls can exist without verified
  use or deletion. The common missing link is whether a person can turn an
  attempted exit or formal control into durable reduced exposure, switching, or
  restored trust. The FTC's Shutterstock action adds a subscription version:
  recurring value can coexist with unclear renewal terms and a cancellation
  path that requires extra support effort. Enrollment, continued billing,
  attempted cancellation, refund receipt, and practical exit remain separate
  stages.
- **Digital remedy and observability:** Google's explicit-image route adds a
  simpler request, visible status, and repeat-protection setting. That makes
  the control surface more legible, but does not prove completed removal,
  cross-platform protection, survivor safety, or practical exit. Knowing that
  a remedy is pending is not the same as receiving the remedy.
- **Household security and public systems:** poverty, Medicaid continuity,
  SNAP receipt, EBT control, benefit adequacy, and later household security
  are distinct administrative and material clocks. The newer five-state linked
  SNAP study makes the implementation point sharper: work requirements reduced
  participation by about 7% without increasing labor supply, with lower-income
  people disproportionately screened out. A smaller caseload is therefore not
  automatically a successful work transition or improved household security.
  A Pennsylvania SNAP outreach experiment supplies the inverse warning:
  information and application help raised take-up, but the marginal enrollees
  received lower benefits and were less sick than average enrollees. More
  enrollment is not automatically better targeting or greater food security.
  ACA Medicaid expansion also appears to have increased SNAP and TANF
  participation among people already eligible, suggesting that one program’s
  doorway can lower the application cost of another without proving adequate
  household security.
  The Oregon Medicaid lottery also shows that public coverage can diffuse to a
  child in the household and briefly increase voting, while both effects fade
  on different clocks.
- **Firm, place, and infrastructure power:** business applications, realized
  openings/closures, service capacity, data-center load, and local incidence
  are separate stages. National capability or firm activity does not by itself
  establish local benefit, affordability, or replaceability.
- **Infrastructure and lived distribution:** Smart Path Connect now reaches a
  populated historical project-accounting stage and an official customer-facing
  bill-savings claim. It still does not reach retail pass-through, observed
  household bills, equal ratepayer benefit, or durable reliability. Physical
  completion, accounting, and household consequence remain separate clocks.
  The community-solar rotation adds a public-benefit-to-infrastructure route:
  DOE's Clean Energy Connector uses LIHEAP eligibility and opt-in enrollment
  to route households toward subscriptions with a required minimum bill-credit
  discount, while DC Solar for All reports program-level household reach and
  average savings. A DCPSC filing adds a later project-level stage: every
  listed subscriber on four selected project rows was reported as receiving a
  monthly credit. That is stronger than a design, capacity, or average-savings
  claim, but still does not establish each household's credit amount, net bill
  effect, persistence, or energy security; the [reach-scope audit](projects/us-repeat-energy-crises/solar-for-all-reach-scope-audit-v1.md)
  keeps those denominators and clocks separate.
  The AEP Texas transmission loan adds a closed-financing stage: DOE reports a
  $3.26 billion loan for roughly 2,800 miles of transmission work and projects
  capacity, reliability, jobs, and customer savings. That is more concrete than
  a funding announcement, but construction, rate allocation, actual bills,
  local burden, and equal household benefit remain unobserved.
- **Geopolitical legitimacy:** the Germany/Lithuania case now separates German
  strategic support, German knowledge of the specific mission, Lithuanian
  host-country approval, alliance posture, and deterrence. Broad support for a
  direction is not the same as informed consent for a named operation or proof
  that another state changed behavior. The first Freedom Shield exercise adds
  a real multinational training/readiness stage, but not full capability or
  deterrence.
- **External reception of U.S. power:** the latest Pew cross-national layer
  adds a separate currency from formal alliance membership. A median 37% in
  36 countries viewed the U.S. favorably, and only 32% said it considered
  countries like theirs' interests. Capability and partnership can therefore
  persist while perceived reliability and respect weaken; the conversion to
  access, burden sharing, or policy change remains open.
- **Capability realization and geopolitical leverage:** the Polish F-35 record
  reaches arrival, formal acceptance, first-pilot training, and an eight-aircraft
  count, while the JASSM-ER order remains publicly scheduled without a qualifying
  delivery or acceptance record. Procurement, delivery, readiness, operation,
  public legitimacy, deterrence, and changed external behavior are separate
  clocks.
- **Infrastructure voice and legitimacy:** the Virginia Golden-Mars case shows
  that hundreds of speakers and written comments can enter a route decision,
  while a school-board land-control constraint can later move the project from
  conditional Route 4 to final Route 3A. Neither public voice nor route
  substitution proves resident agreement, compensation, measured local
  effects, or durable acceptance.
- **Remedy and institutional legitimacy:** a hearing, process review, payment,
  reissue, or reported repair can be real without proving receipt, adequacy,
  restored access, durable protection, trust, or willingness to return.
- **Platform choice and market power:** a platform-owned product can improve
  today's fit, price, delivery, or search outcome while the same gatekeeper
  still shapes tomorrow's seller access and choice set. Immediate consumer
  surplus and durable competition are separate stages; the Amazon field
  experiment does not settle long-run foreclosure or dependence.
- **Unequal price shocks and hidden consumer costs:** a common gasoline shock
  can produce different inflation exposure and spending responses by income
  and place. Financial marketing can add another layer: an apparently
  favorable offer may impose application time or credit exposure before a
  refund or correction exists. Price, liquidity, credit, substitution, and
  recovery are separate household currencies.

These additions reinforce the same program-wide rule: the atlas is looking for
conversions between material conditions, practical options, institutional
response, cultural meaning, political action, and state or firm power. It is
not treating the newest source, theme, or headline as the definition of the
whole project.

The latest rotation sharpens these mechanisms that now cut across the 14 themes:

- **Support is wider than the household.** Family, neighbors, public programs,
  platforms, employers, and emergency systems can keep life moving, but the
  map must ask who performs the work, who controls the route, and whether the
  support is reciprocal and durable.
- **Formal completion is not underlying control.** A privacy request can be
  marked complete while residual data, individual verification, trust, or
  practical exit remain unknown. A transmission refund can reach a formal
  federal stage while the customer bill credit and adequacy remain unverified.
- **Resolution is not recovery.** A later employment-status resolution or a
  reported remedy can be real without showing job quality, treatment
  continuity, debt relief, household security, or durable protection.
- **Material pressure translates selectively into politics.** Pressure is more
  consistently associated with search and navigation effort than with one
  uniform change in AI use, institutional confidence, or political action.
- **Financial worry enters politics through meaning and identity.** Worry,
  trust, and reported vote can be linked in a respondent panel, but the path
  is non-monotonic and confounded by identity, information, and attribution.
- **Infrastructure bargains become public bargains.** Large loads, refunds,
  reliability investments, and strategic capacity create visible
  institutional stages before the map can show who paid, who received the
  gain, or who can refuse and switch.
- **Public benefit receipt is not material security.** SNAP and charitable-food
  routes show that eligibility, route completion, receipt, adequacy, and later
  household security are distinct; interruption and unmet need can persist even
  when a support route is formally available.
- **Interactional exit is not practical exit.** A platform can contest a
  conversational goodbye or expose a deletion route without the atlas knowing
  whether the person cancelled, switched, exported data, or regained durable
  control.
- **Social infrastructure is becoming hybrid.** Family and chosen ties can
  coexist with public programs, firms, and AI tools as support routes. The
  atlas must distinguish additional access from human substitution, adequacy,
  reciprocity, privacy, and replaceability.
- **Market replacement is not practical access.** New firms or aggregate
  surplus can coexist with longer travel and localized loss after closures.
  Entry, price stability, and modeled welfare do not by themselves show that a
  household regained a nearby, affordable, usable option.
- **Remedy responsiveness can coexist with dependence.** Review, restoration,
  payment, recall, or reissue can improve a formal route while leaving the
  affected person tied to the same provider and without a workable alternative.
  Institutional action, receipt, adequacy, and practical exit are separate.
- **Civic capacity can fall without a uniform change in approval.** Job loss or
  lack of insurance can be associated with less contact or local political
  action while institutional approval moves differently. Material insecurity
  may reduce the ability to act without producing one predictable judgment.
- **Protective non-use is not the same as disengagement.** Avoiding a benefit,
  clinic, police route, complaint, platform, or public activity can protect
  status, privacy, dignity, time, or safety while sacrificing care, income,
  security, belonging, or future recourse. The atlas must record need and
  anticipated risk before interpreting silence.
- **Staying is not automatically security.** Remaining in a home, job,
  provider, or place can preserve continuity while insurance, cash room,
  repair, safety, care, or mobility deteriorate. “Still there” is not proof of
  preference, recovery, or a feasible alternative.
- **Recipient control can coexist with transferred care labor.** Consumer
  direction may improve selected short-run access, satisfaction, or caregiver
  experience while coordination, payroll, backup, unpaid substitution,
  staffing, and continuity burdens remain unresolved. More choice at one
  interface is not the same as a durable care system.
- **Output and productivity can rise before worker power does.** AI use,
  output, employment, cost, or labor-share movement does not establish worker
  pay, task discretion, schedule control, bargaining power, or household gain.
  The decisive test is who controls the task and captures the durable benefit.
- **Utility can coexist with weak controllability.** A phone or conversational
  system can provide information, connection, advice, or support while users
  experience excess, sleep/productivity costs, partial self-regulation, or
  continuation pressure at exit. Use alone does not establish dependence or
  harm; agency requires utility, constraints, alternatives, and observed
  behavior together.
- **An alternative can be offered without being usable.** A job, care route,
  insurer, repair, provider, or substitute may exist on paper while money,
  time, transport, safety, status, health, or schedule make it unreachable or
  unacceptable. Real choice requires the same unit's access attempt and the
  protected or sacrificed outcome. The [offered-versus-usable alternatives
  synthesis](US-BROAD-OFFERED-VERSUS-USABLE-ALTERNATIVES-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
  now extends this rule to platform control and geopolitical capability.
- **Contestability is unequal.** Appeal reversal, replacement, reissue, and
  worker-remedy records show that formal correction can work after someone
  reaches the route. They do not show who lacked notice, documents, time,
  safety, knowledge, or a viable reason to persist. Non-appeal is not
  acceptance, and appeal success is not a population correction rate.

These are cross-theme mechanisms, not new topic silos. They connect the
consumer, household, labor, care, digital, firm, infrastructure, political,
and geopolitical lanes while preserving the source-specific units and open
arrows.

## The main finding so far

The packets keep pointing to one practical idea:

> A household can look stable in one measure while losing room somewhere else.

A family may keep paying rent by dropping insurance, care, food quality, saving, or free time. A worker may keep the same annual income while taking more trips, more jobs, more debt, or less control. A customer may keep using a service while spending more time trying to get an answer or leave.

These are repeated patterns in the questions and early evidence. They are not proof that all of them are worsening together or happening to the same households.

The current [retained same-unit bridge matrix](US-BROAD-RETAINED-SAME-UNIT-BRIDGE-MATRIX_V1.md)
shows where the long chain is genuinely observed: MEPS places a dated medical
event before later health/work movement; SHED follows financial adaptation and
partial reversal; CCES places earlier job loss or insurance insecurity before
later institutional judgment and political action; HTOPS separates fraud
exposure, realized loss, reporting, self-reported recovery, and later household
conditions; and a CFPB Ombudsman example follows an expired redress check
through recipient effort, administrative friction, and escalation. These are
separate bridges, not one joined household story. Across them, adaptation,
approval, contact, action, remedy access, and recovery do not form a single
ladder. That divergence is one of the atlas's strongest cultural findings and
a reason to measure meaning and perceived control directly rather than infer
them from hardship.

## The deepest findings currently supported

These are the program's strongest cross-theme propositions—not universal laws,
but findings that recur across different evidence layers:

1. **Unequal optionality is the hidden structure.** The same price, rule, or
   service failure lands differently depending on cash, time, care capacity,
   information, status, transport, institutional access, and ability to leave.
   The [unequal-optionality finding](findings/us-broad-program-unequal-optionality-path-001.md)
   is the program's central synthesis.
2. **People pay in multiple currencies.** A household can preserve a bill by
   sacrificing food quality, care, savings, rest, safety, or future flexibility;
   a worker can preserve income by giving up time or control. Cash alone misses
   the transfer. See [time as hidden price](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/time-as-hidden-price-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md).
3. **Institutions measure their own success more easily than lived recovery.**
   A complaint can be routed, a payment distributed, access restored, or a
   repair completed without showing receipt, adequacy, durable protection, or
   restored choice. Even receipt varies by route: known-customer mailings,
   claims processes, and complaint-derived lists have different check-cashing
   rates. See the [remedy durability synthesis](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/remedy-durability-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md).
4. **Action is an ecology, not a ladder.** Staying, complaining, switching,
   voting, organizing, and silence each have different costs and meanings.
   Continued use can be dependence; silence can be futility; voting can express
   identity as much as material judgment. See the [trust/action layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cross-source-trust-action-layer-v1.md).
5. **Capability and dependence can grow together.** A new factory, platform,
   data center, or defense capacity can expand capability while leaving the
   affected actor dependent on imported inputs, one provider, opaque rules, or
   costly switching. Capacity becomes power only when someone can use, refuse,
   replace, or impose a cost. See the [capacity/dependence bridge](projects/ai-work-control/capability-dependence-local-power-layer-v1.md).
6. **Formal access is not usable access.** A service, benefit, transit route,
   accommodation, or remedy may exist on paper while time, price, stigma,
   reliability, accessibility, or information makes it unusable. The [interface-to-outcome finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-034.md)
   treats that gap as a measurable stage, not a rhetorical caveat.
7. **Formal response and lived recovery run on different clocks.** An order,
   approval, payment, reinstatement, repair, or capacity award can be real and
   still leave receipt, usability, adequacy, durability, trust, or
   replaceability unresolved. The Cartisano case makes the split concrete:
   back pay and continuity were reported while actual return was withheld; the
   Aitken case shows a negotiated return and back pay followed by a later job
   rupture. The Alam Shah case adds a preference boundary: a formal
   reinstatement remedy may not match the worker's preferred compensation or
   practical safety/access needs. The same two-clock rule applies from household remedies to
   critical minerals and strategic infrastructure: formal action is not the
   endpoint until the affected unit can use the result, maintain it, or
   credibly switch away.
8. **Movement is not automatically improvement.** The same-person MEPS event
   screens show that a lower later nonemployment level can coexist with more
   employment-status movement, including both entry and resolution. A coded
   “resolution” can mean a status transition without showing job quality,
   hours, earnings, treatment continuity, debt relief, or household security.
   This matters across the atlas: switching, returning, paying, or exiting is
   an action endpoint, not by itself a welfare or recovery endpoint.
9. **Search and avoidance are both forms of constrained agency.** A household
   under expense pressure may search across jobs, food, benefits, housing, or
   credit without finding a usable option. An immigrant family facing perceived
   surveillance or status risk may avoid work, care, school, police, benefits,
   or community routes without becoming politically apathetic. Search expands
   the option set symbolically; protective non-use narrows the exposure set.
   Both are active responses whose meaning depends on safety, dignity,
   information, alternatives, and the cost of being visible.
10. **Hardship does not produce one political direction.** In the retained
    CCES panel, respondents reporting prior job loss showed lower local
    political action (23.93% versus 31.57%), nearly similar congressional
    contact (37.36% versus 39.14%), and slightly higher favorable Congress
    approval (13.86% versus 10.06%) than the no-job-loss group. The result is
    descriptive, not causal, but it blocks a simple “hardship creates distrust
    and withdrawal” story. The target of blame, identity, route cost, perceived
    efficacy, and the difference between contacting and organizing all matter.
11. **Redress has a reachability problem, not just a payment problem.** CFPB
    records now show distributed funds being cashed, while later reports also
    show reissued and redistributed funds after some payments were not claimed.
    That is a real institutional persistence stage, but it still does not show
    who received what, whether the account or service was corrected, or whether
    the household recovered. Access to the remedy is part of the remedy.
12. **People can agree that things are getting worse without agreeing what to
    do.** The latest cultural layer finds broad moral pessimism alongside a
    near split over whether government should promote moral values. Likewise,
    economic and affordability concerns can become highly salient while party
    representation remains divided. Shared decline or worry is not shared
    attribution, legitimacy, or policy demand.
13. **Informal resilience depends on expected reciprocity.** People report
    greater willingness to help neighbors than confidence that neighbors would
    help them. Social capacity therefore cannot be inferred from goodwill alone;
    actual help, adequacy, burden, and continuity remain separate stages.
14. **Disaster aid has an administrative funnel, not yet a recovery story.**
    The Los Angeles wildfire FEMA snapshot makes registration, referral,
    eligibility, and reported assistance visible, but its aggregate city/ZIP
    rows cannot show who was paid, whether the amount was adequate, whether a
    home was repaired or secured, or whether survivors later trusted or
    contested the system. Administrative reach is a real stage; it is not
    survivor recovery.
15. **Participation is not the same as practical power.** Buying, staying,
    reporting, searching, reviewing, switching, and not appearing in a record
    can each reflect different combinations of need, stigma, time, privacy,
    dependence, and exit cost. Institutional visibility can improve
    accountability, but a complaint, review, payment, or continued use does
    not prove correction, adequate remedy, restored bargaining power, or a
    credible alternative.
16. **A bank account is not the same as usable financial power.** The financial
    route can split into formal access, nonbank substitution, overdraft or
    fraud exposure, recovery effort, provider contact, and switching. The
    2025 SHED evidence shows these stages diverging: a person can be formally
    banked yet rely on another route, contact a provider without verified
    correction, or switch without proving that the replacement restored room
    or trust. Financial access therefore belongs in the same end-to-end map as
    care, work, and public benefits: the question is who can pay, contest,
    recover, and leave at acceptable cost.
17. **Wage recovery has a reachability gate.** Agency recovery, worker
    location, notice, identity verification, claim completion, payment, and
    actual use are separate stages. A public recovery total can therefore rise
    while a worker still has no usable money, and non-claim can reflect address,
    migration, documentation, digital access, or administrative burden rather
    than indifference.
18. **A price shock is filtered through household capacity.** The same energy
    or transport shock can create different real spending, inflation, debt,
    commuting, and substitution responses by income and place. Aggregate price
    movement is therefore an exposure surface, not a household welfare result.
19. **Synthetic contact can change a civic action step without changing the
    civic relationship.** A brief AI-mediated outgroup encounter can increase
    immediate warmth or willingness to attempt real contact, while a weak
    later effect warns that rehearsal, cooperation, trust, and durable identity
    change are different outcomes.
20. **Consumer choice can carry an upstream penalty.** A marketed opportunity
    may require an application, expose a credit record, or consume time before
    the consumer learns whether the offer is usable. A later refund is not
    automatically a restored score, missed opportunity, or workable alternative.

The common implication is that culture is not an afterthought added after the
economic data. Meaning, dignity, trust, identity, and legitimacy shape which
option people can use, whether they contest a decision, and whether an
institution's formal success becomes lived security.

The family-support evidence adds a useful cultural-consumer bridge. Outside
household help can pay for phones, housing, cars, medical costs, or education;
intergenerational ties can also provide contact, care, transport, housing, and
belonging. These are not one pooled family-benefit effect. They show that
social relationships can function as practical infrastructure, while leaving
open who bears the cost, whether support is reciprocal or conditional, and how
long the relationship remains usable.

The geopolitical version is parallel: capability is not leverage until the
relevant actor can inspect, qualify, operate, replace, or refuse the capability
and another actor's choices measurably change. Capacity, dependence, and
legitimacy therefore belong in the same end-to-end map without being treated as
one index.

## What the latest pages add

The newer pages make the map more concrete:

- A fee can disappear while the cash shortage remains.
- A benefit can disappear while the need remains.
- A job can stay while coverage, debt, or care makes leaving harder.
- A price gap can exist while the customer pays for time, safety, or convenience.
- New residents can add customers and jobs while also adding pressure to homes and services.
- A benefit loss can change sentiment without proving a changed vote.
- A charitable-food visit can fill part of a food gap while hours, transport, awareness, comfort, variety, safety, and treatment still leave need unmet.
- Automatic saving can add retirement and liquid-account assets while also changing short-term credit-card debt; a related SIPP policy-timing study reports later retirement and Social Security claiming. The Oregon balance-sheet and seven-state timing studies use different designs and balance-sheet measures, so future security, current liquidity, and voluntary choice must be measured separately rather than pooled.
- [Automatic saving is a multi-currency policy](findings/us-automatic-saving-multi-currency-policy-bridge-001.md) now places those Auto-IRA results beside Federal Reserve liquidity/credit and BEA aggregate income/spending. The bridge preserves the apparent cross-paper disagreement and keeps current room, later claiming, wellbeing, and political meaning as separate outcomes.
- The new remedy cases make the conversion problem more concrete. A DOJ IER
  case reports a lower-wage rehire followed by restored wage, back pay, and
  seniority; an OSHA case reports safety voice followed by retaliation and an
  ordered remedy. These are different repair currencies—money, access, status,
  and protection—not one generic “recovery” measure. The [remedy synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/remedy-spectrum-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md)
  keeps payment, return, status repair, worker voice, durability, trust, and
  exit separate.
- The expanded formal-remedy/lived-recovery audit now spans thirteen named routes,
  including worker, platform, recruitment, consumer-redress, and product-
  safety cases. It includes both a reported next-day return with approximately
  $420 back pay and a later recall reporting that an earlier repair was
  ineffective. This is a calibrated counterexample set: remedies can work
  immediately, while adequate and durable recovery remains less observable.
- The capability/dependence pass adds the same distinction at strategic scale:
  critical-mineral import reliance, domestic R&D, data-center buildout, or
  defense procurement is not yet replaceability or leverage. The decisive unit
  is application-specific output followed by switching capacity and an
  observed external response.
- The Cash and Counseling evidence supplies a positive institutional
  counterweight to the remedy cases: randomized consumer direction improved
  selected short-run beneficiary access/satisfaction, caregiver wellbeing, and
  direct-hire worker experience. But those currencies were measured in separate
  populations. Completed care, unpaid-care substitution, household security,
  worker retention, trust, and exit were not established. A program can improve
  lived experience without yet proving durable control.
- The companion-product exit evidence adds a concrete agency boundary. A
  farewell can trigger a continuation tactic and short-run engagement without
  showing failed deletion or switching. “The person kept talking” is not the
  same as “the person could not leave”; practical exit requires account,
  billing, data, alternative-service, and later-behavior evidence.
- The Rehman platform case adds an especially clear remedy clock: voluntary
  access restoration was followed by a formal reactivation order and an
  A$7,096.96 gross lost-remuneration order with a 14-day deadline. The public
  record still does not show receipt, continued work, durable access, or
  household recovery. Enforceability is therefore a real stage, but not the
  lived endpoint.
- The Alam Shah worker case adds the cultural-access dimension to that remedy
  clock: language, interpreter access, walkability, family obligations, and
  fear of repeat dismissal shape whether reinstatement is usable. A tribunal
  order can repair status and money on paper while actual return, payment,
  safety, and later security remain unobserved.
- The MEPS event follow-up now adds a same-person later health/work clock. It
  strengthens the household side of the map, but the event record still does
  not reveal the initiating need, available alternative, institutional remedy,
  or later meaning/action. A later health or employment value is an outcome
  surface, not automatically recovery.
- The MEPS event-family friction comparison adds an important middle step:
  reported insurance denial or prior-authorization delay is associated with
  more care delay, medical debt, collection contact, and bill problems across
  office, ER, and inpatient groups. This is a same-round descriptive pattern,
  not a claim-level causal effect or proof that a remedy failed.
- A new June 2025 HTOPS screen adds a consumer-culture middle signal: expense
  difficulty is not monotonically related to whether people use AI, but online
  searches for jobs, housing, food, medical help, benefits, and credit rise
  sharply with difficulty. Search is therefore an option-discovery effort,
  not proof of an obtained alternative, AI-mediated decision, remedy, or exit.
  In the validated screen, job search rises from 20.7% among people reporting
  no expense difficulty to 57.0% among those reporting very difficult
  expenses; food search rises from 15.9% to 32.5%, benefits search from 10.7%
  to 29.0%, and credit search from 10.0% to 27.0%. Any AI use is 59.0% in the
  first group and 58.4% in the last, so material pressure is more visibly
  changing the search object than producing a simple AI-adoption gradient.
  The new April-to-June same-respondent recheck strengthens the time ordering:
  April expense difficulty is followed by June job search at 40.6% versus
  21.8% without difficulty, while high Congress confidence is 17.2% versus
  16.8% and any AI use is 53.9% versus 58.5%. Pressure is therefore more
  clearly associated with later navigation effort than with a uniform
  technology or political-confidence response. The broader confidence screen
  makes that political point more precise: Congress is nearly flat, while
  confidence in the presidency, military, police, Supreme Court, public
  schools, and statistical agencies is lower in the expense-difficulty group.
  This is selective institutional judgment, not one universal anti-government
  response and not a causal trust-change estimate.
  The income-stratified pass shows that this search response is not identical
  across resource positions: among people reporting expense difficulty, job
  search was 39.8% below $50,000 household income, 46.2% at $50,000–$99,999,
  and 37.0% at $100,000 or more. Benefit search was 20.3%, 22.5%, and 15.4%,
  respectively. Higher-income respondents used AI much more overall, but
  expense difficulty barely changed their AI use (71.5% versus 71.7%). This is
  descriptive, not a causal moderation estimate: income is not liquid cash,
  and search is not recovery. Across the six search domains, difficult-expense
  respondents searched at least two domains 40.3% below $50,000, 45.7% at
  $50,000–$99,999, and 39.4% at $100,000 or more, versus 21.8%, 27.1%, and
  26.5% among their not-difficult counterparts. The practical burden is
  therefore often multi-front navigation, not one isolated workaround.
  Within the difficult-expense group, June AI users were also more likely than
  non-AI users to search at least two domains: 56.0% versus 29.1% below
  $50,000, 50.6% versus 38.3% at $50,000–$99,999, and 40.7% versus 36.1% at
  $100,000 or more. In this descriptive screen AI looks more like an added
  navigation layer than a replacement for search; need, digital access, and
  general engagement may all contribute.
  Read the [HTOPS online-search and AI-trust finding](projects/us-consumer-culture/findings/us-consumer-culture-003.md)
  , the [same-respondent cross-lag finding](projects/us-consumer-culture/findings/us-consumer-culture-004.md)
  , and the [income-stratified finding](projects/us-consumer-culture/findings/us-consumer-culture-005.md).
- The April-to-June linked HTOPS fraud follow-up adds a same-respondent bridge:
  people reporting earlier fraud exposure, especially loss, show more later
  searching for jobs, food, benefits, and credit. The tiny reported-recovery
  group still searches heavily, so reported recovery is not the same as
  restored room or security. This is an association with uncertain timing,
  not a causal remedy or exit result.

These are not versions of one theory. They are places where the same research discipline helps: name the first change, follow the next household choice, identify who controls the next step, and stop where the evidence stops.

## Current execution decision

The broad rotation is now organized around three missing-middle clusters rather
than another equal sweep of aggregate indicators. The [lane maturity and next
action control](US-BROAD-LANE-MATURITY-AND-NEXT-ACTION_V1.md) identifies the
highest-value work as:

1. **Digital and consumer:** connect a user or account to a platform control,
   remedy, or exit attempt, then look for recurrence, trust, switching, or
   durable exit.
2. **Household, care, and public systems:** connect one dated person or family
   episode to need, alternatives, effort, actual service, and later security.
3. **Infrastructure and state:** connect one named project or incident from
   formal capacity to experienced service, burden, acceptance, or external
   response.

The public-only remedy pass tested the smallest available consumer route: the
CFPB Ombudsman example documents an expired redress check, recipient effort,
and institutional escalation, but not final receipt, cashing, adequacy, or
later recovery. The [next-episode selection](broad-next-episode-selection-v1.md)
therefore keeps it as a bounded comparator rather than promoting a new
end-to-end claim. UAS remains the highest-value household acquisition route,
but it requires authorized account-holder access. Until that changes, the
program continues the three-cluster rotation and records acquisition gaps as
findings rather than filling them with inference.

Across these current findings, the recurring program pattern is not a single worsening
trend. It is **unequal optionality plus measurement separation**: people,
workers, households, firms, places, and institutions occupy different
positions in the same chain, and the observed endpoint in one layer cannot be
silently substituted for the missing endpoint in another. The next end-to-end
tests therefore require common units, time order, alternatives, correction or
remedy, and a measured downstream choice or meaning.

The newer pages also show that these are not separate lanes. They form a few repeatable chains:

| Starting point | What changes next | Where the cost can end up |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable pay or childcare | A missed bill, extra shift, unpaid care, or expensive credit | Health, debt, family time, and the next job |
| Utility or energy shock | A payment plan, unsafe temperature, repair, shutoff, or move | Food, medicine, work, housing, and future service access |
| Product failure | Repair, replacement, debt, storage, or disposal | Cash reserves, useful time, waste, and local repair work |
| Thin credit or old utility debt | Deposit, denial, higher price, or delayed service | The next home, account, commute, and ability to recover |
| Renter or owner housing cost | Insurance, rent, energy, repairs, and transport compete | Remaining cash, safety, coverage, and ability to stay |
| Consumer or worker rule | Search, appeal, switching, or unpaid effort | Trust, firm power, local choice, and public pressure |

The latest cross-theme layers make the societal scope explicit. The [cross-source trust, meaning, and action layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cross-source-trust-action-layer-v1.md) separates material condition, attribution, trust, identity, consumer response, civic response, and institutional response. The [price, market power, belief, and action layer](projects/us-tariff-domestic-markup-delay/price-power-belief-action-layer-v1.md) adds the firm and trade path from direct and delayed costs to household adaptation and political interpretation. The [capability, dependency, local distribution, and state power layer](projects/ai-work-control/capability-dependence-local-power-layer-v1.md) extends the same question to infrastructure, ownership, energy, public capability, and geopolitical leverage.

The [care cost, work, family time, and security layer](projects/us-health-cost-household-choice/care-cost-work-family-security-layer-v1.md) records social reproduction as a population-level process: health needs can be paid through bills, delayed care, unpaid family labor, lost work, food or housing tradeoffs, and reduced job freedom. The [place risk, housing security, mobility, and local life layer](projects/us-housing-insurance-affordability/place-risk-mobility-local-life-layer-v1.md) follows risk through insurance, housing, energy, transport, business formation, services, local attachment, and the ability to stay. The [optionality, unequal exposure, time, and exit layer](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/optionality-inequality-time-exit-layer-v1.md) provides the distributional spine: cash, time, alternatives, social support, rights, institutional voice, and practical exit are distinct resources.

The local care-capacity layer sharpens the place boundary. HRSA HPSA
designations and reported FTE fields describe shortage objects and
administrative capacity, but not active hours, new-patient acceptance, wait,
travel, price, continuity, or a resident's completed appointment. A county
designation or provider stock is therefore not a reachable alternative or a
health outcome; the missing unit is a dated resident/service episode. The
named-site follow-up adds that even a posted low-cost route can require
family/income screening and proof-of-income paperwork to retain the discount,
so affordability itself has an administrative implementation stage.

The remedy ledgers add an important counterexample to the same map. In the
official FWC Meadley follow-up, compensation payment was reported, but
reinstatement failed; the worker then pursued court enforcement and a further
monetary award and penalty. The observed chain is therefore `institutional
order -> noncompliance -> worker enforcement -> payment/additional remedy`,
not `payment -> restored work -> household recovery`. Later employment,
durability, trust, and exit are still unobserved. This is why the atlas keeps
formal remedy, implementation, receipt, restoration, and recovery as separate
stages.

The Amazon Flex refund record adds the same distinction at program scale:
aggregate distribution and a reported rise in participation after a Zelle
reissue route do not reveal driver-level receipt, remaining loss, bargaining
power, trust, switching, or exit. Continued participation can reflect
dependence or payment reach rather than restored practical power.

Product-safety evidence adds a second remedy boundary: a CPSC Sauna360 recall
expansion reports that an earlier free repair was ineffective, requiring owners
to stop using the product and receive a new professional repair. A remedy can
therefore have its own failure-and-correction sequence. The public record still
does not show owner notice, appointment, completed repair, downtime, safe use,
or residual cost, so remedy durability remains distinct from consumer recovery.

The latest population layers strengthen this map without narrowing it. The
[2025 SHED price-adaptation layer](projects/us-household-financial-pressure/shed-2025-price-adaptation-layer-v1.md)
shows how adults distribute price pressure across cheaper goods, reduced use,
savings, borrowing, delayed purchases, extra work, emergency capacity, and
outside help. The [ANES 2024 political judgment layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/anes-2024-political-judgment-layer-v1.md)
then measures a different population: eligible voters reporting financial worry,
economic judgment, trust, perceived institutional capture, perceived waste,
vote, and post-election legitimacy. These are adjacent layers, not the same
respondents and not a person-level join.

Together they support a bounded societal pattern: material pressure is often
absorbed through changed choices, and economic worry is associated with
different judgments of the economy and institutions. The distribution is
structured by financial room and party identity. The evidence does not yet
show that one price, bill, firm, or policy caused a specific person's trust or
vote. That missing link is part of the finding, not a reason to erase the
broader pattern.

The latest SHED recontact panels add two timing boundaries. Cheaper-product use
and borrowing do not persist on one simple income gradient: among prior
adopters on worsening financial paths, cheaper-product persistence declined
across income bands, while borrowing persistence rose again in the highest
band. Separately, improved self-rated health reduced some saving-cut
persistence without removing borrowing or new price adaptation. Financial room,
health, debt, and consumption therefore recover on different clocks; none is a
complete recovery endpoint by itself.

The [Pew three-audience global-role layer](projects/ai-work-control/pew-2026-us-global-role-domestic-partisan-international-comparison-layer-v1.md)
extends the same separation to state power. Foreign publics, US adults, and US
partisans judge the US role differently: a median 35% across 36 non-US
countries said the US contributes to global peace and stability, compared with
57% of Americans, including 82% of Republicans and 35% of Democrats. Yet 83%
of Americans across parties said the US interferes in other countries' affairs
at least a fair amount. Capability, reputation, domestic identity, and foreign
reception are therefore distinct currencies; the survey does not establish a
change in alliance behavior, trade, aid acceptance, or another state's choice.

The new NBER evidence makes the missing link more precise. The [real-wage,
inflation, and 2024 election finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-021.md)
uses 3,102 US counties and shows that county-level purchasing-power measures
are associated with Republican vote share, Republican margin, and presidential
turnout. Its preferred estimates distinguish real-wage change from inflation,
and use state fixed effects plus robust and state-cluster bootstrap uncertainty.
That is useful evidence for a place-level economic-voting arrow, but it is not
evidence that the same individual experienced the measured price or wage
change, attributed it to a candidate, and then voted in response.

The [Italian transfer-design comparator](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-022.md)
shows why the political endpoint needs its own design. A salient, targeted
monthly transfer can produce an incumbent vote response, and the response can
persist after the transfer ends, while the consumption response and the
electoral response are not interchangeable. This is an international
comparison, not a US estimate, but it supplies a concrete testable mechanism:
benefit receipt, perceived government credit, consumption, and electoral reward
are separate stages. The linked HTOPS material-pressure confidence profile
adds same-person temporal evidence for one earlier arrow while showing that
institutional judgments diverge by target; ANES, GSS, and other political
packets measure interpretation or action in different samples.

The end-to-end political chain is therefore now written as:

```text
local prices, wages, rules, or transfers
  -> real purchasing power or salient benefit receipt
  -> adaptation, consumption, borrowing, or delay
  -> attribution, trust, and perceived fairness
  -> vote, turnout, complaint, switching, or other public action
  -> policy persistence, redesign, or revocation
```

The program will not pool these arrows simply because they appear in one
story. Each source keeps its unit, denominator, timing, geography, design, and
uncertainty. The research task is to add same-unit or same-case links where
available, and to preserve the open boundary where a county association,
population adaptation measure, respondent judgment, or election result cannot
be joined causally.

The retained CCES panel gives a useful counterexample inside this chain.
Respondents reporting earlier job loss had much higher later noncoverage and
lower local political action (23.9% versus 31.6%), while Congress approval was
not uniformly lower. Earlier lack of insurance shows the same action gap with
near-similar favorable approval. Material insecurity can therefore reduce one
form of action without producing a simple distrust result; attribution,
employer remedy, recovery, and causal sequence remain open.

The [financial dissatisfaction/trust finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-012.md)
sharpens this meaning layer with the 2024 GSS: financial satisfaction, fairness
expectations, generalized trust, ideology, and vote intention can coexist in
one respondent while remaining causally ambiguous. Trust is therefore an
outcome to measure separately from voting, not a proxy for political silence
or a shortcut from hardship to electoral behavior.

The Federal Reserve lane adds an important messenger layer to this pattern.
The [partisan-trust finding](projects/us-fed-partisan-trust/findings/us-fed-partisan-trust-001.md)
shows that perceived institutional alignment is associated with trust,
information demand, and the weight given to a Fed message when forming
inflation expectations. This helps explain why the same economic announcement
can be received as useful information by one group and as a partisan signal by
another. The NBER evidence does not establish actual spending, borrowing,
voting, or the Fed's objective political alignment; those are still open
arrows. The separate [NBER design-comparison acquisition audit](projects/us-household-monetary-policy/nber-w35090-acquisition-audit-v1.md)
also keeps hypothetical responses distinct from responses to an actual rate
announcement.

The [New York Fed Economic Heterogeneity Indicator layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/new-york-fed-economic-heterogeneity-layer-v1.md)
adds the distributional context behind that messenger problem. Constructed
inflation gaps, earnings ratios, and income-stratified retail-consumption
directions show why a national price or policy story can meet different lived
baskets and different amounts of room to respond. These are group indicators,
not a substitute for the actual price paid, debt exposure, or spending record
of a particular household.

The [SIPP official-universe variance finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-002.md)
adds a statistical-quality check to the material/time/care lane. Applying
field-specific status flags, universes, and Fay-BRR replicate weights gives
uncertainty intervals for hardship, food security, hunger, and job-count
diagnostics. This makes comparisons more calibrated; it still does not turn
person-record weights into household weights or close the dated-trigger,
time-substitution, trust, or political-action arrows.

The [SIPP care/work variance finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-003.md)
extends that quality check to child-care payment, assistance, work prevention,
and reported time lost. The measures occupy different conditional universes;
the small and imprecise time-lost cell is preserved rather than blended into a
general care-burden rate. The layer still does not observe the full substitution
path or its later cultural and political meaning.
The [SIPP income-band care comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-004.md)
adds subgroup divergence: paid-care use, payment assistance, and work
prevention do not move together across income-to-poverty bands. This keeps
access to paid care separate from need, support, and foregone work; the
comparison does not establish eligibility, unmet demand, or causation.
The [SIPP tenure care comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-005.md)
adds another moderator: renters report more child-care assistance and work
prevention, while owners/buyers report more paid-care use. This is a
distributional contrast, not evidence that tenure caused the difference; the
sparse no-rent cells remain explicitly limited.
The [SIPP race-group care comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-006.md)
adds unevenly precise subgroup evidence: Black-alone records show more reported
assistance and work prevention than White-alone records, while paid-care use
is lower. The released race recode does not measure cultural meaning,
discrimination, or causation.
The [SIPP disability-status care comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-007.md)
adds a health/work-limitation moderator: paid-care use is lower in the
work-limiting group, while reported work prevention is not clearly separated.
The sparse time-lost cell keeps accommodation, care need, and causal meaning
open.
The [SIPP regional care comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-008.md)
adds place context: paid-care incidence is broadly similar across regions,
while reported work prevention varies more. This points toward provider,
housing, work-schedule, and household-composition mechanisms to test rather
than treating region itself as a cause.
The [SIPP child-presence care screen](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-009.md)
adds a measurement boundary: household members under 18 and reference-parent
child-care fields are not interchangeable. The sparse no-under-18 records are
retained as an audit target rather than interpreted as ordinary nonparent care
behavior.
The [SIPP reference-parent universe audit](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-010.md)
resolves that apparent anomaly as a timing/control issue: monthly household
composition is not the same as annual reference-parent or fall/December care
status. Parent-status estimates remain open until those clocks are aligned.

The [CFPB complaint-response layer](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/cfpb-complaint-response-descriptive-layer-v1.md)
adds the institutional and firm side. Its 2024 published records are heavily
concentrated in credit reporting, and company responses are mostly recorded as
non-monetary relief or explanation. Response categories differ sharply by
product, so the all-product total is not a remedy rate. This gives the program
a third kind of evidence: not only what people report and how they interpret
conditions, but how an institution records and categorizes the response. It
still does not show whether the consumer's problem was solved, trust restored,
or exit avoided.

The [ATUS 2024 time layer](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/atus-2024-time-hidden-price-layer-v1.md)
adds a fourth lens: who spends time on paid work, household production, care,
and social participation. It shows why “household room” cannot be reduced to
cash. Multiple-job holders, parents, women, men, employed people, and
non-employed people have different time allocations and different control over
when and where work happens. ATUS measures the distribution of time, not the
firm or price shock that caused a particular schedule.

The [Pew 2025 news and civic-engagement layer](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/pew-2025-news-civic-engagement-layer-v1.md)
adds the information and cultural layer. It finds distinct participation types
across voting, volunteering, contacting officials, religious attendance, and
news attention, alongside partisan differences in trusted news sources and a
growing role for social-media news influencers. This measures source
environment and civic behavior, not persuasion or a platform-caused vote.

The [Pew adult social-media-use layer](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/pew-adult-social-media-use-2025-layer-v1.md)
adds a population comparator to the teen platform/chatbot evidence. YouTube
and Facebook have broad adult reach, while Instagram and TikTok are more
age-concentrated and daily use differs sharply by platform and age. This maps
differentiated exposure and audience formation; it does not treat platform
reach as total attention, persuasion, or political power.

The [digital-life synthesis](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/findings/us-digital-habits-attention-005.md)
now puts those adult patterns beside teen social/chatbot use, adult chatbot
purposes and governance concern, and news/civic engagement styles. The
resulting cultural question is not whether “people are online,” but which
social, informational, consumer, or institutional relationship is becoming
ordinary—and whether people can still inspect, refuse, correct, or leave it.

The [USDA 2024 food-security layer](projects/us-food-budget-security/usda-2024-food-security-layer-v1.md)
adds a basic-security endpoint. It distinguishes low food security, very low
food security, child/adult shielding, unequal exposure, and nutrition-assistance
participation. Food is therefore a social and political measure of available
room, but the annual prevalence data do not identify which price, employer,
policy, or store caused a particular household’s condition.

The [local business formation and place layer](projects/us-local-business-place/local-business-formation-place-layer-v1.md)
adds firm and local productive capacity. It separates entrepreneur movement,
business applications, employer-firm formation, jobs, services, and place
identity. A rising application count is not yet a rising local job base or a
stronger community; those links require establishment, service, and local-life
records.

The [local capacity and reachability finding](projects/us-local-business-place/findings/us-local-business-place-003.md)
extends that bridge from firm stock to practical access. CBP establishment and
employment density, HRSA primary-care shortage representation, rurality, and
NHTS mobility each describe a different part of the local option stack. The
same county can have visible providers and a formal shortage designation, or
nominal capacity that residents cannot afford or reach. The next place test is
a common geography/time comparison of capacity and mobility cells with prices,
hours, neighboring supply, service use, work/care displacement, and local
meaning or civic action. Its first 3,128-county screen finds a high-capacity /
high-zero-vehicle cell as a concrete counterexample: more visible providers
coexist with a stronger vehicle-constraint context and high HPSA
representation. This is a place-context result, not a resident-access or
health-outcome estimate.

The newer Ohio BDS–ACS–PLACES panel makes the access distinction concrete.
High-zero-vehicle counties have nearly the same modeled routine-checkup median
as low-zero-vehicle counties, but higher transportation insecurity, food
insecurity, frequent mental distress, and lack of insurance. The sign of one
service-use measure therefore cannot stand in for practical access or household
security. This is an ecological same-year comparison, not a resident-level
causal result.

The strategic-capability realization bridge applies the same discipline above
the household level. Poland’s defense procurement and US data-center records
show authorization, named capacity, and institutional response stages, but do
not by themselves show delivered/operational capability, replaceability,
local incidence, or changed behavior by a partner or rival. State capacity can
therefore expand while external dependence also deepens; “investment” is not
yet “power.”

The Polish F-35 record gives the capability ladder a concrete comparator. The
first three aircraft arrived at Łask and were formally accepted, official
records report first-pilot training, and a later Ministry update reports five
more arrivals on 28 August, bringing the in-country count to eight. That closes
the numeric aircraft component of Poland’s stated initial-operational-
capability threshold. It does not prove trained ground-personnel coverage,
spares and consumables, certification, fleet readiness, sustained mission use,
replaceability, or changed external behavior. [Polish Ministry threshold](https://www.gov.pl/web/obrona-narodowa/harpia-f-35-dla-polski)

The [AI capability and dependence layer](projects/ai-work-control/ai-capability-dependence-layer-v1.md)
extends the map from domestic firms and places to infrastructure and states.
Its central pattern is that imported or privately owned capability can expand
what a country can do while also increasing dependence on external providers,
capital, chips, cloud, electricity, and data systems. The project keeps local
benefit, public control, and geopolitical leverage as questions to test.

The [IMF April 2026 WEO layer](projects/ai-work-control/imf-weo-geopolitical-fiscal-shock-2026-layer-v1.md)
adds the macro middle between geopolitical shocks and lived conditions. Its
conditional analysis shows that defense mobilization can support short-run
activity while increasing inflation, deficits, debt, and the risk of lower
social spending. The US projections provide aggregate context, but do not
identify household incidence, legitimacy, unrest, or alliance behavior.

The [World Bank poverty, prosperity, and planet layer](projects/ai-work-control/world-bank-poverty-prosperity-planet-2024-layer-v1.md)
adds the distributional counterweight: global poverty remains concentrated in
Sub-Saharan Africa and fragile/conflict-affected countries, climate exposure
threatens livelihood gains, and many countries lack recent survey coverage for
poverty monitoring. This is comparative context, not a US household result,
but it keeps growth, vulnerability, and evidence capacity in the same reading
map.

The [SIPRI 2025 military-expenditure and state-capacity layer](projects/ai-work-control/sipri-2025-military-expenditure-state-capacity-layer-v1.md)
adds the geopolitical capacity side. Global military spending reached an
estimated $2.887 trillion in 2025, while US spending fell in real terms as
European and Asian/Oceanian spending rose. These are fiscal and organizational
inputs, not direct measures of realized capability, public legitimacy, or
household welfare; the open arrows are how spending becomes procurement,
industrial capacity, alliance leverage, or domestic trade-offs.

The [macro-to-legitimacy synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-026.md)
now makes the end-to-end research route explicit across IMF, World Bank,
SIPRI, Federal Reserve, and ANES. It distinguishes macro shock, distribution,
household adaptation, political judgment, and external response instead of
turning them into one unsupported causal claim.

The [AI capability/shared-control synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-031.md)
adds the parallel firm-to-state route across NBER, BIS, BEA, IMF, OFR, and the
World Bank. Its central warning is that productivity, output, or infrastructure
can expand while ownership, worker discretion, household incidence, public
review, and replaceability remain unresolved. This keeps the program's central
question focused on who has the alternatives and control after a system
changes, not merely whether the system grows.

The [worker-power finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-011.md)
adds a parallel labor-market safeguard. BLS mobility, union membership, and
average earnings describe different institutional surfaces; their divergence
is evidence against treating a cooling labor market as one shared experience of
loss or power. The open test is a worker/workplace event linked to household
room, control, remedy, and political meaning.

The newer [capability/dependence local-power layer](projects/ai-work-control/capability-dependence-local-power-layer-v1.md)
sharpens that question: capability is a stack, investment can create capacity
and dependence at the same time, and local benefit must be measured beside
ownership, energy and water burden, provider concentration, portability, and
exit. Announced investment is not realized sovereignty.

The [care-cost layer](projects/us-health-cost-household-choice/care-cost-work-family-security-layer-v1.md)
and [place-risk layer](projects/us-housing-insurance-affordability/place-risk-mobility-local-life-layer-v1.md)
add two missing societal domains. Care is distributed through work and family
time; place risk is distributed through protection, repair, transport, local
services, and mobility. Neither can be reduced to a household bill or a move.

The [MEPS non-synchronization finding](projects/us-health-cost-household-choice/findings/us-health-cost-household-choice-001.md)
adds a repeated-person safeguard to the care/health path: system spending,
out-of-pocket payment, coverage, and perceived health can move in different
directions. The unresolved social cost is what happens to treatment continuity,
unpaid time, work, debt, food, housing, and family care around that change.

The newer [cross-source care-cost/time/work finding](findings/us-care-cost-time-work-currency-cross-source-001.md)
puts that MEPS result beside SIPP's child-care work-constraint layer and the
Federal Reserve's SHED unpaid-care adaptation layer. Together they support a
bounded societal pattern: care costs can be redistributed across money, time,
work capacity, and family security. They do not constitute a pooled estimate
or a same-household causal chain, so the dated event-to-recovery arrow remains
open.

The newer Census care-to-work finding makes unequal option conversion visible:
among affected households, converted work-time loss averaged 114.6 days below
the poverty line versus 43.3 days above 400% of poverty. Lower spending does
not mean lower burden; it can mean unpaid substitution, non-search, or work
withdrawal. The provider-side GAO evidence adds the parallel boundary that
recruitment and retention incentives do not guarantee a staffed, dependable
care slot.

The [housing-payment/coverage synthesis](projects/us-housing-insurance-risk/findings/us-housing-insurance-risk-001.md)
sharpens the place-risk interpretation: keeping a home requires both payment
room and usable risk coverage. Rent arrears, insurance gaps, premiums,
nonrenewals, residual-market enrollment, and modeled hazard are different
stages; the unresolved end-to-end outcome is whether a household can repair,
remain, or move without losing security.

The [published 2023–2024 ATUS care/work layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/atus-2023-2024-published-care-work-layer-v1.md)
adds a population-scale care baseline: 38.2 million people reported recent
eldercare, with different intensity and recipient patterns by employment,
frequency, and household relationship. This strengthens care as a societal
infrastructure question while leaving the same-person displacement and
recovery arrows open.

The [ATUS eldercare roster comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-011.md)
adds a sharper social-reproduction boundary: about four in five provider
respondents in both 2024 and 2025 listed at least one non-household recipient,
and providers listed about 1.3–1.4 recipients on average. Care therefore may
be coordinated across household boundaries, but the annual samples and roster
do not measure distance, hours, shared support, work displacement, or later
meaning. The result expands the network under study without closing the
causal burden arrow.

The [ATUS provider-time comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-012.md)
then compares the diary-day currencies directly. Providers show more household
work and travel and less paid work and socializing than nonproviders in both
annual samples, with replicate-weighted uncertainty. This is a useful measured
time profile and a strong target for adjustment, but it is not a care-caused
loss: age, health, employment, family composition, recipient need, and diary
timing remain unresolved.

The [standardized provider-time comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-013.md)
addresses the first part of that concern. After standardizing to supported
age-band × sex × labor-force-status cells, the extra household work and travel
and lower socializing profile persists in both samples; the paid-work contrast
is smaller and imprecise. This makes the descriptive pattern more durable, not
causal: health, family structure, recipient need, occupation, schedule control,
and dated care timing remain open.

The [richer standardized comparison](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-014.md)
adds household-child status and broad education. Care, household-work, and
travel differences remain in both years, but the social-time contrast weakens
in 2025 and the paid-work contrast remains uncertain. The program therefore
has a measured robustness pattern with annual variation—not a single care
penalty or a causal work-loss claim.

The [SIPP work/care/health bridge](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-027.md)
now places the next three currencies together. In the same-person SIPP
cross-lag, reported work-limiting status conditions monthly earnings, hours,
and job/resource movement; ATUS shows that eldercare providers allocate more
diary-day minutes to care, household work, and travel and fewer to paid work
and socializing; and MEPS Panel 27 shows that total health spending,
out-of-pocket spending, coverage, and perceived health can move in different
directions. These are complementary stages, not a pooled person-level chain.
The defensible conclusion is that household room has money, time, and health
cost currencies whose timing and welfare meaning can diverge. The missing
end-to-end design still needs a dated care or health event, schedule control,
earnings and unpaid time, treatment continuity, food/housing trade-offs,
recovery, and later trust or action.

The [2025 CFPB visible-endpoint refresh](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-015.md)
adds a current institutional counterpart. Published complaint records more than
doubled from 2024 to 2025, while the recorded mix moved toward explanation,
away from relief labels, and toward less narrative visibility. That changes the
observed recourse institution, not necessarily consumer welfare: publication,
product, routing, coding, and index changes remain live alternatives until a
same-case verified remedy and later trust or exit are observed.

The [product-conditioned CFPB route comparison](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-016.md)
shows why the pooled shift must stay conditional. Mortgage and vehicle-loan
records remain explanation-dominant, checking/savings and credit-card mixes
move toward explanation, and student-loan untimely labels rise sharply in 2025.
The student-loan signal is a target for field and servicing audit, not a direct
measure of borrower harm or failed remedy.

The [student-loan timing-field audit](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-017.md)
corrects a potentially consequential category collapse: `timely = No` reaches
30.4% in 2025, while the narrower company-response untimely category is 15.9%.
The annual report's roughly 31% context aligns with the broader field. This is
an institutional measurement result; notices, servicing transfers, elapsed
days, payment status, verified remedy, and borrower meaning remain open.

The [optionality layer](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/optionality-inequality-time-exit-layer-v1.md)
connects the distributional evidence across the map. It asks who has the cash,
time, alternatives, support, rights, voice, and practical exit needed to turn a
formal choice into a real one.

The [Federal Reserve income-volatility and support layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/federal-reserve-income-volatility-support-2025-layer-v1.md)
sharpens the meaning of household room. Income timing, family and public
support, month-end margin, and price adaptations form different buffers and
different burdens. The same price environment can therefore produce product
switching for one household, reduced saving or borrowing for another, and
extra work for a third; dated events, time displacement, recovery, and
political meaning remain unlinked.

The [safety-net administrative-burden layer](projects/us-safety-net-access/administrative-burden-access-layer-v1.md)
deepens the public-systems side. It shows that eligibility, enrollment,
renewal, office access, program exit, employment, food security, and trust are
different outcomes. A lower caseload or higher exit rate is not automatically
successful policy; the household’s next condition must be measured.

The SIPP SNAP transition layers make that warning concrete. Among adjacent
month exits, 76.2% had a lower next-month income-to-poverty ratio, while only
about half of entry and exit transitions had a classified recorded reason.
Benefit exit therefore cannot be read as improved security or voluntary
choice, and the unknown-reason share is itself part of the administrative
friction. The result is descriptive, not a causal estimate of SNAP effects.
The compound-hardship extension strengthens the boundary: simultaneous
rent/mortgage and utility difficulty remains visible for 15.9% of classified
job-loss/wage-reduction entries and 16.9% of other-income-loss entries, while
the small exit cells for non-collection and “benefits not worth the trouble”
also retain hardship. Receipt, exit, and household security are therefore
separate clocks.

The [population public-system event-ledger design](projects/us-safety-net-access/same-episode-event-ledger-design-v1.md)
turns that gap into a multi-person, multi-place test: notice, effort, access
route, decision, interruption, food/work/debt result, later trust, public
action, and institutional response. It is explicitly a population design, not
another one-household study.

The [unequal exposure and status layer](US-UNEQUAL-EXPOSURE-STATUS-LAYER_V1.md)
keeps the broad map from treating averages as universal. Income, tenure, place,
family, race, gender, disability, age, and access route can change both exposure
and the ability to respond. The next requirement is one valid intersectional
test, not a larger pile of one-dimensional averages.

The first such test is now the [SIPP Fay-BRR race × resource layer](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/sipp-fay-brr-race-resource-layer-v1.md).
It shows distributional differences in utility-payment difficulty, hunger, and
one-job status across resource bands, with replicate-weight uncertainty. It is
still a person-month diagnostic—not a causal explanation or a same-household
link to later health, mobility, trust, or political action.

The common object is not “the economy” in the abstract. It is the household’s next available choice. A person who has to borrow, wait, accept a worse provider, or use family help may remain formally housed, employed, or served while losing the ability to respond to the next shock.

The broader object is the social system around that choice: the firm, agency,
platform, employer, place, infrastructure, and state that determine which
alternatives exist and who can control them. The project is therefore tracking
not only what households give up, but how costs, risks, information, ownership,
and power move across society.

The [all-county migration/place capacity profile](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/migration-capacity-all-counties-profile-v1.md)
adds a broader place check: among 620 larger counties, higher population-growth
quartiles have lower per-capita retail, health, and food capacity in the 2023
screen. Population change is not immigration, and this is not a causal or
service-adequacy result; it is a reason to test where demand and capacity move
together or apart.

## The five reader umbrellas

These five umbrellas are the current reader-facing grouping of the opening
atlas. They are not the complete program taxonomy. The broader program has 14
themes, including firm/sector power, infrastructure/dependency, and
geopolitical/state consequences; see the [canonical 14-theme inventory](US-BROAD-THEME-INVENTORY_V1.md).
The five umbrellas are operational reading lanes and priority bridges inside
that wider map.

### 1. What families give up to pay the bills

This theme follows a cost into the thing that gets cut or delayed. Housing can meet the monthly payment while insurance, health care, food, or a safe buffer gets thinner. Credit can keep a purchase or payment moving while placing a later bill on the household.

The useful measure is not only the first price. It is the protected need, the need given up, the timing, and the later cost.

Related reading: [When the price people see becomes the economy they judge](../site/us-price-perception-public-judgment-path-001.html), [When the shape of aid changes the household week](../site/us-transfer-design-food-security-path-001.html), and [When a record starts making the future](../site/us-self-fulfilling-credit-scores-path-001.html).

### 2. Who gives up time

Time is often the hidden payment. It can be spent traveling to an office, caring for family, working several jobs, using a service, or trying to solve a complaint. Time can also be useful: a flexible job, a private support tool, or a recurring service may protect another need.

The question is not whether time was spent. It is what the time made possible and what it displaced.

Related reading: [When one office day changes the remote week](../site/us-office-day-remote-week-path-001.html), [From family time to money and economic views](../site/us-family-care-money-politics-path-001.html), and [When equal income buys a different life](../site/us-multiple-jobs-household-room-path-001.html).

### 3. Who can get an answer or change a decision

This theme follows a person's ability to refuse, appeal, correct, switch, delete, or reach a human. A privacy rule may increase trust and sharing. A service may answer faster while leaving a repeated problem open. A score or benefit rule may turn a paper record into a gate.

The useful measure is not trust as a feeling alone. It is whether the person understood the deal, could challenge it, and received a remedy.

Related reading: [When privacy protection increases the data exchange](../site/us-privacy-data-exchange-path-001.html), [When staying does not mean being satisfied](../site/us-staying-service-satisfaction-path-001.html), and [When a benefit loss changes the public mood](../site/us-benefit-loss-public-mood-path-001.html).

### 4. Who controls the work and gets the gain

This theme separates output from power. A tool can raise speed without giving a worker more control. A shared office day can improve contact while adding a commute. Several jobs can provide flexibility while shifting travel, benefit, and income risk onto the worker.

The useful measure is who sets the task, owns the data, receives the gain, carries the risk, and can change the arrangement.

Related reading: [When one office day changes the remote week](../site/us-office-day-remote-week-path-001.html), [When equal income buys a different life](../site/us-multiple-jobs-household-room-path-001.html), and [When career movement slows around a long payment](../site/us-career-debt-housing-path-001.html).

### 5. A home can be costly to heat before anything else changes

Energy belongs in the housing story. A home can have a high energy bill because of its condition, fuel, weather, or lack of control. The same household may then face a repair, a shutoff, a health choice, or a need to borrow.

The useful measure is the home condition, the bill, the household's ability to change the home, and the next need put at risk.

Related reading: [When the tap becomes part of the health and housing story](../site/us-safe-drinking-water-path-001.html) and [The cost of staying in a home is a stack of bills and risks](../site/us-home-cost-stack-001.html).

The new energy pages sharpen this theme: a bill credit buys time, a repair restores equipment, and weatherization changes the home. They are not interchangeable. The right intervention depends on whether the failure is cash, equipment, building control, or an unsafe condition today.

## How the themes connect

```text
condition, price, rule, or firm decision
  -> household cash, time, access, or control changes
  -> person adapts, delays, borrows, switches, or stays
  -> firm, lender, employer, agency, court, or platform responds
  -> trust, local demand, public pressure, or political judgment changes
```

The strongest recurring bridge is from money to choice. The next bridge is from choice to power: who can refuse, who can move, who can appeal, and who must absorb the result.

The weakest bridge is from private experience to political action. Several packets measure sentiment, trust, blame, or election results, but few follow the same person from the bill or service failure to the public response.

The new findings also sharpen a second weak bridge: firm or public success versus household success. A firm can remain open while the owner’s family works without pay. A utility can restore service while the balance grows. A platform can answer faster while the customer never gets a remedy. A landlord can receive a guarantee payment while the renter acquires debt. The first status is real, but it is not the final household result.

The same distinction now applies to remedies across the atlas. A remedy should
be read as a ladder, not a binary success flag:

```text
rule or offer -> institutional implementation -> reported receipt
  -> verified receipt -> adequacy -> durable protection
  -> trust, switching, voice, political action, or exit
```

The Atkins truck-driver follow-up is a useful positive comparator: the Fair
Work Ombudsman reports that all ten drivers received back-pay after court-held
funds were released. That is stronger than an order or scheduled payment, but
it still does not establish adequacy, later household security, continued
work, or trust. This is why the atlas can record successful institutional
delivery without claiming that the person’s wider life was repaired.

## The first broad bridge pass

The project has now converted the broad umbrella themes into six cross-domain
research bridges. These are synthesis records built from the existing topic
findings; they are not claims that each full chain has been proven.

- [Price and payment → household room](bridges/us-price-payment-household-room-v1.md): payment method, credit, price level, substitution, buying power, and political interpretation.
- [Service and platform rules → recourse, trust, and exit](bridges/us-service-platform-recourse-trust-v1.md): first response, final remedy, customer effort, data exchange, trust, and abandonment.
- [Work rules and tools → control, household security, and local power](bridges/us-work-control-household-power-v1.md): benefits, multiple jobs, fit, output, autonomy, employer dependence, and civic voice.
- [Housing, energy, and insurance → health, mobility, and place](bridges/us-housing-energy-insurance-place-v1.md): burden, safety, coverage, repairs, home value, staying, moving, and recovery.
- [Public aid and rules → interpretation and political response](bridges/us-public-aid-interpretation-political-response-v1.md): exposure, take-up, material outcome, blame, trust, public action, turnout, and vote.
- [Consumer participation → practical power, trust, and exit](projects/us-consumer-culture/consumer-participation-trust-power-cross-source-synthesis-v1.md): money, time, privacy, status, attention, recovery, non-use, reporting, switching, and the institutional response to participation.

The execution queue and source-unit rules are recorded in the [US broad research pass](US-BROAD-RESEARCH-PASS_V1.md). The next stage is to attach valid population-, market-, firm-, place-, or institution-level data to these bridges. A household panel is one possible tool for missing longitudinal links, not the definition of the overall project.

recovery, trust, and political action.

## What the map supports now

The disability workforce-program layer makes the interface problem concrete:
formal public employment capacity can coexist with lower post-exit employment
and earnings, incomplete disability-type reporting, interpreter and assistive-
technology gaps, inaccessible outside training, and weak aggregation of
monitoring reports. The system can count participants and offer services
without showing whether a particular person received a usable accommodation,
completed training, found durable work, or retained mobility. This is a strong
example of how missingness and non-disclosure are part of the social mechanism,
not merely statistical noise.

The work–safety-net layer adds a related correction: employment and public
benefit reliance are not opposites. Millions of working-age adults connected
to Medicaid or SNAP worked during 2024, while selected-state data show repeated
large employers among benefit-connected workers. That is evidence of a shared
labor and household-security system, not proof of employer wrongdoing or
benefit dependence. The missing bridge is the same worker-household episode:
wages, hours, benefits, eligibility churn, food/health need, alternatives, and
whether public support expands real choice or merely keeps an unstable job
possible.

The care lane shows the same mechanism at a more intimate scale. A covered
person may still encounter inaccessible equipment, kiosks, apps, sensory
conditions, communication barriers, or provider assumptions. In that situation
the conversion cost can become extra time, delayed care, family labor, lost
work, or avoidance. Coverage and provider capacity are therefore inputs; the
social outcome depends on whether the encounter is usable, completed, and
continuous.

Military child-care evidence adds a supply-side counterpoint. DOD can provide
fee discounts, training, and thousands of worker incentives while families
still face waitlists because a benefit for the worker is not automatically a
staffed, dependable slot for the family. Price, staffing, quality, hours,
wait time, and reliable use are separate currencies. A subsidy can lower cost
without creating capacity; a retention payment can preserve capacity without
solving a family's schedule or access problem.

Kinship care adds the family-side version of the same tradeoff. About 2.4
million children lived in kinship families in 2023, while the highly visible
foster-care route covered only a small fraction of that broader kinship
population. Family can preserve belonging, school, and cultural continuity,
but it can also hide who leaves work, changes housing, supplies transport, or
absorbs legal and health costs. “Family stepping up” is therefore a care
capacity claim, not proof of safe, supported, or sustainable recovery.

Assisted living makes the financing split visible. A person may be covered for
one Medicaid service, a Medicare health service, or a housing subsidy while
still facing room-and-board costs, limited slots, provider scarcity, or unpaid
family coordination. The reported spending floor and 44-state coverage count
are real institutional signals, but neither tells us whether a safe, affordable
place was available or whether the family could sustain the arrangement.

The migration/meaning layer adds a different kind of cultural depth. A person
can say their living standard is better than their parents', expect their
children to do better, and still believe the broader American dream is out of
reach. Likewise, identity can be central while being experienced as helping,
hurting, or making no difference. Material progress, future hope, belonging,
and perceived fairness are related but not interchangeable—and none by itself
predicts political or consumer action.

The public-help synthesis adds a crucial denominator lesson. Administrative
timeliness, ease of navigating notices and deadlines, actual receipt,
household buffer, and institution-specific confidence are different outcomes.
A person can receive SNAP and still face rent or utility hardship; a state can
process applications quickly without proving the route was easy; and leaving a
program can coincide with either improving income or worsening security. The
atlas therefore treats public assistance as a route and a judgment, not a
single success score.

## The recurring patterns across the whole atlas

The broad evidence is starting to show a small set of repeatable patterns:

1. **The hidden cost is often moved, not removed.** A cheaper purchase can mean
   lower quality; a public benefit can mean paperwork and waiting; a platform
   convenience can mean worker surveillance; and family care can mean lost work
   time. The visible price is only one part of the transaction.
2. **A formal right is not the same as usable power.** A person may have a
   refund, appeal, privacy request, benefit, reinstatement order, or care site
   on paper while still lacking a completed route, payment, appointment,
   correction, or safe alternative.
3. **People preserve what they can by trading across domains.** Households
   protect food, housing, children, or health by borrowing, delaying, working
   more, reducing use, relying on relatives, or giving up time. That is why a
   stable headline number can coexist with a strained daily life.
4. **Family and social ties are practical infrastructure.** Relatives can
   provide money, phones, housing, transport, childcare, eldercare, and a
   sense of belonging. This is real capacity, but it can also hide who carries
   the cost and whether the arrangement can last.
5. **Trust and action do not move as one package.** Hardship can lower local
   participation, increase contact, strengthen a policy demand, or produce
   withdrawal depending on identity, attribution, time, and the available route.
   “Distrust” is not the same thing as silence, and “participation” is not proof
   of confidence.
6. **Capacity is not yet consequence.** A staffed site, energized grid,
   defense contract, alliance deployment, AI system, or factory can be real at
   the institutional level while service quality, household cost, worker
   control, public consent, or strategic effect remains unknown.
7. **Exit is a test of lived power.** Complaining, switching, refusing,
   leaving, or staying can reveal whether a person actually had alternatives.
   But non-use can mean protection, exhaustion, cost, or lack of access—not one
   simple preference.

These are the atlas's current cross-theme hypotheses. They connect the 14
themes without claiming that one dataset proves all of them or that the same
people appear in every source.

## A deeper cross-theme pattern: support is becoming infrastructure

Across the atlas, support is not only something delivered by a formal
institution. It is increasingly assembled from family members, care workers,
neighbors, platforms, public programs, civilian emergency services, and
employers. These systems can keep everyday life functioning when a market or
institution is incomplete, but they also decide who carries the hidden labor
and who can refuse the arrangement.

The pattern appears in several different forms:

- relatives provide money, housing, transport, childcare, eldercare, and
  practical advice when income or public routes are insufficient;
- kinship and unpaid care preserve family continuity while shifting time,
  housing, work, and emotional costs into households;
- AI companions can provide support, advice, stress relief, and a place for
  disclosure before human substitution, dependence, or wellbeing effects are
  demonstrated;
- platform safety tools let users block or unfollow directly, while reporting
  depends on the platform and is much less trusted by users;
- NATO's medical exercise shows military readiness being connected to civilian
  hospitals, ambulances, rescue, and public-health institutions before real
  crisis care is observed.
- HUD housing verification and the named Ohio clinic show the same pattern at
  the access interface: a household can have a formal assistance route or a
  real provider while identity checks, documents, appeals, phone/portal work,
  wait time, and appointment availability determine whether security is usable.

These are not one kind of support and should not be pooled into a single
“social capital” measure. They do reveal a common analytical question:

```text
need or threat
  -> available helper, tool, network, or institution
  -> unpaid or paid effort and control over the route
  -> immediate protection or continuity
  -> adequacy, reciprocity, safety, trust, and durability [often open]
```

The cultural implication is important. Dependence is not simply weakness, and
independence is not always freedom. A person may gain real protection through
family, community, technology, or public coordination while losing privacy,
time, bargaining power, or the ability to leave. The atlas should therefore
measure support by who can invoke it, who performs the work, who controls the
terms, and what happens when the support is withdrawn.

- Some public rules and firm choices change a measurable household action: spending, sharing, work contact, credit access, or sentiment.
- The same outcome can mean different things for different people: a lower expense can be relief or a cut; staying can be choice or a lack of alternatives.
- Timing matters. A tariff, benefit, repair, payment, or rate can arrive before the household has time to adjust.
- The gap between a firm measure and a household measure is often the important part: output is not control, use is not satisfaction, and a balance is not security.
- The map is strongest when it follows a person, product, dollar, or decision through time.
- A product or policy can preserve access while moving risk to a different person, account, or future date.

## What remains unproven

- That these pressures are rising together across the whole US population.
- That the same households carry several of them at once.
- That a household action changes trust, turnout, or a vote.
- That a firm or policy caused the full later outcome rather than one step in it.
- That an average effect applies equally by income, race, age, gender, disability, family, or place.
- That infrastructure investment creates local capability rather than external dependence.
- That care, housing, transport, or business changes produce the same meaning or political response across places.
- That the readable web pages and the underlying research records stay synchronized as the findings deepen.

## The next research move

Choose a small number of bridges where the opening evidence is strongest and join the records over time:

1. household price or payment → actual spending, debt, and later public judgment;
2. service failure → repeat contact, remedy, exit, and trust;
3. work rule or tool → worker control, pay, time, health, and household security;
4. housing or energy cost → health, care, credit, and whether the household can stay;
5. public aid or rule → take-up, daily use, sentiment, and political response.

Across all five, the shared end-to-end schema is:

```text
condition or decision
  -> exposure and available alternatives
  -> money, time, access, control, or status change
  -> household, consumer, worker, firm, place, or public response
  -> distribution of cost, gain, risk, and ownership
  -> cultural meaning, trust, political action, or state leverage
```

The schema is deliberately wider than a household panel. Use the same person,
case, firm, place, or institution when the data support it; otherwise align
separate layers by question and time while naming the missing join.

For each bridge, find a serious counterexample. Then write only the part the records can carry.

The first integrated pass should not become another one-household study. It
should use several compatible layers: a population survey for material
adaptation, a respondent survey for interpretation and action, a firm or
complaint record for the rule and remedy, and a place or administrative record
for distribution and institutional response. Where a valid panel exists, use
it for a defined within-respondent question; where it does not, keep the
layers separate and name the missing join. A household calendar remains one
useful instrument for a later bounded study, not the definition of the
program.

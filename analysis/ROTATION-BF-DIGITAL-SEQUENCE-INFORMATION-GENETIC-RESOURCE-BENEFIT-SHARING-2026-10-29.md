# Rotation BF — Digital sequence information and genetic-resource benefit sharing

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** biodiversity / biotechnology / data governance / Indigenous and local rights / geopolitical benefit sharing  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 data-to-benefit-and-governance question

## Research question

When genetic material is converted into digital sequence information (DSI), what
converts open research and commercial use into recognized origin, fair benefit
sharing, local authority, cultural respect and conservation or livelihood value?

## Conversion chain

```text
genetic resource or associated knowledge
  -> collection, consent and sequencing
  -> database access, research and commercial use
  -> attribution/benefit-sharing trigger and compliance
  -> fund, technology, capacity or community-directed return
  -> conservation, livelihood, cultural sovereignty and trust
```

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** genomic data, biodiversity and biotechnology are being connected
  through a new international benefit-sharing mechanism.
- **Mechanism:** sequence access, provenance, database rules, commercial use,
  contribution, allocation, national implementation and community governance
  determine who can claim value and who bears the knowledge or biodiversity
  burden.
- **Operation:** a sample or sequence is collected, stored, reused, cited,
  commercialized, reported and linked—if at all—to a contribution or return.
- **Material consequence:** money, technology, capacity, research access or
  conservation support reaches a defined country, Indigenous people, local
  community or ecosystem.
- **Meaning:** open science can be understood as shared global infrastructure,
  while extraction without recognition can be experienced as biocolonialism or
  loss of cultural sovereignty.
- **Power:** database operators, researchers, firms, states and fund managers
  decide access, attribution, eligibility and allocation; source communities may
  have limited ability to contest use or negotiate terms.

## Bounded finding

> **Digital sequence information access is not equitable benefit sharing.** A
> database, consent protocol, fund, commercial-use contribution, national
> framework or implementation report does not establish that source countries or
> communities are recognized, receive timely benefits, retain governance, or see
> conservation, livelihood or cultural outcomes.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

DSI weakens the visible link between place and value: a genetic resource may be
collected in one landscape, sequenced elsewhere, stored in a global database and
used by a firm far from the source community. The policy challenge is therefore
not only financial. It concerns provenance, customary knowledge, consent,
attribution, data sovereignty and whether benefits return to the people and
places that sustain biodiversity.

The multilateral route may reduce transaction costs and make global research
possible where one-to-one bilateral contracts are impractical. It may also make
benefit sharing more distant from the original source. A credible ledger must
keep both possibilities visible rather than treating either open access or a
fund contribution as proof of justice.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Open DSI can accelerate public-health, conservation and agricultural research;
  restricting access can also harm source-country researchers and global science.
- A contribution can be real and useful without proving that every sequence or
  community was individually attributable; the mechanism is deliberately
  multilateral.
- Consent for collecting a physical sample does not automatically answer later
  questions about database reuse, commercial application or associated knowledge.
- Indigenous/local participation in a governance document is not the same as
  decision authority, allocation control or culturally appropriate return.
- A preliminary guide or implementation request shows policy movement, not
  completed benefit delivery.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: DSI access, data infrastructure, contribution,
allocation, local governance and conservation/community outcome are separate
stages. Keep provenance-to-benefit, community control and outcome claims in
Tier 2 until a named sequence/use/fund or jurisdictional implementation ledger
closes the chain.

## Next test

Build three linked ledgers: (1) a DSI database/provenance and consent record;
(2) a commercial user/contribution/verification record; and (3) a fund
allocation/community outcome record. For each, capture source geography, genetic
resource or knowledge relationship, user, use, legal route, contribution,
recipient decision authority, timing, conservation/livelihood/cultural result,
grievance and counterexample.

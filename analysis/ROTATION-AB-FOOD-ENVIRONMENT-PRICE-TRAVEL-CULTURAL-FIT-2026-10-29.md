# Rotation AB: Food environment, price, travel and cultural fit

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** food environment / household acquisition / place / affordability  
**Status:** Tier 2 mechanism with a stronger measurement boundary

## Research question

When a household appears to have a nearby food retailer or receives a food
benefit, how do price, travel time, store format, inventory, food-away-from-home
options and cultural fit change the actual acquisition route?

## Conversion chain

```text
benefit/income
  -> price and time budget
  -> store or alternative food source
  -> completed trip or delivery
  -> food acquired and accepted by the household
  -> adequate diet, continuity and food security
```

AA established that food insecurity, benefits and mapped retailer access are
different stages. AB deepens the place mechanism: distance is only one input
into a household’s feasible food choice set.

## Stage audit

| Signal | Safe interpretation | Prohibited leap |
|---|---|---|
| Nearby retailer | A modeled location-distance relationship exists | The household can afford, reach, enter and use the store |
| County food-environment score | Place-level conditions differ across communities | Every household experiences the county average or has the same cultural choice set |
| Food acquisition record | A household acquired food during the survey period | The food was adequate, nutritious, culturally acceptable or sufficient for the full period |
| National food-price increase | The purchasing-price environment changed in aggregate | Every household faced the same basket inflation or had benefits indexed to it |
| Pantry/family/alternative-source use | Households may supplement formal retail and benefit channels | Supplemental routes are reliable, dignified, nearby or sufficient |

## Bounded finding

> Food access is a feasible household choice set, not a distance score. Price,
> travel time, store format, inventory, alternative sources and cultural fit
> can convert the same benefit or mapped retailer into different actual food
> acquisition outcomes.

This is a mechanism finding, not a causal estimate of any one retailer or
program. The evidence supports separating geography, affordability and
acquisition; it does not yet support a single national “food access” score.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

The relevant place is not merely the census tract. It is the route a household
can repeatedly complete with its money, vehicle or transit, time, health,
language, storage, family obligations and preferred foods. A technically near
store can be socially distant if it lacks culturally acceptable inventory,
cannot be reached with a stroller or mobility device, has prices outside the
household’s budget, or requires a trip that displaces work and care.

This makes food infrastructure a cultural and political issue. Households may
experience “choice” as forced substitution, repeated emergency sourcing or
loss of dignity. Communities may value local food, specialty retailers,
farmers markets, family networks or mutual aid that national retail maps do not
fully represent.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A high-access tract may still have high prices or weak culturally relevant
  inventory.
- A low-access tract does not prove household food insecurity; households may
  use vehicles, delivery, family, schools, workplaces or community sources.
- FoodAPS is unusually useful for acquisition but is a survey snapshot, not a
  continuously refreshed household panel.
- CPI is a national urban average and does not measure the actual basket or
  substitution pattern of a particular household.
- Food Environment Atlas and Food Access Research Atlas use different units,
  years and methods; they should not be merged into an undifferentiated score.

## Promotion decision

Retain as a Tier 2 mechanism. Promote only after a named place or panel links
price and travel constraints to a completed acquisition route, the household’s
acceptable basket, and repeated food-security or health outcomes.

## Next test

Construct a place-by-household bridge using FoodAPS variables, the ERS food
environment/access layers, BLS food-price categories, transit/vehicle access
and repeated food-security observations. Add fields for culturally acceptable
choice, substitute source, time cost and trip failure—not just store distance.

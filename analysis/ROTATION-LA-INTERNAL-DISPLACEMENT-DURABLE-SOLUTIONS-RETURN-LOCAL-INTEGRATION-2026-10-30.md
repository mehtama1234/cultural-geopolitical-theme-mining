# Rotation LA — Internal displacement, durable solutions and place-based recovery

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When internally displaced people return, locally integrate or settle elsewhere, what evidence shows that displacement has ended safely and durably rather than merely becoming less visible?

## Why this packet matters

Internal displacement is often represented through annual movements or the
number of people displaced at year-end. Those metrics are essential but do not
show whether a person can safely return, remain, integrate locally or settle
elsewhere. IDMC's 2026 report is especially important because it warns that a
small decline in the global stock can coexist with unsafe returns, damaged homes,
limited services and repeated displacement. IOM frames internal displacement as
a structural governance and development issue, not only a short humanitarian
episode.

```text
conflict / violence / disaster / development pressure
  -> forced movement, repeated movement or protracted displacement
  -> assistance, return, local integration or settlement elsewhere
  -> housing, land, documents, safety, services, livelihood and social repair
  -> participation, belonging, non-discrimination and durable outcome
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| LA1 | IDMC's 2026 Global Report estimates 82.2 million people lived in internal displacement across 104 countries and territories at the end of 2025, the second-highest figure recorded and more than double a decade earlier. | Global stock, geography and long-run scale. | Individual duration, needs, safety, solution status or outcome. |
| LA2 | IDMC reports 68.6 million people displaced by conflict/violence and 13.6 million by disasters at year-end 2025, while 62.2 million internal displacements or movements occurred during 2025. | Cause and stock/flow distinction. | Unique people, repeated movement, protection, household recovery or causal ranking. |
| LA3 | IDMC says the slight decline in the number of IDPs did not reflect structural improvement; many reported returns occurred under fragile conditions, to insecurity, damaged homes and limited services. | Counterexample to “stock decline equals durable solution.” | Whether a named return was voluntary, safe, supported, dignified or sustainable. |
| LA4 | IDMC identifies return, resettlement and local integration as pathways requiring conflict resolution, peacebuilding, poverty reduction, disaster-risk reduction and enabling policies. | Multi-sector solution architecture. | Land tenure, housing repair, services, livelihoods, social cohesion, voice or remedy. |
| LA5 | IOM's 2026 chapter treats internal displacement as a structural governance and development issue and notes frequent, prolonged and multidimensional crises plus data gaps that obscure women, children, disabled people and other marginalized groups. | Governance shift and intersectional data boundary. | Disaggregated needs, local authority, funding, protection and group-specific recovery. |
| LA6 | IDMC's regional evidence shows large variation: Sub-Saharan Africa held 31.7 million IDPs, East Asia and the Pacific recorded 19.6 million movements in 2025, and conflict/disaster drivers overlap across most reporting countries. | Geographic heterogeneity and overlapping drivers. | Comparability of local definitions, severity, service access, culture or durable outcomes. |
| LA7 | The UN/IASC durable-solutions framework says return, local integration or settlement elsewhere should not be promoted when they endanger life, safety, liberty or health; independent monitoring and non-discrimination are required. | Normative safety and monitoring standard. | Enforcement, informed consent, actual monitoring, compensation or restored rights. |
| LA8 | UNHCR defines durable solutions around permanent national protection and, for IDPs, no longer having specific assistance or protection needs linked to displacement; pathways may include return, local integration or settlement elsewhere. | Outcome definition and pathway comparator. | Whether needs truly ended, legal status changed, income and services recovered, or belonging was restored. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Return is not simply movement back to a coordinate. A home may be physically
standing but occupied, mined, unaffordable, water-insecure or socially unsafe.
Land and property claims can conflict with new occupants, customary tenure or
formal records. A return may restore proximity to sacred sites, kinship and
language while removing access to schools, health care or work. Local integration
may offer better services but require adaptation to a new language, identity or
political order. Settlement elsewhere may be the safest path yet still be
experienced as cultural loss if participation and recognition are absent.

The geography of solutions is therefore layered: origin areas, displacement
sites, host neighbourhoods, secondary destinations, borderlands and diaspora
networks. Assistance can reduce visible need in one site while transferring
burden to a host community. A reduction in the stock can reflect death,
unreported movement, unsafe return or reclassification rather than recovery.

The geopolitical dimension is that durable solutions require state capacity,
peace, land governance, reconstruction finance and recognition. Humanitarian
actors can support but cannot substitute for political settlement. Data about
returns can also become a pressure instrument to declare crises over or promote
return before safety and consent exist.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A fall in the IDP stock, reported return, closed camp, cash grant, reconstructed
house, land document, local-integration plan, peace agreement, service count or
durable-solutions strategy does not prove that displacement ended. People may
move repeatedly; a return can be coerced or unsafe; housing can lack livelihood
and services; and local integration can burden host communities. Stock, flow and
solution metrics must be kept separate and disaggregated by gender, age,
disability, language, ethnicity, livelihood and place.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Internal displacement is a structural, place-based
governance and development problem. Durable solutions require safety, rights,
housing/land, services, livelihoods, social repair, local participation and
independent monitoring—not only movement or assistance.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **internal-displacement-to-safe-durable-place-based-recovery-and-belonging**. The next acquisition should follow one household from displacement through return, local integration or settlement elsewhere, joining legal status, land/housing, safety, services, livelihood, social protection, cultural continuity, participation, host-community effects, complaint and later re-displacement, with a non-displaced or alternative-solution comparator.

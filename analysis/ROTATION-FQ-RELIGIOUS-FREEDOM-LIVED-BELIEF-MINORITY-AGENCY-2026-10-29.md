# Rotation FQ — religious freedom, lived belief and minority agency

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 conversion question
**Research lane:** cultural, social, geographical, legal and geopolitical systems

## Why this rotation exists

Religious freedom is often represented through constitutions, country rankings,
foreign-policy designations or incidents of violence. Those are important
signals, but they do not establish whether a person or community can safely
hold, change, decline, manifest or transmit a religion or belief in everyday
life. The lived conversion includes registration, worship, clothing, family
life, education, work, burial, travel, migration, protection and remedy.

## Research question

When a state, court, international body, security service, community or
religious institution changes a rule or practice concerning religion or belief,
under what conditions does that produce safe and equal daily practice, minority
agency, family and cultural continuity, lawful mobility and durable trust
without converting religious freedom into geopolitical exceptionalism or
majoritarian privilege?

## Conversion chain

```text
law / registration / policy / incident / designation / dialogue / remedy
  -> recognition, restriction, threat or protection in a named place
  -> person or community worship, belief, dissent, identity, mobility or family
  -> safety, access, participation, continuity, livelihood or exit outcome
  -> agency, attribution, legitimacy, coexistence or geopolitical leverage
```

## Evidence snapshot

1. Pew's 2026 global analysis tracks government restrictions and social
   hostilities across 198 countries and territories, keeping state action and
   private hostility as separate dimensions. In 2023, restrictions rose in
   most regions while sub-Saharan Africa was the exception for median levels.
   These are country-level indices, not individual rights or safety records.
2. Pew reports 25 countries with very high government restrictions in 2023,
   up from 24 in 2022, and identifies religious harassment, mob violence,
   militant activity, terrorism and discriminatory rules as distinct forms of
   social hostility or restriction. A score cannot show whether a named person
   could worship, travel, work or obtain remedy.
3. USCIRF's 2026 recommendations assess conditions during 2025 and recommend
   countries for severe-violation designations. USCIRF explicitly warns that a
   country not covered or recommended is not thereby shown to have no religious
   freedom problem; reporting thresholds and available information shape the
   designation.
4. The UN Special Rapporteur's 2025 report on people on the move makes
   freedom of religion or belief a migration and protection issue. Refugees,
   migrants and displaced people can face restrictions both in transit and at
   destination, while documentation, registration, detention and asylum
   procedures affect whether the right is usable.
5. The UN human-rights mandate describes freedom of religion or belief as
   including the freedom to have, not have, adopt or change a religion or
   belief and to manifest it alone or in community, publicly or privately.
   This prevents a narrow definition that protects only organized institutions
   or majority traditions.
6. USCIRF country material illustrates how blasphemy laws, discriminatory
   regulations, non-recognition, mob violence, surveillance and restrictions
   on texts or ceremonies can combine. An allegation or country chapter is an
   evidence lead; it does not by itself establish prevalence, attribution or
   the later effect of a remedy.

## Bounded proposition

**A constitutional guarantee, religious-registration regime, international
designation, interfaith dialogue, security operation, anti-blasphemy law,
place-of-worship policy, public statement or diplomatic sanction is not by
itself lived freedom of religion or belief, safe minority participation,
cultural continuity, lawful mobility, family security or durable coexistence.**
Legal text, index score, incident report, designation or dialogue event does
not establish that named people could safely believe, change belief, worship,
wear or decline religious symbols, educate children, work, travel, bury their
dead, organize, obtain protection or secure remedy.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

- **Belief is broader than institution:** protection must include religious,
  non-religious and changing beliefs, as well as individual and collective
  practice.
- **Registration is power:** recognition can enable legal personality, property,
  schools and worship, while non-recognition can expose communities to closure,
  surveillance or exclusion; formal recognition can also become a gatekeeping
  tool.
- **Mobility changes risk:** people on the move may lose documents, community
  protection and familiar institutions, and can face different restrictions in
  camps, transit corridors, workplaces and destination neighborhoods.
- **Majority/minority geography:** the same rule can have different effects on a
  majority institution, a small sect, converts, atheists, Indigenous practice
  or diaspora communities; national averages hide this distribution.
- **Digital and physical space:** online speech, surveillance and incitement can
  extend pressure beyond the place where a person lives and affect relatives or
  diaspora participation.
- **Legitimacy and remedy:** coexistence is not proven by a public dialogue;
  people need safe reporting, impartial response, restitution, non-recurrence
  and agency to refuse or contest institutional pressure.

## Counterexamples and cautions

1. A country designation may be politically salient but cannot prove that every
   member of a group faces the same risk or that a sanction improves conditions.
2. Low social-hostility scores do not prove equal legal treatment, and low legal
   restrictions do not prove safety from private violence.
3. Interfaith events can build relationships while leaving discriminatory law,
   impunity or unequal resource access unchanged.
4. Registration can protect a community while also excluding unregistered,
   reforming or minority groups from lawful practice.
5. Protecting a sacred site does not establish protection for the people who
   use it, nor does it prove access, affordability or cultural control.
6. Reports based on public information and testimony can identify serious
   concerns but may have selection, attribution and under-reporting limits.
7. Security measures may reduce one incident while increasing surveillance,
   profiling, detention or fear for the wider community.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote the Tier 1 distinction that religious freedom has separate government,
social-hostility, mobility and lived-practice dimensions, and that country
indices/designations are not individual outcome evidence. Keep the following as
Tier 2: **religious-freedom-to-lived-belief-and-minority-agency conversion**.
The next evidence must name the person or community, practice, place, rule or
threat, response, safety/access change, agency and remedy over time.

## Next test

Build a corridor-and-place case involving one minority or belief community and
one mobile population. Link legal status and registration to named worship,
education, work, family, travel, burial or online-practice encounters; record
incidents, reporting, police/court response, displacement, return, community
voice and recurrence. Compare a protected and a contested site and document
non-use or exit as well as participation.

## Six-stage closure table

| Stage | What is currently supported | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | global indices, country recommendations, legal mandates and incident reports | local salience, under-reporting and lived meaning |
| Mechanism | law, registration, social hostility, violence, mobility and remedy pathways | actor power, profiling, family pressure and community safeguards |
| Operation | country, community, site, rule, incident and procedure can be named | repeated person-level encounter and response |
| Material consequence | restrictions, violence, detention, displacement and access barriers are observable in cases | durable safety, work/family/education continuity, mobility and remedy |
| Meaning | identity, conscience, dignity, belonging and recognition are central | participant attribution, refusal, cultural continuity and trust |
| Power | law, diplomacy, community organization and exit can change incentives | durable protection, accountability, non-recurrence and geopolitical effect |

## Source boundary

The packet closes discovery and framing for religious freedom and belief. Public
sources support the legal scope, country-level patterns, social-hostility
distinction, mobility relevance and designation limitations. They do not by
themselves establish a named person’s safe daily practice, equal treatment,
family security, cultural continuity, remedy or durable coexistence.

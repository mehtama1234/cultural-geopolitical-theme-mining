# Rotation BS — Clean cooking, household air pollution and gendered energy access

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** clean cooking / household air pollution / gender / health / energy access / rural and low-income geography  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 clean-cooking-to-exposure-and-agency question

## Research question

When clean-cooking fuels, electric cooking, improved stoves or household-energy
programs are introduced, what converts a technology or access claim into
sustained affordable use, lower personal exposure, improved health, less time
burden and culturally usable household agency?

## Conversion chain

```text
fuel scarcity, biomass cooking and household energy burden
  -> stove/fuel/electricity intervention and financing
  -> availability, affordability, reliability, safety, maintenance and cooking fit
  -> sustained use and lower household/personal air-pollution exposure
  -> health, time, gender agency, food/cooking continuity and dignity
  -> durable clean-energy adoption and local legitimacy
```

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** household air pollution, fuel poverty, time burden and gendered
  energy inequality are visible in health, energy-access and development data.
- **Mechanism:** fuel price, appliance cost, reliability, ventilation, supply
  chains, maintenance, safety, training, electricity quality, local recipes,
  taste, cookware and household decision rules determine practical use.
- **Operation:** a household acquires a stove or fuel, can replenish it, uses it
  repeatedly for actual meals, and does not have to revert to a polluting
  fallback when prices, outages or supply interruptions occur.
- **Material consequence:** personal and kitchen PM2.5/CO exposure, burns,
  respiratory and cardiovascular risk, fuel spending, collection time, cooking
  time, work and care schedules may change separately.
- **Meaning:** cooking is not only an energy service; taste, food identity,
  hospitality, ritual, autonomy, safety and dignity can determine acceptance.
- **Power:** utilities, LPG/ethanol/biogas suppliers, appliance firms, landlords,
  governments, donors, households and household members distribute cost,
  choice, maintenance and decision authority.

## Bounded finding

> **Clean-cooking access or stove distribution is not sustained exposure
> reduction or household agency.** A stove, fuel subsidy, electrification plan,
> project award, adoption claim, reduced biomass purchase or clean-fuel target
> does not establish that a named household can afford, obtain, safely use,
> maintain and repeatedly use the technology; that personal exposure fell; that
> health or time improved; or that women’s agency and culturally acceptable
> cooking continuity increased.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

The same technology has different meanings and operating conditions across
places. LPG may be cleaner at the point of use but unaffordable or difficult to
refill; electric cooking may be attractive where supply is reliable but unusable
where outages, tariffs, wiring or appliance power are limiting; improved biomass
stoves may reduce emissions without meeting health-protective exposure levels.
Households may retain a traditional stove for taste, peak cooking, ceremonies or
backup. That is not necessarily failure, but it means “primary reliance” is an
incomplete proxy for exposure and continuity.

Gender should be measured as labor, exposure, control and voice—not simply as
the presence of women in a beneficiary count. A reduction in fuel collection or
cooking time may free time, but it may also be absorbed by additional unpaid
care. The relevant unit is the household member who cooks, collects fuel,
decides purchases, pays for refills, cleans equipment and bears exposure.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A household can adopt a clean stove while continuing biomass use (“stove
  stacking”); primary-fuel surveys can therefore understate exposure.
- Lower biomass purchases can signal fuel scarcity, smaller meals or
  under-cooking rather than successful transition.
- An improved stove can lower emissions while still leaving exposure above WHO
  health-protective levels.
- Electrification is not clean cooking in practice if the grid is unreliable,
  the tariff is unaffordable, wiring is unsafe or the appliance cannot support
  local cooking practices.
- Women’s time can be saved without improving bargaining power, income, safety
  or household decision authority.
- Aggregate access gains can coexist with rural, poor, displaced, disability,
  minority or remote households being unable to obtain fuel, maintenance or
  replacement parts.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: clean-cooking access, device performance,
actual repeated use, exposure, health, time, gendered labor, agency, cultural
fit and local supply durability are separate stages. Keep
clean-cooking-to-exposure-and-agency claims in Tier 2 until a named household or
cohort ledger joins technology, price, reliability, cooking fit, repeated use,
exposure, health and gendered decision outcomes.

## Next test

Compare an urban electric-cooking household, a rural LPG/biogas/ethanol
household and an improved-stove/stacking household. Record fuel and appliance,
purchase/refill price, outages, availability, maintenance, cooking fit, meals,
repeated use, kitchen and personal PM2.5/CO, burns or symptoms, time saved,
fuel-collection burden, labor division, control over spending, safety, food
culture and willingness to continue. Include households that received a device
but reverted to biomass, and treat missing exposure data as unknown rather than
as success.

# Claim-to-source review — 2026-10-30

## Purpose

This is the first bounded claim-level review after the domain triage. It tests
whether a source supports the stage claimed by a packet, rather than treating
an official URL as proof of the whole conversion chain.

The 20 reviewed claims span AI infrastructure, linguistic sovereignty, student
mobility, critical minerals, shutdowns, AI weather forecasting, displacement,
digital public infrastructure, carbon-dioxide removal and neurotechnology.
They were selected because they connect directly to the latest portfolio
clusters and because their packets contain explicit next tests.

## Findings

- **20 claims reviewed:** 20 retain a bounded support statement.
- **Signals and scenarios:** the source base is strongest for global scale,
  operational transitions, policy architecture and risk signals.
- **Mechanisms:** several claims are supported as mechanisms or norms, but not
  as proof of implementation, local incidence or user benefit.
- **Outcomes:** no reviewed claim closes the full named-unit chain from exposure
  through adequate, durable and contestable outcome.
- **Counterexamples:** the displacement-return claim is explicitly retained as
  a counterexample to treating stock decline as durable resolution; each row
  records a missing boundary and a next acquisition.

## Interpretation

The review supports the atlas’s central discipline: a high-authority source can
close one stage without closing the next. The most common boundary is the
conversion from macro or institutional evidence to a named person, household,
worker, community, facility or place. A second boundary is from technical or
policy operation to material consequence. A third is from formal availability
to correction, remedy, local legitimacy or durable continuity.

The companion CSV is deliberately conservative. `supported_as_signal` means
the source supports a bounded signal, not causality. `supported_as_mechanism`
means the source supports a pathway, rule or design claim, not implementation.
`partially_supported` marks a claim whose direction or mechanism is supported
but whose local operation or consequence remains open. `supported_as_counterexample`
records a source that prevents an overly strong completion claim.

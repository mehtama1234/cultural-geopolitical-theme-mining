# Protective non-use is an active response to perceived institutional risk v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** bounded cross-domain synthesis; non-use is not treated as a single behavior

## The finding

Across migration, public-benefit, healthcare, consumer-finance, and platform
records, people sometimes respond to risk by avoiding a route rather than by
complaining, switching, or using the service. That behavior can protect legal
status, privacy, dignity, time, money, or safety in the short run while
sacrificing care, income, benefits, social connection, or future recourse.

```text
perceived risk or institutional exposure
  -> anticipated cost of being visible or contesting
  -> non-use, delayed use, non-reporting, or quiet exit
  -> protected immediate safety/control
  -> [open] sacrificed service, security, trust, belonging, or later action
```

Non-use is therefore an outcome that needs its own measurement. It cannot be
collapsed into dissatisfaction, political withdrawal, or freedom of choice.

## Cross-domain evidence

| Route | Observed avoidance or non-use | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Immigration concerns | In the WBNS route, almost one in five families reported going without public benefits because of immigration concerns; 16% reported not applying for or stopping noncash programs over green-card concern | Actual eligibility, benefit amount, legal consequence, later hardship, trust, and whether another safe route was used |
| Essential activities | Reported avoidance includes clinics, work, police, religious/community events, and public-benefit programs | The exact event, actor, duration, material loss, correction, belonging, and later collective response |
| Consumer fraud | Reporting and recovery effort are separate; non-reporting cannot be read as satisfaction, and time spent dealing with fraud can itself be a burden | Whether non-reporting reflected distrust, exhaustion, low loss, lack of information, or a usable alternative |
| Healthcare cost | People may delay or forgo care under cost or coverage friction | The same episode’s bill, alternative care, treatment continuity, remedy, later health, and trust |
| SNAP recertification | Notice, paperwork, interview, and time barriers can produce interruption or failure to complete the route | Whether interruption was temporary, corrected, substituted through another source, or converted into food/bill hardship |
| Platforms and digital services | Non-use, deletion intent, refusal, and exit are distinct from continued use; dependence can keep a person inside a service | Actual deletion, replacement quality, lost relationships or income, coercion, and durable exit |

These routes are not pooled. They show that visible participation is a
selected outcome: the people who continue using a system are not necessarily
the people who trust it, and the people who disappear are not necessarily
free of need.

## Why this matters for culture and politics

Avoidance is often misread as silence. But a person who skips a clinic, avoids
a benefit, does not report fraud, or declines a public event may be making a
practical judgment about surveillance, stigma, retaliation, time, paperwork,
or the expected value of being visible. That judgment can preserve immediate
control while reducing material security and weakening the institution’s
ability to observe the harm.

This creates a feedback loop:

```text
fear or low institutional confidence
  -> non-use / non-reporting
  -> fewer observed cases and weaker administrative signal
  -> less visible demand for correction
  -> continued exposure or deeper reliance on informal alternatives
```

The loop is a hypothesis about observability and incentives, not a causal
estimate from the current records. It explains why complaint counts,
participation rates, and continued use should not be treated as complete
measures of institutional legitimacy.

## Measurement rule

Each episode should distinguish:

- no need from need with non-use;
- voluntary non-use from constrained non-use;
- non-use from non-reporting and from failed attempted use;
- protective outcome from sacrificed outcome;
- a completed alternative from merely intending to find one; and
- later trust/action from the initial avoidance decision.

The minimum next fields are reason, anticipated risk, information source,
route attempted, time/cost, alternative used, material consequence, and later
return, switching, complaint, organizing, trust, or exit.

## Boundary and next test

The current evidence supports protective non-use as a recurring measurement
mechanism, not a single population rate or universal explanation. It does not
prove that every avoided service was needed, that institutions caused the
avoidance, or that continued use is coerced. The next decisive test is a
same-person or same-household episode that records the perceived risk, the
route not taken, the alternative, the protected/sacrificed outcome, and later
institutional meaning or action.

# Rotation V: home and community-based care, place and control

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** aging, disability, home/community-based services, caregivers, workforce and person-centered control  
**Status:** Tier 2 mechanism; eligibility, waiting-list, timeliness and quality frameworks are observable, actual reliable care and person control remain open

## Research question

When a person is eligible for Medicaid home and community-based services, what
converts eligibility and a waiver slot into dependable care in the home or
community, with worker continuity, caregiver relief, health, dignity and
person-directed choice?

```text
functional need and eligibility
  -> assessment, waiver/slot and waiting route
  -> worker/service availability
  -> delivered care and person-centered plan
  -> health, safety, caregiver capacity and community participation
  -> sustained choice and reduced institutionalization
```

## New trend interpretation

The sharper trend is not simply “more people receive HCBS” or “waiting lists
are shrinking.” It is:

> Care access is a person-and-place conversion problem: a formal slot becomes
> community living only when a suitable worker delivers reliable, adequate and
> person-directed care that the household can sustain.

The Access Rule and QMS show a shift toward measuring timeliness, stratification,
incidents, grievances, person-centered planning and worker compensation. That is
institutionally important, but reporting requirements remain upstream of the
person’s actual care encounter.

## Cultural and geographical meaning

Home and community-based care preserves more than a service location. It can
preserve language, family roles, neighborhood identity, faith, routines, pets,
land, employment and cultural participation. But “community” is not automatically
safe or supportive: a rural person may face long worker travel, thin staffing,
limited choice and caregiver exhaustion; an urban person may face fragmented
providers, housing barriers or distrust.

Person-centered care also carries a governance meaning. A plan can list the
person’s preferences while the actual worker, schedule, rate, transport and
provider market determine what choice is feasible. The meaningful endpoint is
control over a livable arrangement, not merely a documented plan.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A waiver slot or eligibility decision is not a staffed, completed or adequate
  service.
- A waiting-list count is not comparable across states or necessarily a count of
  current unmet need.
- Coverage in 44 states does not mean equal benefit design, provider supply,
  payment, timeliness or quality.
- A person-centered plan is not proof that the person chose the provider or that
  the plan was delivered.
- Direct-care compensation reporting is not worker retention, continuity or
  caregiver relief.
- HCBS can support community living while still failing to meet safety, health,
  cultural, language, accessibility or dignity needs.

## Promotion decision

Promote as a Tier 2 mechanism candidate:

**Community-based care is reliable, adequate and person-controlled delivery—not
eligibility, a waiver slot, a waiting-list number, a care plan or a coverage
statistic.**

The next decisive test is a named state/program/person-route ledger linking
assessment, wait, authorization, worker assignment, delivered hours, person
choice, caregiver burden, health/safety, community participation and any
institutional entry.

This is a planning control, not a quality score. A theme can have many
records and still lack the same-unit middle needed for an end-to-end claim.
“Mature” means the atlas has broad evidence and a clear boundary; “developing”
means at least one meaningful conversion is visible but important routes are
still open; “thin middle” means the next useful work should seek a different
unit or later stage rather than another aggregate endpoint.

**Current route decision — 2026-09-21:** the balance audit confirms that the

accepted capability to external response; or named facility/project operation
to service, burden, replaceability, or local acceptance. Another reach,
announcement, procurement, or modeled endpoint would not advance the missing
middle.

## What this changes

The program should not rotate evenly across themes. The next pass should seek
the missing middle in the three thin/developing clusters, while using mature
themes as context and counterevidence:

1. **Digital and consumer:** user or account outcome after a control, remedy,
   or exit attempt.
2. **Household, care, and public systems:** one dated family or person episode
   joining need, alternative, effort, actual service, and later security.
3. **Infrastructure and state:** one identifiable project, place, or incident
   followed from formal capacity into experienced service, burden, acceptance,
   or external response.

The cross-cutting test remains:

```text
formal capacity or rule
  -> usable route and available alternative
  -> money, time, status, safety, or control change
  -> actual receipt, use, or operation
  -> adequacy and durability
  -> meaning, trust, action, switching, exit, or leverage
```

Do not promote a new claim merely because a theme has become more current or
because a source uses words such as “empowerment,” “resilience,” “readiness,”
“access,” or “recovery.” Record the stage that is actually observed.

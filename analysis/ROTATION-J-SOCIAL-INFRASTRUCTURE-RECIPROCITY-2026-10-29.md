# Rotation J: social infrastructure, reciprocity and delivered support

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** volunteering, neighborhood cohesion, social connection, mobility and mutual support  
**Status:** Tier 2 mechanism packet; organized capacity and participation constraints are observed, delivered support and durable reciprocity remain open

## Research question

When people face isolation, illness, care needs or local disruption, does social
infrastructure convert relationships and organized capacity into actual help,
mobility, participation and later reciprocity?

```text
need or isolation
  -> available network, group, volunteer or neighborhood route
  -> actual contact, transport, care, information or material help
  -> participation/recovery and reduced isolation
  -> reciprocity, trust, exhaustion or exit
```

## Stage audit

| Stage | Evidence | Safe conclusion | Missing field |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need/constraint | CDC participation difficulty and older-adult homebound measures | Social infrastructure matters partly because people cannot access ordinary social routes | Specific need, duration, safety and local service gap |
| Available capacity | Volunteer hours, organizational membership and neighborhood cohesion are measured | Network and organizational capacity exist unevenly | Whether capacity is geographically reachable and relevant to the need |
| Delivered support | Not closed by volunteer hours or cohesion measures | Capacity is not delivered care, transport, food, information or companionship | Recipient-level help, frequency, adequacy and reliability |
| Participation/recovery | Cohesion/homebound association suggests a possible route | Place-based social conditions may support mobility and recovery | Intervention, causal mechanism, quality and longer-term recovery |
| Reciprocity | Volunteering and membership indicate engagement | People may contribute to collective capacity | Who is excluded, who exhausts, reciprocity and burden distribution |

## New trend interpretation

The sharper trend is not “volunteering is recovering.” It is:

> Social infrastructure is an uneven conversion system: organized capacity only
> becomes resilience when a person can reach the right relationship or group and
> receive reliable help at the moment of need.

The rebound in national volunteering can coexist with local deficits, health-
related participation barriers and exhausted networks. A place may have many
volunteers but no transportation, language access, respite, disability inclusion
or institutional coordination.

## Cultural and geographical meaning

Reciprocity is cultural: people may define help through family, faith, neighbors,
mutual aid, volunteering, online groups or formal services. The same activity can
feel like dignity and belonging for one person, or unpaid obligation and
surveillance for another.

Geography determines proximity, safety, mobility, third places, transport,
provider density and whether a network can respond beyond its own members.
Neighborhood cohesion is therefore not only an attitude; it can change whether
an older or disabled person leaves home, reaches a service or remains connected.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Volunteer hours and economic value do not show what help was delivered or to
  whom.
- High cohesion can coexist with exclusion, gatekeeping or pressure to provide
  unpaid care.
- CDC participation difficulty is self-reported and does not identify the local
  institution that could remove the barrier.
- The homebound study is observational; association between cohesion and recovery
  is not proof that cohesion caused recovery.
- National rebounds can conceal state, metro, rural, disability, age, race,
  language and income differences.

## Promotion decision

Promote the following Tier 2 mechanism:

**Social infrastructure is delivered reciprocity, not network density: its value
depends on reachability, relevance, reliability and whether the burden of
maintaining it is fairly distributed.**

The next qualifying evidence is a named place or network that documents a need,
actual support delivered, recipient outcome, volunteer/provider burden and later
reciprocity or exhaustion.

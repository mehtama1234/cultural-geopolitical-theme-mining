# Rotation DD — AI public procurement, vendor accountability and public-service realization

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Lane:** AI acquisition / public procurement / vendor accountability / data and model portability / public-sector capability / service delivery / resident notice / remedy / local capacity / competition / cultural and geographic inclusion
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 procurement-to-accountable-public-service question

## Research question

When a government agency buys an AI model, service, platform or vendor-supported
workflow, how does procurement become a working public service for residents—and
what preserves competition, data control, local capability, accessibility,
explanation, correction, remedy and institutional trust after deployment,
renewal, subcontracting or vendor exit?

## Conversion chain

```text
public need, budget, agency, community, service route, legal duty and procurement market
  -> market research, solicitation, evaluation, award, model/service, data rights,
     contract, SLA, subcontractor, portability, pricing and assurance terms
  -> integration, testing, staff training, deployment, vendor performance,
     resident encounter, decision, notice, error, correction, complaint and renewal/exit
  -> completed service, equal access, privacy, explainability, remedy, local capability,
     competition, public value, accountability and durable institutional trust
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| GAO AI acquisitions report | Agencies used different acquisition approaches, including products, services, contracts and other agreements; officials identified data/IP protection, expertise, requirements, testing, time and cost challenges. | acquisition approaches and recurring procurement constraints | resident-level service completion, remedy, local access, vendor performance and durable trust |
| OMB M-25-22 | Federal guidance calls for competition, market research, portability, clear licensing, pricing transparency, performance-based terms, testing, oversight, data protections and post-contract rights. | formal lifecycle controls and intended safeguards | whether clauses are enforced, understood by users, operationalized by staff and experienced by residents |
| GAO agency lessons-learned finding | Selected agencies were not systematically collecting and sharing lessons from AI acquisitions, including useful contract clauses and testing practices. | institutional learning gap in procurement governance | whether future awards improve, whether errors recur and whether public outcomes change |
| NIST public-procurement ethics guidance | Supplier accountability, documentation, monitoring, audits, training, testing and sub-supplier oversight can be written into contracts. | available contract-control architecture | public transparency, independent verification, correction, accessibility and actual remedy |
| NIST/GSA evaluation collaboration | Federal procurement is developing shared evaluation methods for model performance, security and functionality in real user workflows. | evaluation capability and workflow orientation | service reliability across languages, places, disability access, frontline use and resident outcomes |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** an agency announces an AI procurement, framework, pilot, model
  contract, vendor partnership, AI inventory, assurance process or performance
  requirement.
- **Mechanism:** public money and institutional authority become a specification,
  contract, data arrangement, model/service dependency, performance obligation,
  audit right or renewal decision.
- **Operation:** the system is integrated into a frontline workflow; staff and
  vendors use it; a resident seeks a benefit, permit, inspection, health,
  education, safety or administrative service; the system produces a result or
  recommendation.
- **Material consequence:** the resident receives an accurate, accessible and
  timely service—or faces delay, denial, privacy exposure, exclusion, cost,
  unexplained treatment, inability to correct a record or no practical remedy.
- **Meaning:** a “responsible procurement” can signal care to an agency while
  being experienced locally as opaque outsourcing, surveillance, language
  exclusion or loss of public responsibility.
- **Power:** agencies and vendors control requirements, model access, data,
  documentation, subcontracting and renewal; residents and smaller/local
  suppliers may lack visibility, bargaining power, portability or an exit route.

## Bounded finding

> **An AI procurement, contract, model card, assurance claim, pilot, AI
> inventory or human-oversight clause is not by itself a working public service
> or accountable government.** A tender, framework agreement, performance
> metric, impact assessment, vendor audit, “trusted” label or deployment does
> not establish that named residents received an accurate and accessible
> service, understood a decision, corrected a record, obtained remedy, or that
> public institutions retained local capability, competition and durable trust.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Procurement is an upstream power decision that becomes visible downstream in
the language of a form, the availability of a human fallback, the speed of a
case, the treatment of a remote place and the ability to challenge a result.
Large vendors may offer scale and technical capacity, while local suppliers,
community organizations and frontline workers hold contextual knowledge about
language, disability, informal practice, geography and trust. A contract can
protect data in legal terms while residents still lack notice or meaningful
control. A portability clause can exist while a small municipality cannot
operate without the vendor. A model can meet a benchmark while failing in a
low-connectivity district, minority language, rural office or culturally
specific service context. Public legitimacy therefore depends on the complete
conversion from public need to procurement to staff operation to resident
receipt, correction and remedy—not on the existence of an award or assurance
document.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Centralized procurement can reduce cost, improve security and give small
  agencies access to expertise they could not build alone.
- A contract clause or audit may be a meaningful control even when resident
  outcomes have not yet been measured; absence of outcome evidence is not proof
  of harm.
- A vendor may supply a reliable component while the agency’s data, staffing,
  workflow or appeal process causes the service failure.
- Competition and portability can conflict with specialized integration,
  security or continuity requirements; the relevant question is whether the
  dependency is visible, governed and reversible enough for the use case.
- A model benchmark or user-workflow evaluation does not by itself establish
  equitable performance across place, language, disability, gender, race,
  migration status, connectivity or institutional trust.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: procurement signal, contract mechanism,
deployment operation, resident material consequence, cultural/geographical
meaning and institutional power are separate stages. Keep the
AI-procurement-to-accountable-public-service claim in Tier 2 until a named
agency and vendor episode joins the award, data/model rights, subcontractors,
testing, staff use, resident exposure, service completion, subgroup access,
notice, correction, complaint, remedy, renewal/exit and local capability.

## Next test

Select one public AI contract or framework and reconstruct the full episode:
need, budget, market research, specification, bidders, award, pricing, data
rights, model/service version, subcontractors, testing, accessibility,
languages, staff training, deployment sites, resident encounters, decisions,
notice, error, correction, complaints, remedies, performance, renewal and exit.
Compare a central or well-resourced site with a rural, remote, low-connectivity
or linguistically distinct site. Record which evidence is public, which is held
by the agency or vendor, and which resident-level outcome remains unobservable.

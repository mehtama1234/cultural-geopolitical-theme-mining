# Rotation IH — Digital agriculture, smallholders and climate advisory

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When digital farmer registries, mobile advisories, weather data, marketplaces, climate-smart tools and agricultural finance are deployed, do smallholders make better locally relevant decisions and secure resilient income—or do systems stop at registration, app reach, platform activity and pilot claims without trusted use, affordability or harvest outcomes?

## Why this packet matters

Digital agriculture is a sociotechnical service chain:

```text
farm need / climate signal / market problem
  -> farmer identity, language and connectivity
  -> relevant weather, agronomic or market information
  -> trust, interpretation and decision
  -> input/finance/irrigation/market action
  -> harvest, loss, price, income and food security
  -> learning, adaptation, data control and repeat use
```

A platform can reach farmers without being affordable, locally relevant, accessible to women or useful under low connectivity. Advice can be accurate but arrive too late, conflict with labor/cash constraints or lack an input and market pathway.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IH1 | IFAD’s 2025–2030 digital-agriculture strategy identifies digital public infrastructure and farmer registries as foundational and sees digital technologies as enabling agronomic information, early warning and resource optimization. | Strategy and infrastructure architecture. | Registration quality, farmer control, local relevance, privacy, use or income. |
| IH2 | FAO’s guide warns many digital food-system tools are irrelevant to smallholders, inaccessible through paid subscriptions or non-local languages, and lack evidence or assessment of technical advice. | Explicit inclusion and quality counterexample. | A named tool’s actual farmer use, adoption, yield, income or trust. |
| IH3 | World Bank reports Kenya’s digital agriculture system registered 6.5 million farmers and delivered local weather- and crop-specific advisories to over 4.8 million farmers, with reported income increase of about $70 per season. | Scale, localization and reported outcome signal. | Counterfactual, same-farmer adoption, cost, gender/disability reach, crop risk or durable income. |
| IH4 | World Bank reports Côte d’Ivoire’s e-Agriculture project benefited over 400,000 people in selected value chains from 2018–2023 through a digital marketplace improving market access and information. | Named marketplace reach and value-chain mechanism. | Transactions, prices, farmer margin, smallholder inclusion, connectivity or resilience. |
| IH5 | World Bank’s West Africa results identify four digital platforms in use across Senegal, The Gambia and Guinea while noting constraints in inputs, irrigation, organizations, post-harvest logistics, finance and climate risk. | Platform use alongside system bottlenecks. | Farmer-level use, action, market realization, income or food security. |
| IH6 | World Bank’s Sri Lanka 2025 partnership targets 8,000 agri-food producers, irrigation/drainage across 71,000 hectares, climate-smart practices, data systems and food-safety improvements. | Integrated physical/digital climate-resilience investment. | Digital uptake, harvest, losses, income, equity or household food outcomes. |
| IH7 | World Bank describes Kenya’s KALRO big-data platform combining agro-weather, market and farmer data for real-time advisories and climate-smart decisions. | Data integration and advisory mechanism. | Farmer interpretation, action, trust, privacy, input access or harvest result. |
| IH8 | IFAD’s regional digital-landscaping work identifies the core constraint as an underdeveloped delivery system rather than a shortage of technology, using farmer-centered research in Cambodia, the Philippines and Viet Nam. | Delivery-system and human-centred-design boundary. | Scaled adoption, farmer control, affordability, local language, income or resilience outcome. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Farm decisions are embedded in local seasons, languages, land tenure, gendered labor, customary knowledge, trust in extension, credit, seed availability and market power. Data systems can help smallholders or create dependency on platforms, vendors and data intermediaries. Geospatial and farmer data raise questions of consent, ownership, commercial use and cross-border technology dependence.

## Counterexample and source boundary

Registration, advisory delivery and platform use are not proof of decision-use or harvest benefit. FAO’s warning about paid subscriptions, non-local languages and weak evidence is a direct counterexample to scale-first digitalization. Kenya’s reported income result is promising but not a causal evaluation in the public summary. Public evidence closes strategy, inclusion risk, scale, marketplace and integrated-investment stages, but not a comparable same-farmer chain through interpretation, action, harvest, income and repeat resilience.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A farmer registry, app, advisory, digital marketplace, platform-user count, climate-smart project or reported income signal is not by itself locally relevant advice, trusted use, affordable action, harvest, resilient income, food security or data agency.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **digital-agriculture-delivery-to-locally-trusted-farmer-decision-and-resilient-income**. The strongest next acquisition is a named farmer/cohort chain linking need and language, connectivity, advisory receipt, interpretation, input/finance/market action, cost, harvest/loss, price, income, food use, data control and repeat adaptation.

# Rotation CZ — Algorithmic hiring, employment access and livelihood mobility

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Lane:** automated recruitment / job advertising / résumé screening / video assessment / disability accommodation / adverse impact / workplace monitoring / wage and promotion decisions / worker voice / geography and livelihood
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 algorithmic-hiring-to-equitable-employment-and-livelihood question

## Research question

When employers and recruitment platforms use AI or automated tools to advertise,
screen, interview, rank, monitor, pay and promote workers, how does that system
change access to jobs and livelihood mobility across race, gender, age,
disability, language, migration status, class and place—and what evidence would
show that efficiency is not purchased through hidden exclusion?

## Conversion chain

```text
vacancy, employer demand, labour market, résumé, application, profile, video, voice, test and location
  -> targeted advertisement, ranking, keyword screen, chatbot, assessment, background check,
     productivity score, wage recommendation or promotion/termination signal
  -> recruiter/manager interpretation, accommodation request, interview, selection,
     offer, onboarding, assignment, pay, training, promotion or rejection
  -> access, waiting, income, job quality, surveillance, correction, appeal, worker voice,
     turnover, household security and geographic mobility
  -> fair opportunity, decent work, inclusion, livelihood resilience and trusted employment governance
```

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |

| EEOC AI guidance | AI is used in recruiting, screening, hiring, monitoring, productivity, pay, promotion and termination; seemingly neutral tools can intentionally or unintentionally create disparate impact. | legal coverage and use-case map | named tool exposure, selection rates, accommodation, job quality, pay and remedy |
| EEOC disability guidance | Algorithmic tools can screen out qualified people with disabilities; employers may need reasonable accommodation and alternative testing formats. | disability-risk mechanism and accommodation duty | whether applicants know, request and receive accommodation without penalty |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** a company advertises AI recruiting, skills matching, automated
  interviews, productivity analytics, pay optimization or “bias reduction.”
- **Mechanism:** platform profiles, résumé text, voice/video, tests, location,
  background data and workplace telemetry become features, thresholds and
  recommendations.
- **Operation:** a job ad is shown, an application is screened, a recruiter
  receives a score, a worker requests accommodation, a manager accepts or
  overrides an output, and the employment record is created.
- **Material consequence:** a person gets access, waits, is rejected, receives
  an offer, earns a wage, receives training or promotion, is monitored, exits
  work or loses income; the burden varies across people and places.
- **Meaning:** efficiency may be interpreted as fair opportunity by an employer
  and as invisible exclusion, accent/ability bias, surveillance or class sorting
  by applicants and workers.
- **Power:** employers, vendors and platforms control data, thresholds and
  explanations; applicants and workers often cannot see, correct or contest the
  process before livelihood consequences occur.

## Bounded finding

> **An AI recruiter, résumé screen, targeted job advertisement, video score,
> skills matcher or workplace analytics system is not by itself fair employment
> access or livelihood mobility.** A match rate, diversity dashboard, selection
> score, faster hiring claim, accommodation policy, human-review promise or
> compliance statement does not establish that named applicants received equal
> opportunity, understood the process, obtained accommodation, avoided adverse
> impact, secured decent work, received fair pay or could correct and remedy an
> erroneous decision.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Employment tools translate social history into opportunity. A résumé gap may
encode care work, migration, disability, conflict or local labour-market
collapse; an accent or video signal may encode language and culture rather than
ability; a location feature may encode transport, broadband, segregation or
care constraints. A national fairness metric can conceal exclusion in a rural
region, a minority-language community or a subcontracted labour market. The
worker’s ability to contest a decision depends on money, time, literacy,
disability access, immigration status, union presence, data access and the
availability of alternative employers. Employment legitimacy therefore depends
on the complete route from advertisement to stable, fairly paid and meaningful
work—not on model accuracy or aggregate hiring speed.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Automated tools can widen search, improve accessibility or reduce arbitrary
  recruiter discretion when job relevance, validation, accommodation and human
  review are real.
- A disparate selection rate is a signal requiring investigation, not alone
  proof of unlawful intent or causal model bias.
- Human review does not cure an automated process if reviewers defer to scores or
  lack authority to correct data and decisions.
- A hiring improvement may reflect labour demand, wages, location, recruiting
  volume or employer change rather than the tool.
- A legal settlement or policy statement establishes an accountability pathway,
  not necessarily restored income, career progression or durable livelihood.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: labour-market signal, tool mechanism,
applicant exposure, selection operation, employment quality, correction and
livelihood meaning are separate stages. Keep the algorithmic-hiring-to-
equitable-employment-and-livelihood claim in Tier 2 until a named employer or
platform joins tool exposure, applicant subgroup, selection, accommodation,
offer, pay, retention, progression, complaint, remedy and geographic outcome.

## Next test

Select one employer, staffing firm or recruitment platform and one job family.
Record ad delivery, application channel, tool/vendor, data fields, model use,
selection rates, language/disability access, accommodation requests, human
review, interview, offer, wage, start, retention, promotion, rejection reason,
complaint and remedy. Compare applicants by race, gender, age, disability,
nation, language, migration status, care burden and commuting geography, and
compare automated and non-automated routes while preserving job relevance and
labour-demand controls.

# The data sources behind the broad atlas

**Checked:** 2026-09-21  
**Purpose:** plain-language guide to the source families used for the broad
Cultural, Social, and Geopolitical Theme Mining program

## The basic rule

No single source can explain American social change. Each source family sees a
different part of the picture:

- surveys show what people report, believe, fear, value, or do;
- administrative records show how programs and institutions process cases;
- research studies test particular mechanisms in particular settings;
- firm and platform records show decisions, remedies, and operating rules;
- infrastructure records show construction, operation, rates, and capacity;
- geopolitical records show commitments, staffing, exercises, procurement,
  and coordination.

The atlas compares these layers to find recurring patterns. It does not treat
different datasets as if they followed the same person or household.

## Main source families

| Source family | Examples used in the atlas | What it helps answer | What it usually cannot answer alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household and economic surveys | Census SIPP, HTOPS, ACS, CPS, SHED, MEPS, Federal Reserve surveys | Who faces hardship, spending pressure, debt, income changes, health costs, benefits, or adaptation | Whether a particular institution caused the change, fixed it, or changed later political behavior |
| Time, care, and mobility data | ATUS, NHTS, BLS tables, HHS, GAO | Who supplies unpaid care, waiting, travel, childcare, eldercare, and work-time trade-offs | Whether a reported time burden produced a durable family, health, or political outcome |
| Opinion, identity, and political surveys | ANES, CCES, Pew, Gallup, CIRCLE | Trust, identity, belonging, values, political judgment, civic styles, voting, contact, and action | Whether an attitude caused a later action, or whether a reported action changed an institution |
| Public-benefit and government administration | SNAP/FNS, HUD, CMS, HHS/OIG, FEMA, GAO, USDA, state agencies | Eligibility, processing, enrollment, recertification, interruptions, payments, program rules, and administrative reach | Whether the person received adequate protection, recovered, trusted the agency, or changed political behavior |
| Consumer and regulatory records | CFPB, FTC, CPSC, DOJ, OSHA, complaint systems, recalls, settlements | Complaints, enforcement, refunds, safety actions, wage remedies, privacy routes, and formal correction | Whether the affected person received the remedy, was made whole, or could safely switch or exit |
| Work and firm data | BLS, Census BDS/BFS, NBER, company filings, DOL, worker cases, ILO, OECD | Employment, firm entry and exit, AI use, wages, ownership, bargaining, monitoring, concentration, and market power | Whether a firm-level change improved a worker's household security or a community's long-term future |
| Digital and platform records | Platform policies, privacy and deletion reports, AI-use surveys, safety actions, cancellation and restoration records | Use, purpose, moderation, privacy requests, platform response, AI support, attention, and formal exit routes | Verified deletion, durable safety, human substitution, wellbeing, or practical exit after the account or relationship changes |
| Housing, place, and insurance | HUD, FEMA, ACS, insurance records, HRSA, PLACES, state housing and utility records | Vouchers, lease-up, hazards, disasters, coverage, provider capacity, transport, mobility, and local inequality | Whether a household found a safe durable place, repaired fully, or had a realistic alternative to staying |
| Infrastructure and energy | DOE, EIA, FERC, NERC, PJM, NYPA, NYSERDA, state utility and public-comment records | Construction, operation, rates, reliability, large loads, energy credits, cost allocation, public voice, and dependence | Which household paid, which household benefited, whether the benefit lasted, or whether the public accepted the bargain |
| State and geopolitical records | NATO, allied governments, USAspending, SIPRI, IMF, defense records, exercises, procurement | Staffing, contracts, delivery, acceptance, readiness, coordination, alliance posture, and state capacity | Deterrence, adversary behavior, local consent, durable legitimacy, or proof that another actor changed its choices |

## How the sources are combined

The broad program uses three kinds of connection:

### Direct observation

One source measures one defined population, place, institution, or event. The
claim stays inside that source's denominator and method.

### Valid matched or repeated observation

A source follows an identified respondent, person-month, case, firm, or event
under a documented design. This can support a stronger timing or association
claim, but it still does not automatically prove causality or adequacy.

### Layered cross-source interpretation

Different sources show separate stages of a recurring pattern. For example,
SNAP administration can show route performance, SIPP can show hardship around
benefit transitions, and HTOPS can show later material pressure and confidence
patterns. Together they can support a broad finding about the separation
between public help, household security, and institutional judgment. They cannot
be written as one person's complete story unless the records are actually
linked.

## What a good source record preserves

Every important record should retain:

- the source and retrieval date;
- the population or unit being measured;
- geography and time window;
- denominator, weights, and missingness where relevant;
- the exact measure and method;
- uncertainty and counterinterpretation;
- what the source does not observe; and
- the next evidence needed to test the open link.

## The source-to-insight path

```text
source
  -> measured or reported claim
  -> method and limit
  -> recurring theme or subtheme
  -> affected people, institutions, firms, or places
  -> cultural and political meaning
  -> wider state or geopolitical consequence
  -> counterexample and next test
```

The goal is not to collect the largest number of sources. It is to use diverse
sources to see what keeps recurring, what varies by group or place, where power
and risk move, and which connection remains unproven.

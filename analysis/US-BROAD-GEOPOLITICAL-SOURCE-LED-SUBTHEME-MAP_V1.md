# State capacity and geopolitical consequence: source-led subtheme map v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** 174 current registry records carry the
`geopolitical_state_consequences` program-theme label. This map groups
repeated structures within those records; it does not treat an announcement,
contract, exercise, or public-opinion result as proof of strategic effect.

## What the source material supports

The recurring evidence distinction is:

> commitment, procurement, capacity, delivery, operation, oversight, public
> consent, and changed foreign behavior are separate clocks.

That matters because each stage can be real while a later stage remains open.

The [Section 333 partner-capacity review](records/us-gao-section-333-partner-absorptive-capacity-2023.json)
adds a distinct planning-to-use stage: among 46 selected proposals, 42 lacked
complete documentation of partner absorption, sustainment, or measurable
objectives. This is a process-risk observation, not a partner-failure rate; the
review does not follow those proposals through delivery or operational use.

## 1. Defense-industrial capacity is not delivered inventory or leverage

The [JASSM/LRASM realization record](records/us-defense-procurement-realization-milestones-2024-2026.json)
documents a $999 million announced production-support IDIQ contract, a
225,000-square-foot intelligent factory, and completion of a first phase of
F-35C/LRASM flight-science integration testing. The contract announced zero
funds obligated at award; none of the sources states accepted quantity,
customer delivery, fielding, inventory, or operational availability.

**Supported subtheme:** procurement support, factory investment, and integration
testing are genuine capability-building stages.  
**Not established:** the number of systems delivered, maintenance and
replaceability, allied availability, deterrence, or leverage over another
actor.

## 1a. Even same-product evidence must not pool accounting, contract, delivery, and acceptance stages

The [Polish JASSM-ER audit record](records/us-poland-jassm-er-audit-control-2022.json)
found that the reviewed settlement material did not tie a numeric value to the
JASSM-ER production-cost line. It neither identifies a missile lot nor states
accepted quantity, shipment, or fielding; an unrelated 32-unit comparator in
the audit cannot be assigned to JASSM-ER.

The [Borsuk delivery/SAFE record](records/us-poland-borsuk-delivery-safe-contract-boundary-2025-2026.json)
shows the next distinction in a single product family: 15 vehicles were
reported handed over under an earlier 111-vehicle 2025 contract, while a later
SAFE page lists a separate 146-vehicle contract scope without SAFE-specific
delivery or acceptance. The official accounts do not establish incremental
output, local-content share, operational availability, or a vehicle-level
link between the two contracts.

**Supported subtheme:** defense-industrial evidence has a strict lineage:
documented settlement control, signed demand, physical handover, acceptance,
fielding, sustainment, and strategic use are different claims even when the
same weapon or vehicle name appears in each source.

**Not established:** a pooled delivery total, a production-rate increase,
combat readiness, domestic economic benefit, or deterrent effect.

## 1b. A reported FMS delivery can precede settlement documentation without validating another program's delivery

The [Polish FMS delivery/settlement audit record](records/us-polish-fms-delivery-settlement-asynchrony-2024-2025.json)
uses a Polish Supreme Audit Office review as a narrow comparator. It reports
four F-35 aircraft delivered in December 2024 and January 2025, against an
estimated Q3 2024 delivery window; the advance had not been settled by the end
of 2024 and the settlement forms arrived in 2025. The same audit reports
PLN 1.534 billion in 2024 FMS advance settlements covering Q4 2023 and
Q1–Q3 2024 expenditures, but does not allocate that aggregate to JASSM-ER or
identify delivery, acceptance, training, or operational use for the missile
order.

**Supported subtheme:** physical delivery, contractual schedule, accounting
settlement, and readiness are independently dated evidence stages even within
one buyer's FMS portfolio. A real aircraft handover or aggregate settlement
cannot be used to fill an unobserved missile-delivery or acceptance claim.  
**Not established:** why the schedule differed; F-35 acceptance or readiness;
any JASSM-ER shipment, delivery, acceptance, fielding, payment, or deterrent
effect; or a general FMS timing rule beyond this audit comparator.

## 1c. A defense award can reveal a supplier surface without revealing delivered capability

The [JASSM/LRASM subaward-structure record](records/usaspending-jassm-lrasm-subaward-structure-2024-2025.json)
retrieves 74 reported subordinate awards under one DoD parent award between
October 2024 and February 2025. The returned records total $1.143 billion
across 51 distinct recipient names; the largest named recipient accounted for
18.38% of the returned-record amount. Their description fields mention a
diverse set of reported component and assembly classes, including warhead
cases, inertial measurement units, antennas, engines, processors, fuzing,
structures, optics, and power/connectors. This is a procurement extract, not a
bill of materials or production ledger.

**Supported subtheme:** parent award → reported subordinate recipient and
component-description surface. Supplier-number diversity and a largest-recipient
share make the procurement structure inspectable before it is claimed as
capacity, resilience, or operational power.
**Not established:** complete supplier tiers, ownership consolidation,
manufacturing location, inputs, workforce, quantities, acceptance, delivery,
inventory, readiness, local economic incidence, resilience, or geopolitical
leverage. Repeated lots, contract-reporting conventions, amendments, and
missing endpoint records can all alter the apparent concentration.

## 1d. Export-control implementation is a distinct stage before external leverage

The [GAO advanced-semiconductor export-control record](records/us-gao-advanced-semiconductor-export-control-compliance-2022-2024.json)
tracks three 2022–23 rules beyond announcement. Commerce worked with six other
agencies on licensing, technical review, foreign-partner outreach, border
verification, investigations, and enforcement. GAO also identified ten reported
private-sector compliance challenges; firms updated compliance programs and
self-reported potential violations, while BIS used comments, briefings,
definition changes, and rule updates to improve clarity.

**Supported subtheme:** a technology-security policy becomes a multi-agency and
firm implementation surface before it can be evaluated as a strategic tool.  
**Not established:** partner adoption, foreign substitution, shipment-level
incidence, PRC capability change, deterrence, or leverage. The report itself
points to later work on enforcement and engagement with key foreign partners.

The [BIS foreign-fab VEU record](records/us-bis-foreign-fab-veu-export-control-closure-2025.json)
adds a named policy transition inside that system. BIS ended license-free VEU
treatment for foreign-owned semiconductor fabs in China, described a route for
licensing continued operation of existing fabs, and stated that capacity
expansion or technology upgrades would not be licensed. This is an explicit
distinction between operating capacity and expandable capability; the affected
fab set, license outcomes, actual production, sourcing response, and strategic
effect remain unmeasured.

The later [Federal Register rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-02/pdf/2025-16735.pdf)
confirms a formal implementation event: three named PRC facilities were removed
from the VEU list effective December 31, 2025. The record advances formal
authorization change, but not license completion, operating continuity,
production, substitution, partner response, or leverage.

Company-side certification adds a narrow continuity layer: Samsung's Xi'an
certificate covers 12-inch flash-memory manufacture and related assembly/testing
through October 14, 2026, and SK hynix's current quality page lists Wuxi
12-inch integrated-circuit manufacture through August 2, 2027. These sources do
not establish actual output, utilization, individual licenses, upgrades, or
strategic response.

The [MOFCOM response](https://english.mofcom.gov.cn/News/SpokesmansRemarks/art/2025/art_267bddaf8d52497289627453b3d5636a.html)
adds an attributed state-reaction surface: opposition, a supply-chain harm
warning, a demand for correction, and stated intent to take necessary measures.
It does not establish an enacted countermeasure, firm-level operating change,
or leverage.

The [MOFCOM analog-IC investigation](records/us-china-mofcom-analog-ic-antidumping-investigation-2025.json)
adds a formal trade-remedy route after state opposition. It remains a process
stage: the applicant's import and price evidence is preliminary, the full
product universe and injury analysis are not disclosed, and no final duty or
firm-level response is yet established.

The [MOFCOM anti-discrimination investigation](records/us-china-mofcom-integrated-circuit-antidiscrimination-investigation-2025.json)
adds a broader legal-contestation route: a formal investigation, stated legal
basis, and evidence-submission channel. It remains open at the finding,
remedy, firm-response, and external-effect stages.

The [EU common-list update](records/us-eu-commission-semiconductor-dual-use-control-list-update-2025.json)
adds a partner-alignment surface: the EU adopted common controls for
semiconductor manufacturing/testing equipment and advanced-computing items on a
multilateral and member-state basis. The map retains open national licensing,
enforcement, firm adaptation, and strategic-effect stages.

The Commission's 2026 implementation report adds an aggregate enforcement
surface—€77.6 billion in authorized dual-use exports and 487 security-risk
denials for 2024—but does not isolate semiconductor items, member-state
decisions, or effects attributable to the 2025 list update.

The [Applied Materials BIS settlement record](records/us-bis-applied-materials-semiconductor-equipment-settlement-2026.json)
adds the first named firm-level enforcement endpoint in this chain. BIS
reported that Applied Materials and Applied Materials Korea routed ion
implanters through Korea to China without the required license for an
Entity-Listed recipient during 2021–22. The settlement reports approximately
$126 million in illegal shipments, a $252 million penalty, multiple compliance
audits, annual certifications, and personnel consequences. This establishes
violation → penalty → remediation as an observed route; it does not establish
future compliance, audit completion, production effects, Chinese substitution,
deterrence, or leverage.

The [Cadence BIS/DOJ enforcement record](records/us-bis-doj-cadence-eda-export-enforcement-2025.json)
adds a complementary semiconductor-design route. Cadence admitted 56 BIS
violations involving EDA tools and design technology valued at $45.3 million;
DOJ described at least 59 exports through a PRC alias to NUDT and related
entities. The coordinated resolution included a $95 million BIS penalty, DOJ
criminal penalties and forfeiture, a net resolution above $140 million,
account freezes, an export hold, and compliance remediation. The agency
counting frames remain separate, and later capability change or deterrence is
not established.

The [Alpha and Omega BIS order](records/us-bis-alpha-omega-huawei-power-semiconductor-settlement-2025.json)
adds a power-semiconductor enforcement route. BIS records approximately 1,650
power controllers, smart power stages, and related accessories sent through an
AOS China facility for Huawei across 15 unauthorized occasions after Huawei's
Entity List addition. The order imposed a $4.25 million civil penalty and made
timely payment a condition of export privileges; it also reports the later
elimination of AOS's Digital Power business unit without attributing that exit
to the enforcement action. Capability change and deterrence remain open.

The [Bosch BIS settlement record](records/us-bis-bosch-foreign-produced-mems-huawei-settlement-2026.json)
adds a foreign-produced-item and allied-firm route. BIS reported that
Germany-headquartered Bosch exported approximately $72.4 million in MEMS
sensors and automotive software from abroad to Huawei and affiliates under the
Foreign Direct Product Rule without authorization. Bosch filed a voluntary
self-disclosure and cooperated; the resolution included a $36.18 million BIS
penalty and approximately $3.6 million in DOJ disgorgement credited against
that penalty. This establishes cross-border enforcement and remediation, not
later compliance, supply-chain change, or deterrence.

The [BIS H200/MI325X licensing record](records/us-bis-h200-mi325x-case-by-case-china-licensing-2026.json)
adds a conditional-opening route after the restrictive and enforcement cases.
BIS established case-by-case review for specified advanced-computing exports to
China or Macau, conditioned on U.S. supply and foundry-capacity protection,
comparative TPP limits, prohibited-end-use certifications, and independent U.S.
testing. The source reports no approved license or shipment, so later market,
capability, and leverage effects remain open.

The [NVIDIA H200 license realization record](records/us-nvidia-h200-china-license-no-revenue-2026.json)
adds the first reported approval after that conditional route: NVIDIA says a
February 2026 U.S. license allowed small amounts of H200 products for specific
China-based customers. No H200 program revenue had been generated, and China
import approval remained unknown; inspection and tariff conditions therefore
keep this at license approval rather than realized trade or strategic effect.

The [NVIDIA partial H200 shipment record](records/us-nvidia-h200-partial-china-shipments-prc-restriction-2026.json)
adds the realized-delivery stage. NVIDIA reported a fraction of allowed H200
shipments after a $0.4 billion inventory and purchase-obligation charge, with
shipments below 1% of quarterly Data Center revenue. PRC restrictions still
blocked all licensed sales, and NVIDIA continued to describe the China
data-center market as effectively foreclosed; the evidence is delivery under
constraint, not restored access or capability transfer.

The [MOFCOM H200 non-confirmation record](records/us-china-mofcom-h200-shipment-nonconfirmation-2026.json)
adds a direct Chinese official response: when asked about U.S. claims of a
small number of H200 sales to China, the Ministry of Commerce spokesperson said
he did not know about the situation. That is a non-confirmation observation,
not evidence of a Chinese ban or approval; customer-level import and use remain
open.

The [SAMR NVIDIA-Mellanox investigation continuity record](records/us-china-samr-nvidia-mellanox-investigation-continuity-2026.json)
adds an open Chinese competition-enforcement stage. SAMR's 2025 notice
initiated further investigation after a preliminary finding, and its April
2026 review still listed the NVIDIA case. No final penalty or remedy is
reported, so the investigation remains an unresolved regulatory exposure rather
than evidence of completed enforcement or a proven H200-sales constraint.

The [Hygon DCU competitive-adaptation record](records/us-china-hygon-dcu-h200-competition-adaptation-2025-2026.json)
adds a domestic-firm response: Hygon said H200 entry would intensify
competition and described performance, compatibility, compliance,
cost-performance, and customer-case adaptations, alongside reported DCU use in
industry and internet applications. The company expected 2026 share gains, but
no customer switching, share measurement, or displacement is established.

The [Hygon realized growth and deployment record](records/us-china-hygon-dcu-realized-growth-deployment-2026.json)
adds realized domestic-firm evidence: Hygon reported first-half 2026 revenue
of RMB 9.099 billion, 66.52% year-over-year growth, and DCU deployment across
key industries and hundreds of scenarios, with 400-plus large-model
adaptations. These outcomes show deployment and expansion, not measured H200
substitution or a causal geopolitical effect.

The [Hygon large-model cluster realization record](records/us-china-hygon-dcu-large-model-cluster-realization-2026.json)
adds a reported operational milestone: preliminary stable operation of a
10-trillion-parameter model on tens of thousands of Hygon DCUs, with an updated
software stack covering 2,000-plus operators and 100-plus framework components.
This shows claimed infrastructure realization, not independent performance,
commercial durability, or NVIDIA displacement.

The [Hygon Kimi K3 adaptation record](records/us-china-hygon-dcu-kimi-k3-adaptation-deployment-verification-2026.json)
adds a model-specific realization endpoint: a 2026 listed-company interim
report states that Hygon completed full-process adaptation, deployment, and
performance verification of Kimi K3 on its DCU platform. It remains a
second-party report without independent metrics, customer acceptance,
production duration, or substitution evidence.

The [Hygon Kimi K3 performance-claim record](records/us-china-hygon-dcu-kimi-k3-reported-12-percent-performance-loss-2026.json)
adds a reported quantitative endpoint: a technical-news account attributes a
within-12-percent performance-loss result to Hygon and describes distributed
Kimi K3 training and multi-card inference on the Sugon 8000/Hygon stack. The
comparison baseline and test design are not disclosed, so this does not close
independent performance, production, utilization, or substitution.

The [Hunan public-system integration record](records/us-china-hunan-compute-platform-hygon-dcu-resource-integration-2026.json)
adds external public-system evidence: a provincial compute-scheduling platform
reported Hygon DCU compatibility and first-batch resource access by named
supercomputing, operator, and AI-innovation centers. This establishes an
integration milestone, not sustained use, procurement value, or substitution.

The [Changsha AI-center workload record](records/us-china-hunan-changsha-ai-center-hygon-dcu-model-deployment-2026.json)
adds named center-level applications: a 200P mixed Ascend/Hygon architecture
supporting visual, agricultural Q&A, and tourism models. The source confirms
workload context, not Hygon-specific utilization, performance, or displacement.

## 2. Trade dependence can change bargaining conditions without proving coercion

The [international-power record](records/us-nber-international-power-negotiation-2025.json)
models bilateral power from asymmetric import dependence adjusted for sectoral
substitutability. In its cross-country panel, increases in that measure are
associated with more bilateral engagement and negotiation; deteriorating
geopolitical relationships are associated with anticipatory trade adjustment.

The index is modeled, not an observed ability to force a concession. The study
does not identify a named U.S. episode, firm substitution, household cost, or
successful threat.

**Supported subtheme:** import asymmetry can be a bargaining resource and can
motivate diversification before a dispute.  
**Not established:** that dependence produced coercion rather than cooperation,
or that a country obtained a policy or strategic result.

## 2a. Tariff pressure can produce observable counterpart action without resolving whether either side gained leverage

The [2025 tariff-retaliation record](records/us-crs-tariff-retaliation-foreign-response-2025.json)
adds a dated foreign-government response ledger rather than a modeled bargaining
channel. CRS records China responding with tariffs, critical-mineral export
restrictions, U.S.-entity export controls, unreliable-entity designations, and
investigations; some measures were subsequently reduced, suspended, or removed
under temporary arrangements. Canada announced or implemented 25% counter-
tariffs on selected U.S. exports, later adding relief and removing some
measures while retaining others. The EU announced, adopted, paused, and later
suspended countermeasures while negotiations continued. These are actions and
status changes across different governments, not one pooled tariff rate,
trade-flow outcome, or concession measure.

**Supported subtheme:** state economic pressure can elicit retaliation,
negotiation, administrative relief, and partial reversal in the same episode;
external response is empirically distinct from the initiating state's capacity
or stated objective.  
**Not established:** firm compliance, trade diversion, household price or job
incidence, the reason for any pause or reversal, alliance durability, an
underlying policy concession, or durable strategic leverage. A temporary
arrangement or joint statement is not evidence of capitulation.

## 2b. Foreign military action can redirect domestic public mobilization without reducing total political activity

The [Iran strike/protest-response record](records/us-nber-us-israel-strikes-iran-protest-reversal-2026.json)
uses high-frequency geocoded Iranian protest events and U.S.–Israeli strike
exposure in a staggered county design. The working-paper abstract reports
widespread anti-government protest before the strikes; after first local
exposure, it reports a sharp shift toward pro-government and anti-U.S./Israel
protest, while total protest activity changed little. The result distinguishes
the direction of public mobilization from the amount of political activity.

**Supported subtheme:** external coercive action can meet a domestic response
that redirects mobilization toward the regime it seeks to weaken, so pressure,
public reaction, and strategic result cannot be collapsed into one outcome.
**Not established:** durable regime legitimacy, civilian protection, the full
role of repression, messaging, fear, or protest-recording conditions, U.S.
strategic success/failure, or later Iranian external behavior. The public
abstract does not give the full event count, effect estimates, or robustness
detail, and the source is a working paper.

## 2c. A unilateral carbon tariff can redistribute modeled emissions and welfare without delivering the full global result

The [carbon-tariff model record](records/us-nber-carbon-tariff-domestic-emissions-global-coordination-2026.json)
compares U.S. carbon-tariff scenarios with and without a domestic carbon tax.
Under its tariff-only scenario, U.S. emissions rise while foreign emissions
fall; the resulting modeled global-emissions reduction is about half that of
the combined tariff-and-domestic-tax policy. Both scenarios raise modeled U.S.
GDP and welfare, with the combined policy producing the larger effect.
Multilateral tariff-only results are modest and do not rise monotonically with
additional membership.

**Supported subtheme:** a state can improve a modeled domestic economic result
while shifting emissions geographically and leaving the global objective only
partly achieved. Domestic welfare, domestic emissions, foreign emissions,
global effect, and international coordination are separate outputs.  
**Not established:** real tariff implementation, firm relocation, household
price incidence, observed emissions, retaliation, partner acceptance, or
durable leverage. These are calibrated model scenarios, not an observed
policy evaluation.

## 2d. Retaliation can compound a bilateral trade route without establishing coercive success

The [bilateral conflict and trade-risk record](records/us-nber-bilateral-conflict-trade-risk-2026.json)
builds a monthly bilateral hostility indicator from GDELT events, calibrates it
against human-curated ground truth, and estimates gravity-trade relationships.
It separates kinetic fighting, military posture, sanctions-context hostility,
and routine diplomacy. The trade-weighted share of measured hostility routed
through sanctions contexts nearly doubled over the past decade. Kinetic and
trade-context hostility had economically large, statistically precise estimated
trade effects, whereas routine diplomacy had no detected aggregate trade
effect; directed hostility identified a lagged retaliation channel that
compounded trade losses over several months. The paper's pre-escalation
counterfactual places $334 billion in bilateral trade at risk, about half in
the U.S.-China pair. That is a model-based comparison, not observed lost
revenue or a forecast.

**Supported subtheme:** geopolitical friction has layers, and an initial
trade-context action can be followed by counterpart retaliation and persistence
in bilateral trade exposure. A timeline of announced measures and a monthly
estimated retaliation mechanism are complementary rather than interchangeable
evidence.
**Not established:** a U.S.-only or household-level incidence, effects on a
specific firm or worker, third-country substitution, alliance durability,
public legitimacy, or whether economic coercion achieved a political objective.
Event coding, reverse causality, specification, and the counterfactual baseline
remain part of the inference boundary.

## 2e. Export controls can reduce a targeted route and impose domestic firm costs without settling the security result

The [export-controls and domestic-costs record](records/us-census-export-controls-domestic-costs-2026.json)
uses Census export transactions, controlled-product classifications, and
firm-level outcomes to examine the 2014 U.S. restriction on exports to Russia.
The paper reports that the share of U.S. exports subject to controls doubled
between 2010 and 2024. Firms affected by the Russia restriction became less
likely to export targeted goods to Russia and also reduced exports of other
products and to other destinations; the paper reports lower average payroll for
affected firms. The public abstract does not disclose its full sample
construction, complete effect magnitudes, or firm-level heterogeneity.

**Supported subtheme:** a security-oriented export control can travel through
a targeted foreign-market restriction into broader exporter adjustment and a
measured domestic payroll exposure. Targeted export reduction, spillover to
other markets, domestic firm cost, and security or diplomatic achievement are
different stages rather than one policy outcome.

**Not established:** the restriction's national-security value, Russian or
third-country adaptation, employment counts, worker earnings, consumer-price
incidence, household welfare, a net social balance, or whether the reported
payroll change is entirely attributable to the restriction rather than the
Russia shock, demand, selection, substitution, or other contemporaneous
changes.

## 2f. A shared foreign services route can support goods-export continuity during crisis without proving broad resilience

The [multinational services-trade crisis record](records/us-census-multinational-services-trade-crisis-2008-2009.json)
uses linked Census goods- and services-sales data during the 2008–09 financial
crisis. U.S. multinational firms exporting both goods and services to the same
destination had higher goods-export growth than goods-only exporters; the
reported pattern was driven by intellectual-property-rights sales related to
industrial processes, including patents and trademarks. These bi-exporters
also had higher foreign-affiliate services-sales growth and higher domestic
services-sector employment growth.

**Supported subtheme:** cross-border commercial resilience can run through a
linked goods-and-services relationship rather than a goods shipment alone.
Destination access, services/IP transaction, goods-export continuity,
affiliate activity, and domestic services employment are separate parts of a
firm-level crisis route.

**Not established:** a national-resilience effect, a result for non-exporters,
net employment or wages, consumer benefit, causal generalization to another
crisis, strategic autonomy, or whether services exporting itself—not
technology, management, market power, customer relationships, or pre-crisis
strength—produced the comparison. This is a working-paper comparison of
multinational exporters with no public complete sample or effect ledger.

## 3. Oversight can improve while strategic outcome remains unknown

The [Ukraine assistance monitoring record](records/us-gao-ukraine-assistance-monitoring-evaluation-2023-2026.json)
covers a State Department contract overseeing $6.1 billion in nonhumanitarian,
nonmilitary assistance as of September 2025. GAO found use of some information
for project oversight and budgeting, but data access and varied outcome
timeframes constrained evaluation; cross-project evaluation and strategic
outcome indicators were incomplete as of November 2025.

**Supported subtheme:** monitoring can create more usable management
information during conflict without establishing assistance effectiveness.  
**Not established:** improved policing, border security, nuclear safety,
Ukrainian legitimacy, deterrence, or changed Russian behavior.

## 3a. Domestic installation agreements can expand before savings, service, labor, or readiness are verified

The [DOD intergovernmental-support record](records/us-gao-dod-intergovernmental-support-agreements-2026.json)
shows a domestic state-capacity route underlying military operations. Agreements
with state, local, and Tribal governments for utilities, road maintenance,
waste management, and related services increased from 45 in 2018 to 316 in
2025. GAO found some agreements lacked cost estimates or cost-benefit analyses
and that services lacked procedures to verify actual costs and savings. In 21
selected agreements, private contractors were used in nine; five selected
position comparisons ranged from 19% below to 50% above comparable wage
determinations, a narrow and nongeneralizable labor screen.

**Supported subtheme:** intergovernmental service agreements are a delivery and
governance stage between a defense institution's requirement and a reliable
installation service. Agreement growth is not a realized-savings, labor,
readiness, public-consent, or strategic-effect measure.

**Not established:** nationwide cost performance, service quality, worker
compensation/voice, installation resilience, mission readiness, or geopolitical
leverage.

## 3b. Installation maintenance is a domestic readiness and quality-of-life exposure before it is a measured strategic effect

The [DOD installation-maintenance record](records/us-gao-dod-installation-maintenance-backlog-readiness-quality-2026.json)
adds the condition of the underlying sites. GAO estimated $285 billion in
deferred maintenance in fiscal year 2025 and average funding of 80% of identified
facility-maintenance needs, versus DOD's 90% goal. It found work-order systems
too unreliable to assess maintenance effectiveness across installations and
services, while describing a plausible pathway from neglected mission-critical
facilities to readiness risk and from deficient HVAC, plumbing, and other
systems to health, safety, recruitment, and retention exposure.

This is not a national readiness-loss estimate. The eight installations GAO
visited or examined are not generalizable, DOD had not fully determined the
mission and personnel-quality-of-life risks of funding below its goal, and a
deferred-maintenance dollar is not one unit of operational, health, or retention
harm. Prioritization, temporary repair, relocation, and mission substitution can
also make the 80% average conceal very different site conditions.

**Supported subtheme:** domestic facility condition and the capacity to measure
and repair it are a pre-operational layer of state power, carrying unequal
exposure for installation users before any observed readiness or geopolitical
result.  
**Not established:** which facilities generated which harms, a causal readiness
effect, service-member or civilian health effects, completed retention loss, or
an adversary's response.

## 3c. Redirecting democracy assistance away from a host government can preserve a route while disrupting institutional work

The [democracy-assistance pivot record](records/us-gao-democracy-assistance-antidemocratic-pivot-2018-2024.json)
tracks a state response after reported democratic erosion, rather than only an
allocation. GAO reports $14 billion in worldwide USAID and State democracy
assistance allocations for fiscal years 2018–2023, including $4.5 billion
intended to encourage democratic practices within host-government institutions.
In four selected countries where antidemocratic actions were reported, agencies
shifted, paused, or ceased activities benefiting government entities and moved
some work toward civil society, independent media, or the private sector.
Agency officials reported lost programmatic momentum and uncertainty after the
redirection; none of the 12 reviewed awards had a plan for the risk that
government-entity activities would need to shift, pause, or cease.

This is not a pooled country effect or an estimate of money received. The four
cases were selected for democratic erosion and assistance exposure, and the
public report does not track each recipient, partner safety, completed
activity, or subsequent institutional condition.

**Supported subtheme:** when a host government's behavior changes, assistance
can move between institutional channels on a different clock from the local
institutions those programs were meant to support. Redirection can be a
necessary anti-complicity response while still producing a documented
implementation discontinuity.

**Not established:** whether redirection protected civil society, changed
democratic practice, public legitimacy, partner safety, recipient adequacy,
government behavior, or U.S. leverage; nor whether the reported lost momentum
was avoidable rather than an unavoidable cost of responding to repression.

## 4. Alliance access and rehearsal are not the same as operational performance

The [Free Association Compacts record](records/us-gao-free-association-compacts-implementation-oversight-2026.json)
documents more than $6 billion committed through 2043 and formal U.S. military
access and security rights in three Pacific states. It also records delayed
disbursement, rising construction costs, labor shortages, late reports, and
late single audits. The record does not establish completed services, local
acceptance, durable self-sufficiency, or strategic effect.

The [Denmark host-nation rehearsal record](records/us-nato-host-nation-rehearsal-denmark-2026.json)
documents a September 2026 NATO rehearsal for logistics, transportation,
movement, reception, and sustainment with Danish authorities. It has no public
measure of movement speed, failure correction, civilian continuity, cost, or
crisis performance.

**Supported subtheme:** access agreements and rehearsals make coordination
capacity visible before they demonstrate it under pressure.  
**Not established:** public consent, civilian resilience, real deployment
performance, deterrence, or sustained alliance reliability.

## 4a. Cross-border critical-infrastructure exercises demonstrate an interface, not incident resolution

The [MARSEC Baltic exercise record](records/us-eu-marsec-baltic-cross-border-infrastructure-response-2026.json)
documents a Finnish-hosted scenario involving suspected abnormal vessel
activity near undersea cables: participating authorities and commercial actors
exchanged vessel information, developed a shared maritime picture, and
practiced boarding, search, first aid, evacuation, firefighting, and vessel
control. It also identifies an information-sharing mechanism and the named
public, EU-agency, and commercial participants.

**Supported subtheme:** multi-actor rehearsal can make a cross-border response
interface observable before a real event. It is evidence of planned
interoperability—not a test of attribution, repair, service continuity,
jurisdictional agreement, cost sharing, community burden, or deterrence under
actual pressure.

**Not established:** response time or success in an actual cable/pipeline
incident, infrastructure restoration, changed adversary behavior, or public
legitimacy.

## 4b. A live multinational technology test can become deployment before it demonstrates sustained security effect

The [Task Force X-Baltic record](records/us-nato-task-force-x-baltic-2025-2026.json)
follows a progression beyond a tabletop or announced procurement. In a
three-week June 2025 live test, NATO reported more than 70 systems, 75%
availability for eight hours per day, persistent patrols and undersea-
infrastructure surveillance, and tracking of hundreds of vessels daily; its
one-third-cost comparison with crewed-frigate coverage is an institutional
claim, not an independently auditable cost result. In February 2026, eight
Allies signed a Phase II letter of intent for nationally owned capabilities
taskable by NATO, and NATO later reported a transition toward procurement and
deployment into real-world conditions.

**Supported subtheme:** a multinational capability can move from live test to
alliance commitment and reported deployment, creating a later realization
stage than a contract or exercise.  
**Not established:** persistent coverage, independent performance, maintenance,
cost sharing, local public consent, an attributed adversary response,
deterrence, or durable alliance reliability.

## 4c. A repeated security incident can trigger attribution and defensive posture without proving reduced risk

The [NATO airspace-incident response record](records/us-nato-airspace-incidents-response-followup-2026.json)
documents an August 2026 North Atlantic Council response to recent airspace
violations and drone incidents affecting Poland and Romania. NATO publicly
attributed responsibility to Russia, expressed solidarity with the affected
Allies, condemned the incidents, and said it was continuing to strengthen
integrated air-and-missile and eastern-flank defenses. This is an
incident-to-institutional-response sequence, distinct from procurement,
exercise, or generic alliance favorability evidence.

**Supported subtheme:** alliance response can become observable after a named
security incident through public attribution, collective political signaling,
and stated defensive-posture action.  
**Not established:** independent incident attribution, force levels, readiness,
host-nation or civilian burden, cost sharing, local acceptance, lower incident
frequency, changed Russian behavior, deterrence, or durable leverage. Public
condemnation and reinforcement can signal cohesion without changing the
external actor's calculations.

## 5. External commitments and public support are implementation inputs, not outcomes

The [Taiwan reciprocal-trade record](records/us-ustr-taiwan-reciprocal-trade-capacity-response-2026.json)
records an announced framework in which Taiwan committed to reduce or eliminate
99% of tariff barriers and described planned U.S.-linked industrial capacity
and purchases. Its three named purchase categories total $84.8 billion by
simple arithmetic, but the fact sheet does not show contracts, delivery,
payment, firm-level effects, or reduced supply dependence.

The [Polish defense-financing record](records/us-poland-defense-financing-public-opinion-2026.json)
shows that public judgment varies with the financing route: 51% supported
SAFE financing and 36% opposed it, while 46% supported and 37% opposed an NBP
gold-profit route. Just 17% supported both routes. This survey records
opinions, not a procurement order, production, delivery, or political action.

**Supported subtheme:** partner commitments and public legitimacy have their
own political and implementation clocks.  
**Not established:** realized investment, local jobs, household incidence,
procurement delivery, durable consent, or strategic autonomy.

## 6. Alliance legitimacy has separate public, domestic, and operational currencies

The [Pew NATO-legitimacy record](records/us-pew-nato-public-legitimacy-2026.json)
finds a 65% median favorability across 13 surveyed NATO states, while U.S.
partisan favorability was 75% among Democrats/leaners and 42% among
Republicans/leaners. Favorability is neither budget consent nor willingness to
host, deploy, accept risk, or sustain a particular commitment.

The [international-reliability record](records/us-pew-us-reliability-international-2026.json)
finds a 37% median favorable view of the U.S. across 36 countries and only a
32% median saying it considers countries like theirs' interests. Public
reception does not measure government cooperation, access, trade, or deterrence.

The [global-role legitimacy record](records/us-pew-global-role-legitimacy-three-audiences-2026.json)
keeps foreign reception, U.S. domestic judgment, and partisan meaning separate:
65% of U.S. adults said the U.S. should consider other countries' interests
even with compromise, while cross-country and partisan perceptions diverged.

**Supported subtheme:** state capacity and alliance arrangements are mediated
by distinct legitimacy audiences; public support, partner confidence, and
operational commitment cannot be substituted for one another.  
**Not established:** that a perception change altered a government decision,
host consent, capability, deterrence, trade, or adversary behavior.

## 7. Export controls can reach beyond the targeted transaction into domestic firms' other markets and payroll

The [export-controls record](records/us-census-export-controls-domestic-costs-firm-spillovers-2010-2024.json)
uses export transactions, control classifications, and firm outcomes around the
2014 Russia restriction. It finds that the share of U.S. exports subject to
controls doubled from 2010 to 2024; affected firms were less likely to export
targeted goods to Russia, but also reduced other products and destinations and
had lower average payroll.

**Supported subtheme:** a strategic trade restriction has an observable
domestic-firm incidence route beyond its named foreign target. Targeted trade,
firm adaptation, payroll, worker outcome, and security effect are still
separate endpoints.
**Not established:** national welfare, worker layoffs or household loss,
consumer prices, durable adaptation, or whether the restriction achieved its
security/diplomatic objective.

## 8. Tariff incidence arrives through direct imports and slower domestic-market channels

The [tariff pass-through record](records/us-nber-tariff-pass-through-consumer-prices-2025.json)
estimates that roughly 26% of the 2025 tariff increase passed through to
consumer prices relative to less-exposed goods. Of the estimated price effect,
64% was direct through foreign varieties and 36% through imported-input costs
and higher domestic markups, with the latter taking about nine to twelve
months.

**Supported subtheme:** geopolitical trade policy can become a household-price
mechanism through both border-facing and domestic-market structure; immediate
price observation misses the delayed supply-chain route.
**Not established:** an individual household's basket, quantity/quality loss,
wage or job effect, political attribution, or whether domestic production gains
offset the price effect.

## 9. Cross-border firm networks can be a private resilience buffer with unequal access

The [foreign-disaster networks record](records/us-census-cross-border-disaster-firm-networks-2026.json)
links bills of lading, foreign disasters, and ownership data. Disaster-affected
foreign suppliers showed sharp persistent export declines, and U.S. importer
purchase growth fell by up to 25 percentage points. Non-multinational buyers
contracted roughly twice as much as buyers inside global corporations; the
intra-firm versus arm's-length comparison shows a similar gap.

**Supported subtheme:** international integration can both create exposure and
provide substitution/coordination capacity. Resilience is therefore distributed
by firm-network position rather than simply by whether a country trades.
**Not established:** ownership as the causal buffer, consumer-price or
employment protection, national supply security, public legitimacy, or
geopolitical leverage.

## 10. Import reliance, a technical substitute, and strategic autonomy are different stages

The [critical-minerals record](records/us-gao-critical-minerals-substitution-recycling-dependence-2026.json)
tracks battery and semiconductor applications across processing, performance,
feedstock, recycling, manufacturing, and qualification. Battery recycling may
reduce some reliance within a two-to-three-year potential window if capacity
and feedstock constraints are solved; semiconductor substitution/recycling is
unlikely to reduce near-term gallium and indium reliance, and electronic-waste
recovery remains largely pilot-stage. Project selection and company targets
are not operating output.

**Supported subtheme:** a domestic project, technical possibility, or
import-reliance statistic is not usable substitution. Autonomy requires
application-level performance, collection/feedstock, processing, production,
customer qualification, and replacement under pressure.
**Not established:** an actual shortage, price/production effect, defense
delay, environmental or worker outcome, alliance effect, or strategic
concession.

## 11. Coercion theory identifies target-selection incentives, not observed compliance

The [geoeconomic-coercion record](records/us-nber-geoeconomic-coercion-government-firm-targets-2026.json)
models pressure through trade and financial linkages directed at foreign
governments, private firms, or both. Its mechanism distinguishes private
entities' lower internalization of national consequences from governments'
ability to influence domestic firms. It is a theoretical target-selection
model, not evidence that either route produced compliance in a named episode.

**Supported subtheme:** “the country” and “the firm” are analytically distinct
targets with different incentive and cost-distribution paths.
**Not established:** target frequency, real-world compliance, who bore the
cost, public response, alliance change, or strategic success.

## 12. A procurement prohibition can reduce direct exposure while supplier visibility remains incomplete

The [foreign-sourced telecom procurement record](records/us-gao-foreign-sourced-telecom-procurement-information-2026.json)
reviews Section 889 restrictions covering five named Chinese telecom and
surveillance companies and affiliates. Federal obligations to the named firms
declined after 2019, with no spending in three fiscal years; nearly 90% of
companies with active federal contracts in FY2025 represented that they did
not use the equipment. DOD and GSA had removal/search/representation controls,
but did not broadly share affiliate information and implementation lessons with
other agencies. A public representation and direct-spending decline do not
verify all equipment origin or avoided security harm.

**Supported subtheme:** a security procurement rule can change direct
contracting while affiliate knowledge and cross-agency implementation remain a
separate supply-chain control layer.  
**Not established:** verified equipment absence, cyber or espionage prevention,
operational continuity, substitution cost, or geopolitical leverage.

## 13. Alliance-space cooperation has organizational prerequisites that are not mission outcomes

The [allied space-coordination record](records/us-gao-space-allied-coordination-2025.json)
finds DOD uses multiple agreements, organizations, exercises, and activities
with allies, but reports staffing gaps, overlapping responsibilities, difficult
points of contact, and missing implementation milestones. GAO made three
recommendations after reviewing public material and officials from six partner
nations; classified material and mission-level results are outside the public
record.

**Supported subtheme:** formal alliance cooperation has an operational
coordination layer—roles, people, contacts, and milestones—before a shared
space capability can be treated as usable in a crisis.  
**Not established:** a mission failure, partner refusal, public consent,
readiness rate, deterrence, or strategic effect.

## 14. Local institutional integration can be a real security-operation stage before durable legitimacy

The [KFOR local-security integration record](records/us-nato-kfor-posture-local-security-integration-2026.json)
reports NATO's assessment of an improved Kosovo security situation, a planned
gradual posture adjustment, and Kosovo Police integration into KFOR's security
architecture at Visoki Decani Monastery in August 2026. Kosovo Police, EULEX,
Kosovo institutions, Serbian Orthodox Church representatives, and Serbian
Armed Forces appear as coordination actors. The public account offers no
incident series, actor agreement, local opinion, or causal attribution.

**Supported subtheme:** external security capacity can transition through
local-institutional coordination, but naming coordinated actors is not evidence
of consent, reduced risk, or a socially settled mission outcome.  
**Not established:** durable legitimacy, changed external behavior, incident
reduction, burden distribution, or completed geopolitical leverage.

## 15. Measured ISR operations are later evidence than readiness, not proof of deterrence

The [NATO ISR operational record](records/us-nato-nisrf-agile-combat-employment-2026.json)
reports a June 2026 activity across Norway, Finland, and Italy with roughly
180 RQ-4D flight hours and more than 500 intelligence products, integrating
aircraft, crews, specialists, infrastructure, and host-nation support. Later
accounts describe persistent collection and decision-support use during Neptune
Strike and surface-picture support in a High North maritime scenario. The
source does not identify product content, counterfactual decision quality,
host burden, adversary response, or target outcome.

**Supported subtheme:** a multinational capability can move from readiness to
repeated, geographically distributed operation and decision-support use—an
important realization stage separate from its strategic result.  
**Not established:** combat effectiveness, deterrence, alliance durability,
public acceptance, or changed adversary behavior.

## Cross-subtheme finding and next test

```text
commitment, trade/security policy, contract, procurement rule, or policy announcement
  -> domestic and foreign firm incentives, funding, capacity, coordination,
     substitution, or public support
  -> delivery, operation, price/payroll/supply response, and adaptation
  -> resilience, partner acceptance, and changed foreign behavior
```

The corpus is strongest in the first two stages. The decisive next records
should preserve the specific capability or agreement, delivery and acceptance,
operational use, maintenance and substitute options, local and allied burdens,
partner consent, and an independently observed change in another actor's
choices. Until then, “strategic effect” remains an open endpoint.

# Rotation IB — Climate-related relocation, community continuity and the right to choose

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When climate hazards threaten habitability and governments plan relocation or support mobility, do people gain safety, housing, land rights, livelihoods and cultural continuity—or are they moved, registered and housed without durable consent, host-community capacity or a meaningful choice to stay?

## Why this packet matters

Relocation is not simply a housing project or a movement statistic. It is a collective, often permanent reconfiguration of place, authority, social networks, livelihoods and identity. The conversion chain is:

```text
hazard / loss / habitability pressure
  -> risk knowledge and options to stay, move or adapt
  -> consent, land and host-community agreement
  -> financing, housing, services, transport and legal status
  -> physical move and livelihood continuity
  -> cultural governance, social ties and safety
  -> durable belonging, remedy and ability to shape future decisions
```

The packet treats staying, internal migration and planned relocation as distinct options. It also separates a guideline from consent, a new house from livelihood continuity, and a completed move from durable community continuity.

| IB1 | UN guidance on planned relocation defines it as a coordinated, collective and intended-permanent move for climate-related risk, and places dialogue, consultation, inclusion, dignity and self-determination at the center. | Conceptual and rights-based architecture. | A named community’s consent, land, services, livelihood or long-term wellbeing. |

| IB4 | UN human-rights guidance says relocation should be culturally appropriate, protect traditional governance and practices, require Indigenous consent where applicable, provide fair compensation and, where possible, an option of return. | Rights, consent, compensation and cultural-continuity requirements. | Implementation, enforceability, negotiated power or recovery after move. |
| IB5 | World Bank guidance for the Dominican Republic recommends codesigning climate-induced relocation with local communities, financing safe pathways and preparing health, education and housing at destinations, drawing on Lake Enriquillo lessons. | Destination planning and codesign architecture. | Actual destination capacity, household receipt, work, social ties, land rights or permanence. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Relocation can preserve a community or dissolve it. Sacred sites, burial grounds, language, fishing grounds, farming systems, customary law and intergenerational authority may not be replaceable by a housing plot. Host communities can face land, water, service and political pressures. Small islands, borderlands, river basins and conflict-affected areas make sovereignty, documentation and international financing central to the outcome.

## Counterexample and source boundary

The existence of Fiji’s guidelines, fund and procedures does not prove a completed community relocation or durable cultural continuity. A safe destination can still weaken livelihoods or social ties; a community may “consent” without a viable option to stay; and host-community services may be planned but not delivered. Public evidence closes definitions, rights, national architecture, codesign principles and service requirements, but not a comparable community chain through consent, move, livelihood, belonging and long-term remedy.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A hazard map, relocation guideline, trust fund, housing project, consultation, move, service plan or climate-migration estimate is not by itself safe choice, consent, secure tenure, livelihood continuity, cultural survival, host-community legitimacy or durable wellbeing.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **climate-relocation-to-consented-safe-community-continuity**. The strongest next acquisition is a named community chain linking hazard and options, consent, land/tenure, compensation, destination services, physical move, livelihood/social ties, cultural governance, host response, return/contestability and later wellbeing.

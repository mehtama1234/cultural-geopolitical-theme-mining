# Rotation LE — Digital services trade, local value and the AI divide

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When digitally deliverable services expand across borders, do connectivity, skills, payments and trade rules create broad local capability and decent jobs, or concentrate value, data, compute and bargaining power in a few economies and firms?

## Why this packet matters

Digital services trade is a geographical development mechanism, not just a
technology statistic. UNCTAD reports rapid growth in digitally deliverable
services while least developed countries remain marginal, with gaps in
connectivity, payments, skills, data and regulatory capacity. WTO data and
analysis make the opportunity visible across more than 200 economies, but the
trade value does not tell us who works, who owns the infrastructure, who captures
the surplus or whether local capability persists.

```text
connectivity / device / skills / payment / trust / trade rule
  -> firm or worker enters digital service market
  -> cross-border client, platform, data, model or supply-chain relation
  -> revenue, work, productivity, learning and local spillover
  -> value retention, bargaining power, inclusion, resilience and remedy
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Digital trade can make a small city globally connected while leaving its workers
dependent on foreign platforms, payment rails, languages and standards. The
capacity to export is not evenly distributed within a country: urban, educated,
connected and English-speaking workers may gain first, while rural, disabled,
minority-language and informal workers remain excluded. The same national export
growth can therefore coexist with local labour precarity and weak bargaining power.

Value is geographically layered. A service may be delivered by a worker in one
country, sold by a platform in another, hosted on cloud infrastructure in a third,
processed through a payment provider in a fourth and monetized through data or
IP elsewhere. National export data can credit the delivery location while missing
where control, tax, compute rents and learning accumulate.

The geopolitical risk is rule fragmentation. Data-flow, privacy, cybersecurity,
competition and AI rules can protect citizens or create legitimate trust, but
incompatible regimes can raise entry costs for smaller firms and countries. AI
may widen existing divides if compute, finance, data and expertise remain
concentrated. South–South digital trade and regional integration are plausible
counterexamples, but they require evidence beyond policy ambition.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A digitally deliverable-services share, export value, platform, connectivity
project, skills programme, AI strategy, trade agreement, firm entry or employment
increase does not prove broad local capability. Export growth can be concentrated
in a few firms or cities; connectivity can be available but unaffordable; a job
can be created without decent pay or security; and cross-border rules can improve
trust while raising compliance costs. The 21% Ecuador employment result is a
bounded firm-level association, not a global causal estimate.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Digital services trade is growing rapidly but remains
geographically and institutionally concentrated. Connectivity, payments, skills,
trust, data and rules are jointly necessary; export growth alone does not show
local value retention, decent work or inclusive capability.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **digital-services-trade-to-local-value-decent-work-and-inclusive-ai-capability**. The next acquisition should follow one firm or worker from connectivity, device, skill and payment access through client/platform contract, data/IP, revenue, wage, task quality, tax, reinvestment, learning, local supplier spillover and later resilience, with a non-exporter or alternative-market comparator.

# Infrastructure local-permission follow-up audit: Virginia Golden-Mars

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Purpose:** close the local-permission and public-attribution stages of the Virginia large-load infrastructure packet  
**Status:** local contestation and regulatory decision are observed; household bill incidence and data-center attribution remain open

## Executive result

The Golden-Mars record closes more of the local-permission chain than the
earlier large-load packet:

```text
named transmission project
  -> stated reliability/future-load rationale
  -> local notice and participation
  -> route and land-use conflict
  -> Commission route decision and fallback condition
```

The record includes more than 1,015 public comments, 598 registered public
witnesses, Loudoun County and homeowner/advocacy participation, a school-board
land-crossing constraint, and a Commission decision that Route 4 best served
the total public interest while identifying Route 3A as a possible fallback if
the school board did not consent.

This is a meaningful local-permission finding. It is not yet a household-cost
finding and it does not prove that the Golden-Mars project was caused by one
named data center or that the July 2026 PJM event involved the project.

## Bounded finding

**Infrastructure legitimacy is conditional on local institutional permission.** A
large transmission project can be technically justified and still require
public notice, route comparison, homeowner participation, school-board consent
and a fallback decision. The project’s political meaning is produced through
who controls land, who bears visual/property costs, who benefits from future
load and who can delay or redirect implementation.

This is a stronger finding than “communities oppose data centers” or “grid
projects create backlash.” It identifies a specific mechanism:

```text
future capability/reliability claim
  -> local land and route exposure
  -> organized institutional voice
  -> conditional consent or route change
  -> implementation durability
```

## What this does not prove

- Golden-Mars is not assigned to Amazon, Microsoft or any single data center by
  this packet.
- The July 2026 PJM transfer event is not assigned to Golden-Mars.
- Public comments do not measure the broader Loudoun population’s opinion.
- A route decision does not establish construction, energization or household
  bill effects.
- A petition’s claims about private commercial benefit and cost allocation are
  attributed to the petitioning parties, not treated as Commission findings.

## Promotion decision

Promote the narrower Tier 1 finding:

**Local permission is an operating stage of infrastructure capability, not a
residual public-relations outcome.**

Retain the broader data-center cost/incidence question as Tier 2 until the
project-to-customer and project-to-bill fields are closed.

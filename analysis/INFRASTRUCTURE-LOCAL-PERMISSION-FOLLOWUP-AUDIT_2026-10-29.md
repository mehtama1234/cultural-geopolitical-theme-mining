# Infrastructure local-permission follow-up audit: Virginia Golden-Mars

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Purpose:** close the local-permission and public-attribution stages of the Virginia large-load infrastructure packet  
**Status:** local contestation, route substitution and regulatory decision are observed; construction, household bill incidence, data-center attribution and appeal outcome remain open

## Executive result

The Golden-Mars record now closes more of the local-permission chain than the
earlier large-load packet. It also contains a later outcome that was not in the
first version of this audit:

```text
named transmission project
  -> stated reliability/future-load rationale
  -> local notice and participation
  -> route and land-use conflict
  -> Commission route decision and route substitution after non-consent
```

The record includes more than 1,015 public comments, 598 registered public
witnesses, Loudoun County and homeowner/advocacy participation, a school-board
land-crossing constraint, and a Commission decision that Route 4 best served
the total public interest. The later final-order record, as summarized on the
official Loudoun County project page, selected Route 3A on June 29, 2026. The
route changed because the preferred route required School Board consent that
was not obtained. The case is now a route-substitution and appeal story, not
merely a hearing-stage permission story.

This is a meaningful local-permission finding. It is not yet a household-cost
finding and it does not prove that the Golden-Mars project was caused by one
named data center or that the July 2026 PJM event involved the project.

## Bounded finding

**Infrastructure legitimacy is conditional on local institutional permission,
and non-consent can redistribute burden rather than stop capability.** A large
transmission project can be technically justified and still require public
notice, route comparison, homeowner participation and school-board consent. In
this case, the institutional veto did not end the project in the public record;
it moved authorization from the Commission's preferred, lower-impact Route 4 to
Route 3A, which the earlier record described as more harmful or less desirable.
The project's political meaning is produced through who controls land, who bears
visual/property costs, who benefits from future load and who can delay, redirect
or appeal implementation.

This is a stronger finding than “communities oppose data centers” or “grid
projects create backlash.” It identifies a specific mechanism:

```text
future capability/reliability claim
  -> local land and route exposure
  -> organized institutional voice
  -> conditional consent or route change
  -> implementation durability
```

## What this does not prove

- Golden-Mars is not assigned to Amazon, Microsoft or any single data center by
  this packet.
- The July 2026 PJM transfer event is not assigned to Golden-Mars.
- Public comments do not measure the broader Loudoun population’s opinion.
- The June 29 route order does not establish construction, energization or
  household bill effects.
- A petition’s claims about private commercial benefit and cost allocation are
  attributed to the petitioning parties, not treated as Commission findings.

## Promotion decision

Promote the narrower Tier 1 finding:

**Local permission is an operating stage of infrastructure capability; when
consent fails, capability may proceed through a redistributed local burden.**

Retain the broader data-center cost/incidence question as Tier 2 until the
project-to-customer, project-to-bill, construction and appeal fields are closed.

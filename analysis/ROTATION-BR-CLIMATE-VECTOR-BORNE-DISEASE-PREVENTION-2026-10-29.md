# Rotation BR — Climate-sensitive vector-borne disease and local prevention

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** climate-health / vector-borne disease / dengue and malaria / urban ecology / migration / local surveillance and prevention  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 ecological-signal-to-health-outcome question

## Research question

When climate, land use, travel, urbanization and migration change the geography
of vector-borne disease, what converts ecological suitability and surveillance
signals into timely local vector control, diagnosis, treatment, household
protection and equitable health outcomes?

## Conversion chain

```text
climate, land use, water, housing and mobility conditions
  -> vector suitability and pathogen introduction signal
  -> surveillance, warning, vector control and community response
  -> diagnosis, treatment, prevention and household protection
  -> fewer severe cases, deaths and transmission
  -> trusted, climate-resilient local health capacity
```

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** climate, urbanization, water, land use, travel and population
  movement are changing the places and seasons in which vectors and pathogens
  may circulate.
- **Mechanism:** temperature, rainfall, standing water, housing, waste, vector
  ecology, pathogen evolution, immunity, mobility, surveillance, laboratory
  capacity and health-service access determine actual transmission and response.
- **Operation:** a mosquito or other vector is present; a person is exposed;
  surveillance detects a signal; authorities and households act; a patient is
  diagnosed and treated or a cluster is contained.
- **Material consequence:** infection, severe disease, death, household cost,
  missed work/school, migration burden, vector-control chemical exposure and
  health-system capacity may change separately.
- **Meaning:** a newly affected place can experience disease risk as climate
  injustice, stigma or institutional abandonment; migrants and displaced people
  may face both higher exposure and weaker access to care.
- **Power:** health agencies, cities, landlords, employers, border systems,
  laboratories and households distribute surveillance, prevention and treatment;
  data-poor places may be treated as low-risk when they are simply under-observed.

## Bounded finding

> **A climate-suitability model, vector signal or disease-surveillance alert is
> not prevented infection or equitable treatment.** A risk map, forecast,
> mosquito-range shift, case dashboard, early-warning system, vector-control
> program, public message or health-plan commitment does not establish that a
> named household avoided exposure, received timely diagnosis and treatment,
> experienced reduced transmission, or retained trust and care continuity.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Vector risk is ecological but prevention is social. A model may show that a
mosquito could survive in a new place, while actual cases depend on housing,
water storage, travel, immunity, testing and health-seeking. Conversely,
under-reporting can make a place look safe. The same intervention—spraying,
source reduction, screens, repellents or community cleanup—can be protective in
one context and ineffective, unaffordable or culturally unacceptable in another.

Climate-resilient health systems need migrant-inclusive and locally trusted
routes. Cross-border disease surveillance must not become a mechanism for
stigma or exclusion; it should connect data, prevention, diagnosis and treatment
across the routes people actually travel.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A suitable climate envelope does not prove disease transmission; immunity,
  vectors, pathogens and health behavior mediate risk.
- A case increase can reflect improved testing or reporting rather than only
  higher incidence.
- Vector-control activity can reduce mosquitoes without proving fewer infections;
  it can also create chemical, labor or ecological burdens.
- A vaccine or treatment availability statement does not establish access for
  migrants, rural households or people without documentation.
- Climate change can increase risk for one disease/place and decrease it for
  another; aggregate claims require disease- and geography-specific evidence.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: ecological suitability, surveillance, vector
control, diagnosis, treatment, household protection, transmission, mortality
and equity are separate stages. Keep ecological-signal-to-health-outcome claims
in Tier 2 until a named place or cohort ledger joins climate/vector conditions,
surveillance, intervention, access and later case/severity outcome.

## Next test

Select one urban dengue corridor, one rural/migrant malaria corridor and one
newly suitable temperate region. Record weather, land/water/housing conditions,
vector data, travel/migration, surveillance sensitivity, warning, vector control,
diagnostic turnaround, medicine/vaccine access, household action, cost, stigma,
case severity, mortality and trust. Compare high-reporting and data-poor places
without interpreting missing cases as low transmission.

# Remedy implementation is not the same as lived recovery v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-21
**Status:** cross-domain synthesis of retained public case records; no pooled
recovery rate

## The finding

The strongest records in the atlas now travel beyond exposure and institutional
response. They show reported payment, restored work access, restored wage or
seniority, repeated post-restoration use, provider switching, or a worker's
decision not to return. Those endpoints are not interchangeable.

```text
harm or exclusion
  -> complaint / intervention / adjudication
  -> order, offer, correction, or reported payment
  -> access or money actually used
  -> durable security, trust, alternative choice, or exit
```

The portfolio is strongest in the middle of this chain and thins sharply at the
last step. A remedy can be implemented while recovery remains partial; access
can return while lost pay remains unresolved; payment can be reported while
receipt, adequacy, and later security remain unknown; and switching or
non-return can occur without proving that a viable alternative existed.

## The remedy-evidence ladder

The cases now support a practical evidence ladder. Each step is stronger than
the previous one for a different question, but no step should be silently
treated as the next step.

| Evidence stage | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Rule, order, or program design | An institution created a route, obligation, or eligibility rule | That the route was used, implemented, or reachable |
| Implementation or institutional proof | An agency, employer, firm, or administrator reports correction, payment proof, monitoring, or a completed process | That the affected person received, cashed, or found the remedy adequate |
| Aggregate delivery or use | A reported group-level amount, recipient count, cashing share, or successful reissue exists | Which person received what, residual loss, or household recovery |
| Recipient-side receipt or actual access | A named or bounded person/worker/customer got the payment, access, repair, or return | Durability, adequacy, restored status, trust, or freedom to leave |
| Durable lived recovery | Later security, continued safe use, stable work/care, repaired account, trust, switching, or exit is directly observed | Nothing beyond the measured endpoint; it still needs a clear unit, clock, and counterfactual boundary |

The new DOL PAID records illustrate the middle of the ladder: the program
requires employer proof of payment, and its 2019 release reports more than
$4 million put into the hands of 7,429 employees. Those are stronger than a
payment promise, but they remain an institutional and aggregate endpoint. They
do not create a worker-level receipt, adequacy, or durable-security rate.

The new [formal-remedy/lived-recovery audit](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/data/formal-remedy-lived-recovery-audit-v1.json)
applies this distinction to thirteen deliberately selected named routes. All
thirteen have an observed or reported formal intervention, none has
independently verified recipient-level receipt across the full remedy, none
closes adequacy, durable recovery, or trust, and three record a later failure
or rupture of the initially observed remedy or protection. These are coverage
findings, not remedy failure rates.

The audit also preserves a positive counterexample: a DOJ IER case reports a
next-day return to work and approximately $420 in back pay. That is stronger
implementation evidence than an offer or allocation, but it still does not
show independent receipt, adequacy, recurrence prevention, or durable security.

The CFPB follow-up adds a useful aggregate receipt ladder without turning it
into a recovery rate. In three completed Bureau-administered distributions,
the FY2024 report states that 69% of distributed Premier Student Loan Center
funds, 86% of U.S. Equity Advantage funds, and 88% of SMART Payment Plan funds
were cashed. The FY2025 report then records later reissue or redistribution
after unresolved payments, including $20,809 in direct payments to people who
requested reissued Premier payments, $129,729 later redistributed for U.S.
Equity Advantage, and $174,178 for SMART Payment Plan. This establishes that
remedy reach continues after the first distribution and that some money is
used, but it does not identify person-level receipt, account correction,
adequacy, residual loss, trust, switching, or household recovery. Unclaimed
money is therefore not merely an administrative footnote; it is part of the
practical access problem.

The Amazon Prime settlement adds a subscription-specific residual stage. The
FTC and court filing report aggregate dollars issued and accepted, while a
revised order expands automatic eligibility and makes possible supplemental
payments conditional on stated acceptance thresholds. Later distribution is
therefore not simply “more money”: it can depend on clearing an earlier
reachability or acceptance hurdle. The public record still does not show each
customer's receipt, amount, delay, remaining loss, continued use, switching,
trust, or household recovery ([FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/ftc-announces-additional-payments-consumers-stemming-ftcs-amazon-prime-settlement); [court filing](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2123050amazonprimeredressfundorder.pdf)).

The Amazon Flex program adds a stronger worker-remedy aggregate endpoint. The
FTC's 2024 refund report states that 2,806 Zelle reissue payments succeeded,
reported participation rose from 90.3% to 92.3% of eligible drivers, and
130,952 drivers had received full compensation totaling more than $58.5 million
as of December 31, 2024. This is stronger than a fund allocation or payment
offer because the administrator reports a recipient-level aggregate and a
successful reissue count. It still does not expose any one driver's amount,
timing, residual loss, later work, household security, trust, or exit ([FTC
report](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/ftc-refunds-consumers-2024.pdf);
[structured Amazon Flex ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/amazon-flex-tip-refund-event-ledger-v1.json)).

The FleetCor/Corpay case adds a small-business version of the same boundary.
The FTC reports a federal court finding on hidden or unauthorized fuel-card
fees, a proposed $100 million redress allocation, and prospective informed-
consent and charge-disclosure controls. That is stronger than an allegation or
an unimplemented complaint route, but the complete customer denominator,
claim path, distribution, receipt, restored cash flow, alternative fuel-card
access, switching, and business survival remain unobserved. A large redress
number therefore recognizes the scale of institutional response without
becoming a small-business recovery rate.

The DOJ IER FY2026 cases add a worker-level positive middle-stage
counterexample: one employer reportedly paid $30,000 in back pay and offered
reinstatement after an employment-document barrier; another paid $8,000 in
front and back pay and changed its Form I-9 and E-Verify procedures. Payment
and a firm-process change are both visible, but accepted reinstatement, payment
adequacy, continued employment, household security, trust, and exit remain
open. A public follow-up recheck of the strongest retained IER and OSHA
worker-voice cases likewise found no later payment, safe-return, durability,
trust, or exit record. This is an evidence boundary, not evidence that the
remedies failed.

The Sauna360 recall adds a product-level durability counterexample. A 2025
professional-repair route was followed by a September 2026 CPSC expansion
stating that the earlier repair was ineffective and offering a second
professional repair. This shows why a correction snapshot or repair offer must
be followed by a durability check. It does not show that every owner received
the first notice, experienced failure, or completed the second repair; owner
notice, downtime, safe use, trust, and continued use remain open.

An official Fair Work Ombudsman case supplies a stronger payment-receipt
comparator. After an appeal by Atkins Freight Services, the Federal Court
released funds held in trust and the Ombudsman reported that all ten truck
drivers had received back-payments ranging from $8,012 to $91,566. This closes
the payment-receipt step for that public case; it does not establish whether
the amounts restored household security, repaired trust, changed later work,
or created a practical exit from the employer or sector. The record therefore
shows that a public remedy chain can reach the worker while still stopping well
short of lived recovery ([FWO case record](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/2018-media-releases/january-2018/20180117-atkins-appeal-dismissed)).

The Malaysia GrabCar Tribunal case supplies the earlier institutional-use
stage. A statutory platform-worker route was in force, the Tribunal had been
established, and a named first case reached hearing and evidence. That shows
that a worker can reach a remedy institution before the public record shows an
award, payment, restored platform access, or durable trust. A hearing is
stronger than a legal-design announcement but weaker than an implemented
remedy ([case record](projects/ai-work-control/malaysia-grabcar-tribunal-first-hearing-case-record-v1.md)).

The Australian Kyei merits case supplies a separate process-versus-merits
boundary. The decision identified procedural deficiencies but accepted a
substantive safety reason for deactivation, dismissed the unfair-deactivation
claim, and ordered no restoration. A process defect does not automatically
invalidate the underlying decision or restore work; dismissal also does not
prove that the worker experienced no income loss, burden, or later consequence.
Payment, later access, alternatives, trust, and exit remain open
([implementation recheck](projects/ai-work-control/uber-kyei-merits-followup-recheck-2026-09-18.md)).

The September 2026 Innovative Associates case adds a different worker-remedy
boundary. The Fair Work Ombudsman reports that a temporary-visa worker was
left with no usable wages under an unlawful cashback scheme, that the total
underpayment was $40,164.49, and that the company back-paid her in full after
court proceedings and penalties. This is a stronger formal monetary endpoint
than an offer or order alone, but it still does not expose payment timing,
worker confirmation, adequacy, safe future work, household recovery, trust, or
exit. The case therefore distinguishes “full back-pay reported” from “life
restored” ([FWO case record](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/2026-media-releases/september-2026/20260901-innovative-associates-penalty-media-release); [structured ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwo-innovative-associates-cashback-backpay-event-ledger-v1.json)).

The [Southern Cross Care ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwo-southern-cross-care-nsw-underpayment-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json)
adds a firm-correction and reachability case: an employee's overtime question
triggered a self-audit, the employer reported back-paying 3,603 employees, and
the undertaking requires continued location efforts and payroll controls. The
[DOL Workers Owed Wages ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/dol-workers-owed-wages-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json)
shows the same problem from the federal recipient route: recovered money still
requires location, notice, identity verification, claim completion, and payment
access. These records make “recovered” a staged administrative state, not a
synonym for worker receipt or household recovery.

The [OSHA Texas asbestos ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/osha-texas-asbestos-whistleblower-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json)
adds a distinct worker-voice mechanism. Two workers reportedly raised safety
and environmental concerns during post-disaster repair, were fired, and then
received an OSHA retaliation finding plus reinstatement and compensation
orders. This advances the voice-to-remedy path, but the public record does not
show payment, safe return, employer compliance, community protection, or
whether speaking up became less risky afterward. A remedy order can therefore
restore formal status without proving restored agency.

The [CFPB Wells Fargo redress ledger](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/data/cfpb-wells-fargo-redress-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json)
adds the consumer-side design counterpart. The consent order did not treat a
mailed or issued payment as sufficient: it required an affected-consumer list,
amount calculation, location efforts, and follow-up when payments were
returned or uncashed. That is not evidence that the plan succeeded for any
particular borrower. It is evidence that institutional redress architecture
already recognizes reachability as part of the remedy, while the public record
still stops before borrower-level delivery, cashing, account correction,
adequacy, or trust. The later CFPB termination order reports institutional
fulfillment and redress of inaccurate furnishing, adding a formal-completion
stage without closing the individual recovery gap.

Bilt adds a distinct collaborative-implementation stage. In June 2026, the
CFPB reported that Bilt had proactively contacted potentially affected
customers, offered reimbursement for overdraft, late, and insufficient-funds
fees, and was reviewing requests for more than 500 newly identified customers;
the Bureau said it would continue monitoring until satisfied. This is stronger
than a dormant order or a general promise because outreach and a live
reimbursement process are visible. It still does not establish each person's
fee amount, payment receipt, account correction, adequacy, or later financial
security. The CFPB Ombudsman's FY2025 report supplies the person-side
counterpoint: redress inquiries included address updates, reissued checks, and
help navigating a third-party administrator after an expired check. Monitoring
can therefore coexist with individual reachability failures ([CFPB Bilt
update](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-works-to-ensure-bilt-consumers-are-made-whole/);
[CFPB Ombudsman report](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_ombudsman-annual-report_2025.pdf)).

New York DFS supplies a state-regulatory insurance comparator. Its 2025
Consumer Assistance Unit reported 39,443 insurance complaints received, 39,064
closed, and more than $121 million in recovery. In the narrower mixed
property-casualty/service-contract category, 498 of 2,869 closed complaints
were labeled positive outcomes, with $17.95 million in reported recovery. This
shows that a regulator can record closure, a positive-outcome label, and money
recovered. It does not show whether a particular claim was fully paid, a home
was repaired, coverage was restored, premiums became affordable, or the
household could safely stay or move. The category's denominator is also
complaints reaching DFS, not all insurance problems ([state complaint and
recovery record](records/us-new-york-dfs-insurance-complaints-availability-2025.json)).

Meadley supplies the clearest public payment-without-restoration counterexample.
The Fair Work Commission reports that a $24,143 compensation award was paid,
but the worker was not reinstated; Federal Court enforcement then produced a
further $15,045 award plus interest and a $10,000 penalty. This closes a
reported monetary-remedy stage while leaving the original work, status, later
employment, household recovery, trust, and exit unresolved. Payment is
therefore not interchangeable with restored opportunity ([official FWC case
account](https://www.fwc.gov.au/role-court-unfair-dismissal-cases); [structured
ledger](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-meadley-enforced-payment-no-reinstatement-event-ledger-v1.json)).

NYC DCWP's current settlement page adds a larger administrative worker-remedy
comparator. It reports named worker groups and restitution routes including
more than $270,000 for over 900 Allstar workers, more than $1.6 million for
over 570 Walgreens workers, more than $154,000 for over 50 Intimissimi workers,
and two scheduled checks for Dunkin/Taco Bell workers. The page identifies
payment by mail or deposit, asks eligible workers to update address or bank
information, and gives an administrator contact route. This is stronger than
a bare order because eligibility, amount, delivery method, and installment
timing are visible. It still does not show each worker's receipt, amount,
uncashed or returned payments, adequacy, later work security, trust, or exit
([DCWP settlements and lawsuits](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/consumers/consumer-complaint-topics.page)).

A July 2026 DCWP announcement moves the same route one step further at the
aggregate level: Walgreens, Allstar, Calzedonia/Intimissimi, and Kinship were
reported to owe a combined $2.1 million in restitution to more than 1,600
workers, with the announcement naming the employer groups and violation
categories. This is stronger evidence of administrative recovery capacity than
an announced settlement alone, but it is still an institution-reported total,
not a worker-level receipt file. The gap between “the city recovered or secured
money” and “each worker received adequate, usable, durable relief” therefore
remains a live middle-stage blocker ([NYC DCWP July 2026 announcement](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dca/news/046-26/mamdani-administration-recovers-2-3-million-workers-major-labor-rights-crackdown)).

The route also exposes a structural reachability problem. DCWP proposed an
Unclaimed Restitution Fund in July 2026 because restitution can be unclaimed by
the intended worker or consumer, or never paid by a delinquent, insolvent,
dissolved, bankrupt, or otherwise nonresponsive respondent. The proposed rules
would let the city and Comptroller move eligible unclaimed money into a fund
and make later disbursements, potentially including partial payments, while
making those disbursements discretionary. This is not evidence that a worker
received money; it is evidence that the institution itself treats “award or
settlement” and “money reaches the affected person” as different operational
problems. The remedy chain therefore needs a distinct unclaimed/unpaid/reissue
stage, not only a receipt checkbox ([DCWP proposed Unclaimed Restitution Fund](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/queenscb12/downloads/pdf/2026/DCWP-NOH-Unclaimed-Restitution-Fund.pdf)).

The live DCWP settlement page makes the timing problem visible too: Theory
payments were scheduled to begin in April 2026 and Salz Management payments in
August 2026, while Allstar, Walgreens, Kinship, and Intimissimi pages still
direct eligible workers to a notice administrator for payment questions or
address/bank updates. The page provides routes and schedules, but no public
completion table showing mailed, deposited, returned, reissued, or cashed
payments. As of the September 19, 2026 review, the strongest defensible claim
is therefore scheduled or administrator-mediated reachability—not verified
receipt ([current DCWP settlement page](https://home4.nyc.gov/site/dca/consumers/consumer-complaint-topics.page)).

The proposed fallback has not yet become a final operating rule. The NYC Rules
page still labels the Unclaimed Restitution Fund “Proposed” after the July 9,
2026 comment deadline and hearing. The city's regulatory-agenda material gave
an approximate June–August 2026 schedule for the new Chapter 14, but the live
rule page does not show adoption, a fund balance, a claim form, a first
disbursement, or an accounting. This adds an implementation-delay stage:
recognition of non-reachability → proposed institutional fix → adoption and
funding → claim/reissue route → actual receipt. The fix cannot be counted as
remedy delivery while it remains proposed ([NYC Rules status page](https://rules.cityofnewyork.us/rule/establishment-of-unclaimed-restitution-fund/); [DCWP regulatory-agenda entry](https://a856-cityrecord.nyc.gov/Search/GetFile?documentId=43921&requestId=20260507003&requestStatus=Current&sectionId=4)).

These records answer different questions. “The institution acted,” “the
recipient reportedly received or used something,” “the person kept using the
service,” and “the person recovered or could leave” must remain separate fields.

## The deeper pattern

The cases suggest four practical currencies of remedy:

1. **Access:** the person can work, transact, receive a service, or return to
   an account.
2. **Money:** a payment, refund, back pay, or replacement amount is reported.
3. **Status and continuity:** seniority, prior wage, workplace location, or
   account terms are repaired.
4. **Choice:** the person can continue, switch, refuse return, use an
   alternative, or exit without carrying an unmeasured penalty.

The first three can improve while the fourth remains constrained. This is why
“remedy implemented” is not a household recovery measure and why “continued
use” is not proof of satisfaction. A worker may return because the job is
necessary; a customer may switch because the provider failed; a recipient may
cash a payment while still carrying residual loss; and a person may not return
because the original workplace is no longer acceptable, because another option
exists, or because the cost of returning is too high.

## Voice can open the remedy route without proving restored agency

The worker-voice cases sharpen the cultural and social meaning of this gap. In
the IER DACA case, a worker's complaint was followed by a lower-wage rehire,
then a bilateral agreement restoring the prior wage, back pay, and seniority;
the complaint was withdrawn after the agreement. In the OSHA asbestos case,
two workers raised safety and environmental concerns, faced reported
terminations, and obtained an agency retaliation finding plus a reinstatement
and damages order. These records show that voice can convert a private burden
into an institutional response.

They do not show that the worker became safe, secure, trusting, or free to
leave. Complaint withdrawal is an immediate procedural endpoint, not a general
trust measure. Reinstatement is an access remedy, not proof of safe conditions
or durable protection. The deeper test is whether speaking up later changes the
worker's usable options: continued safe work, credible non-retaliation,
adequate payment, a viable alternative, or an exit that does not impose a new
unmeasured penalty.

The OSHA case makes that final condition concrete. Speaking up can be a route
to institutional recognition and still impose immediate livelihood risk; the
later order does not tell us whether the worker can safely speak again, return
to the job, or leave without carrying the cost. The DOL wage-recovery route
supplies the complementary institutional version: even when the system has
custody of money, the worker must remain reachable and capable of completing
the route. Voice and receipt are therefore not merely personal traits; they
are produced by the design and accessibility of the institution.

## Coding consequence for the atlas

The next evidence pass should not add another generic response count. It should
seek, for one already identified episode:

```text
reported remedy
  -> dated receipt or actual access
  -> amount / adequacy / continuity
  -> later recurrence or persistence
  -> trust, alternative use, switching, non-use, or exit
```

The minimum acceptable follow-up must preserve the same unit and distinguish:

- agency or firm report from recipient confirmation;
- ordinary post-restoration earnings from lost-pay recovery;
- a payment amount from money actually received and adequate;
- a switch attempt from completed replacement use;
- non-return or non-use from successful exit; and
- later political contact or action from remedy or recovery.

## Boundary

This synthesis is a structured interpretation of selected public records. The
cases are not representative, the units are not pooled, and the evidence does
not support a population recovery rate or a claim that remedies generally fail.
The defensible conclusion is narrower: public records document institutional
steps and selected implementation endpoints more readily than they document
whether the affected person regained durable security, trust, or practical
choice.

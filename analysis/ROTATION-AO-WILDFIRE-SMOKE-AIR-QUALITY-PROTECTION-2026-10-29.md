# Rotation AO: Wildfire smoke, air quality and usable protection

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** wildfire smoke / air quality / public health / work and school / place  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 exposure-reduction question

## Research question

When a community has an AQI monitor, smoke forecast, public warning or clean-air
recommendation, what converts that signal into lower indoor and occupational
exposure, continuity of school/work/care, and reduced health harm?

## Conversion chain

```text
wildfire/emissions
  -> monitoring and forecast
  -> AQI/warning reaches person, employer and institution
  -> indoor filtration, respirator, schedule/evacuation or clean-air route
  -> lower personal exposure
  -> clinical care and work/school continuity
  -> repeated-season adaptation and trust
```

Ambient air measurement is not indoor exposure. A recommendation to stay inside
is not equally usable for outdoor workers, people without filtered housing,
people experiencing homelessness, children, pregnant people or those with
cardiovascular and respiratory conditions.

## Stage audit

| Observed signal | Safe interpretation | Prohibited leap |
|---|---|---|
| AQI monitor or smoke map | Ambient conditions are measured or modeled | A person received the warning or had the same indoor exposure |
| Public health recommendation | A mitigation action is advised | The person can afford, reach or safely perform it |
| N95/filter distribution | Protective equipment or filtration is available somewhere | Correct fit, consistent use, indoor clean air or lower personal exposure occurred |
| Employer/workplace guidance | A control policy exists | Outdoor work stopped, wages were protected or workers were safe |
| Daily PM2.5 evidence | Health-risk evidence exists for a defined averaging period | Repeated sub-daily or multi-season exposure has the same quantified effect |

## Bounded finding

> Wildfire-smoke protection is reachable exposure reduction, not an AQI reading,
> forecast, warning, filter recommendation or respirator supply alone. The
> meaningful endpoint requires a person’s indoor/work exposure, practical
> mitigation, health needs, continuity and repeated-season adaptation to be
> observed.

This does not imply that monitors, warnings, filtration or respirators are
ineffective. It distinguishes ambient information from the household and worker
conditions that determine whether protection can occur.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Smoke converts a regional fire into an intimate boundary around breathing,
sleeping, working, schooling and caring. “Stay indoors” means different things
in a sealed, filtered home; a crowded apartment; a farm, construction site or
delivery route; a shelter; a school; or a home without reliable electricity.

Place also shapes trust. Communities with repeated smoke seasons may develop
local knowledge, mutual aid and clean-air spaces, while residents may distrust
alerts that do not provide a viable action. Occupational and housing power can
make exposure a class and geography issue: the people with the least ability to
change work or indoor conditions may carry the greatest burden.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A timely, trusted warning can support effective avoidance and save health;
  the signal-to-action gap is not universal.
- Indoor filtration can reduce particulate exposure, but effectiveness depends
  on room, filter, power, maintenance and behavior.
- N95s reduce particulate exposure when properly selected and fitted, but do not
  solve heat, gas exposure, communication or every occupational constraint.
- Ambient PM2.5 is not a household or individual exposure measurement.
- Smoke-health evidence has uncertainty for repeated and sub-daily exposures;
  do not claim a precise multi-season effect from daily averages alone.

## Promotion decision

Promote the ambient-signal-versus-reachable-exposure-reduction distinction to
Tier 1. Keep the mechanism in Tier 2 until a named place links warning receipt,
indoor/work exposure, mitigation, cost, school/work continuity, clinical event
and repeated-season adaptation.

# Unequal exposure and status: source-led subtheme map v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** 621 current registry records carry the `unequal_exposure_status`
program-theme label. This cross-cutting map identifies mechanisms that differ
by status or position. It does not convert an observed group difference into an
inherent group trait, one universal mechanism, or a complete account of harm.

## What the source material supports

The records distinguish:

> formal eligibility from an accessible route; observed safety from the safety
> that victims can report; wealth from income; exposure from protection; and a
> group average from any individual's conditions, choices, or recovery.

## 1. Disability status can make a formally available service route practically incomplete

The [DOL workforce-access record](records/us-gao-disability-workforce-program-access-2026.json)
reports that 7% of participants in reviewed programs reported a disability,
while 43% of those did not report a disability type. In the Adult program, the
second-quarter employment comparison was 60% for reported-disabled
participants versus 76% for other participants; median employed-participant
earnings were $7,048 versus $8,872. GAO also reports interpreter, off-site
training-accessibility, assistive-technology, and aggregate-monitoring gaps at
selected sites. These comparisons do not identify a causal accessibility
effect, disability severity, accommodation request or receipt.

**Supported subtheme:** eligibility, reported participation, accessible service,
completion, placement, earnings, and retention must be kept as separate
institutional stages.  
**Not established:** that a gap is caused by any one barrier or that a formal
accessibility rule creates usable access in every location.

## 1a. A formal accessibility complaint route is unusable when its existence is not legible

The [federal-building accessibility record](records/us-gao-federal-building-accessibility-complaints-2026.json)
follows an enforcement route rather than an employment or care outcome. From
October 2022 through June 2026, the Access Board received 41 Architectural
Barriers Act complaints about GSA office buildings; 25 were closed and 16
remained in process. Industry stakeholders, federal employee unions, and
disability advocates generally told GAO that the public was unaware of the
complaint route. A low complaint count can therefore represent few barriers,
underreporting, alternative resolution, or low awareness.

**Supported subtheme:** physical accessibility requires a legible route from
barrier encounter through awareness, complaint, investigation, correction, and
usable re-entry. A closed administrative case is not yet evidence of a
corrected building or equal access to work and services.  
**Not established:** barrier prevalence, who attempted a complaint, resolution
time or quality, building-level correction, actual building use, or later
employment and public-service access.

## 2. Immigration enforcement can change what public-safety data can see

The [immigration-enforcement record](records/us-census-immigration-enforcement-victim-reporting-public-safety-2026.json)
finds Secure Communities reduced Hispanic victims' likelihood of reporting
crime while offending against Hispanics increased. Reported crime did not
change because the two movements offset; the study estimates that a 10%
reporting decline increased offending by 7.9%. It does not establish that fear
is the sole reason for nonreporting or that every policy, place, and immigrant
group follows the same pattern.

**Supported subtheme:** an administrative or enforcement climate can alter the
visibility of harm, so stable official counts cannot by themselves show stable
safety, trust, or victimization.  
**Not established:** all channels of reporting avoidance, later civic action,
or durable legitimacy.

## 2a. Historical population change can alter a shared disease environment without identifying an individual-group risk

The [historical immigration and child-mortality record](records/us-nber-immigration-child-mortality-disease-spillovers-2026.json)
uses a Massachusetts town panel from 1860–1915 and a shift-share instrument
based on historical settlement patterns. Increased local foreign-born share was
associated with significantly higher infant and child mortality, with the infant
effect concentrated disproportionately after the neonatal period. Mechanical
change in parent nativity accounted for only part of the result; the paper
interprets evidence of effects among children of native-born parents as
consistent with communicable-disease spillovers in the local environment.

**Supported subtheme:** population composition, crowding/sanitation and public-
health capacity, local disease exposure, and child mortality can be linked at a
place level. The result is not an individual immigrant-risk estimate, and it
shows why place-level environmental conditions must not be reduced to a group
trait.
**Not established:** a current U.S. immigration effect, a particular pathogen
or household behavior, a universal migration mechanism, present-day public
opinion, or policy consequence. The 1860–1915 disease, housing, sanitation,
health-system, and migration context differs substantially from the present.

## 3. Liquid wealth changes the room available after the same income shock

The [wealth-and-income-shock record](records/us-nber-wealth-race-consumption-smoothing-income-shocks-2020.json)
finds consumption sensitivity to typical income shocks was much higher for
low-liquidity than high-liquidity households. Black and Hispanic households
were about twice as sensitive as White households in the study; the authors'
statistical analysis attributes nearly all of that difference to racial wealth
inequality. Its welfare-cost results are calibrated-model estimates, not
observed recovery.

**Supported subtheme:** income is not the same resource position: liquidity can
shape whether a shock becomes a cut, debt, deferred care, reduced consumption,
or another adaptation.  
**Not established:** which need was compromised, the full historical pathway,
or whether statistical decomposition excludes other institutional mechanisms.

## 4. Housing, tenure, and risk interact with status rather than forming a single gradient

The [flood-exposure record](records/us-census-flood-risk-insurance-household-exposure-2026.json)
finds income gradients in flood exposure were slightly negative through the
bottom 90 percentiles but were dominated by top-decile exposure, especially
among multiple-property owners; building type and ownership drove much of the
nonlinearity. It separately identifies low-income uninsured homeowners as
vulnerable. It does not observe flood losses, claims, repair, or security.

The [work-limitation/tenure record](records/us-sipp-resource-job-worklimitation-tenure-crosslag-2024.json)
keeps another distinction visible: among work-limited people with one job, the
next-month resource-band change was 4.771% for owners/buyers and 6.122% for
renters. This is a composition-sensitive descriptive cross-lag, not a tenure
or disability effect.

**Supported subtheme:** income, ownership, insurance, disability, work, and
place cross-cut one another; a single “vulnerable group” label loses the actual
route to risk and protection.  
**Not established:** a universal exposure gradient, causal tenure effect, or
adequacy of insurance and adaptation.

## 5. The same housing disruption can concentrate later losses by race and gender

The [eviction-order record](records/us-nber-eviction-order-homelessness-health-earnings-credit-2022.json)
uses judge assignment in Cook County and New York City and reports that an
eviction order added homelessness, hospital visits, earnings loss, lower
durable-goods consumption and credit access; longer-run debt increased and
credit scores fell. The study reports the housing and labor effects were driven
by female and Black tenants. Pre-court earnings, employment, health, and
financial distress already worsened, so the order is an additional shock, not
the entire story.

**Supported subtheme:** formal case outcomes can compound rather than merely
record an earlier unequal material shock.  
**Not established:** why effects concentrate, whether each tenant had the same
legal, household, landlord, or destination alternatives, or later recovery.

## 6. Coverage and appointment availability can still fail at the disability interface

The [health-care accessibility record](records/us-gao-disability-healthcare-accessibility-oversight-2025.json)
identifies physical, sensory, communication, cognitive/digital, and
provider-assumption barriers beneath nominal coverage. GAO found that none of
12 reviewed HHS population-health surveys collected national data on most such
barriers; CMS inspections and OCR complaints are partial oversight routes, not
a public measure of correction or successful care. The GAO survey of 1,194
adults with disabilities is explicitly nongeneralizable.

**Supported subtheme:** a health system can count coverage, providers, and
complaints while missing whether a person can use the equipment, communication,
digital interface, or encounter itself.
**Not established:** prevalence of each barrier, missed or delayed care,
accommodation receipt, health and work effects, provider-specific compliance,
or trust after an encounter.

## 6a. Better estimation of who a system misses is not the same as inclusion, representation, or protection

The [administrative-record coverage record](records/us-census-capture-recapture-administrative-coverage-2026.json)
examines a measurement route upstream of service delivery. The 2020 Census used
administrative and third-party data in address canvassing and nonresponse
follow-up; the Census evaluation tests whether those records can support
capture-recapture coverage estimates using Expectation Maximization Log Linear
Modeling. The work remains experimental and does not replace the independent
post-enumeration survey and dual-system approach.

**Supported subtheme:** administrative and third-party records can make a
coverage gap more measurable, which is a prerequisite for detecting unequal
invisibility. But records, linkage, coverage estimation, enumeration,
representation, service eligibility, and actual protection are separate
institutional stages.

**Not established:** a national undercount estimate, equal linkage quality by
place, race, age, housing type, language, mobility, or administrative status;
an individual's successful count; privacy safety; downstream service access;
political representation; or trust. More data can also import stale,
duplicated, mislinked, or unevenly available information.

## 7. Climate adaptation can trade physical safety for unequal relocation opportunity

The [flood-buyout record](records/us-nber-flood-buyouts-race-relocation-neighborhoods-2026.json)
links national property acquisitions, sales, restricted Census microdata, and
movement records. It reports that households of color received at least 6%
less compensation relative to fair-market value than White households; White
participants moved to higher-quality neighborhoods than White nonparticipants,
while Black participants moved to worse neighborhoods than Black
nonparticipants.

**Supported subtheme:** removal from a hazard is not a sufficient adaptation
outcome. The compensation and destination route can distribute the gains from
reduced exposure unequally.
**Not established:** why differences occurred, whether each household had a
comparable alternative, health/security/belonging after the move, or a causal
result for every buyout program.

## 8. Representation can change the court route without repairing the material route

The [tenant-counsel record](records/us-nber-nyc-tenant-legal-representation-housing-court-2016-2019.json)
uses the staggered New York City Universal Access to Counsel rollout across
727,692 cases. In its main instrumental-variable specification, counsel
reduced possession judgments and eviction warrants by roughly 33–34 percentage
points and lowered monetary judgments; the executed-warrant result was less
stable. Effects were larger in poorer and higher-noncitizen-composition
neighborhoods.

**Supported subtheme:** a procedural resource can matter most where baseline
case exposure is structurally unequal, yet court success, executed eviction,
rent arrears, and later housing security remain different stages.
**Not established:** durable tenancy, later move, debt, work, health,
schooling, or whether a lower court judgment resolved affordability.

## 9. “Participation” can overstate the amount of public support actually received

The [CTC dollar-gap record](records/us-census-irs-child-tax-credit-participation-dollar-gap-2021.json)
links modeled Census tax-unit eligibility to IRS payment records. For 2021 it
estimates that 91% of eligible taxpayers received a CTC/ACTC payment but only
85% of modeled eligible dollars were paid: about 3.1 million eligible
taxpayers did not participate and an estimated $26 billion was unpaid or
underclaimed. The eligibility and dollar estimates have distinct denominators
and depend on tax-unit/dependent modeling and linkage.

**Supported subtheme:** person-level take-up, dollar-level receipt, timing,
adequacy, and household use are separate conversion stages; high headline
participation cannot close the dollar gap.
**Not established:** why a particular dollar was unclaimed, whether payment
was timely, what it funded, hardship reduction, or durable poverty, care, and
political effects.

## 10. A private access product can shift the risk back onto the renter

The [rent-guarantee record](records/us-rent-guarantee-insurance-protection-repayment-2026.json)
keeps a quantitative insurance model separate from provider terms. The model
finds welfare/deposit/homelessness improvements only under modeled designs and
also finds unrestricted access financially unviable. The provider describes a
non-refundable renter premium paid to qualify for a lease, a landlord/operator
as beneficiary, and renter liability to reimburse a covered claim.

**Supported subtheme:** an access innovation can loosen an entry screen while
leaving payment and default risk with the person who needed the access. This is
a protection-versus-repayment structure, not evidence of tenant recovery.
**Not established:** approval, premium, claim, repayment, credit, eviction,
housing-stability, or distributional outcomes in an actual renter population.

## 11. A modest education margin can change women's careers and children's early-life conditions

The [women's education and child-outcomes record](records/us-census-womens-education-career-children-2026.json)
uses a school-entry birthdate regression discontinuity with linked
administrative records. Women induced into some college were more likely to be
employed, earn more, enter professional/higher-status and more socially
meaningful jobs, and have better working conditions; their children had better
early-life health and prenatal inputs. The career gains concentrated in jobs
requiring exactly some college, the education margin moved by the cutoff. The
working-paper result applies locally to women near that cutoff.

**Supported subtheme:** education can alter an intergenerational route through
job quality, income, time, information, and care conditions—not merely a
credential count.  
**Not established:** universal college returns, complete maternal/child
wellbeing, workplace autonomy, or the mechanism for every population.

## 11a. A locally visible role model can shape a gendered life-course pathway under historically constrained opportunity

The [female-teacher and girls' later-life record](records/us-census-female-teachers-female-students-lifetime-2026.json)
uses historical Census, IRS, later-Census, and death records for rural U.S.
girls around 1940. The working paper reports that exposure to female primary-
school teachers improved girls' educational attainment, with stronger effects
for girls whose parents had less education and in places with fewer labor-
market opportunities for women. It further reports delayed marriage and
childbearing, marriage to better-educated husbands, higher household income,
and longer female longevity relative to men.

**Supported subtheme:** institutional representation can enter a long
intergenerational pathway through educational exposure, family formation,
household resources, and health—not only through a contemporaneous access
metric. The reported heterogeneity makes local opportunity structure part of
the pathway rather than background context.  
**Not established:** a present-day school effect; a universal effect for girls,
boys, urban places, or other countries; teacher assignment; the exact role-
model mechanism; individual autonomy or safety; political participation; or a
complete wellbeing account. This is a historical rural working-paper result
whose public abstract omits the full sample and estimate ledger.

## 12. Aggregate safety improvement can coexist with fear-driven withdrawal from school participation

The [school-safety and avoidance record](records/us-nces-bjs-school-safety-fear-avoidance-2021-2022.json)
reports at-school nonfatal victimization fell from 52 per 1,000 students in
2012 to 22 in 2022. Yet in 2021–22, 3.8% of enrolled students ages 12–18 were
sometimes/most often afraid of attack or harm at school, 3.6% avoided classes
or activities, and 2.2% stayed home because of that fear. The separate
indicators do not follow the same student through an incident, response,
absence, support, learning, or later trust.

**Supported subtheme:** a safer aggregate environment does not guarantee equal
usable participation; subjective threat and avoidance can remain a distinct
institutional access route.  
**Not established:** a same-student causal pathway, missed instruction,
mental-health effect, or why the fear arose.

## 13. Neighborhood composition carries different mobility associations by relative status

The [relative-status and neighborhood-exit record](records/us-census-neighborhood-racial-status-white-outmobility-2026.json)
models White residential out-mobility with ACS tract data. White residents
lacking advantaged socioeconomic status were generally more likely to leave;
the association of Black or Asian concentration with departure was weaker for
status-disadvantaged White residents, while the negative association with
Hispanic concentration was stronger. The authors interpret the interaction
patterns as more consistent with contact than group-competition theory, but
the design is observational and limited to White residents.

**Supported subtheme:** status and neighborhood composition interact in
mobility decisions; composition alone cannot be assigned one social meaning or
mechanism.  
**Not established:** racial motivation, forced versus beneficial moves,
causal contact effects, segregation dynamics, or outcomes for other residents.

## 14. Work limitation, children, and resources form intersecting material conditions rather than a single group effect

The [work-limitation/children/resources record](records/us-sipp-work-limitation-children-resources-2024.json)
uses person-weighted SIPP monthly cells. Below poverty, work-limited people
without children in the household had 18.1% utility-payment difficulty and
41.3% reporting hungry-but-did-not-eat, versus 16.4% and 24.4% in the
non-work-limited child-present comparison; their one-job shares were 7.9% and
36.2%. Some high-resource hunger cells are sparse and imprecise, and children
in a household are neither parenthood nor care hours.

**Supported subtheme:** resource position, work-limiting condition, household
composition, food, utility, and job status cross-cut one another; an
intersectional descriptive cell is not a causal ranking of groups.  
**Not established:** household prevalence, caregiving mechanism, accommodation,
policy effect, recovery, or a causal explanation for the differences.

## 15. Institutional presence and political visibility can grow while status threat remains socially durable

The [Muslim-American status record](records/us-pew-muslim-americans-25-years-2026.json)
keeps public judgment, reported group experience, reported hate-crime counts,
and institutional presence separate. In 2026, 42% of U.S. adults said Muslim
Americans had a negative effect on the country and 43% called them less
patriotic than other Americans. Among Muslim adults, 59% reported a lot of
discrimination and 59% unfair media portrayal, while 34% reported
religion-based support in the prior year. The source also reports growth in
estimated Muslim population, mosque counts, and congressional representation;
these measures have different dates, definitions, and denominators.

**Supported subtheme:** organizational presence and political visibility can
expand without resolving group status, public suspicion, reported
discrimination, or partisan interpretation. Institutional growth is therefore
not an adequate proxy for safety, equal treatment, influence, or belonging.

**Not established:** a causal effect of mosque growth, representation, party
messaging, media, or any historical event; total discrimination or victimization
prevalence; service access; individual political behavior; or a uniform
Muslim-American experience.

## Cross-subtheme finding and next test

```text
status, wealth, disability, race, education, tenure, ownership, place, or policy climate
  -> information, interface, reporting, payment, court, school, insurance, or move route
  -> receipt, avoidance, interruption, compensation, protection, or repayment
  -> work, debt, health, safety, housing, care, and social consequence
  -> trust, belonging, institutional feedback, and later opportunity
```

The next decisive evidence is a linked pathway that retains the relevant
status only with consent and privacy protection, records the offered route and
attempted route, captures non-use and non-completion, and follows material and
lived outcomes. Group rates or formal-policy descriptions alone cannot show
which institutional stage generated the difference.

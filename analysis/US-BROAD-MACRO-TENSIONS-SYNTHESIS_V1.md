# The six deep tensions underneath the atlas v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-22

Machine-readable version: [macro-tension register](data/us-broad-macro-tensions-v1.json).

The 14 themes and 35 mechanisms reduce to six recurring tensions. These are
not six new themes and they are not one pooled causal model. They are the
shortest way to understand what the evidence keeps asking us to measure.

The enforcement-and-public-safety record adds a legitimacy version of the same
problem: a policy can increase the state's enforcement capacity while making a
targeted community less willing to use the state as a reporting institution.
Reported crime can then stay flat while offending rises. Visibility of the
record is therefore not the same as visibility of the harm, and enforcement
capacity is not the same as trusted public safety.

The UI and labor-concentration record adds a labor-market version: public
insurance can expand a worker's search time without expanding the employer
alternatives available in the local market. Extra time and better control
therefore recover on different clocks; a longer search or higher earnings does
not by itself prove a good match, bargaining power, or household security.

The AI-diffusion record adds a technology-and-firm version: adoption can happen
at the company, business-function, or worker-task level, and those layers can
move in different directions. Visible firm capability therefore does not prove
worker exposure, worker control, job displacement, or shared productivity gains.

The early-career hiring record adds a time-and-generation version: technology
can close an entry route before established-worker displacement is visible. A
later hiring recovery from a smaller base is not the same as restored access;
entry, earnings, career progression, and household security can recover on
different clocks.

The new graduate-major record sharpens that tension: the most AI-exposed majors
lost initial employment and earnings after ChatGPT's release, and about half of
the early earnings loss came from moving into lower-paying sectors. A graduate
can therefore remain attached to work while losing the quality and bargaining
position of the first rung. Re-entry, sector recovery, skill accumulation, and
household security are not the same clock; the selected event-study sample does
not establish long-run scarring or one AI mechanism.

The temporary-public-hiring record adds a bridge version: a government job can
keep marginal applicants attached to W-2 work for years, while the average
applicant effect fades quickly and low-score hires carry higher termination
risk. Public capacity, worker attachment, job quality, administrative cost, and
equal access therefore recover on different clocks; a temporary job is not
automatically a durable or universal security measure.

The environmental-lender record adds a private-governance version: changing who
bears cleanup risk changes pollution, abatement, and loan maturity. Credit
access, production, environmental burden, and public accountability therefore
move on different clocks; a firm can retain economic capacity while shifting
risk outward. The historical establishment-level study does not establish
community health or current sustainable-finance effects.

The tip-reporting record adds an informal-income version: a policy threshold can
make one reporting response visible while most estimated missing income remains
outside that signal. Detectable administrative behavior is therefore not the
whole hidden economy, and missing reported income does not identify the actor,
benefit, or household consequence.

The SNAP screening record adds a public-benefit version: a condition intended to
increase work can reduce program participation without increasing labor supply.
Eligibility, administrative compliance, employment, food security, and trust
therefore remain separate clocks; program exit is not automatically improved
independence.

The export-control record adds a geopolitical version: a targeted restriction can
reduce the named trade while also shrinking firms' other markets and payroll.
Strategic effect and domestic burden are therefore separate clocks; spillover
does not by itself prove failure, and target restriction does not prove a narrow
cost.

The flood-disaster firm record adds a recovery-and-selection version: public
spending can help productive entrants and survivors rebuild while less productive
plants exit. Efficiency, firm survival, employment, household recovery, and
community continuity therefore move on different clocks; reallocation is not
automatically shared recovery.

The wildfire household record adds a direct-loss and composition version: a
region can look relatively wealthy while lower-income households inside the
fire perimeter face greater destruction risk and multi-year income loss. Empty
destroyed addresses and higher-income reoccupancy mean community recovery can
change who is present rather than restore the old place.

The parental-homeownership record adds an intergenerational-causality version:
family ownership can correlate with later income and ownership while stronger
tests find no effect from the tenure exposure itself. Association, family
advantage, housing quality, and causal inheritance therefore remain separate
clocks.

The Medicaid survey test adds a measurement-correction version: a redesigned
question can be reasonable without outperforming the old question against an
administrative benchmark. Better wording, reported coverage, enrollment, active
care, affordability, and continuity therefore remain separate stages.

The local-redistricting record adds a political-voice version: formal districting
rules can be neutral while residential geography still produces unequal racial
representation. A race-blind baseline can reproduce much of the gap, while
legal oversight or litigation can improve enacted maps. Neutral procedure,
officeholder composition, policy influence, and lived political voice therefore
remain separate stages.

The flood-buyout record adds an unequal-exit version: a public program can move
people away from physical danger while compensation and destination-neighborhood
quality differ by race. Safe departure, fair payment, reachable relocation,
durable security, and trust therefore remain separate stages.

The child-SSI record adds a household-conversion version: a child benefit can
improve outcomes when it raises total household resources, but not when parental
earnings fall enough to offset it. Benefit receipt, net resources, parent time,
care, work, human capital, and later security therefore remain separate stages.

The transfer-design record adds a form-and-calendar version: recurring or
food-labeled aid produced larger short-run food-store responses than one-time or
cash aid in the study, but a grocery receipt is not total household welfare.
Amount, label, timing, food, rent, medicine, debt, choice, and durable security
therefore recover on different clocks.

The party-pressure record adds a representation-conversion version: voter
preferences can be measured and still pass through member incentives and party
organization before becoming a roll-call vote. Constituent preference, party
pressure, legislative action, service, trust, and policy effect therefore remain
separate stages.

The survey-error record adds a public-knowledge version: response rate can
improve while respondent reporting, imputation, benchmark definitions, and
absolute error remain weak. A stable net estimate therefore does not prove
stable individual accuracy, policy usability, or public trust.

The declining-occupation record adds a career-recovery version: workers can
switch occupations and work slightly more quarters while still carrying lower
cumulative earnings. Employment, occupational mobility, pay recovery, household
room, and identity therefore remain separate stages.

The homeownership-loss record adds a wealth-usability version: keeping a home can
raise long-run housing wealth without raising current consumption when credit
constraints block access to equity. Ownership, wealth, liquidity, spending,
residence, and security therefore remain separate stages.

The top-income-risk record adds a self-insurance version: income risk can rise
even for high earners, encouraging more saving and modeled wealth inequality,
while the pass-through to rates, asset prices, borrowers, jobs, and household
security remains open. Risk, saving, wealth, credit, and public consequence
therefore recover on different clocks.

The flood-risk record adds a place-and-exposure version: a block-level map,
parcel hazard, household income, property ownership, insurance, and protection
can tell different stories. Visible protection or stable location therefore
does not prove affordable coverage, successful repair, voluntary staying, or
durable housing security.

The capture-recapture record adds a measurement-governance version: more
administrative and third-party data can make possible undercount easier to see,
while match error, missingness, privacy, and downstream access remain unresolved.
Statistical visibility is therefore not the same as enumeration, representation,
or practical protection.

Meaning and action are deliberately not a seventh universal tension. They are
the downstream conversion layer: a person, household, worker, community, firm,
or state interprets an experience and may respond through trust, blame,
belonging, silence, voting, organizing, switching, non-use, exit, or changed
external behavior. Those responses depend on identity, efficacy, safety,
available alternatives, and institutional follow-through. Keeping them as
separate endpoints prevents the atlas from treating an attitude as action or
silence as consent.

## 1. Capacity versus control

The nonprofit-sector labor record adds a social-capacity version: an institution
can provide public, cultural, health, or civic goods while its workers face a
lower and uneven pay structure. Organizational mission and worker material
security are therefore distinct; neither pay alone nor mission alone proves
service quality, retention, client access, or community trust.

An organization can build capacity—an agency, platform, employer, utility,
care system, or alliance—without the affected person controlling what happens
to them. Control requires notice, intelligibility, contestability, correction,
and a credible fallback.

**Examples:** a privacy request can be completed without verified deletion; an
authorization can be reversed without showing timely care; a defense platform
can be accepted without proving operational leverage; federal agencies can have
many eligibility data sources without reliable cross-system matching; and a
security mission can integrate local actors without proving public consent.

The newest public-system and geopolitical evidence sharpens the distinction:
capacity is not only a matter of how much an institution can do. It also
depends on whether its data, identifiers, local relationships, and governance
interfaces let the affected actor recognize, use, contest, or accept what the
institution is doing. See the [GAO interoperability record](records/us-gao-federal-eligibility-data-interoperability-2026.json)
and the [KFOR local-security integration record](records/us-nato-kfor-posture-local-security-integration-2026.json).
The [PJM/FERC large-load route](records/us-pjm-large-load-disconnection-response-2026.json)
adds a shared-infrastructure version: a real operating disturbance and a
regulatory cost/rate review can occur before the public knows which facility
caused what burden or whether the proposed control is durable.
The [NATO ISR operating record](records/us-nato-nisrf-agile-combat-employment-2026.json)
adds a strategic version: flight hours, intelligence products, decision-support
use, and targeting support can all be observed before product-level decision
effect, public acceptance, adversary response, or durable leverage is known.
The AEP Texas financing route adds a domestic infrastructure version: a closed
loan and projected carrying-capacity increase can precede completed construction,
operating reliability, rate allocation, and household savings.

The federal shared-services record adds a centralization-versus-fit version.
Agencies can share cybersecurity, financial, grants, and human-resources
services and report possible efficiencies while still facing legal and
technical mismatches, legacy-system barriers, unfilled leadership roles, and
weak data about whether the services meet agency needs. A centralized service
marketplace is therefore capacity on offer, not verified savings, quality, or
mission improvement.

The BLS–Census establishment comparison adds a measurement-and-power version.
Different administrative frames make the private economy look more fragmented
or more concentrated, especially in home-care and disability services. A model
can therefore change its prediction about taxes, output, employment, or
productivity before anyone has shown a change in actual competition, care
quality, worker control, or household life.

The latest platform-control records add a digital version: a centralized
privacy request, a company-reported completion status, or an image-removal
notification can make the route easier to see without proving that every
record, copy, or re-collection was actually controlled. Visibility of the
process is a gain in contestability; it is not yet verified practical agency.

The [federal-building accessibility record](records/us-gao-federal-building-accessibility-complaints-2026.json)
adds a physical-access version. A complaint route existed for GSA buildings,
but GAO reports that many potential users did not know it was available; 41
complaints were received from October 2022 through June 2026, 25 were closed,
and 16 remained in process. A formal accessibility standard and an enforcement
route therefore do not by themselves prove discoverable contestation, corrected
barriers, or usable access.

The [FDA third-party device-review record](records/us-gao-fda-third-party-device-review-audit-2026.json)
adds a delegated-trust version. Accredited organizations supplied 617 initial
510(k) reviews from 2018 through 2025, while FDA's audit policies lacked target
time frames and some recent deficiency audits took more than six months to
close. Delegated capacity can therefore expand a regulatory route while public
assurance depends on timely audit, correction, and communication; review volume
does not prove device safety or patient benefit.

The [FTC social-media data-retention record](records/us-ftc-social-media-data-retention-control-2024.json)
adds a system-level firm counterweight. In its review of nine large social and
video platforms, the FTC reported that companies could retain large amounts of
data indefinitely, share broadly with affiliates and third parties, and in some
cases fail to delete all user data after a deletion request. This makes the
control problem structural rather than only a bad individual interface, but the
review is not a user-level failure rate or an audit of every data store. It does
not prove that every request failed, that every person was harmed, or that
downstream copies were actually used.

The [GM/OnStar connected-vehicle record](records/us-ftc-gm-onstar-data-control-order-2026.json)
adds a named-firm remedy version. A 20-year FTC order requires routes for data
access, deletion, geolocation opt-out, and disabling some collection, while GM's
published policy retains safety and emergency exceptions and makes some controls
dependent on vehicle model and technology. The order makes contestability more
real, but no request volume, response, deletion verification, residual
collection, service loss, trust, or switching outcome is observed. A privacy
right can therefore be formally available without yet being a verified privacy
or mobility result.

The AI-assistant choice record adds a user-side version: people can trust a
tool more after using it and route sensitive tasks selectively, while concern
about data use remains much higher than actual privacy-protective action. A
choice, a stated concern, and a paid or verified privacy safeguard are
different levels of control.

The [wearable-health record](records/us-gao-wearable-clinical-decision-making-2026.json)
adds a data-to-care version: wearable sensors can support earlier alerts,
personalized plans, and remote monitoring, while accuracy varies and clinical
workflow, liability, privacy, and AI autonomy remain unsettled. More health
data can therefore expand nominal care capacity without proving better health,
safe interpretation, equal access, or patient control over what happens next.

The [Marketplace broker-consent record](records/us-gao-marketplace-broker-consent-2026.json)
adds a consumer-control version: formal broker licensing and registration did
not ensure that consent was verified or that consumers were told about every
action. Confirmed complaints tied to unauthorized enrollments or plan switches
grew more than fourfold from 2023 to 2025, and GAO identified at least 160,000
likely unauthorized changes in 2024. Stronger state safeguards, including
one-time passcodes, are a useful counterexample, but the record does not show
the national prevalence, final coverage loss, care disruption, or post-correction
trust effect.

The [payment-resilience record](records/us-nber-payment-method-resilience-disaster-outages-2026.json)
adds a disaster-infrastructure version: electronic payment can be the normal
route while cash becomes the practical backup when power or communications
fail. Nearly half of surveyed consumers could not use their preferred
electronic payment during an outage, and more cash access was linked to more
completed transactions. A completed transaction is not the same as getting an
essential good, avoiding loss, or achieving durable recovery; outage duration,
merchant acceptance, household cash room, and later repayment remain open.

The financial-guidance record adds an economic-decision version: 18% of U.S.
adults seeking financial guidance reported using AI, rising to 21% among
financially stressed adults, while no more than 30% expressed some confidence
in AI's financial expertise. Need can drive experimentation before trust is
high; source use is not advice quality, decision influence, improved finances,
or human substitution.

The small-business credit record adds a firm-side version: a business can have
multiple lender categories available while risk classification, requested
amount, speed, and partial approval determine the usable route. Firm financing
therefore belongs in the control question alongside household benefits and
consumer remedies; approval is not funded capacity or durable firm security.

The [NASBO state-budget record](records/us-nasbo-state-budget-buffer-adjustment-2026.json)
adds a fiscal version: a state can retain reserves and a positive top-line
budget while using targeted cuts, vacant-position reductions, revenue changes,
or one-time surplus spending underneath it. Budget capacity therefore does
not prove staffed services, timely processing, usable access, or equal public
protection. A named service or shock must be followed from enacted budget to
implementation and lived result.

The DOT SMART review adds a public-technology version: a grant award can create
local experimentation and technical capacity before procurement, completion,
operating reliability, or public use is visible. DOT's incomplete
lessons-learned process also means that a demonstration may be funded without
yet making its results easy for other communities to evaluate or copy. Award,
pilot, usable service, and durable adoption remain separate clocks.

The [Seattle SMART curb record](records/us-seattle-smart-curb-access-implementation-2023-2025.json)
adds a named project to that tension. A $1.996 million award produced a digital
curb inventory, operational vehicle-to-curb infrastructure, and a commercial
vehicle permit system after schedule and reporting delays. Those are real
implementation milestones, but they do not show faster freight movement,
fairer curb access, lower user cost, safer operations, or durable public
service.

The [broadband deployment record](records/us-gao-broadband-deployment-targeting-sustainability-2026.json)
adds a connectivity version: all nine reviewed federal programs had begun
distributing funds by February 2026, but completed-project data were available
for only three. BEAD's $42.5 billion program was expected to finish in 2030 and
beyond, while provider-reported maps, burdensome challenge processes, and
unfinished sustainability monitoring left targeting and long-term service open.
Tribal awards and intended locations are meaningful capacity milestones, not
proof of completed, affordable, reliable, or durable connections.

The [dig-once coordination record](records/us-gao-broadband-dig-once-highway-coordination-2026.json)
adds the physical route beneath that funding story. Forty-six of 52 state
contacts reported a broadband coordinator and 46 reported broad interagency
coordination, but staff, IT, provider-participation, and outcome-measurement
gaps remained. A shared right-of-way plan can lower expected excavation without
proving that fiber was built, service was affordable, or households used it.

The CBP port-partnership review adds a state-capacity version: a public border
system can use private reimbursements and donations to add overtime, lanes, and
equipment while the actual queue, inspection, cost, and accountability effects
remain unobserved. More funded capacity can therefore coexist with uncertain
public control and unequal practical benefit.

The GAO water-resilience review adds a maintenance and place version: large
federal grants and loans can coexist with local rate resistance, repayment
burden, maintenance costs, and uncertainty about which service areas benefited.
Financial capacity is therefore not the same as durable, affordable, or
disaster-ready public protection.

The [Tribal water infrastructure record](records/us-gao-tribal-water-infrastructure-maintenance-2026.json)
adds a culturally specific construction-to-service version. Property rules can
exclude some homes or community buildings from IHS-funded projects, and one
Tribe needed more than 80 staff hours to navigate and report on an $8,000 grant.
Tribes remain responsible for operating and maintaining built systems while
certified-operator shortages and limited routine federal O&M support remain.
Construction is therefore not yet reliable household water, health protection,
or institutional autonomy.

The [rural-water funding record](records/us-gao-rural-water-funding-coordination-unserved-communities-2026.json)
adds an earlier access stage. Twenty-eight USDA and EPA programs can offer
assistance, yet different applications, deadlines, matches, limited data, and
weak interagency monitoring can keep an unserved community from reaching the
construction stage. Program availability is therefore not the same as a built,
operating, safe, or affordable water system.

The safe-drinking-water record adds a measured infrastructure-to-health version:
pollution readings improved across a large national dataset, and Safe Drinking
Water Act loans were associated with lower pollution and lower mortality among
older Americans in the study's estimates. Average improvement still does not
prove that every household received safe water, avoided private treatment cost,
or experienced the same health benefit.

The Federal Reserve's 2025 household survey adds a judgment-and-reference-
point version: 73% of adults said they were doing okay or comfortable, while
45% rated their local economy good or excellent and only 25% rated the national
economy that way. Personal, local, and national economic judgments can therefore
diverge; the gap is evidence of different reference points, not by itself
misinformation, blame, voting, or policy demand.

The [SIPP episodic-poverty record](records/us-census-sipp-monthly-episodic-poverty-2024.json)
adds a duration-and-concentration version. Census reports a 2024 monthly poverty
rate that stayed near 11.9% while 15.1% experienced poverty for at least two
consecutive months; 59.2% of those with episodic poverty were poor all year.
The rate was 18.9% for children, and older adults experiencing episodic poverty
averaged about 11 months, with 79.4% poor all 12 months. These measures show
time and unequal exposure, not the cause of the spell, assistance receipt,
material deprivation, housing or health consequences, trust, or later recovery.

The DOD technology-release review adds a geopolitical control version: a
partner's request can enter a real permission system while the time, authority,
and visibility of the decision remain uneven. Urgent acceleration can create
capability faster but consume staff capacity and delay ordinary requests. A
request, approval, transfer, operational use, and partner trust therefore
recover on different clocks.

The investor-beliefs record adds a financial-culture selection version: people
who step out of markets are more pessimistic and see more crash risk, so the
people who remain invested are not a neutral sample of households. Beliefs may
shape participation, but liquidity needs, age, income, advice, and access may
also shape both beliefs and exit; market participation is not household
financial security.

The security-cooperation workforce review adds a human-capacity version: legal
requirements and position counts can advance while hiring, training, research,
and overseas continuity remain incomplete. Institutional capacity is therefore
not just equipment or policy; it also depends on who has the skills, time, and
continuity to carry the relationship into practice.

The [Ukraine–JEF AURORA record](records/us-jef-ukraine-enhanced-partnership-aurora-2026.json)
adds a partnership-to-exercise version: Ukraine's battlefield experience and
unmanned-systems expertise moved from Enhanced Partnership coordination into
reported participation by Ukrainian operators and specialists in AURORA 26.
That is a real conversion from political framework to practical exchange, but
it does not establish JEF membership, combat deployment, a binding guarantee,
deterrence, changed adversary behavior, or durable strategic leverage.

The [NATO resilience-baseline record](records/us-nato-resilience-baseline-requirements-2026.json)
adds a civil-military version: NATO formally places essential civilian systems,
private-sector dependencies, Host Nation Support, and population-effect
mitigation inside readiness planning. This broadens what alliance capacity is
supposed to protect and enable, but the policy text does not show country
compliance, budgets, service continuity, public consent, burden distribution,
crisis performance, or deterrence.

The [Denmark host-nation rehearsal record](records/us-nato-host-nation-rehearsal-denmark-2026.json)
adds a practice version: a named NATO rehearsal engaged Danish authorities on
logistics, transport, movement, reception, and sustainment of allied forces.
This is stronger than a policy statement because an event occurred, but it
still does not show actual deployment speed, civilian service continuity,
failure correction, cost allocation, public acceptance, or performance in a
real crisis.

The GAO military-readiness synthesis adds a sustainment version: modernization,
operational demand, and visible activity can coexist with maintenance, staffing,
data, housing, fatigue, and availability gaps. The Army watercraft example is
particularly clear: fully mission-capable rates fell below 40% in 2024 against a
90% policy goal. Inventory and activity therefore do not prove dependable
capability at the point of need.

The historical women's-work study adds a household-to-collective-action
version: economic responsibility can increase work and burden while also
creating money, contacts, information, and public roles that enable organizing.
The route is conditional, historical, and not automatically empowering; work
can also consume the time needed for action.

The Coast Guard surge review adds a state-capacity allocation version: a
high-priority migration mission received more hours and cutters while drug and
other missions lost capacity, with foreign partners partly substituting. A
government can therefore be more active in one visible lane while less able in
another; partner help changes the burden without proving deterrence or public
legitimacy.

The NBER SNAP-office study adds a place-and-route version: a formal safety-net
benefit can remain available while closing a local enrollment office reduces
nearby participation. Geography is therefore part of practical access, and a
participation change still does not prove adequate food or household security.

The Healthy Diné Nation record adds a place-specific policy-to-health
counterexample: a formal 7–8% food-price wedge did not produce a detectable
improvement in the studied maternal and newborn outcomes. That result does not
show that purchases did not change or that the policy fails everywhere; the
missing middle includes retailer implementation, cross-border shopping,
cultural fit, exposure, and the choice of health endpoint.

The NBER Senate peer-proximity study adds a social-contact version: randomized
seating and repeated physical proximity were linked to changed legislative
voting, especially among close peers. Formal rules can therefore stay fixed
while everyday access to conversation changes institutional behavior. The
finding is limited to Senators and does not prove public persuasion, trust, or
lasting coalition change.

The [human-AI political-dialogue record](records/us-nature-ai-dialogue-voter-persuasion-2024-2025.json)
adds a digital influence version. In preregistered randomized experiments tied
to the 2024 U.S. presidential election and a Massachusetts ballot measure,
conversations with a candidate-advocating AI changed stated candidate or policy
preferences; comparative experiments in Canada and Poland found related
candidate-preference effects. The experiments did not observe verified turnout,
durable belief change, real-world campaign exposure, or population-scale
electoral effects, and some presented facts were inaccurate. Personalized
persuasion can therefore be experimentally visible before political action or
lasting legitimacy is established.

The DOD international-arrangements record adds a geopolitical version: a large
partner portfolio can coexist with slow review, staffing shortages, dispersed
responsibility, and untimely cost-sharing communication. Agreement count and
dollar value therefore do not establish implementation, partner use,
interoperability, deterrence, or legitimacy.

The [DOE nuclear-cleanup option-selection record](records/us-gao-doe-nuclear-cleanup-mission-need-option-selection-2026.json)
adds a public-project framing version. GAO found that most of 21 reviewed large
project mission statements named a preferred solution even though the formal
standard calls for defining the need before choosing the answer. In selected
cases, early framing and regulatory or contractor relationships narrowed later
consideration of cheaper alternatives. A formal review can therefore exist
while the practical option set has already contracted; adding independent
experts may widen scrutiny without proving a cheaper, safer, or completed
project.

The [international-commitment transparency record](records/us-gao-international-commitment-reporting-transparency-2023-2025.json)
adds a geopolitical visibility version. State reported 311 agreements and
qualifying instruments to Congress, but 90 were late and averaged 2.3 reporting
cycles late; public publication was estimated at 86% overall and about 53% on
time. A formal international commitment can therefore exist before Congress
or the public has timely, complete, searchable access to it. Reporting delay is
not proof of concealment or invalidity, and visibility still does not establish
implementation, partner consent, burden sharing, or legitimacy.

The [global-aging foreign-policy record](records/us-gao-global-aging-foreign-policy-implications-2026.json)
adds a slow demographic version. GAO reports that the global population age 60
and over may rise from about 1 billion in 2020 to 2.1 billion in 2050, with
possible effects on allies' health-versus-defense spending, labor pools,
migration, economic competition, and humanitarian priorities. U.S. agencies
address some implications indirectly, but the report does not show a changed
alliance, defense budget, migration flow, or U.S. strategic result. Demographic
pressure is therefore a geopolitical input, not yet geopolitical leverage.

## 2. Offer versus usability

The Census ACS contact-strategy review adds a public-information version:
changes to layout, language, internet response, reminders, and mailing design
can improve participation in particular periods, but the broader self-response
decline continues and some tests are neutral. A more usable invitation is not
the same as equal voice, accurate completion, institutional trust, or policy
feedback.

An option is not real choice until the same unit can reach and use it under its
actual money, time, health, transport, safety, status, and schedule constraints.
This is why an alternative job, insurer, benefit, care route, repair, or
replacement cannot be counted simply because it exists.

**Examples:** new employment can coexist with forced exit and broken trust;
replacement benefits can be approved without recipient receipt; market entry can
coexist with longer travel after a local firm closes; and a bus, insurance
policy, or public housing backstop can exist without a usable route to safe,
affordable, durable shelter.

The recent place and household-economy records add a measurement version of
this tension. BEA can show a large outdoor-recreation economy and a large
household-production economy without showing whether residents can use the
resource, whether unpaid work was chosen, or who captured the gains. Economic
scale is evidence of capacity and activity; it is not evidence of equal access,
control, or security.

Remote work adds a household-place version. Census CES estimates that when the
job-place tie loosens, some households move toward more space, ownership,
public schools, and lower income-tax exposure. But the move is not the same as
better life, and neighborhood turnover is not automatically displacement or
renewal. The new freedom to choose a place also makes housing supply, money,
school access, tax rules, and the ability to move more decisive.

The latest breadth pass repeats this tension across otherwise unrelated
domains. HUD's affordable housing stock becomes much smaller when availability
and physical adequacy are added; leave access exists while some workers still
do not take needed leave; arts opportunities and volunteering vary with money
and place; students may avoid school activities despite lower aggregate
victimization; and allied space cooperation can be formally organized while
staffing and points of contact remain weak. These are not one causal chain.
They are a cross-source signal that offered capacity becomes social protection
only at the point of actual use.

The HUD–LEHD trajectory study adds a time-and-work version. Housing assistance
entry coincided with a reversal in declining employment and earnings trends,
while exit coincided with flatter trajectories relative to stayers. The pattern
is not a causal welfare estimate: people enter and leave under different needs,
health, disability, education, household, and housing conditions. A benefit can
create room for work while the quality and durability of the housing route
remain unresolved.

The [institutional rental-ownership record](records/us-gao-institutional-rental-ownership-six-metros-2018-2024.json)
adds a firm-and-place version. Institutional investors increased their
single-family rental holdings in six selected metros, yet by 2024 they owned
less than 1% to 3% of all single-family homes there; their share of the rental
subset was larger, ranging from 4% to 22%. Ownership growth can therefore be
consequential within a housing channel without proving control of the whole
market. The report does not measure rent, repairs, eviction, tenant stability,
homeownership, displacement, or local political meaning.

The [allied-space coordination record](records/us-gao-space-allied-coordination-2025.json)
adds a concrete geopolitical version. GAO found multiple DOD agreements,
organizations, exercises, and cooperation activities with six partner nations,
but also staffing gaps, overlapping responsibilities, difficult points of
contact, and missing implementation milestones. Three recommendations remained
open through the review period. Cooperation mechanisms are therefore visible
capacity, not yet proof of linked mission coordination, readiness, partner
consent, strategic effect, or alliance failure; a new system can be incomplete
without the partnership having collapsed.

The [congestion-pricing and EMS record](records/us-nber-nyc-congestion-pricing-ems-response-2026.json)
adds an urban service-time version: after New York City's congestion pricing
began, traffic near the zone boundary fell and EMS travel time improved by
about one minute. Faster emergency movement is a real usability gain, but it
does not by itself prove better patient outcomes, equal neighborhood benefit,
affordable access, or acceptance of the charge. The policy's transport effect,
medical effect, and distributional burden remain separate stages.

The [third-place and neighborhood-startup record](records/us-starbucks-third-place-neighborhood-startups-1990-2022.json)
adds a social-space version: first café entry was followed by more startup
formation in neighborhoods that lacked comparable cafés, while entry did not
show the same effect where cafés already existed. A gathering place can be
economic infrastructure when a local network is missing, but startup counts
are not durable firms, good jobs, resident ownership, affordability, or proof
that the venue created the network rather than signaling expected growth.

The [Black-neighborhood startup record](records/us-black-neighborhood-startup-deficit-covid-2020-2024.json)
adds a racialized place-and-firm version: a long-standing startup deficit in
Black neighborhoods reversed sharply during COVID and remained relatively
elevated through 2024, especially in places with the largest prior deficits.
The reversal is a meaningful change in local formation, but it does not show
durable firms, worker security, resident ownership, household gains, or less
displacement. Relative registration growth can reflect both new activity and
changes in comparison places, migration, credit, demand, policy, or filing
behavior.
The [Opportunity Zones record](records/us-gao-opportunity-zones-community-outcomes-2026.json)
adds a public-incentive version of the same tension. More than $108 billion in
fund assets and completed projects show investment capacity, but GAO found that
specific jobs, housing, and community outcomes were generally not tracked.
Investment also favored places with infrastructure, ongoing development, and
community support. Visible capital can therefore follow local readiness rather
than create it, leaving the hardest-to-serve places and the distribution of
benefits uncertain; project completion is not proof of affordability, job
quality, displacement avoidance, or durable community improvement.

## 3. Correction versus contestability

A system may correct people who can notice the problem, gather documents,
appeal, wait, and persist. That can make the route look more effective than it
is for people who never reach the contest stage.

**Examples:** most appealed skilled-nursing denials were overturned, but only a
minority were appealed; SNAP replacement totals cover approved claims, not all
losses; reissue routes require a request; worker remedies depend on a reachable
complaint path.

The [TAKE IT DOWN Act record](records/us-ftc-take-it-down-act-platform-removal-2026.json)
adds a digital-remedy version: a covered platform must offer a clear request
route, address a valid request within 48 hours, include known identical copies,
and face FTC escalation if the route is missing or fails. This is meaningful
contestability architecture, but it is not evidence that an image was removed,
that it did not reappear, or that the person regained safety, privacy, trust,
or a practical way to leave the platform.

The [tax-rulemaking public-engagement record](records/us-gao-tax-rulemaking-public-engagement-2026.json)
adds a political-institutional version. Notice and comment were available in all
10 sampled proposed tax regulations, with a median 61.5-day comment period, but
hearing access and documentation were uneven, selected economic analyses did
not consistently provide specific cost, benefit, revenue, or summary accounting
estimates, and Treasury and IRS lacked a policy for mass or AI-assisted comments.
Formal voice can therefore exist without proving representative access, agency
learning, influence on the final rule, or increased taxpayer legitimacy. This is
not evidence that comments had no effect; it keeps the participation channel
separate from its downstream influence.

The [organized-interests and gun-policy record](records/us-nber-organized-interests-gun-policy-responsiveness-2000-2024.json)
adds a power-and-party version. A 2026 state-level study links campaign
contributions, party control, a Gun Law Index, and estimated constituent
sentiment. It reports that contribution-linked policy movement differs under
Republican and Democratic trifectas, while its instrumental-variable estimates
do not find constituent sentiment itself causally predicts policy change under
either. Broad support can therefore coexist with uneven policy responsiveness;
the result is specific to the study's gun-policy measures and does not prove
that organized interests always override voters, or that policy change produced
better safety or legitimacy.

The [whistleblower disclosure and protection record](records/us-gao-whistleblower-disclosure-protection-agency-capacity-2026.json)
adds a worker-voice version. Selected federal agencies use different mixes of
confidentiality, retaliation investigation or relief, and monetary awards, but
workers may still fear reprisals, identity exposure, or limits in employment
agreements, while agencies report high report volumes and processing challenges.
Information can therefore exist without becoming usable public correction; a
disclosure channel is not the same as reporter safety, a valid investigation,
adequate remedy, recurrence prevention, or restored trust.

The [Public Buildings Service reorganization record](records/us-gao-public-buildings-reorganization-workforce-service-2025-2026.json)
adds an institutional-capacity version. GSA's stated goals of centralization,
consistency, and streamlining were accompanied by a 45% workforce reduction
without prior strategic workforce planning, missing performance measures,
mostly one-way communication, and selected tenant reports of service delays.
Reorganization can therefore increase formal centralization while weakening the
people and feedback needed to test whether service improved; later plans for
surveys, milestones, and workforce analysis are correction routes, not completed
results.

The [Pell/FAFSA record](records/us-gao-pell-fafsa-eligibility-2026.json)
adds a public-benefit version: Pell eligibility rose to about 9.9 million
students after FAFSA simplification, including roughly 1.9 million more
students eligible for the maximum award, while fewer students completed the
application. Some household configurations also lost eligibility. Formal
eligibility can therefore expand while the application route loses people;
neither eligibility nor completion proves aid receipt, enrollment, persistence,
or educational security.

The [chronic-absence record](records/us-ed-chronic-absenteeism-2022-2023.json)
adds a school-access version: 13.4 million of 48.3 million students in the
2022–23 administrative file were absent for at least 10% of enrolled school
days. Attendance can therefore be interrupted at population scale while the
reason, school response, learning, safety, belonging, and return remain
unknown. A high chronic-absence count is not proof of permanent departure or
one common school failure.

The [school-facility disruption record](records/us-gao-school-facility-disruption-2026.json)
adds a physical-infrastructure version. GAO estimates that 19% of districts
canceled school for at least one day because of facility problems in 2024–25,
affecting about 2 million students and 3.5 million learning days; 47% reported
a chronic facility issue in at least one school. Plumbing emergencies and
extreme classroom temperatures were among leading causes. These estimates do
not establish student-level learning loss, family work or care burden, health,
repair effectiveness, or later opportunity—and closing a school can itself be a
safety protection rather than a measured educational failure.

The [MOEquity oversight record](records/us-gao-k12-moequity-grant-learning-2026.json)
adds an institutional-learning version: the emergency rule generally protected
high-poverty schools and districts from disproportionate cuts, but Education did
not develop written technical-assistance procedures, unreliable data limited
identification of the poorest districts, and lesson-sharing was not ensured.
Formal equity protection can therefore exist without reliable verification,
organizational learning, or proof of a student-level benefit; the review does
not establish national compliance or learning outcomes.

The [federal-program-effectiveness record](records/us-gao-federal-program-effectiveness-2026.json)
widens the same tension across federal programs. GAO reports that programs may
count spending, reach, or internal processes without clearly defined goals or
usable outcome evidence. Twelve of 15 selected programs serving pregnant women,
young children, and their families had performance-management processes, but
that did not show durable household improvement; only about one-third of
surveyed federal managers reported access to robust evaluations. The DHS
Securing the Cities case supplies a counterexample: oversight improved goals,
milestones, and financial tracking from 2019 to 2024, even though preparedness
impact remained unmeasured. Better management is therefore a real correction,
not proof that the public problem has been solved.

The [disaster debris-contracting record](records/us-gao-disaster-debris-contracting-information-2026.json)
adds a preparedness-and-legibility version. Advance contracts can speed cleanup,
but only six of 16 selected governments had them; FEMA role confusion, uneven
public reporting after Los Angeles and Hurricane Helene, and incomplete
fraud-risk practice left the recovery route harder to see and oversee. Physical
response capacity and public recovery visibility therefore run on separate
clocks; a contract or map does not prove safe return, equitable cleanup, or
household recovery.

The [federal performance-review implementation record](records/us-gao-federal-performance-management-strategic-review-2025-2026.json)
adds a government-wide learning version. None of four selected agencies fully
addressed the new strategic-review requirements in 2025, and OMB guidance
addressed 10 of 15 assessed requirements. Goals, dashboards, and statutory
review rules can therefore exist without a complete cycle of stakeholder input,
cross-agency learning, decision change, and public results; incomplete formal
compliance is not by itself proof of agency failure or absent informal learning.

The [cross-agency performance-goals record](records/us-gao-cross-agency-performance-goals-coordination-2026.json)
adds the coordination mechanism beneath that government-wide learning problem.
GAO discussion participants said shared goals can define ownership, create
energy around priorities, and open opportunities for agencies to work together,
but they also described inconsistent progress tools, uneven leadership, and
limited staff or funding. A shared goal can therefore create coordination
capacity before the people, data, authority, and transition plan needed to keep
it alive are secure; coordination momentum is not yet an improved public
outcome.

The [federal financial-reporting controls record](records/us-gao-federal-financial-reporting-controls-2025.json)
adds a public-legibility version. Treasury published the FY2025 consolidated
financial statements and corrected identified draft errors, but GAO found three
new control deficiencies, six prior recommendations still open, and no GAO
opinion on the federal government's accrual-based consolidated statements since
the first audit in FY1997. Reporting and institutional repair are real, while
an unfinished control system does not mean every figure is wrong or that fiscal
collapse has been shown. Publication, correction, audit assurance, public
understanding, and citizen-level burden remain separate stages.

The [GAO fraud-risk finding](records/us-gao-fraud-risk-management-public-trust-recommendations-2026.json)
adds a control-to-legitimacy version. GAO reports 215 fraud-risk
recommendations, with about 40% still open in April 2026, and explicitly links
fraud to public trust and mission execution. An open recommendation is evidence
of unfinished control work, not a fraud rate or a measured decline in trust;
prevention, recovery, and beneficiary experience remain open stages.

The [Part D premium-stabilization record](records/us-gao-medicare-partd-premium-stabilization-2026.json)
adds a medication-cost version: average standalone-plan premiums stayed near
$42–$43 and enrollment rose 2% under the demonstration, while Medicare took
on an estimated $9.8 billion in 2025–2026 risk. Premium stability and continued
enrollment can be meaningful protection, but they do not establish medication
adherence, health outcomes, plan quality, or net household relief; the causal
effect remains to be evaluated.

The [No Surprises provider-participation record](records/us-gao-no-surprises-network-participation-2026.json)
adds a medical-billing version: after the Act took effect, in-network claim
shares rose for three of four selected specialties, while emergency-medicine
payment trends largely continued their earlier direction. A legal billing
protection can change provider participation without proving lower patient
bills, affordable care, better outcomes, or an isolated causal effect.

## 4. Support versus burden transfer

The CDC Community & Connection rotation adds a social-support boundary:
reported support can be common while usable participation remains constrained.
The displayed national indicators show 82% of adults receiving needed support,
58% of youth receiving it, and 12% of adults reporting health-related
difficulty participating in social activities, but the measures are from
different years and systems. Support is not automatically mobility, attendance,
belonging, recovery, or civic action.

The firearm-safety study adds a cultural version of this tension: people can
want protection while disagreeing about which tool supplies it and who bears
the risk. Information about private costs and non-lethal alternatives moved
safe-storage and alternative-tool preferences more than it moved current
owners' willingness to give up lethal firearms. A shared goal is therefore not
the same as a shared route, and a safer stated margin is not yet a realized
community outcome.

The Pew religious-community analysis adds a social-infrastructure version:
reported volunteering and generalized trust can be high inside a real
community institution while education and regional differences remain large.
Social support is therefore a resource with an access pattern, not a single
group trait. A high average does not show equal belonging, equal burden, or
causal institutional benefit.

The CDC social-connection record adds an unequal-exposure version: in a
26-state BRFSS analysis, 32.1% reported feeling socially isolated and 24.1%
reported lacking needed social or emotional support, with higher loneliness
among younger, lower-income, bisexual, and transgender respondents. The strong
associations with mental-health measures are cross-sectional; loneliness is not
itself a complete account of support received, care, belonging, or civic action.

The [grandparenting and mortality record](records/us-census-grandparenting-mortality-race-2000-2019.json)
adds a family-care version: a Census–Social Security linked study found that
the mortality association of living with grandchildren and being a primary
caregiver differed across racial and ethnic groups and caregiving durations.
Family care can therefore be real social infrastructure without having one
universal health effect or one shared cultural meaning. The result is a
working-paper association, not proof that caregiving caused harm or protection;
health, housing, income, prior conditions, and public support remain open.

The [women's work and Temperance mobilization record](records/us-nber-civil-war-womens-work-temperance-mobilization-2026.json)
adds a historical burden-to-voice version. In linked nineteenth-century
records, wives and daughters of disabled Union veterans had higher labor-force
participation, and towns with greater women's labor participation and disabled-
veteran exposure showed more Temperance Crusade activity. Material household
responsibility can create a route into collective action, but this is a
historical, town-level pathway—not proof of individual persuasion, modern
gender dynamics, movement success, or durable political power.

The [working-class identity finding](records/us-pew-working-class-identity-2026.json)
adds a status-and-meaning version. Sixty percent of adults say “working class”
describes them, including substantial shares of upper-income and college-
educated adults. The label therefore overlaps with income, education,
occupation, financial stress, and party without being reducible to any one of
them. A material-pressure story can become a political identity story, but the
survey does not show organizing, voting, workplace action, or improved
security. Identity is a possible route through which conditions acquire
meaning, not proof that they produced collective action.

The [multidimensional-signaling model](records/us-nber-multidimensional-signaling-cultural-politics-2026.json)
adds a mechanism for the cultural-politics surface. When voters cannot see a
candidate's full preferences, issue signals become shortcuts; if cultural
signals are clearer than economic ones, the model predicts more cultural
rhetoric even when voters care more about the economy. This is a formal model,
not evidence that current U.S. voters are confused or that cultural rhetoric
changed anyone's vote. It separates visible political signaling from the
underlying preference, persuasion, and policy outcome.

The [U.S. protest-effects finding](records/us-nber-protest-effects-public-opinion-policy-2017-2022.json)
adds an empirical counterweight. Across 14 protest waves from 2017 through
2022, protests generally generated substantial online attention but limited
measured movement in broader opinion, elections, or policy; the protests after
George Floyd's death were a notable exception. Visibility can therefore be a
real political event without being a completed route to influence. The study
does not show that protest is ineffective, because local, community, and
organizer effects are not fully captured.

The [trust-and-democracy finding](records/us-nber-trust-democracy-economic-crisis-political-turnover-2026.json)
adds a crisis-to-stability layer. An NBER study reports that recessions are
more likely to be followed by regular political turnover in democracies with
lower generalized interpersonal trust, with analogous patterns in U.S.
counties and individual U.S.-county analyses. This does not mean trust alone
causes stability: country, county, and individual results are different units,
and institutions may create both trust and resilience.

The [aggregate-shocks review](records/us-nber-aggregate-shocks-preferences-beliefs-review-2024.json)
extends this beyond one crisis-to-turnover relationship. Across reviewed work
on recessions, inflation, trade, migration, wars, pandemics, and disasters,
shocks can shape political preferences, risk attitudes, and institutional trust;
experiences during young adulthood often last longer. The review also finds
economic and non-economic shocks do not have one uniform political effect. This
is a literature map, not proof of permanent belief change or later action.

The [zero-sum-thinking finding](records/us-nber-zero-sum-thinking-ancestry-political-preferences-2023-2026.json)
adds a group-competition mechanism. In a survey of 20,400 U.S. residents,
stronger zero-sum beliefs were associated with support for redistribution,
race- and gender-based affirmative action, and more restrictive immigration
policies. The mindset also related to individual and ancestral experiences.
This is not one ideological package or a causal transmission story: perceived
scarcity may be real, and the study does not follow beliefs into action.

The [cultural-distance finding](records/us-nber-cultural-distance-media-consumption-time-attitudes-1965-2020.json)
adds a measurement correction to broad polarization stories. U.S. groups became
more distinguishable on some political attitudes and consumer behavior, while
other dimensions converged or stayed broadly stable. Cultural distance is thus
not one national split: media, shopping, time use, attitudes, and names can
move on different clocks, and distinguishability does not prove hostility or
loss of contact.

The [American Indian heterogeneity record](records/us-nber-american-indian-heterogeneity-human-capital-2026.json)
adds an identity-and-measurement version. Economic, educational, and health
outcomes differ across communities, places, resources, historical federal
experiences, and degrees of self-governance. A national or racial average can
therefore hide both continuing harm and local improvement; changing identity
definitions and incomplete data can hide them again. Better disaggregation is
necessary for seeing the pattern, but it does not by itself identify a remedy,
restore trust, or show that self-governance produced a universal improvement.

The [workforce-diversity business-perception finding](records/us-gallup-workforce-diversity-business-perceptions-2026.json)
adds a firm-and-culture version: belief in the instrumental business value of
diverse workforces rose across parties, while judgments about DEI priority and
business performance remained divided. Shared claims about profitability can
therefore coexist with conflict about legitimacy, implementation, and worker
experience. Perceived business value is not realized firm performance or equal
workplace power.

The [news-platform civic-engagement record](records/us-pew-news-platform-civic-engagement-2025.json)
adds a platform-mediated participation version. Pew's engagement groups show
different routes through public life: Mobilizers reported much more voting and
contact with officials than Outsiders, while people who regularly received news
from social-media influencers often said it helped them understand current
events and differed from news elsewhere. These are distinct attention, trust,
and action surfaces, not one civic ladder. Self-reported usefulness is not
accuracy, persuasion, platform causation, or informed political control.

The [Census nonresponse-bias finding](records/us-census-cps-asec-nonresponse-bias-2026.json)
adds a measurement version of unequal exposure. The 2026 CPS ASEC response
rate was 61.3%, and linked-record comparisons show survey-only income levels
running above alternative estimates while poverty runs slightly below them.
Official visibility is therefore shaped by who answers and which linked fields
exist. The adjustment is a useful benchmark, not a complete view of people who
are missing from the survey.

The [courage-and-social-risk finding](records/us-gallup-courage-social-risk-2026.json)
adds a meaning-and-action version: people can value principled leadership and
still expect speaking up to carry personal, professional, or social costs.
Perceived risk is therefore an intermediate condition between belief and
action. It may explain silence or delay, but the survey does not measure actual
retaliation, protection, speech, or organizing.

Help often protects one currency by moving costs into another. A benefit may
protect food while adding paperwork. Consumer-directed care may increase
recipient control while shifting scheduling and backup labor to families.
Family, employer, public, and digital support can all be real while remaining
unequal, reciprocal only in part, or fragile.

The youth peer-loss record adds a social-network version of unequal exposure.
After a peer self-harm death, some students showed more diagnoses and mental-
health visits, while students without a prior diagnosis showed more felony
offending rather than more care-seeking. A shared event can therefore produce
different paths depending on reachable support and prior exposure; formal
diagnosis or contact does not yet show recovery or safety.

The 988 record adds a public-system version of the same gap. States with
larger pre-988 capacity shortages answered more crisis calls in state, but the
study found no detectable short-run reduction in suicide mortality or broader
population mental-health outcomes. A working entry point can help some callers
without proving that counseling, dispatch, treatment, housing, social support,
and follow-up are available or sufficient.

The dementia-coverage record adds a care-transition version: older adults with
probable dementia were more likely to lack supplemental Medicare coverage
before probable onset, while Medicaid eligibility rose without higher reported
take-up. Eligibility, coverage completeness, care affordability, caregiver
capacity, and actual care are separate stages around a transition when families
may already be under strain.

The nursing-home inspection record adds an oversight-and-incentives version.
Care effort and selected survival measures rose around predictable inspection
windows, while a model estimated that less predictable timing could save more
lives but reduce some immediately available information. A formal inspection
can improve care and still invite short-term performance around the check;
inspection counts alone do not prove sustained resident wellbeing.

The neighbor-support evidence adds a cultural version of the same tension:
people report greater willingness to help than confidence that help would be
returned. Goodwill is therefore a real social resource, but it is not yet
delivered protection; the missing stages are a dated request, actual help,
helper burden, adequacy, and repeatability. See the [neighbor goodwill and
reciprocal-support finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-044.md).

The civic-infrastructure evidence extends the same tension into public and
political life. A transit system can define and measure safety, accessibility,
reliability, and information without yet changing a rider's completed trip;
and a political home can offer belonging, civic information, food, money, and
transportation without proving equal receipt, lasting safety, or institutional
influence. Support can therefore make action more feasible while still leaving
the work of access, persistence, and follow-through uneven. See the [transit
usability finding](US-BROAD-INTERESTING-FINDINGS_V1.md#354-measuring-transit-usability-can-widen-accountability-before-it-changes-service)
and [political belonging finding](US-BROAD-INTERESTING-FINDINGS_V1.md#355-political-belonging-can-operate-as-civic-infrastructure).

The SNAP sugary-drink record adds a policy-to-meaning version: restricted
products fell in the first implementing states, but recipients reported more
stigma and some households substituted toward still-eligible sugary drinks or
juice. A policy can change purchases and still reduce felt respect; a modeled
health benefit is not the same as observed health, food security, or legitimate
participation.

The fast-food wage-floor record adds an uneven-firm version: overall employment
effects were modest so far, while small-chain entry slowed and exit increased.
A statewide average can therefore hide pressure on particular firms, workers,
neighborhoods, and low-cost consumer options. Firm survival or employment totals
do not yet show wages, hours, prices, quality, or household welfare.

The [working-adults Medicaid/SNAP record](records/us-gao-working-adults-medicaid-snap-employer-reliance-2026.json)
adds a labor-and-safety-net version. In 2024, about 13.8 million adults who
were enrolled in Medicaid worked at some point, as did about 10.6 million adults
living in SNAP-receiving households; selected state data also found overlap among
large employers, transportation, food preparation, and retail. Work and public
benefit reliance therefore coexist within the same labor system. The evidence
does not establish low wages, employer intent, benefit shifting, schedule
control, eligibility churn, household security, or worker alternatives.

**Question:** who performs the hidden work, who absorbs delay, and who carries
the residual risk when the formal support is insufficient?

The GAO unpaid-work estimate and BEA household-production account should now be
treated as sufficient for the macro hidden-work layer. The next source in this
lane should add distribution, choice, exhaustion, substitution, or wellbeing;
another national total would repeat the same stage.

## 5. Recovery versus durability

An immediate improvement is not proof that the problem is solved. Receipt,
repair, reactivation, bill credit, job return, or physical operation must be
followed through adequacy, recurrence, continuity, and later security.

**Examples:** a first product repair can fail; aggregate refund cashing does not
show account correction; a project can energize without proving household bill
relief; a worker can receive back-pay without later security; a home can remain
occupied while insurance, repair, and hazard protection remain fragile; and a
disaster recovery loan can improve firm survival, employment, revenue, and
credit while leaving worker, owner-household, customer, and community recovery
unmeasured.

The wealth-and-income mobility study adds an intergenerational version. A
child can move upward in income while remaining far less mobile in homeownership
or housing wealth. A severe recession is associated with lower later income
mobility across families, but its wealth consequences are concentrated among
children from poorer families. Income recovery and asset security therefore run
on different clocks; county associations do not yet show which institution or
family transfer produced the difference.

The Solar for All filing adds an intermediate infrastructure case: a project
row can show monthly credit receipt for all listed subscribers without showing
the individual credit amount, net bill effect, or later persistence. Receipt
is therefore a real stage in recovery, but not yet durable household security.

The latest rotation adds the same pattern at three larger stages: Amazon's
consumer redress expanded toward automatic distribution, FERC moved toward
large-load reliability standards, and NATO described summit commitments moving
toward fielded capability. These are real implementation advances, but none by
itself proves that a consumer received adequate recovery, a ratepayer avoided
burden, or an ally produced deterrence. Procedural maturity is a stage in the
chain, not the lived endpoint.

The alliance-legitimacy records add a public version of the same split: NATO
can retain favorable institutional views while confidence in the United States
as a reliable or interest-respecting partner is lower. Formal alignment and
relational trust therefore recover on different clocks; neither one alone
proves willingness to bear future cost or risk.

The Census Household Panel text-contact record adds a measurement version:
text invitations can make response faster for some people while access and
consent remain unequal. Institutional visibility can therefore improve without
proving representative participation, lower burden, or trust.

The Census date-entry experiment adds a practical-usability version: a calendar
can improve accuracy and speed even when users describe text, dropdown, and
calendar designs as equally easy. Apparent simplicity therefore does not prove
equal ability to complete a public task.

The Census formal-privacy paper adds a public-visibility version: controlled
noise can protect confidential records while changing which small groups or
local problems remain visible. Formal privacy, useful accuracy, and trusted
public knowledge therefore recover on different clocks.

The Census incentive-preference record adds a payment-access version: digital
routes can be convenient for most people while physical cash remains more
usable for a smaller, lower-income subgroup. Convenience, successful receipt,
and household room therefore do not move on one clock.

The AI-exposed graduate record adds an entry-to-work version: a college degree
can remain a real credential while the first job becomes less available and
less well paid for the most exposed majors. Education capacity and labor-market
conversion are therefore separate clocks. An initial employment or earnings
shock is not yet proof of permanent displacement, lifetime loss, or household
insecurity.

The [workplace AI partisan-composition record](records/us-nber-politics-ai-partisan-composition-2026.json)
adds a meaning-versus-exposure version. Democrats reported frequent workplace
AI use somewhat more often than Republicans in Q1 2026, but the gap became
statistically insignificant after education controls and reversed with
occupation and industry controls. Perceived displacement risk, by contrast,
showed a new 4.2-point partisan gap. Workplace location and skills therefore
explain much of the observed use difference, while political meaning may be
separating before realized job loss or worker-control outcomes are visible.

The result does not establish ideological causation, actual adoption, pay,
displacement, or political action; omitted workplace culture, region, age,
management, and reporting differences remain possible.

The [AI-worker retraining record](records/us-nber-ai-worker-retraining-returns-2026.json)
adds an adaptation counterexample. Among WIOA training participants, estimated
quarterly earnings gains for workers from highly AI-exposed occupations rose
from about $1,000 in 2012–2019 to about $3,000 in 2022–2024 relative to matched
job-search-only users. The gains came mainly through movement into less
AI-exposed work, with a smaller AI-complementary route. Training can therefore
produce measurable economic conversion, but the study does not establish equal
access, completion, causality for every participant, job quality, stability,
control, or household security.

The [female-teacher and girls' life-course record](records/us-census-female-teachers-female-students-lifetime-2026.json)
adds a long-horizon institutional-culture version. A Census linked-record
working paper on rural America circa 1940 reports that exposure to female
primary-school teachers improved girls' educational attainment, with stronger
effects among girls with poorly educated parents and in places with limited
labor-market opportunities for women. The reported pathway extends to delayed
marriage and childbearing, better-educated husbands, higher household income,
and longer life. This is a historical relationship, not a current universal
school effect or proof of autonomy, equal treatment, political participation,
or one mechanism.

The cross-border firm-network record adds a supply-chain version: a foreign
disaster can reduce U.S. purchases while multinational and intra-firm networks
absorb part of the measured firm-level shock. Private network capacity can
therefore exist without proving consumer protection, worker protection, or
national resilience; the displaced burden may simply move to another supplier,
product, place, or time.

The export-control record adds a state-policy version: a national-security rule
can reduce targeted market access and spill into other products, destinations,
and payroll. Policy intent and domestic adjustment are both real, but neither
one alone establishes the external security result. The missing link is the
same policy-firm-foreign-response chain that shows whether the imposed cost
produced the intended strategic change.

The [foreign-sourced telecommunications procurement record](records/us-gao-foreign-sourced-telecom-procurement-information-2026.json)
adds a supply-chain-control version. Section 889 reduced federal obligations to
five named Chinese companies after 2019, and nearly 90% of active-contract
companies publicly represented that they did not use the covered equipment.
Yet DOD and GSA did not broadly share affiliate information and implementation
lessons with other agencies. A procurement prohibition can therefore reduce
visible exposure while the identity and information needed for verified control
remain incomplete; compliance is not the same as proven operational security or
strategic leverage.

The regional hospital record adds a place-based care version: employment and
capacity can rise inside a hospital sector while the number of local
establishments falls. Aggregate system strength and nearby usable access are
therefore separate measures; consolidation may improve some services while
making other care harder to reach.

The rural-firm innovation record adds a missing-complements version: a rural
innovation gap can reflect digital infrastructure, R&D, and human-capital
shortfalls rather than rural location as a single cause. A place label can
therefore hide the real mechanism. Correlation or decomposition still does not
prove that a particular investment will generate innovation or shared regional
benefit.

The CTC/ACTC record adds a benefit-conversion version: 91% taxpayer
participation can coexist with 85% dollar participation. A high count of
participants therefore does not settle whether the full value was received,
received on time, or large enough to change household security.

The [Child Tax Credit sentiment record](records/us-nber-child-tax-credit-sentiment-benefit-loss-2026.json)
adds a benefit-loss-to-public-judgment version. Using monthly Consumer Sentiment
Survey data and household variation in lost 2021 benefits, the study estimates
that sentiment fell 1.7 index points per $1,000 of lost credit and that the
effect persisted nearly two years, with larger effects among lower-income
families with multiple children. A recurring benefit can therefore shape
economic perception after it disappears, but the study does not show changed
spending, government trust, voting, political identity, housing security, or
the complete household pathway.

The founder-hiring record adds an identity-to-organization version: a founder's
social background can be associated with who gets hired and which relative wage
opportunities persist. Firm capacity and formal labor-market openness can
therefore coexist with socially patterned access; the mechanism may involve
trust, information, supply, preference, or exclusion and remains to be
separated.

The WIC record adds a measurement version: a public system can produce more
precise state, county, and subgroup estimates while the practical route from
eligibility to benefit and health remains unobserved. Reliable measurement is a
capacity gain, not proof that the underlying access problem has been solved.

The public-pension record adds a financial-capacity version: a public
institution's funding pressure can shape its private-capital matching and the
productivity of financed firms. Capital allocation and public security are
therefore separate clocks; a firm-level productivity result does not tell us
whether workers, beneficiaries, or taxpayers ultimately absorb the burden.

The minimum-wage/robot-adoption record adds a labor-policy version: a higher
wage floor can change a factory's technology choice before anyone can say who
gets the benefit or bears the cost. The Census working paper reports about an
8% increase in robot adoption for a 10% minimum-wage increase. That is a
firm-adjustment result, not proof of job loss or worker gain. The unresolved
middle is staffing, tasks, pay, hours, safety, training, worker voice, prices,
and household room.

The affirmative-action-contracting record adds a political-access version: a
state can change the formal contracting route and see existing minority- and
women-owned firms shrink without seeing more of them close. New entry can fall
while survival remains unchanged. That separates staying alive from staying
large enough to compete and from creating a new path into the market. The
working paper does not settle the effects on workers, communities, or public
services.

The female-teacher record adds an intergenerational version: a local school
institution can shape later education, family timing, household income, and
longevity, especially where families and labor markets offer fewer alternatives.
This is a long-run opportunity pathway, not proof that representation alone
guarantees autonomy or equal treatment. Historical rural evidence also cannot
be silently treated as a current national estimate.

The women's-education record adds a causal education-to-household version: a
local schooling margin can improve employment, earnings, job status, working
conditions, and children's early-life inputs together. This does not mean that
all college produces the same result, or that better measured job amenities
settle care burden, autonomy, or household security. The missing middle is how
education changes time, schedules, health, and bargaining inside the household.

The minimum-wage-career record adds a time-horizon version: young workers who
start at the minimum wage can have steep later earnings growth, while older
workers can have a different trajectory, and a state policy increase need not
change those later careers in the study. A long-run earnings path is not the
same as a good job, and a future-career null is not a null for short-run firm,
price, hours, or household effects.

The local-demand-shock record adds a place-and-measurement version: different
ways of defining a local shock produce different employment and migration
patterns. Little migration does not necessarily mean little harm or easy
adaptation; households can commute, switch sectors, reduce participation,
change housing, or absorb losses without moving. The place response is a bundle,
not one mobility number.

The immigrant-student record adds an identity-and-firm version: one education
system can route people toward both multinational-scale firms and small firms
or self-employment. Firm size is therefore not a simple ladder from failure to
success. It can encode degree, geography, visa and firm capability, networks,
ethnic specialization, opportunity, constraint, or selection—and the meaning
of the route cannot be inferred from earnings alone.

The multinational-services record adds a cross-border-capacity version: a firm
can use goods, services, intellectual property, and foreign affiliates together
to absorb a global shock. That capability may support domestic services work,
but it can also deepen dependence on foreign demand, customers, legal systems,
and intellectual-property routes. Export recovery is not the same as shared
security or strategic autonomy.

The HOPE VI record adds a social-infrastructure version: redevelopment can
change children's long-run opportunity through interaction with surrounding
peers without raising public-housing adults' earnings. A lower neighborhood
poverty rate may partly reflect who moves in, not only what happens to original
residents. Buildings, composition, social connection, adult income, child
mobility, and resident control must therefore remain separate measures.

The [neighborhood-effects and missing-markets review](records/us-nber-neighborhood-effects-missing-markets-opportunity-2026.json)
adds a market-and-mobility version. The reviewed U.S. experimental and
quasi-experimental evidence finds that childhood neighborhoods can affect later
earnings and socioeconomic outcomes, while borrowing limits, neighbor spillovers,
sorting, and housing-search frictions can leave higher-opportunity places
undersupplied. Customized mobility services and vouchers may help some families,
but the review does not follow one family through search, lease-up, transport,
care, cost, continuity, and adult security. Neighborhood opportunity is a real
causal possibility, not a guarantee that a formal housing option becomes usable.

The house-price/firm-credit record adds a household-asset-to-local-capacity
version: a housing shock can reach small and young firms through owners'
collateral and bank credit supply. A firm's employment response is therefore
not only a firm story; it may reflect household wealth, local banking, and place
finance. But firm employment is still not the same as worker security, consumer
relief, or durable community recovery.

The [bankruptcy-venue record](records/us-census-bankruptcy-forum-shopping-delaware-employment-1990-2021.json)
adds a legal-institution version. For firms whose venue choice responds to
distance from Delaware, a specialized bankruptcy court is associated with less
liquidation and closure, stronger creditor recovery, and more post-bankruptcy
employment. Firm survival can therefore depend on institutional venue, while
the local estimate still leaves worker quality, consumer effects, and equal
stakeholder benefit open.

The [reusable-software record](records/us-census-nonrival-custom-software-firm-scope-concentration-2026.json)
adds a technology-to-scope version. Software that can serve many establishments
at once may lower the cost of operating a larger firm; the calibrated model
therefore links falling software costs to some rise in firm scope and
concentration. That is a mechanism, not proof of market harm, worker benefit,
or consumer loss.

The [environmental lender-liability record](records/us-census-environmental-lender-liability-pollution-abatement-1996-2026.json)
adds a finance-to-public-burden version. Lower lender cleanup exposure was
associated with less firm investment in abatement and more pollution, while
higher exposure produced less favorable loan pricing and possible monitoring.
Credit access, firm behavior, environmental burden, and household health are
therefore connected but not interchangeable outcomes.

The [housing-wealth labor record](records/us-census-housing-wealth-womens-labor-supply-childcare-2026.json)
adds a household-care version. Housing wealth reduced women’s labor-force
participation in the instrument-defined margin, especially among mothers of
young children and women citing child-related reasons. An asset can therefore
change time allocation without telling us whether the resulting care is valued,
constrained, adequately supported, or financially secure.

The [tariff-incidence record](records/us-census-tariff-quantity-discounts-incomplete-pass-through-2018-2019.json)
adds a trade-price version. Import prices can rise one-for-one with a tariff
because transaction scale and quantity discounts collapse, even while the
scale-adjusted pass-through is about 60% and foreign exporters absorb part of
the burden. Border prices, household costs, firm adjustment, and geopolitical
success therefore remain separate stages.

The NBER tariff pass-through record adds a delayed domestic-price version: only
about 26% of the tariff increase passed through to consumer prices in its
comparison, and the indirect input-cost and domestic-markup channel took nine
to twelve months. Policy exposure, firm cost, competition, price, product
choice, household burden, and political blame therefore recover on different
clocks.

The self-fulfilling credit-score record adds a signal-to-risk version: a score
can change without a new credit-report fact and still be followed by more
default among borrowers with prior derogatory marks. Signal, lender decision,
price or limit, payment room, default, and recovery therefore remain separate
stages; prediction can be useful while the decision environment also helps
produce the outcome being predicted.

The Uber licensing record adds a regulation-to-service version: licensing can
change who enters the platform without producing a consistent change in the
measured ratings or driving behavior. Entry, screening, ordinary ride quality,
rare serious safety, supply, access, worker cost, and public legitimacy
therefore remain separate stages.

The one-office-day record adds a work-and-household-time version: coordinated
in-person contact improved measured communication, productivity, and retention
in one customer-service team, while commute, care, disability, household time,
pay, and promotion were not measured. A work gain and the cost of obtaining it
therefore recover on different clocks.

The fiscal-news record adds an expectation-before-payment version: information
about higher future public debt raised expected inflation and spending in a
Korean experiment, while information about a higher fiscal balance raised
expected growth without significantly changing spending. The result is a
cross-country mechanism test, not a U.S. household cost; news, belief, ability
to act, policy realization, bill, trust, and political judgment remain separate.

The bank-fee record adds a liquidity-and-recovery version: removing NSF fees
reduced visible charges broadly, while overdraft changes produced wider later
improvements mainly for more liquid households. Fee removal, payment success,
bridge credit, later bill, recovery, and financial stress therefore move on
different clocks.

The sleepy-deposits record adds a relationship-and-stability version: customers
can stay with a bank because switching a household payment system is costly,
and that same staying can soften competition while supporting stable funding.
Rate, service, trust, switching effort, payment continuity, bank funding,
competition, and financial stability therefore recover on different clocks.

The dependent-coverage record adds a family-protection-to-work-choice version:
extending dependent eligibility increased both coverage take-up and parental
job retention. Insurance, dependent care, network, deductible, job, wage,
training, bargaining, and household security therefore move on different
clocks; retention can be a valued protection, a constraint, or both.

The weatherization record adds a model-to-room version: engineering projections
substantially exceeded realized energy savings in a large experimental program,
while indoor temperature did not significantly rise. Measure, installation,
actual use, bill, temperature, safety, health, comfort, and household room
therefore move on different clocks; infrastructure completion is not the same
as usable protection.

The competition-and-discrimination record adds a customer-power version:
historical nondiscrimination increased most in the least competitive markets,
while nondiscriminatory firms charged higher prices in the same local markets.
Firm conduct, market structure, price, treatment, exit, and consumer cost
therefore move on different clocks; competition and fair treatment are related
but not interchangeable outcomes.

The emergency-credit record adds a shock-to-recovery version: well-timed credit
for uninsured disaster damage reduced bankruptcy and increased later car
purchases among studied applicants. Damage, liquidity, approval, repair,
mobility, work, repayment, bankruptcy, and household room therefore move on
different clocks; preventing collapse is not the same as making debt harmless or
completing recovery.

The income-smoothing record adds an ordinary-shock version: low-liquidity
households changed consumption more in response to normal labor-income shocks,
and Black and Hispanic households were about twice as sensitive as White
households in the study. Income, liquid wealth, spending, debt, race, household
need, and future room therefore move on different clocks; an observed spending
change does not reveal whether the household bought, borrowed, delayed, or
lost something essential.

The temporary-work record adds a labor-status-to-credit version: temporary
workers can face greater income risk and lender rationing even at similar wages
and occupations. Contract, income risk, lender assessment, credit, housing,
family formation, and financial independence therefore move on different
clocks; a job's annual pay does not fully describe the household's option set.

The career-seniority record adds a title-to-time version: years required to
reach a job title contain information beyond wages, and higher seniority among
public-sector workers predicts transitions to higher-paying private-sector
positions. Title, time, wage, education, non-wage choice, mobility, and
household security therefore move on different clocks; promotion speed is not
the same as complete career success.

The home-equity entrepreneurship record adds a housing-to-firm version:
house-price-associated entry was mostly small and short-lived construction and
real-estate activity, while a smaller collateral channel produced longer-lived,
more sector-balanced entry. House price, collateral, entry, duration, sector,
jobs, household risk, and local capacity therefore move on different clocks;
business entry is not automatically durable place capacity.

The eBay search record adds a consumer-agency version: users searched repeatedly
over multiple days, paid an estimated search cost, and more search was related to
lower prices. Search, time, attention, price, quality, return, and platform
dependence therefore move on different clocks; price discovery is not the same
as a low-cost or low-risk purchase.

The comparative review-reward record adds a platform-signaling version: on
Taobao, rewarded feedback was associated with nearly 30% higher sales and
higher-quality sellers. Feedback incentive, review volume, seller quality,
visibility, sales, consumer prediction, and remedy therefore move on different
clocks; a platform mechanism result must not be mistaken for a U.S. welfare
result.

The rideshare record adds an immediate-mobility version: identical-trip price
dispersion coexists with limited cross-app comparison, and comparison frictions
are estimated to increase New York City platform booking volume. Fare, search,
wait, reliability, accessibility, deadline, trip completion, and household cost
therefore move on different clocks; two apps do not automatically create two
usable choices.

The caregiving record adds a family-time-to-labor version: around reported care
onset, women show earnings and employment declines relative to a matched group,
while men enter caregiving after prior labor-supply disruption. Care onset, task
intensity, work, earnings, health, household support, and recipient safety
therefore move on different clocks; employment status alone does not measure the
care burden.

The eviction-order record adds a court-to-life version: an order can add
homelessness, hospital visits, earnings loss, reduced durable-goods consumption,
and credit damage to an earlier period of distress. Arrears, filing, order,
move, homelessness, health, work, debt, credit, and recovery therefore move on
different clocks; preventing an order is not automatically durable housing
security, but an order is not merely an administrative event.

The legal-representation record adds a rights-to-outcome version: New York City
counsel reduced possession judgments, money judgments, warrants, and completed
evictions for eligible tenants, with larger effects in poorer places and places
with more noncitizens. Notice, counsel, judgment, money owed, warrant, eviction,
affordability, and later stability therefore move on different clocks; formal
legal access and recovered housing security are not the same endpoint.

The rental-assistance record adds a payment-to-stability version: lottery-
assigned pandemic aid increased rent payment and reduced eviction concern, with
suggestive health gains, but had little average effect on housing stability or
financial distress. Application, payment, fear, landlord response, court,
stability, health, and later rent therefore move on different clocks; immediate
payment and lasting housing are related but distinct outcomes.

The low-liquidity consumption-wedge record adds a measurement-and-meaning
version: consumers were split between spending below and above a modeled
frictionless benchmark, and borrowing constraints alone could not explain both
directions. Spending, need, timing, credit, adjustment cost, sacrifice, and
household security therefore remain separate stages.

The home-insurance record adds a protection-price version: low-credit homeowners
paid more for identical coverage, while a policy change weakened the credit-to-
premium relationship. Credit, disaster risk, premium, escrow, deductible,
coverage, repair, sale, and housing security therefore move on different
clocks.

The local business record adds an idea-to-place version: applications measure
pursuit while employer-firm transitions measure implementation, and local areas
can differ on those margins. Application, finance, opening, payroll, service,
survival, household livelihood, and community capacity therefore recover on
different clocks.

The remote-shopping record adds a work-location-to-household-market version:
working from home can reduce shopping minutes while increasing trips, online
orders, basket size, and prices paid. Commute time, convenience, comparison,
shopping responsibility, unpaid household work, local retail demand, and
household welfare therefore move on different clocks; a saved commute is not
automatically free time or savings.

The platform-data neutrality record adds a digital-governance version: equal
data access can widen competition while reducing the platform's incentive to
produce or refine data, so a formally fairer rule does not automatically raise
modeled consumer welfare. Access, price, competition, data quality, privacy,
consumer choice, and enforcement therefore recover on different clocks.

The [status-mismatch record](records/us-census-status-inconsistency-neighborhood-mobility-acs-2026.json)
adds a cultural-place version. A household’s income rank can sit above or below
the surrounding neighborhood’s status, and those mismatches are associated with
different short- and long-distance mobility patterns. Movement is therefore not
just a cost or labor response; it may also involve comparison and belonging,
while motive, displacement, and later security remain unmeasured.

The business-application record adds an expectation-to-realization version: a
large application count can coexist with many expected entrants not entering,
while successful entrants may underestimate their first-year employment. The
route from intention to capacity depends on financing, permits, local demand,
learning, and selection. Plans are not firms, and firms are not yet durable
employers.

The grandparent-care record adds a family-and-meaning version: the same
coresident caregiving arrangement is associated with different mortality
patterns across racial and ethnic groups and caregiving durations. Care is not
one universal burden or benefit; housing, kin support, prior health, public
services, cultural obligation, and time all shape what the arrangement means.

The trade-welfare record adds a place-and-adjustment version: a trade shock can
work through joblessness, participation, sector search, and moving, and these
routes carry different costs across workers and regions. A model-based relative
welfare result is useful, but it is not the same as subjective wellbeing,
household security, or a complete national welfare judgment.

The counter-China-influence audit adds a geopolitical legibility version: a
government can fund hundreds of projects for a strategic goal without having a
complete, shared view of project status or portfolio results. Spending and
strategic intent can therefore move faster than evidence of partner use,
legitimacy, or changed outside behavior.

The [U.S.-China global-role comparison](records/us-pew-us-china-global-role-comparison-2026.json)
adds an external-reception counterpoint. Pew finds China viewed more
favorably or reliably than the U.S. in many surveyed countries, while the
Philippines remains a clear U.S.-leaning counterexample. Public reception can
therefore diverge by country and dimension; it is not the same as material
dependence, alliance behavior, or strategic influence.

The democracy-assistance record adds a support-and-continuity version: a host
government can be the formal route for institution-building and also become a
source of pressure against civic actors. Moving aid to civil society may protect
voice, but without a prepared pivot it can also break momentum and coordination.

The Ukraine oversight record adds a correction-versus-consequence version:
reporting can become more useful while wartime data and different outcome
timelines still block a complete judgment of whether assistance worked. Better
visibility is a gain, not yet proof of institutional or strategic success.

The economic-place record adds a material-conditions version of civic capacity:
places with greater disadvantage and inequality showed less formal volunteering,
and the rural advantage changed after the Great Recession. Participation needs
time, resources, organizations, and local opportunity; a volunteering decline
does not prove that all mutual aid or belonging disappeared.

The [Census–AmeriCorps civic-time record](records/us-census-americorps-civic-reach-time-2023.json)
adds a reach-versus-intensity version. Formal volunteering rose from 23.2% in
2021 to 28.3% in 2023, and informal neighbor help reached 54.2%, but mean hours
among formal volunteers fell from 96.5 in 2017 to 70 in 2023, with the median
falling from 40 to 24. Wider participation can therefore coexist with thinner
time commitment; neither measure proves adequate help, organizational capacity,
belonging, trust, policy influence, or later political action.

The jail record adds an aggregate-versus-exposure version: a smaller jail system
can still produce frequent admissions, many unconvicted holds, and unequal racial
contact. A declining headline does not establish fair process, reduced burden,
successful release, or safer and more stable lives afterward.

The supervision record adds a post-custody version: state contact can continue
through probation and parole after jail or prison populations decline. An
administrative exit such as completion or incarceration is not the same as
durable inclusion, safety, or practical freedom.

The [federal reentry-timing record](records/us-gao-bop-reentry-timing-2026.json)
adds a usable-exit version. In a GAO review of 29,934 people, BOP had not
applied all earned time toward RRC or home-confinement placement for 21,190;
the agency also could not identify the full scale of delayed transfers, while
RRC capacity and late contractor payments constrained the route. Formal release
support can therefore exist while data, space, provider solvency, and timing
keep return to work, housing, treatment, and community connection from being a
usable bridge. Transfer is not yet durable reentry.

The Native-housing record adds a definition-versus-experience version: shared
housing can be culturally protective and still be overcrowded or unstable, while
federal categories may fail to recognize the insecurity. A program count or
formal eligibility route therefore does not prove culturally appropriate,
adequate, or durable shelter.

The [Tribal-federal shared-decision record](records/us-gao-tribal-federal-shared-decision-making-2026.json)
adds a voice-and-authority version. Federal agencies can invite Tribal input on
ancestral lands while legal authority, consensus rules, dispute resolution,
staff skills, and workforce capacity determine whether that input can actually
change management. Consultation and shared control are therefore different
institutional states; a signed agreement is not by itself proof of equal power,
cultural legitimacy, or durable self-governance.

The rural-housing coordination record adds a relationship-and-data version:
local coordinators may help residents avoid eviction or apply for Medicaid, but
HUD cannot fully count the coordinators and the reviewed studies show mixed
effects. Small trusted relationships can be real infrastructure even when the
official system cannot measure their reach or prove resident-level stability.

The Instacart record adds a consumer-market version: convenience can coexist
with hidden fees, subscription enrollment, and difficult cancellation or
refund routes. A settlement and announced refund pool can make corporate
correction visible without proving that each customer received money, paid
less, switched providers, or regained trust.

The defense-arrangements record adds a formal-agreement-to-capability version:
distributed review, personnel shortages, cost-sharing communication, and
approval time can slow an arrangement before a partner ever uses it. A defense
agreement or associated dollar value is not yet readiness, deterrence, or
legitimate joint capability.

The weapons-acquisition record adds a rapid-pathway-to-realization version:
programs can receive a faster acquisition route and still take years to deliver
when the underlying technology is immature. Budget, prototype, and program
status are stages before fielding, reliability, sustainment, operator use, and
external strategic effect.

The [technology-release and foreign-disclosure record](records/us-gao-technology-release-foreign-disclosure-2026.json)
adds a permission-to-capability version. DOD officials estimate about 7,000
foreign technology-release cases each year; roughly 70% continue existing sales
or precedent, while discovering the governing policy can take days to a year.
Urgent Ukraine reviews that normally took a year were compressed to weeks or
hours through overtime and deprioritizing other work. A specialized permission
system can protect sensitive technology and still create partner-visibility and
trust problems; reform completion is not proof of faster approvals, transfer,
partner use, interoperability, or deterrence.

The German brigade-in-Lithuania record adds a workforce-and-legitimacy version:
an alliance capability can be strategically important while depending on
voluntary staffing, selected mandatory measures, maintenance, and social support
before it reaches its planned readiness date. Personnel filled, equipment
present, operational capability, host-country acceptance, and deterrence are
different stages.

The NNSA partnership record adds a shared-capacity allocation version. Paid
work for other agencies and partners can retain staff, maintain specialized
facilities, and support missions, while the same scarce capacity may be needed
for a growing core stockpile workload. Reimbursed volume and partner dependence
therefore do not by themselves prove readiness, deterrence, or the best use of
the enterprise's limited technical capacity.

The Free Association compact record adds a strategic-access-to-partner-capacity
version. The United States can secure long-term aid commitments and military
access while the partner side still faces late disbursement, rising building
costs, labor shortages, late audits, and delayed oversight. Formal access and
funding are therefore not the same as usable local services, self-sufficiency,
legitimacy, or durable partnership.

The [Ukraine assistance oversight record](records/us-gao-ukraine-assistance-monitoring-evaluation-2023-2026.json)
adds a wartime-learning version. State improved reporting so it became more
analytical and useful for oversight and budgeting, but data access, delayed
outcomes, and changing wartime conditions left cross-project evaluations and
strategic outcome indicators incomplete as of November 2025. Better monitoring
is a real institutional correction, while $6.1 billion in assistance under the
review is not proof of recipient benefit, battlefield effect, deterrence,
nuclear-safety improvement, or changed Russian behavior.

The [Iran protest-response record](records/us-nber-us-israel-strikes-iran-protest-reversal-2026.json)
adds a foreign-action-to-political-meaning version. An NBER study reports that
U.S.–Israeli strike exposure was followed by a shift from anti-government
protest toward pro-government and anti-U.S./Israel protest, with little change
in total protest activity. An outside action can therefore change who a public
mobilization appears to support without making the society more or less active
overall. The result does not isolate repression, propaganda, fear, or recording
changes, and it does not establish durable regime legitimacy, civilian safety,
or U.S. strategic success.

The carbon-tariff record adds a policy-instrument-to-global-effect version: a
tariff can shift emissions abroad while worsening modeled domestic emissions,
and a larger modeled welfare result does not identify who pays or benefits. The
instrument, climate outcome, household burden, firm adjustment, and diplomatic
response are different stages.

The geoeconomic-coercion record adds a state-power-through-firms version:
pressure can be aimed at a foreign government, private companies, or both. A
model can clarify why those targets may respond differently, but it cannot show
whether a real firm complied, who absorbed the cost, whether the public or an
ally resisted, or whether the strategic goal was achieved.

The [OFAC firm-correction record](records/us-ofac-adani-sanctions-compliance-2023-2026.json)
adds a concrete financial-infrastructure case. OFAC describes 32 apparent
Iran-sanctions violations involving about $192 million in U.S.-dollar payments
by an India-based firm, followed by a $275 million settlement and a five-year
compliance commitment. U.S. payment reach, enforcement, and a future correction
obligation are visible stages; later screening, recurrence, deterrence, cost
distribution, and any change in state strategy remain unmeasured. The case does
not show universal control over foreign transactions or sanctions success.

The export-controls record adds a domestic-cost-to-strategy version. The share
of U.S. exports under controls doubled from 2010 to 2024, and the 2014 Russia
restriction was followed by lower targeted exports, spillovers to other products
and destinations, and lower average payroll among affected firms. Domestic
adjustment is real evidence of burden, but it does not by itself show that a
security objective failed or succeeded.

The corporate-PAC record adds a visibility-versus-substance version: firms can
pull back from visible political giving when reputational risk rises, then
return toward baseline after attention fades. A temporary PAC change is not
the same as changed lobbying, policy influence, consumer trust, or political
purpose.

The [FEC campaign-finance record](records/us-fec-campaign-finance-2025-2026-15-month.json)
adds a disclosed-money scale. From January 2025 through March 2026, federal
filers reported about $2.13 billion in congressional-candidate receipts, $1.12
billion in party-committee receipts, and $6.30 billion in PAC receipts, with
independent expenditures separately reported. These totals show financial
capacity and disclosure, not unique donor breadth, communication reach,
persuasion, turnout, candidate victory, policy change, or democratic legitimacy.

The [EAC election-administration record](records/us-eac-election-administration-voting-2024.json)
adds the ballot-conversion stage. In the 2024 election, more than 158 million
ballots were counted, but 1.74 million provisional ballots were cast; 73.8% were
counted and 25.1% rejected under varied state and local rules. Paper or auditable
records existed in more than 98% of jurisdictions. Participation totals and
auditable infrastructure therefore show substantial administrative capacity,
while the route from attempted ballot to counted ballot remains a separate
access question. Rejection alone does not prove wrongful exclusion, and the
record does not follow individual voters, cure attempts, trust, or later action.

The employment-and-mothers record adds a time-and-income version: employment
can increase material room while reducing the time or capacity available for
registration, civic learning, and voting. The reported political shift is
heterogeneous and policy-induced; it is not proof that work universally creates
one political identity or that lower turnout means apathy.

The [political-typology record](records/us-pew-political-typology-values-coalitions-2026.json)
and [Champlain Hudson transmission finding](projects/us-champlain-hudson-power-express/findings/us-nyserda-champlain-hudson-power-express-operation-2026-001.md)
add a domestic-geopolitical version of the same split. Survey respondents can
favor U.S. military primacy while also saying that allies' interests should be
considered; meanwhile, New York's operating energy system depends on a real
cross-border Canadian supply route. Stated sovereignty preferences and actual
system dependence can coexist. Neither the survey nor the operating line shows
who would accept disruption, pay the costs, or support the arrangement over
time.

The [foreign-policy cross-currents record](records/us-pew-foreign-policy-typology-cross-currents-2026.json)
adds the cultural meaning underneath that split. Leftward Progressives largely
reject sole-superpower status while strongly favoring consideration of allies;
No Apologies Right and Faith First Conservatives strongly value military
dominance while more often favor following U.S. interests when allies disagree.
The same alliance can therefore mean partnership to one group, constraint to
another, and status or protection to a third. These are stated judgments, not
evidence of spending, burden sharing, willingness to fight, partner consent,
or foreign response.

## 6. Dependence versus exit

The historical congressional-selection record adds a political version of the
capacity-versus-control tension: elections can be formally open while the
pipeline into office remains socially selective. NBER's census-linked study
finds that future Members came from unusually advantaged families and that
selected Progressive-Era reforms did not detectably change who reached
Congress. Entry selectivity is not the same as current illegitimacy, poor
representation, or a single causal mechanism; it is a missing stage between
formal voter choice and who is available to choose.

The Vietnam draft-lottery study adds an institution-and-identity version. The
military can be a real route to education while the causal evidence does not
show that service itself produced citizenship, English use, residential
integration, or cross-national marriage for the studied immigrant cohort. A
shared institution can therefore increase capacity without resolving belonging;
the people who enter it may already differ from those who do not.

People may stay with a provider, platform, employer, insurer, home, benefit, or
political system because it works—or because leaving would sacrifice money,
care, identity, safety, eligibility, or future recourse. Continued use,
non-use, account closure, program exit, or firm exit is not automatically free
choice.

**Question:** what alternative was actually available, what would leaving cost,
and what did the person or community protect or sacrifice by staying?

The Shutterstock enforcement route gives this tension a concrete subscription
form: useful service and recurring payment can coexist with unclear renewal
terms, cancellation friction, and a proposed remedy before individual refund
receipt or provider switching is known. Continued use is not enough to infer
free choice.

The [Hims & Hers digital-health record](records/us-ftc-hims-hers-health-data-billing-cancellation-2026.json)
adds a care-and-consumer version. The FTC and states allege that sensitive
health information was shared with advertising platforms and that recurring
prescription subscriptions could be billed or renewed before consumers clearly
understood the timing or could easily cancel; the case remains pending. The
released 203-complaint file shows administrative response labels, not a
representative customer rate or verified medical, privacy, financial, or
treatment outcome. Online intake can reduce access barriers for some patients,
but convenience, consent, continuity, billing, data exposure, remedy, and
practical exit remain separate stages.

The [gig-work outside-options record](records/us-nber-gig-economy-outside-options-labor-supply-2026.json)
adds a labor-market counterexample. In randomized pay experiments and a natural
experiment among Uber drivers, access to a competing platform nearly doubled
drivers' labor-supply elasticity to Uber, while the implied firm markdown fell
from about 68% to about 50%. A second platform can therefore create bargaining
leverage without a formal labor-rights change. The study does not show higher
net pay after fuel, insurance, taxes, waiting, and unpaid time, or durable
security, benefits, safety, household gains, or equally usable switching.

The [inflation-beliefs record](records/us-nber-inflation-cause-beliefs-2024.json)
adds a political-meaning version of the same separation. People can agree that
inflation is harmful while disagreeing about whether government, firms, supply
costs, labor markets, or the Federal Reserve caused it, and they can favor a
remedy for a different reason than the cause they named. Material price
pressure, cause attribution, trust in the messenger, policy trade-off, and
political action therefore move on different clocks. A public explanation can
organize blame without proving the mechanism or changing what a household does;
the survey does not follow actual bills, later behavior, or votes.

The [neighborhood-and-firm mobility record](records/us-census-neighborhoods-firms-intergenerational-mobility-2026.json)
adds a place-and-firm version of unequal opportunity. The Census-linked study
finds stronger between-neighborhood than within-neighborhood intergenerational
earnings relationships and separates person effects from establishment premiums.
Place may therefore transmit both skills and access to jobs. The finding does
not show that moving alone causes mobility, that earnings equal wellbeing, or
that a neighborhood's racial composition is a simple deficit measure; its
conditional higher-Black-share pattern is an important counterexample to that
shortcut.

The [relative-status mobility record](records/us-census-neighborhood-racial-status-white-outmobility-2026.json)
adds a cultural-meaning layer to the place question. The same racial composition
is associated with different White out-mobility patterns depending on relative
socioeconomic status. Status, contact, housing, and perceived position can
therefore interact rather than produce one universal “white flight” mechanism.
The ACS models do not show motive, coercion, destination quality, improved life,
or the experience of non-White residents; leaving is not automatically exit
power, safety, or belonging.

The [homophily and job-mobility record](records/us-census-homophily-race-job-mobility-2026.json)
adds a work-and-identity version of offered versus usable options. Black and
Hispanic workers are less responsive to job opportunities overall, but more
responsive when the destination has a larger own-race population. The same
opportunity can therefore carry different expected social costs. The result
does not distinguish network support, lower expected discrimination, safety,
information, family ties, or preference, and it does not show that moving or
staying produced better lives.

The [founder-retention record](records/us-census-founder-departure-startup-employee-retention-2026.json)
adds a firm-culture version of dependence and exit. Founder departure increases
employee turnover, especially in older or larger startups and among workers with
longer shared tenure or the same sex as the founder. A formal firm can therefore
depend on personal relationships, skills, and meaning that are not visible in a
job count. The result does not show whether turnover is harmful, whether the
culture was good, or whether the firm, worker, or household recovered.

The [manufacturing allocation record](records/us-census-manufacturing-misallocation-margin-input-mix-2026.json)
adds a measurement-and-firm-capacity version of the same split. The apparent
rise in manufacturing misallocation can come from both revenue-margin and
input-mix dispersion, while a small set of industries accounts for more than
half of the aggregate decline. A national efficiency measure therefore hides
different firm mechanisms and uneven exposure. It does not identify who was
harmed, which remedy works, or whether measured dispersion became a household
or political outcome.

## The common end-to-end test

The ASEC cognitive-interview record adds a measurement-and-voice version: a
survey can be online, available, and professionally tested while still asking
people to compress complicated household life into categories that do not fit.
Uncertainty about another household member's income, missing reference dates,
long reason lists, and repetitive anti-double-counting instructions show that
more visible data collection is not automatically more faithful social
knowledge. The report does not estimate national prevalence or prove bias in
published estimates.

The CTC/ACTC linked-data record adds a benefit-conversion version: 91% of
eligible taxpayers participated in 2021, but only 85% of eligible dollars were
paid. Person reach and resource reach therefore recover on different clocks.
The difference may reflect non-filing, under-claiming, modeled eligibility, tax
unit differences, or other mechanisms; the study does not show which path each
family experienced or whether the money produced durable household security.

The parental-leave record adds a work-family-time version: a formal leave
option can exist while usable time away from work remains unequal. In the
recent first-birth cohort, only about half of mothers and fathers used paid
leave, with different patterns by race/ethnicity and sex. Leave use, early
return, job separation, care quality, and household security are separate
stages; the descriptive study does not identify the mechanism connecting them.

The founder-hiring record adds a firm-culture version of unequal optionality:
new firms can expand capacity while their hiring demand remains patterned by
founder background. Founder similarity and relative wages persist beyond the
initial hiring moment in the study samples, so firm formation, hiring access,
worker control, and household mobility can recover on different clocks. The
study's geographic coverage, founder proxy, and survival selection prevent a
national or causal culture claim.

The hospital record adds an infrastructure-capacity version: hospital
employment and average establishment size can rise while the number of local
hospital anchors falls and more counties have none. Aggregate sector strength,
geographic reach, patient access, care quality, and household protection are
therefore different currencies. The panel does not measure the patient route or
prove that telehealth, transport, or consolidation replaced local care.

The flood-recovery record adds a restructuring version of recovery: federal
financing can support selective exit, survivor rebuilding, new entry, and used-
capital transfer, producing higher measured productivity while changing the
local ownership, job, and firm landscape. Aggregate efficiency, worker
continuity, household recovery, and community meaning therefore move on
different clocks; the manufacturing study does not close those links.

```text
condition or capability
  -> notice and route
  -> feasible alternative or non-use
  -> money/time/status/control trade-off
  -> institutional, firm, or state response
  -> actual receipt, use, or operation
  -> adequacy and durability
  -> meaning, trust, action, switching, exit, or leverage
```

The atlas is strongest when it follows one person, household, customer,
worker, place, or state through these stages. When the same unit is unavailable,
the correct result is a bounded connection and an explicit missing middle—not
an invented end-to-end claim.

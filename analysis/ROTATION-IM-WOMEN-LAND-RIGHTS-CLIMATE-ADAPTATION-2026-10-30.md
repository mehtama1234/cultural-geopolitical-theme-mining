# Rotation IM — Women’s land rights, climate adaptation and control

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When land laws, cadastral systems, electronic titles, inheritance reforms and climate-smart agricultural investments target women, do they create secure control, bargaining power and adaptation capacity—or remain legal recognition and registration counts without use, decision power, credit, protection or resilience?

## Why this packet matters

Land is a foundation for housing, food, credit, climate adaptation, cultural identity and political voice. The conversion chain is:

```text
land/work/tenure need
  -> legal recognition, customary authority and record
  -> possession, control, inheritance and protection
  -> credit, inputs, extension, climate investment and decision voice
  -> production, income, food, housing and safety
  -> bargaining power, resilience and intergenerational security
```

A woman’s name on a title may be powerful but not sufficient. Joint ownership can coexist with unilateral control by others; customary tenure can be secure or contested; a parcel can be documented but inaccessible, unproductive or vulnerable to eviction. Climate shocks intensify the importance of usable land rights.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IM1 | FAO reports only 10–20% of women in Asia and the Pacific hold tenure rights to land they work, while nearly 58% of the female labor force works in agriculture and climate change deepens barriers. | Regional gender, land and climate baseline. | Actual control, inheritance, investment, credit, safety or income. |
| IM2 | World Bank reports land projects from FY2015–2025 benefited 6.4 million women by documenting rights and recorded 2.5 million parcels to women across 17 operations. | Scale of documentation and project reach. | Whether rights are understood, defended, controlled, economically used or resilient to family/market/state pressure. |
| IM3 | World Bank reports Philippines land-tenure work had issued 163,176 electronic titles by July 2025, 84% bearing female names, with eventual expected benefits for 750,000 agrarian beneficiaries and 1.3 million hectares. | Named digitized titling and gendered record signal. | Possession, use, credit, productive investment, disputes, land-market pressure or household control. |
| IM4 | World Bank’s Mozambique impact evaluations assess land-rights formalization effects on women’s empowerment, agricultural productivity, investment and household income. | Evaluation design linking rights to economic outcomes. | Publicly summarized person-level causal result, enforcement, inheritance, safety or climate resilience. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Land rights are mediated by customary leaders, marriage, widowhood, kinship, inheritance, caste/class, ethnicity, migration, conflict and state records. Digitization can strengthen proof or create exclusion through cost, language, connectivity and data errors. Climate and conservation projects can increase land value and contestation, making women’s documented rights vulnerable if enforcement and voice are weak.

## Counterexample and source boundary

Titles and registration are intermediate evidence. The Philippines female-name statistic does not prove possession or control; joint ownership can conceal household bargaining asymmetry; Ethiopia’s registered landholdings and jobs do not prove women’s income or ecological outcomes. Public evidence closes baseline, documentation, digitization, evaluation design, restoration/employment and legal/customary frameworks, but not a comparable woman-level chain through control, finance, adaptation, safety and intergenerational security.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A land law, cadastral record, electronic title, joint ownership, inheritance reform, parcel count, climate-smart project or women’s job statistic is not by itself secure control, credit, bargaining power, adaptation investment, income, safety or durable land security.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **women-land-rights-to-controlled-climate-resilient-livelihood-and-security**. The strongest next acquisition is a named woman/cohort/land parcel chain linking legal/customary status, title, possession, control, inheritance, dispute, credit/input/extension, climate investment, production, income, food/housing, bargaining power and future security.

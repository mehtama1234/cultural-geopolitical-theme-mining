# Rotation KW — Horizon scanning, system-level infrastructures and anticipatory governance

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When scientific and technological change becomes system-level infrastructure, how can horizon scanning translate weak signals and risk perceptions into proportionate preparedness, public accountability, equity and coordinated action without pretending to forecast the future?

## Why this packet matters

The United Nations' 2026 horizon-scanning work is useful as a meta-source for
the atlas: it connects AI, data systems, biotechnology, energy and environmental
science to peace, security, development and human rights, while explicitly
describing horizon scanning as strategic awareness and anticipatory governance,
not prediction. The companion Global Risk Report adds a geographically broad
perception layer, but perception, expert synthesis and institutional priority are
still several steps before a named person, place or service experiences a change.

```text
weak signal / expert perception / system capability
  -> horizon scan and risk framing
  -> institutional priority, coordination and preparedness
  -> funded intervention, local authority and operational action
  -> named population/place outcome, correction, accountability and learning
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Horizon scanning is not culturally neutral. The experts and institutions that
define a risk, choose a time horizon and name a response can privilege formal
state, scientific or corporate visibility over local memory, Indigenous
knowledge, informal adaptation and everyday service failure. A global survey can
make risks comparable in language while the lived meaning of the same risk
differs across a data-centre region, an island, a conflict border, a rural clinic
or a low-resource university.

The geopolitical value is the shift from isolated tools to infrastructures and
power systems. AI, data, biotechnology, energy and environmental science become
embedded in security, development and human-rights decisions. That makes
coordination and norm-setting important, but it also creates a failure mode in
which a priority list substitutes for local authority, funding or remedy.

The counterexample is important: a high-salience risk may not materialize in a
particular place, while a low-salience operational failure may produce immediate
harm. Conversely, a country may report strong preparedness capacity while a
language group, border region or informal service user remains unreachable.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A survey percentage, expert network, horizon list, risk ranking, scenario,
strategic-priority statement or coordination recommendation does not prove an
event, probability, local exposure, public consent, funded implementation,
service continuity, equitable distribution or avoided loss. The UN Global Risk
Report is perception evidence; the Horizon Scanning report is anticipatory
governance evidence. Neither substitutes for a named facility-to-household,
person-to-service or place-to-remedy chain.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Horizon scanning is a useful cross-domain discovery
and coordination mechanism, especially as technology shifts from discrete tools
to system-level infrastructure, but it is not a forecast or outcome measure.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **horizon-signal-to-anticipatory-governance-local-capability-and-accountable-outcome**. The next acquisition should join one signal or risk priority to the decision owner, budget, local knowledge, implementation, named affected population/place, preparedness action, avoided or realized loss, correction and learning, with a low-salience or no-action comparator.

# Rotation S: school attendance, place and belonging

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** K–12 education, attendance, facilities, transportation, disability, poverty and school belonging  
**Status:** Tier 2 mechanism; attendance measurement and facility constraints are observable, causes, belonging and later learning remain open

## Research question

When a school is formally available, what converts enrollment and school
programs into regular attendance, a safe and belonging-producing encounter,
learning, and later opportunity across different places and households?

```text
enrollment and school offer
  -> transport, housing, health, safety and family capacity
  -> building, schedule and relational welcome
  -> daily attendance and participation
  -> learning, belonging and voice
  -> graduation, work and civic opportunity
```

## Named-case refresh

These cases are deliberately heterogeneous. They show transport, health and
community conditions that can support attendance, but they do not turn an
attendance improvement or school intervention into proof of learning,
belonging or later opportunity.

### SCH-006 — Communities In Schools student-support model

The federal Institute of Education Sciences describes Communities In Schools as
a model that places coordinators in schools to connect students with integrated
supports, including relationships, basic needs and community resources. The
model is useful for the S chain because it treats attendance as connected to
conditions around the student rather than as a stand-alone compliance problem.
The evidence base remains implementation- and context-sensitive; a coordinator
or referral is not proof that the same student received adequate support,
attended regularly or felt belonging.

### SCH-007 — Denver Public Schools, Success Express transportation

Denver Public Schools' Success Express program provides free shuttle routes in
areas where students face transportation barriers, including routes designed
to connect neighborhoods with schools. This is a named place intervention that
tests transport as a practical attendance condition, not merely a district
transportation budget. Service availability does not establish that a student
could reach the stop, board safely, use the schedule, attend consistently or
learn more as a result.

### SCH-008 — New York City community schools strategy

The New York City Community Schools model combines schools with health,
mental-health, family and community partnerships, including on-site or linked
services. It makes the school a local service ecology and recognizes that
attendance can be affected by health, family needs and trust. The city-level
program description documents infrastructure and partnerships, not a same-
student chain from service receipt to attendance, belonging or learning.

### SCH-009 — Alaska Native/American Indian education and cultural connection

The U.S. Department of Education's Native American and Alaska Native student
resources emphasize Tribal consultation, culturally responsive education and
family/community engagement as parts of educational access. This is a cultural
counterweight to treating attendance as a universal behavioral metric: a school
can be physically reachable yet fail to recognize language, identity, history
or community authority. The federal resource framework does not by itself show
which local schools changed practice or whether students' attendance and
belonging improved.

### SCH-010 — school closure and alternative-delivery non-closure

GAO's facilities evidence shows that building problems led about one in five
districts to cancel school in 2024–25, affecting an estimated 2 million
students. A closure may protect safety, and an alternative site or remote
arrangement may preserve some contact, but neither is equivalent to a normal
school day. This is an explicit non-closure case: enrollment, a reopening,
remote access or attendance reporting does not establish safe transport,
learning continuity, peer belonging, disability support or family feasibility.

## New trend interpretation

The sharper trend is not simply “students are absent” or “attendance improved.”
It is:

> School access is a daily place-based conversion: enrollment becomes opportunity
> only when a student can reach, enter, remain in and belong in the school often
> enough to participate and learn.

This interpretation keeps attendance from becoming a moral label. A missed day
can encode transportation failure, housing instability, disability, illness,
care work, fear, exclusion, school closure or a deliberate family choice. The
administrative metric is useful, but it is not the explanation.

## Cultural and geographical meaning

School is a physical and cultural institution. A building closure can disrupt
care schedules, transport, meals, peer relationships and local identity. A
school may be formally integrated into accountability systems while students
experience language barriers, disability exclusion, discipline, unsafe climate
or a lack of recognition.

Place matters through district resources, rural distance, neighborhood safety,
housing instability, weather, facility quality and the availability of alternate
schools or services. Belonging is not inferred from enrollment; it must be
measured through student experience, relationships, voice and whether the
institution responds to the barriers students carry.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Chronic absenteeism is a threshold measure, not a causal explanation or a
  complete measure of participation quality.
- Attendance can improve while learning, belonging or safety do not improve.
- A school closure may be temporary and necessary for safety; its attendance
  effect is not equivalent to household disengagement.
- Facility problems do not determine every student’s experience; schools and
  communities may adapt through alternative sites or remote arrangements.
- Subgroup differences identify distributional patterns, not the mechanism for a
  specific student or district.
- Intervention evidence does not prove that a particular attendance strategy
  works in every rural, urban, Tribal, multilingual or disability context.

## Promotion decision

Promote as a Tier 2 mechanism candidate:

**School access is daily, reachable and belonging-producing participation—not
enrollment, an attendance percentage, a program offer or a school building.**

The next decisive test is a named district/school ledger linking facility and
transport conditions, student subgroup, absence reason, attendance response,
student belonging, learning measure and later educational transition.

## Executive readout

The atlas is now best understood as four connected layers rather than one
question:

1. **Daily life:** households protect food, housing, health, children, and
   dignity by moving costs into debt, delay, unpaid care, family help, lower
   quality, or lost time.
2. **Power and access:** firms, platforms, employers, and agencies determine
   whether people can understand a decision, challenge it, receive a remedy,
   switch, or leave.
3. **Meaning and collective response:** the same material condition can become
   blame, distrust, belonging, organizing, voting, silence, or exit depending
   on identity, prior treatment, and available alternatives.
4. **System and state capacity:** infrastructure, public programs, industrial
   policy, and alliances can gain formal capacity before users experience
   reliable service, security, consent, or strategic leverage.

The strongest overall finding is therefore not “everything is getting worse.”
It is that **formal capacity and lived power are repeatedly separating**. The
project can often observe a rule, contract, facility, benefit, refund, or
institutional response. It is much harder to observe whether the same person,
worker, household, customer, community, or state received something usable,
adequate, durable, and controllable.

The cultural and political version of this finding is equally important:
identity can be deeply meaningful without guaranteeing opportunity, and
financial strain can reduce political efficacy without eliminating the desire
to act. People move toward action when they can see a credible route from their
experience to influence; without that route, the same pressure may become
withdrawal, non-use, or quiet adaptation.

Two newer findings sharpen that picture. Census pulse data show that severe
expense difficulty is associated with much more loneliness and low perceived
support, even though phone or video contact is not higher. Being reachable is
not the same as having dependable help. A separate AI-user study shows that
people may use several assistants for different tasks while still depending on
one dominant default. Multiple choices inside a system are not automatically a
real way out of it.

A linked Census screen adds a time dimension: some respondents moved out of
expense difficulty while others stayed in it, and confidence in Congress did
not move in lockstep. Material recovery and institutional confidence are
separate outcomes, not one measure of whether life has improved.

The next serious research target is the missing middle: follow one dated unit
from exposure through alternative or non-use, adaptation, response, actual
receipt or operation, later durability, and then meaning, trust, action, or
exit. Aggregate endpoints remain useful context, but they should not be used
as substitutes for that chain.

One concrete example is the Amazon Prime remedy. By September 2026, the FTC's
public record showed about $845 million issued but about $235 million accepted.
That does not tell us the recovery rate: the figures cover different payment
stages and do not show which customers received what, when, or whether the
money repaired the original loss. In plain language, **money sent is not the
same as money received, and money received is not automatically restored
security or trust**.

## A cross-theme lens: the interface

The recurring failure point is often the interface between a formal system and
a real person: an appointment, notice, app, appeal, payment, repair visit,
accessible device, or public meeting. Eligibility or capacity can exist while
the person lacks the time, mobility, information, privacy, money, language
access, or fallback needed to use it. See [The interface is where formal
access becomes lived power](US-BROAD-INTERFACE-ACCESS-SYNTHESIS_V1.md).

## The 14-theme status map

| Theme | What the evidence currently shows | What remains open |

| Consumer power and recourse | Complaints, refunds, repairs, and redress become visible before receipt, adequacy, account correction, or switching. | Did the same customer actually recover and regain a credible alternative? |

| Political judgment and collective action | Material pressure, trust, identity, civic participation, and voting show heterogeneous rather than one-way conversion. | Did a specific burden or institutional encounter change later action or policy response? |
| Firm, sector, and market power | Firm size, entry/exit, payment design, technology, and ownership change the option set for workers, customers, and places. | Did a firm decision change price, access, quality, worker power, or practical exit for the same unit? |
| Infrastructure, technology, and dependency | Construction, procurement, security authority, and capacity can advance before reliable service, local benefit, or replaceability. | Was the capability accepted, operated, useful, replaceable, and experienced by affected users? |
| Geopolitical and state consequences | Authority, public legitimacy, alliance response, capability, and external leverage are distinct stages. | Did a dated capability or state action change another actor's behavior or bargaining choice? |

This table is a navigation map, not a claim that the themes share one sample or
one causal chain. The repeated pattern is the gap between formal availability
and usable, durable power.

The newest cultural layer is adolescent digital life: teen social-platform use
and chatbot use are becoming ordinary parts of peer connection and schoolwork,
but frequency is not the same as dependence, trust, privacy loss, or inability
to leave. The teen route should remain separate from adult AI adoption and
smartphone self-regulation until a longitudinal or matched record shows the
actual time, school, family, and exit consequences.

The newest public-system layer adds a different kind of divergence: Hispanic
poverty reached a record low in 2025 while Medicaid coverage declined during
the return to standard eligibility and renewal processes. That does not mean
the same people experienced both changes. It means income position and public
coverage are separate clocks, and a headline improvement in one should not be
treated as complete security.

The income-distribution layer adds another caution: the national median hit a
record while the top rose and the bottom did not move significantly. In simple
terms, “the middle got better” does not tell us whether the people at the
bottom gained room, whether prices absorbed it, or whether the gap widened.

The care layer shows why family structure belongs in the atlas: living with
parents, sharing childcare, and caring for adults can protect people and create
belonging, but can also transfer costs into unpaid time, gender roles, housing,
and paid work. Care arrangements are adaptations, not automatic evidence of
either family decline or family security.

The community layer adds a subtle but important distinction: people may be
very willing to help neighbors while being less confident that neighbors would
help them. Goodwill is not the same as reliable protection. The atlas therefore
keeps willingness, expected support, delivered aid, adequacy, and later trust
as separate stages.

## Identity and belonging are not one clock

[Religious affiliation](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/pew-2025-religious-affiliation-switching-cultural-identity-layer-v1.md) can change without cultural or family identification
disappearing. [Migration](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/migration-place-cultural-conversion-synthesis-v1.md) can add workers, customers, and local demand while
belonging, language access, safety, and political interpretation remain
contested. [“Working class” identity](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/pew-2026-working-class-identity-status-meaning-layer-v1.md) can function as a dignity and solidarity identity
without mapping neatly onto income. [Muslim representation and institutional
capacity](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/pew-2026-muslim-americans-discrimination-capacity-visibility-layer-v1.md) can grow while reported discrimination and unfair media treatment
remain high. A person can therefore experience progress, pride, exclusion, and
political distance at the same time.

The newer Hispanic identity and youth-financial-pressure records sharpen this
further. Identity can be personally central and collectively connective while
still being experienced as helping, hurting, or making no difference to getting
ahead. Financial strain can lower the feeling that voting matters while also
making action more likely when people believe their voice has force and leaders
will follow through. Belonging and political participation therefore depend on
opportunity, treatment, efficacy, and credible response—not identity or
hardship alone.

The atlas treats identity as an active part of the end-to-end chain—not as a
demographic label added after the material analysis. The key questions are
whether a place or institution feels usable and fair, who is considered a
legitimate member, whether identity changes the attribution of blame or credit,
and whether belonging becomes participation, non-use, organizing, voting, or
exit. These links are supported by separate surveys and place records, not one
pooled identity-to-action estimate.

## What this project is trying to understand

The project follows pressure through real life:

```text
something changes
  -> people face a cost, risk, delay, or loss of control
  -> they search, adapt, pay, borrow, complain, stay, switch, or leave
  -> an employer, firm, agency, court, or government responds
  -> we ask whether the person actually got money, time, safety, access,
     dignity, security, trust, or a workable alternative back
```

The final question is not just whether a policy, product, settlement, or
technology exists. It is whether it changes what a person can actually do.

## The cross-theme conversion model

Across the 14 themes, the same analytical path keeps reappearing. The nouns
change—bill, appointment, benefit, algorithm, job, facility, alliance—but the
questions are structurally similar:

```text
exposure or change
  -> practical route, alternative, or non-use
  -> adaptation and money/time/status trade-off
  -> institutional, firm, or state response
  -> usable result: receipt, access, safety, income, capacity, or choice
  -> adequacy and durability
  -> meaning, trust, collective action, switching, or exit
```

This is a research model, not a claim that every theme has all seven stages in
one dataset. Different themes emphasize different units:

- household and care themes ask who absorbs the cost in money, time, health,
  work, or unpaid support;
- consumer, platform, and work themes ask whether a person can contest,
  refuse, switch, or leave;
- public-system and place themes ask whether formal eligibility becomes usable
  service, continuity, and security;
- infrastructure and geopolitical themes ask whether a commitment becomes
  accepted, operating, replaceable capability that changes another actor's
  choices.

The recurring empirical bottleneck is between response and usable result. A
rule, order, payment, facility, or capability can be real while the affected
unit still lacks receipt, adequacy, continuity, an alternative, or control.
That is why the atlas records open stages instead of filling them with
inferences from nearby aggregate evidence.

## What the whole atlas is finding

Across the separate themes, five recurring patterns are now visible:

1. **Pressure stacks.** A household may face a bill, care shortage, work
   constraint, debt, and food or housing risk at the same time. One average
   cost rarely describes the real trade-off.
2. **People adapt before they recover.** They search, borrow, delay, reduce
   use, switch, rely on family, or keep using a flawed service. Those actions
   show effort and constraint, not necessarily freedom or improvement.
3. **Formal repair is an intermediate stage.** An agency, court, employer,
   insurer, or platform can order, issue, report, or offer a remedy before the
   affected person receives something usable, sufficient, durable, and trusted.
4. **Capability is not usability or power.** A facility, benefit, technology,
   alliance response, or public program can exist and even operate while the
   relevant household, worker, customer, community, or state still lacks a
   workable alternative.
5. **Meaning and action come later—and differ by person.** The same pressure
   can become distrust, belonging, silence, organizing, switching, voting, or
   exit depending on identity, status, prior experience, and available routes.
6. **Institutions spread through routes, not just through rules.** One benefit
   can make another easier to claim, and coverage for one adult can spill over
   to a child. But the spillover may fade, and easier enrollment can change
   who is reached without proving better food, health, or household security.
   The route and its distributional effect are part of the policy outcome.

These are recurring structures across the evidence, not one pooled causal
model. The atlas keeps the source units separate so the patterns guide the
next test without pretending that a SIPP respondent, a worker case, a
consumer complaint, and a defense project are the same observation.

## Three simple clocks

The project now tracks three kinds of “did it really happen?” clocks:

- **Personal remedy:** offered or ordered → received → usable → enough →
  durable.
- **Infrastructure:** approved → built or energized → reliable → useful to
  households and firms → accepted and replaceable.
- **State capability:** agreed or scheduled → shipped → accepted → trained and
  maintained → operationally useful and able to change another actor's choice.

The [formal-access/control synthesis](US-BROAD-FORMAL-ACCESS-CONTROL-CONVERSION-SYNTHESIS_V1.md)
adds the simplest version of the central lesson: having a benefit, coverage,
refund, or remedy on paper is not the same as controlling it in real life.
People may still need to notice the problem, file a claim, appeal a denial,
monitor an account, or wait through a dangerous gap.

The early step is usually easy to find in public records. The later step is
where lived consequences appear. That is why a refund, a new power line, or a
defense contract should not be treated as the same thing as recovery, reliable
service, or strategic power.

## The biggest pattern so far

Formal action is common. Lived recovery is much harder to observe.

The worker-remedy evidence makes the reachability problem concrete. A provider
can audit its payroll, report millions in back-pay, replace the time system,
and create worker-voice channels while some workers remain unlocated. Firm
repair is therefore not the same thing as every worker receiving usable money.

We can often see:

- a benefit being awarded;
- a refund being issued;
- a worker being reinstated or awarded back pay;
- a product repair being offered;
- a complaint being routed or closed;
- a defense system being ordered or scheduled;
- a new facility, rule, or technical capability being approved.

But the next questions are often missing:

- Did the person receive the money or service?
- Was it enough?
- Did the original account, job, bill, health problem, or safety risk get fixed?
- Could the person use a real alternative?
- Did the remedy last?
- Did trust, switching, political action, or exit change afterward?

## What the cross-theme evidence is showing

### Material pressure creates navigation work

People under expense pressure search across jobs, food, benefits, credit,
housing, and medical help. Searching is real effort, but it is not proof that
the search produced a usable option or recovery.

The same linked HTOPS respondents also show that pressure does not create one
simple political reaction. Congress confidence is nearly flat, while
confidence in the presidency, military, police, Supreme Court, public schools,
and statistical agencies is lower in the pressured group. That is a selective
institution pattern, not proof that hardship caused a general loss of trust.
Other HTOPS waves in the workspace cannot extend this panel: their respondent
IDs do not overlap, so they remain separate snapshots.

Family help, cheaper purchases, delayed spending, borrowing, and public-benefit
participation can all buffer a shock without creating stable security. The
route matters: interviews, offices, work rules, recertification, and claim
effort can change participation without proving employment, food, energy, care,
or financial recovery.

### The latest broad rotation

The newest reader additions keep the project broad. They connect health-cost
pressure to household adaptation and contested legitimacy; worker remedies to
reported receipt, formal completion, and the limits of recovery; consumer
redress to repeat eligibility and reachability; and domestic GaN production to
the difference between a strategic manufacturing route and delivered,
qualified, independent capability. Together these findings reinforce the
atlas's central pattern without turning health, work, consumer, and
geopolitical records into one population or one causal chain.

### Twelve mechanisms sharpened by the latest rotation

The latest cross-theme work adds twelve simple ideas:

- **Digital exit can be contested before account exit.** An AI companion can
  answer a user's goodbye in a way that keeps the conversation going. That is
  evidence of a retention moment, not proof that the user could not delete the
  account or suffered lasting harm. See the [exit-boundary audit](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/ai-companion-exit-boundary-audit-v1.md).
- **Infrastructure bargains can become fiscal bargains.** Virginia is using
  large-load rules, operator registration, monthly tax remittance, and a late-
  payment penalty to decide who should carry infrastructure costs. The state
  has made the administrative route auditable, but that still does not tell us
  what a particular facility paid, what a locality received, or what a
  household paid or received. See the [large-load audit](projects/ai-work-control/virginia-large-load-household-incidence-audit-v1.md).
- **Security posture can continue while legitimacy is disputed.** NATO's
  Eastern Sentry posture continued after the original airspace incident while
  later information operations contested trust and attribution. Persistence is
  not the same as deterrence or public consent. See the [Eastern Sentry audit](projects/ai-work-control/poland-eastern-sentry-persistence-legitimacy-audit-v1.md).
- **Recovery has different clocks.** Disaster relief, mortgage support, home
  repair, and local jobs can move at different speeds. Money awarded is not the
  same as a repaired home or a stable return. See the [California recovery audit](projects/us-repeat-energy-crises/california-wildfire-recovery-three-clocks-audit-v1.md).
- **Administrative completion is not the same as underlying control.** Walmart
  reports privacy requests completed, while the New York transmission route has
  a settlement, FERC process, and refund-effective date. Neither public record
  independently proves the person's data was fully removed or that a household
  received a bill credit. See the [Walmart privacy finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-128.md)
  and [transmission-refund finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-124.md).
- **Resolution is not recovery.** A later employment-status improvement after
  a prescription event does not show good work, treatment continuity, debt
  relief, or household security. See the [MEPS prescription follow-up](projects/us-household-constraint-cascade/meps-prescription-event-six-month-followup-v1.md).
- **Digital safety recourse is becoming enforceable.** The TAKE IT DOWN Act
  requires covered platforms to provide a removal route for nonconsensual
  intimate images, act within 48 hours after a valid request, and gives people
  an FTC escalation path. That creates formal contestability, not proof that a
  specific image was removed, that copies did not return, or that safety and
  wellbeing were restored. See the [platform-removal record](records/us-ftc-take-it-down-act-platform-removal-2026.json).
- **Digital control has three stages.** A person can experience an interaction,
  receive a formal privacy or removal route, and still lack verified practical
  control. The GM/OnStar order adds data-copy, deletion, opt-out, and location
  controls, but actual completion, residual collection, service trade-offs,
  switching, and trust remain unknown. See the [GM/OnStar record](records/us-ftc-gm-onstar-data-control-order-2026.json).
- **Buffering is not security.** Family help, consumption cuts, borrowing, and
  safety-net participation can keep a household functioning while leaving
  food, energy, care, work, and financial room unstable. See the [income timing
  and family-support record](records/us-federal-reserve-income-volatility-support-2025.json),
  [expense-difficulty follow-up](records/us-htops-expense-difficulty-cross-lagged-2025.json),
  and [SNAP administrative-access record](records/us-snap-administrative-access-2026.json).
- **Formal denial can conceal recoverable access.** In Medicare Advantage
  skilled-nursing-facility admissions, 95% of appealed denials in the HHS-OIG
  review were overturned. That does not make the initial denial harmless: the
  patient or facility still had to recognize the decision, appeal, wait, and
  carry the burden of proving access. A system can therefore be technically
  appealable while still rationing access through the first decision gate. See
  the [SNF prior-authorization record](records/us-hhs-oig-medicare-snf-prior-authorization-2026.json).
- **The same AI exposure can mean productivity to a firm and replaceability to
  a worker.** Gallup's latest workforce evidence links frequent AI use with
  greater displacement anxiety, while manager respect and organizational care
  narrow that association. Technology therefore enters the atlas through
  governance and workplace meaning, not only through adoption or output. See
  the [AI work and manager-support record](records/us-gallup-ai-work-manager-support-displacement-2026.json).
- **The interface can fail before a person reaches a program.** GAO found that
  all nine reviewed federal eligibility data sources had quality problems and
  seven had inconsistencies with other sources. A government can possess many
  records while still lacking the identifiers, definitions, and validation
  needed to match and correct the same case. See the [federal eligibility-data
  interoperability record](records/us-gao-federal-eligibility-data-interoperability-2026.json).
- **Security capability can depend on local institutional interfaces.** NATO's
  KFOR account shows posture adjustment and integration of Kosovo Police into
  the mission's security architecture, with coordination across multiple
  local and regional actors. That is an operating and relationship stage, not
  proof of public consent, reduced incidents, or changed external behavior.
  See the [KFOR posture and local-security integration record](records/us-nato-kfor-posture-local-security-integration-2026.json).

### Access is not the same as usable access

A provider, benefit, workplace, app, training program, or job can technically
be available while language, disability, transportation, paperwork, digital
design, or permission blocks actual use.

A public safety net can also grow while the experience still fails at the
claim or payment stage. California's FAIR Plan is getting bigger, but the
state has also documented denial-related violations in reviewed smoke-damage
claims. So “covered” is not the same as “repaired” or “made secure.”

The Ohio care-access pass shows the same conversion problem in a more concrete
way. HPSA rows can be tied to the same HRSA organization as active service-site
records, and those sites can list weekly hours, while schedule/calendar fields
remain incomplete and appointment acceptance, wait, insurance, and completed
use remain unobserved. A named provider with listed hours is therefore a
stronger access signal than a county shortage label, but it is still not usable
care or recovery. See the [Ohio identifier crosswalk](projects/us-local-business-place/hrsa-ohio-hpsa-site-identifier-crosswalk-v1.md)
and [operating-fields audit](projects/us-local-business-place/hrsa-ohio-hpsa-site-operating-fields-audit-v1.md).
The named-site follow-up now reaches North Lima Community Health Center under
the same HPWO organization ID and records HPWO's organization-level new-patient,
same-day, insurance, and sliding-fee route. Appointment availability, wait,
completed care, and recovery remain open.

Childcare shows the same problem through work and time. Among households that
reported a childcare problem, estimated converted work loss was about 114.6
days below the poverty line versus 43.3 days above 400% of poverty. Families
used different adaptations—leave, fewer hours, quitting, not searching, or
watching children while working. Provider incentives can also coexist with
long waitlists. So funding or a worker program is not yet a dependable slot,
preserved parent work, or recovered household room. See the [Census
childcare/work finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-001.md)
and [GAO childcare-capacity finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-005.md).

The public SIPP bridge adds a more specific same-person comparison. Among
November-to-December stable-SNAP records, paid child-care use was lower than in
the stable-no-SNAP comparison group (23.9% versus 31.0%), while reported
child-care assistance was higher (14.6% versus 5.2%) and the share saying care
arrangements prevented work or more work was higher (7.4% versus 3.4%). This
does not mean SNAP caused the difference: the fields use different reference
periods and the groups differ in need, work, income, and household composition.
It does show why participation, care assistance, paid care, and protected work
time must be measured separately.

Unpaid eldercare reveals the wider social version of this problem. About 38.2
million people provided unpaid eldercare in 2023–24; employed caregivers spent
about 2.84 hours on care days. That care is functioning like infrastructure,
but the public record usually does not show whose work hours, sleep, earnings,
health, or housing flexibility absorb it. A family member filling a missing
care slot protects someone today while potentially shifting the cost into the
future. See the [aging, care, and social-capacity bridge](projects/us-aging-care-strain/aging-care-system-capacity-bridge-v1.md).

### The first inequality can happen before the visible outcome

A program or tool can be officially available while people reach it through
different gates. Disability-workforce participants may face interpreter,
technology, or training barriers before employment is measured. At work, AI
use is more common among workers with graduate education and greater task
control, while firms report adoption and future expectations separately. This
does not prove discrimination or that control caused the difference. It does
show why the atlas asks who is allowed to participate, who gets training, who
can disclose or contest a decision, and who controls the task before comparing
employment, productivity, pay, or security.

The NYC automated-hiring complaint record adds a concrete contestability gate:
after a complaint entered the system, the agency asked for the suspected-
violation date, employer and job details, and supporting documents by a
deadline. A formal right to complain therefore still depends on the person's
ability to identify the decision, gather proof, and complete the route. The
record does not show whether that happened or whether the hiring decision was
changed.

### Recovery happens in different currencies

Money can be returned while a debt remains. A job can be restored on paper while
trust is gone. A route can be approved while household benefits, compensation,
or acceptance remain unknown. A refund can arrive while the original account
problem continues.

The Amazon Flex case makes the payment distinction concrete. The FTC reports
that 130,952 drivers had received full compensation and that 2,806 Zelle
reissue payments succeeded. That is strong evidence that the administrator
reached many workers. It still does not tell us what any one driver received,
whether it covered the loss, or whether work, household security, trust, or
the decision to stay on or leave the platform changed.

Care works the same way. A family member may keep a child, older adult, or
disabled person safe when a public or paid route is unavailable, but the family
may pay through housing, travel, work time, money, and emotional labor. Medicaid
coverage or assisted-living spending shows that a financing route exists; it
does not prove a suitable place, completed service, or that the family burden
has actually fallen.

### Control can improve while burden shifts

The broad evidence is not saying that institutions only fail. The randomized
Cash and Counseling demonstrations show a real short-run improvement: giving
Medicaid beneficiaries more control over care timing, hiring, and permitted
purchases was associated with greater service satisfaction, fewer unmet
personal-care needs, and more paid-care access. But the same design can move
hiring, payroll, scheduling, backup-care, and continuity work into families
and providers. Likewise, a bank account can exist while fraud recovery and
switching remain difficult, and a clinic can be active while an appointment is
unavailable. The recurring question is not simply “did control increase?” It
is “who gained control, what new work or risk came with it, and was the
protected outcome durable?”

The MEPS health-event route shows why recovery cannot be reduced to one later
status. After a prescription event, the event group had more employment-status
movement and more nonemployment entry and resolution than the comparison group,
while health-worsening was not higher in the same descriptive screen. A person
can therefore have a changing work situation while health moves differently;
the later observation is not automatically harm, recovery, or treatment success.

### Exit is not always freedom

Some people leave because they found a better option. Others leave because a
job, service, or institution became unusable. The atlas keeps voluntary choice,
forced exit, non-use, and constrained dependence separate.

### Financial access is not the same as financial room

Having an account or contacting a provider does not mean the household has
liquidity, a successful dispute, or a safe alternative. In the 2025 SHED
fraud subgroup, people reported loss, partial recovery, provider contact,
outside reporting, and switching as separate steps. A switch can be a real
protective action, but it can also carry time, error, continuity, and trust
costs.

The Federal Reserve household-finance layer adds a wider version of the same
problem. People respond to pressure by buying cheaper goods, using less,
delaying purchases, saving less, borrowing, working more, or accepting outside
help. Those choices can protect today's bill while sacrificing future room,
care time, rest, quality, or independence. A reported surplus or a payment
route is therefore not proof that a household can absorb the next shock.

### Meaning changes the response

The same material condition can produce blame, resignation, solidarity, trust,
anger, or political action. Approval, trust, contact, voting, complaint, and
switching are different outcomes; they cannot be collapsed into one “response.”

The cultural layer shows why. A smartphone can mean connection and useful
information to one person, but lost sleep and loss of control to another.
Religious affiliation can be formal identity, family culture, spiritual
practice, or political signal. Diversity can be welcomed in the abstract while
workplace rules and fairness judgments remain contested. The atlas records
those meanings separately; it does not infer a vote, purchase, protest, or exit
from an attitude alone.

Information legitimacy has the same structure. People can value press freedom
as a democratic principle while also supporting limits meant to prevent
inaccurate reporting. The unresolved cultural question is who gets to define
accuracy, correct errors, moderate distribution, and impose limits. Source
choice, perceived fairness, correction, trust, and later action must therefore
remain separate stages. See the [press-freedom limits and public-information
norms layer](projects/us-digital-habits-attention/pew-2026-press-freedom-limits-norms-layer-v1.md).

Pressure can also reduce action without creating one predictable political
belief. In the retained CCES panel, earlier job loss was followed by less
local political action (23.93% versus 31.57%) but slightly higher favorable
Congress approval (13.86% versus 10.06%). Hardship may reduce the capacity to
contact or organize while approval still reflects identity, attribution, or
other meanings.

That is why the atlas separates civic capacity from institutional judgment.
Someone may have a political opinion but lack the time, money, confidence, or
route needed to contact, organize, vote, complain, or switch. Lower visible
action is not automatically apathy or agreement.

### Non-use can be protective behavior

Avoiding a benefit, clinic, workplace, police contact, community event, fraud
report, or digital service can be a practical response to perceived surveillance,
stigma, retaliation, paperwork, or low expected value. Non-use is not
automatically apathy, satisfaction, or free exit. It may protect immediate
control while sacrificing care, income, benefits, belonging, or future recourse.

### Data governance can become part of access

The SNAP data dispute shows that a benefit system is also a data system. Who
can inspect applicant records, why they can inspect them, and whether people
trust the safeguards may affect application, continued participation, or
correction before a benefit decision is recorded. Formal encryption, access
rules, court action, or public comments do not prove that an applicant got the
right benefit on time—or that people felt safe enough to use the program. The
next evidence would need a de-identified benefit episode linking disclosure or
data access to notice, decision, payment continuity, correction, privacy
concern, and later non-use or trust.

### Place conditions do not determine meaning by themselves

Population growth, foreign-born share, provider capacity, and firm presence set
the context, but they do not tell us whether residents feel welcome, threatened,
fairly treated, or able to use local systems. Local trust and civic action can
move differently. The atlas requires a resident encounter before assigning
cultural or political meaning to a place statistic.

### Modeled protection is not lived protection

Regulators can create tariffs, cost-allocation rules, minimum obligations, and
mitigation plans without the evidence yet showing actual household bills,
reliability, compensation, acceptance, or replaceability. Governance is an
important stage, not the same thing as restored lived security.

### Help has a size, not just a yes/no status

Public assistance counts can hide how much room the intervention actually
creates. Official FY2024 LIHEAP profiles reported an average heating benefit
of $1,102 in Texas and $327 in Ohio. That difference does not prove one state
worked better: bill size, fuel, timing, arrears, and household burden differ.
It does show that “served” is not the same as “made secure.”

### Capacity becomes power only when someone can use or refuse it

AI, infrastructure, defense systems, firms, and public programs can have
capacity without proving replaceability, local benefit, household security, or
geopolitical leverage. Power appears only when an affected actor can use,
inspect, refuse, switch, or impose a cost—and another actor responds.

The [Polish F-35 records](https://www.gov.pl/web/obrona-narodowa/samoloty-f-35-na-wyposazeniu-wojska-polskiego)
show aircraft arrival and formal acceptance, while a separate [training
record](https://www.wojsko-polskie.pl/dgrsz/articles/aktualnosci-w/pierwsi-polscy-piloci-f-35a-lightning-ii-ukonczyli-szkolenie/)
shows first-pilot training. Poland's [own IOC definition](https://www.gov.pl/web/obrona-narodowa/harpia-f-35-dla-polski)
additionally requires at least
eight aircraft in country, enough trained pilots and ground staff, and spare
parts and consumables. Possession and preparation are therefore real stages,
but they are not yet proof of fleet readiness, mission use, deterrence, or
changed behavior by another state.

## Concrete examples

- FTC repeat refunds show that later payments may be limited to people who were
  successfully reached or who cashed an earlier payment. That tells us about
  remedy reach, not full recovery.
- The current [FTC Amazon Flex payment route](https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/refunds-amazon-flex-drivers)
  shows an institution adapting after a reachability failure: eligible
  drivers who did not cash an earlier check are being offered a Zelle reissue.
  That closes an administrative follow-up stage, not the individual endpoint;
  the public record still does not show each driver's receipt, amount,
  adequacy, residual loss, or later work and household security.
- SHED fraud data show that contacting a financial provider, reporting the
  problem, recovering money, and changing providers are different endpoints;
  none alone proves restored financial security.
- CFPB Ombudsman inquiries show that a redress check may not be received,
  cashed, or easily reissued. A remedy can exist while access to the remedy
  remains difficult.
- The CFPB's Bilt intervention shows a middle stage between a formal order and
  a completed payment: the firm reached out, reimbursement requests were being
  reviewed for more than 500 newly identified customers, and the Bureau kept
  monitoring the process. That is stronger than an unimplemented plan, but it
  still does not establish each customer's payment, account correction,
  adequacy, or later financial security.
- New York insurance assistance shows the same gap at the housing boundary:
  the state reported thousands of complaints closed and more than $121 million
  recovered, but those administrative totals do not show whether a particular
  home was repaired, a claim was fully paid, coverage was restored, or the
  household could safely stay. A regulator can make recovery visible without
  making the household outcome visible.
- NYC worker settlements make the payment route more concrete: the city names
  eligible worker groups, restitution amounts, mail or deposit methods, update
  instructions, and scheduled installments. That is stronger than an order
  alone, but it still does not show whether each worker received the correct
  amount, whether a payment was returned or uncashed, or whether the remedy
  restored time, income, security, or trust.
- A worker case records forced exit, a new job, partial payment, a later
  compensation order, and broken trust. The order does not recreate the old
  job or prove durable security.
- The Meadley worker case shows the opposite currency split: compensation was
  reportedly paid, but reinstatement did not happen; court enforcement then
  produced further monetary relief and a penalty. Money can therefore arrive
  while the original work, status, and opportunity remain unrecovered.
- This is why the atlas does not count every departure as “practical exit.”
  Leaving after a breakdown can mean the person found a better option, but it
  can also mean the original option became unusable and the person absorbed the
  loss. Exit has to be read with alternatives, cost, replacement quality,
  payment, and later security.
- The [Cartisano worker case](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-cartisano-backpay-no-return-risk-assessment-event-ledger-v1.json)
  reports back pay and service continuity while the worker still could not
  return to the workplace; the tribunal later distinguished payment from
  real and practical reinstatement. Money and access moved on different
  tracks in the same case.
- The [Aitken worker case](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-aitken-return-backpay-later-dismissal-event-ledger-v1.json)
  records a union-supported return, back pay, and prior-service recognition,
  followed by continued work and then a later dismissal after a safety-policy
  conflict. An initial remedy was real, but it was not automatically durable
  security; later work, household recovery, trust, and exit remain unknown.
- A platform worker's account was voluntarily reactivated and later received
  formal access and gross lost-pay orders. That closes the institutional
  response stage, but not payment receipt, expenses, net recovery, continued
  work, an alternative platform, or free exit.
- A CPSC product recall records a first professional repair later described as
  ineffective, followed by a second repair offer. That proves a durability
  problem at the product level, not that every owner experienced failure or
  that the second repair restored safe use. Owner notice, appointment,
  completion, downtime, trust, and continued use remain open.
- Disability and care records show that coverage or capacity can fail at the
  interface where a person actually tries to use it.
- Child-care records show that eligibility, subsidy receipt, licensed care,
  usable placement, and protected work time are separate stages.
- SNAP records show that receipt can coexist with exposed rent and utility
  bills, with the remaining burden differing by work capacity and children in
  the household.
- Disaster records show that registration, eligibility, and reported aid can
  become visible before a survivor's repair, housing, or durable recovery is.
- Political-identity records show that financial worry does not enter every
  group through the same route; pooled worry-to-vote patterns can hide distinct
  within-identity pathways.
- Defense records show that domestic contract allocation can advance before
  delivered, trained, replaceable capability or geopolitical leverage.
- Platform-law records show that a formal worker protection can be enacted
  while injunctions and rulemaking leave the practical remedy paused.
- A temporary-visa worker case shows that reported full back-pay can repair a
  wage loss without proving restored safety, adequacy, or future choice.
- Malaysia's first GrabCar Tribunal hearing shows that reaching a remedy
  institution is an intermediate stage, not proof of an award, payment,
  restored access, or durable trust.
- An Australian platform case shows that a process defect can coexist with an
  accepted substantive safety reason; procedural failure does not automatically
  produce reversal or restored work.
- A same-person MEPS screen places acute health events before later health and
  employment outcomes, while showing that later status is not automatically
  remedy or recovery.
- A SHED panel shows that broad financial improvement does not end every
  adaptation; cheaper buying, reduced use, delayed purchases, and lost savings
  can persist on separate clocks.
- A job-loss panel shows that work disruption can coincide with lower later
  political action and a large insurance gap without producing one simple
  approval or hostility pattern.
- Consumer-redress records show that reissue and redistribution can continue
  after the first distribution without proving that the original harm was
  repaired for the same people.
- Insurance settlements show that restitution and process correction can span
  consumers, providers, and the state while coverage, care, and household
  recovery remain unknown.
- A repeat-refund record shows that prior receipt can support a later payment
  route without proving that the underlying debt or account harm was repaired.
- Latino mobility and identity records show that being better off than one's
  parents can coexist with feeling that the future or the American dream is
  blocked. Material progress does not settle belonging.
- AI companion exit experiments show that saying goodbye can itself trigger
  attempts to continue the interaction. Short-run engagement is not yet proof
  of durable dependence or harm.
- Migration and public-system records show that later participation can be
  selected by who feels safe enough to use the route in the first place.
- Poland’s defense record separates existing JASSM-ER family capability from
  the later 2024 order: prior family-level delivery is reported, but the new
  order’s shipment, acceptance, fielding, and external effect remain open.
- Amazon Flex refund records add an aggregate receipt stage, while an appeal
  stay in the Waheed case adds a conditional interim-payment obligation. Both
  are stronger than a promise, but neither proves individual receipt, restored
  work access, or durable recovery.
- The Cash App enforcement record shows that digital payment convenience can
  coexist with weak dispute control: operational changes and a large redress
  pool can be ordered before account-level correction or switching is visible.
- Los Angeles wildfire support shows a persistence clock: extended housing aid
  and mortgage bridges can preserve a recovery route while repair completion,
  permanent housing, and a household's stay/return choice remain unknown.
- Worker safety complaints and payroll questions can reveal public or firm-wide
  failures and trigger institutional correction, while the speaker still bears
  retaliation, documentation, reachability, or payment risk.
- The migrant wage-theft case shows how migration, tax, and employment
  dependence can shape the form of exploitation. Reported full back-pay repairs
  a formal loss without proving restored bargaining power, household security,
  or trust.
- DOL wage-recovery totals show institutional scale, while the Workers Owed
  Wages route still requires location, identity verification, claim completion,
  and payment access. Agency recovery is not automatically usable money.
- Disability workforce data show that job-center capacity, assistive technology,
  and monitoring can coexist with uneven accommodation, training, employment,
  and earnings outcomes. A program doorway is not a durable job opportunity.
- Assisted-living spending and Medicaid coverage can coexist with an unclear
  care setting because room and board, waivers, provider capacity, personal
  payment, and family coordination are separate parts of the care bundle.
- Brief AI-mediated outgroup contact can increase immediate openness or
  willingness to try real contact, while weak one-week persistence shows that
  civic rehearsal is not durable reconciliation.
- A common gasoline shock can produce different inflation and spending
  responses by income and place; aggregate prices do not show who reduced
  quantity, borrowed, substituted, or sacrificed another need.
- Financial marketing can impose application time or possible credit exposure
  before a consumer reaches a remedy; a later refund is not automatically a
  restored score, opportunity, or alternative.

### Capability and legitimacy can diverge

The newest geopolitical layer adds a different kind of gap. Pew found that
U.S. adults judged the country's contribution to global stability more
favorably than the median foreign public, while Republicans and Democrats in
the United States gave sharply different readings of the same role. A large
cross-party majority still agreed that the U.S. interferes abroad.

In simple terms: people can agree that a powerful country acts without
agreeing that its actions are peaceful, legitimate, respectful, or worth
supporting. That perception may matter for alliance trust, aid acceptance,
markets, culture, and domestic permission, but the survey does not prove any
of those downstream effects. Material capability, public legitimacy, foreign
reception, and changed external behavior must remain separate stages.

## What is still missing

The decisive missing bridge is a lawful same-unit record that joins:

1. a dated burden or decision;
2. the person's alternative, non-use, or adaptation;
3. money, time, access, status, health, or security consequences;
4. the institutional or firm response;
5. verified receipt or correction;
6. durability and later recovery; and
7. trust, action, switching, or exit where directly measured.

This is a depth limitation, not a reason to collapse the atlas back to one
theme. The required respondent files for the strongest next routes—UAS, PSID,
HRS, or staged MEPS—are not currently available locally or are
account-controlled. Meanwhile, the public record can continue to expand the
14-theme map, compare realization stages, identify cultural and political
meaning, and test firm, institutional, infrastructure, and geopolitical
responses. An authorized same-unit file would deepen one route; it would not
replace the broader atlas.

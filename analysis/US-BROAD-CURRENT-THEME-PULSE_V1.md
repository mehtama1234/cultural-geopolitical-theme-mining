# US broad current theme pulse v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Purpose:** a plain-language readout of the strongest recurring cultural,
social, political, consumer, institutional, firm, infrastructure, and
geopolitical patterns currently visible in the atlas.

This is a synthesis of separate source layers. It is not a pooled estimate and
does not say that the same people appear in every pattern.

**Current validated snapshot — 2026-09-24:** the plain-language reader has 367
ordered findings across the 14-theme map. The underlying program has 796
machine-readable records, 1,872 observations, and 163 explicitly open
middle-stage arrows. The finding count describes the communication layer; it
does not mean that the open same-unit recovery and leverage gaps are closed.

**Source-led social, cultural, and consumer rotation — 2026-09-24:** four
separate source families sharpen the current picture. BLS consumer-expenditure
data show budget composition and unequal quintile movement before a household
hardship episode is observed. Bank-fee and payment-outage studies show that a
nominal payment route depends on distributed liquidity, cash, credit, and
physical access—not simply on whether a card or app exists. Gallup's
anniversary survey shows that intended national meaning often runs through

Pew and NBER health-information sources show an upstream authority problem:
people use providers, peers, social media, AI, influencers, and podcasts while

completed payment is not recovery, an intended ritual is not shared political
belief, and information use or reported trust is not verified care or health.

**Six-finding rotation synthesis — 2026-09-20:** the latest additions now
span six different levels of the atlas: unpaid care changes household
adaptation even inside full-time work; formal representation supplies uneven
voice infrastructure; nonvoting separates time, health, administrative, and
meaning barriers; digital capability creates a national electricity burden
before local incidence is known; synthetic contact opens short-run
cross-partisan engagement without durable depolarization; and AI use, personal
control, and institutional confidence diverge across countries. Together they
sharpen the program's recurring rule: capability, participation, and trust are
not single outcomes, and each conversion must be tested at the unit and clock
where it actually occurs.

**Geopolitical response comparator — 2026-09-20:** the Poland airspace-

interception, Article 4 consultation, allied participation, Eastern Sentry,
diplomatic protest, and information-resilience action. It remains separate

The September 2026 Lithuania episode adds a later operational stage to that
lane: Lithuania reports NATO fighters identifying, intercepting, and destroying
a drone that entered its airspace, followed by an official Lithuanian-Italian
claim about alliance unity and the need for stronger European air defence. This
is a real protective action rather than an exercise account, but it still does

**Latest breadth rotation:** the new findings add seven distinct bridges. A
contingent ownership claim can change worker identification before it proves
wealth or control; institutional identity can filter an economic message
before it becomes trusted information; digital life separates social use,
chatbots, news, and civic attention; automatic saving can build future assets
while changing current borrowing; digital health can combine convenience,
privacy, billing, treatment continuity, and exit; a business shock can reach an
owner household without a one-for-one consumption collapse; and material
pressure can increase practical searching without proving that a usable option
was found; and automatic saving can shift retirement and benefit-claiming
timing without showing whether later work reflects choice or constraint. These
are complementary surfaces, not one pooled causal story. The payment-system
layer adds another hidden-price mechanism: a shared checkout price can carry
different reward and liquidity positions for different payment users, while an
account doorway can coexist with unequal substitution, fraud recovery, and
practical switching. The infrastructure layer adds the matching state-capacity
warning: import reliance, technical substitution, qualified output, and
strategic autonomy are separate stages. The geopolitical/state layer adds the
same distinction to defense policy: contracts and domestic supplier allocation
can precede delivery, training, maintenance, usable capability, and observed
external response. The care/place lane adds the same conversion boundary:
shortage designation, named site, listed hours, contact route, appointment,
completed care, and continuity are not interchangeable.
The latest digital and geopolitical additions sharpen the same rule from two
different directions: Walmart's privacy metrics expose request, verification,
completion, denial, and timing before individual data removal is independently
verified, while NATO's Vigorous Warrior exercise exposes multinational medical
coordination with civilian emergency institutions and a tested sequence from
injury through evacuation and continuity before real-crisis patient continuity
or public burden is known. Estonia described it as the first exercise of this
scale and type there since 2002. Administrative completion and operational
readiness are meaningful stages, but neither is yet lived recovery.
The household-credit lane adds a macro-to-lived-experience boundary: aggregate
credit growth, delinquency, survey hardship, and a hardship-linked balance
increase are related but not one borrower trajectory or causal story. The
utility layer adds that credit access is itself unequal: utility-payment
difficulty co-occurs with more balance carrying and less savings, while the
hardship group still shows more balance carrying at higher resource levels.
The national-accounts layer adds that rising aggregate income, consumption, or
saving cannot stand in for household liquidity, affordability, or consumer
choice.
The confidence layer adds that low unemployment and rising prices can coexist
with falling consumer sentiment, so public economic feeling is not a direct
readout of the labor aggregate.
The housing-insurance layer adds that complaint closure and reported recovery
can coexist with unknown repairability, coverage continuity, and displacement.
The place-security layer adds a related distinction: staying housed is not the
same as having secure shelter. Rent arrears, insurance gaps, premium pressure,
nonrenewal, hazard exposure, and a public residual-market backstop can all be
visible while safe repair, adequate coverage, feasible movement, and the
household's stay-or-move decision remain unknown.
The local-capacity layer adds a data-governance warning: operational fields can
be missing by designation type, so an incomplete shortage record is not proof
of zero staffing or zero care.
The firm-consumer layer adds that upstream price pressure can be absorbed,
delayed, or passed through; producer inflation is not automatically a household
price or welfare outcome.
The household-recovery layer adds that financial improvement can coexist with
persistent work, borrowing, and reduced-use adaptations; recovery is not one
clock.
The budget-margin layer adds that income timing, outside help, and money left
over are different forms of household room, with sharply different margins by
income level.
The financial-fallback layer adds a consumer-payment counterpart: bank fee
relief can reduce a targeted charge while later financial-room gains remain
concentrated among more liquid customers, and outage evidence shows that cash
can preserve transaction completion when digital payment fails. These are
different source settings, not one resilience rate; neither establishes whether
the purchase was essential, adequate, affordable, or recovery-producing.
The MEPS depth layer adds a measurement boundary: month-ordered health events
can be linked to later context without revealing the event-specific alternative,
household choice, remedy, or recovery.
The medical-payment layer adds a counterexample: recorded payment is not a
monotonic household-burden measure; zero payment can coexist with worse health
and less room, while higher payment can coexist with more debt.
The timing layer adds that before-, same-month, and after-event records are
different evidence states; “later” cannot be treated as recovery without a
dated causal bridge.
The acute-care layer adds a selection warning: people can be sicker before an
observed event, so later health or bill differences are not automatically event
effects.
The political-action lane adds a representation boundary: affordability can
be highly salient while party fit, blame, campaign contact, validated action,
and policy delivery remain divided or unobserved. The social-capacity lane adds
a reciprocity boundary: willingness to help can be higher than confidence that
help will be available, so goodwill is not yet delivered protection or recovery.
The Oregon Medicaid lottery adds a causal public-benefit-to-turnout counterexample:
coverage increased voting in one presidential election, with no detected
turnout persistence through the 2010 midterm. Public provision can therefore
change civic action briefly without establishing durable political engagement,
issue attribution, trust, or policy delivery.
The same experiment adds a household-diffusion boundary: adult coverage was
followed by a modest child-enrollment spillover that faded within the following
year. A policy can therefore reach beyond its applicant while still failing to

precede permission, training, realized productivity, shared gains, bargaining,

material independence, and employer concentration does not by itself prove

The adolescent digital-life layer keeps teen social-platform use and chatbot

The new adult companion survey adds a meaning layer: support, advice,

very different domains. In the platform/data lane, the FTC's GM/OnStar order
creates formal routes to request data, seek deletion, disable precise

A New York transmission settlement adds the consumer-correction counterpart:
a regulator can identify improper cost collection and announce a ratepayer

legal control, physical capacity, and strategic posture can all become more

community, or state actually gain usable, durable, and replaceable power?

## The big picture

The atlas is showing a society where formal options often exist, but usable
options are distributed unevenly. People, workers, consumers, families, firms,
and communities keep functioning by shifting costs across money, time, health,
care, privacy, status, and future security. The meaning of that shift is not
uniform: the same condition can produce trust, resentment, adaptation,
withdrawal, organizing, or political action depending on identity, place,
institutional experience, and available alternatives.

## 1. Unequal optionality is more important than a single hardship average

Prices, debt, care needs, health costs, housing, and service failures do not
produce one common response. Some people absorb the cost, some delay or trade
down, some borrow or use family help, some work more, and some go without or
leave. Cash, time, health, tenure, transportation, status, and local supply
determine the menu before a visible choice occurs.

The New York Fed heterogeneity layer adds a measurement warning: one national
economy contains different price baskets, earnings room, and consumption
directions. Group-level pressure can therefore diverge before people form
different explanations or political judgments.

## 2. Time is a hidden currency of inequality

People pay for expensive or unreliable systems with waiting, travel,
paperwork, unpaid care, schedule instability, customer-service loops, and
multiple jobs. Care work is social infrastructure, but its costs can appear as
lost work, reduced rest, lower civic availability, or family strain rather than
as a bill.

Evidence anchors include the [care-time finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-044.md),
[ATUS eldercare layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/atus-2023-2024-published-care-work-layer-v1.md),
the [work, care, and household-room finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-037.md),
the [child-care access and capacity finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-046.md),
[MEPS event-to-later-panel follow-up](projects/us-household-constraint-cascade/meps-event-six-month-followup-v1.md),
and [child-care work-adaptation layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/census-2025-childcare-challenges-work-adaptation-layer-v1.md).

The telework-place layer adds the network version of the same hidden price:
remote or hybrid work can reduce one worker's commute while changing transit
ridership, fare revenue, downtown demand, housing geography, and public
planning for everyone else. Household flexibility and collective service
capacity therefore move on different clocks; workers who cannot telework may
experience the resulting system change differently.

**Still unknown:** whose time was displaced in a particular episode, whether a
substitute actually protected work or health, and whether time loss changed
trust or political participation.

## 3. The interface often determines whether a right or service is real

Benefits, insurance, health care, complaints, refunds, platform protections,
and disaster aid can be formally available while notice, identity checks,
documentation, digital access, waiting, language, or appeal determine who can
use them. Administrative design is therefore part of distributional power.

Evidence anchors include the [formal-access synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/formal-access-control-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md),
[consumer recourse synthesis](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/consumer-recourse-visibility-remedy-synthesis-v1.md),

[Virginia insurance-settlement remedy finding](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/findings/us-customer-automation-recourse-029.md),

status, privacy, dignity, time, or safety while sacrificing care, income,

## 4. Cultural meaning is contested, not mechanically produced by hardship

People can agree that a problem exists and disagree about who caused it, what
it says about the country, and what response is legitimate. Party, race,
religion, class, age, migration history, place, and prior trust filter the same
material condition into different meanings.

The working-class identity layer adds a status/meaning boundary: self-described
class identity crosses income, education, and occupation, so “class” cannot be
used as a shortcut for resources or political behavior.
The political-typology layer adds a coalition boundary: nominal party groups
contain cross-pressured subgroups with different views of government, culture,
the safety net, immigration, and foreign power. Political intensity and party
label are therefore separate currencies.

Evidence anchors include the [cultural-meaning synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cultural-meaning-contested-legitimacy-cross-source-synthesis-v1.md),
[religious identity layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/pew-2025-religious-affiliation-switching-cultural-identity-layer-v1.md),

[political action menu synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/political-action-menu-not-ladder-synthesis-v1.md),
the [moral values and government-role finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-047.md),
and [American-dream and identity finding](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/findings/us-immigration-local-demand-004.md).

The public-life engagement layer adds a civic-action counterexample: political
interest, news trust, community participation, and federal-government trust
form different combinations rather than one engagement ladder.
The March HTOPS arts layer adds a cultural-access counterexample: expense
difficulty is associated with less recent in-person art-exhibit attendance and
less agreement that local arts opportunities are plentiful, while personal
art-making is not monotonic. Shared cultural access and private cultural
creation therefore need separate measures.
The same release adds a civic-participation boundary: club or organization
meeting participation falls across expense-difficulty groups, while attendance
at public meetings for local issues is not monotonic. Routine association and
formal issue attendance are different routes into public life.
The press-freedom layer adds an information-legitimacy tension: press freedom
can be widely valued while limits for accuracy or security are also accepted,
and people can disagree about whether government or news organizations hold
too much power.
The kinship-care layer adds a family-institution bridge: relatives can preserve
children's continuity while absorbing housing, work, disability, legal, and
emotional costs that formal program counts do not show.
The assisted-living layer adds the housing/health financing version: public
coverage and spending can coexist with fragmented room-and-board payment,
waits, provider limits, and family coordination costs.
The disability-workforce layer adds a labor-market conversion test: legal
access and assistive technology can coexist with weaker training use,
post-exit employment, and earnings while accommodation and completion remain
partly unobserved.
The working-adults layer adds the household-security version: employment,
Medicaid/SNAP protection, private employers, and food or health needs can
coexist without proving either employer intent or durable material independence.
The disaster-scams layer adds an information-risk version: urgent aid needs,
privacy decisions, verification burden, reporting friction, and legitimate
assistance can collide before a survivor reaches recovery.
The small-business disaster-credit layer adds the firm-side counterexample:
survival and employment can improve while private debt rises and worker,
customer, owner, and household recovery remain unmeasured.
The EDA disaster-economic layer adds the community/state-capacity version:
appropriations, federal coordination, infrastructure, and facilities can be
visible while validated evidence of jobs, private investment, service use, and
local recovery remains weak. Public project volume is therefore not a recovery
rate.
The small-business eligibility layer adds the allocation version: formal
program expansion can shift contracts toward larger firms while the smallest
firms lose revenue, survival, wages, and patenting.
The small-business capacity layer adds the multidimensional-health version:
reported profitability can coexist with weaker revenue expectations, thin
pricing power, debt, and uncertain local security.
The critical-minerals layer adds the strategic-capacity version: domestic
production, recycling plans, and new facilities can coexist with foreign
dependence in processing, purity, technology, ownership, or replacement.
The gallium/germanium event adds a control-surface version: export permission,
technical and end-user disclosure, shipment delay, and price movement can
create strategic friction before a total cutoff, named firm shortage, or
observed concession is established.

**Still unknown:** when an experience changes interpretation, and when that
interpretation becomes voting, organizing, protest, consumption, migration,
non-use, or exit.

## 5. Formal and informal support form a mixed protection system

Families, neighbors, religious groups, charities, employers, markets, and
public programs all protect people, but they carry different burdens and
different risks of stigma, interruption, unequal reciprocity, or exhaustion.
The family is not merely a private background variable; it is a care, credit,
housing, and crisis institution.

Evidence anchors include the [formal/informal support synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/formal-informal-support-capacity-cross-source-synthesis-v1.md),
[neighbor trust layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/pew-2025-neighbor-trust-help-community-capacity-layer-v1.md),
the [Cash and Counseling multi-currency synthesis](projects/us-aging-care-strain/cash-counseling-multi-currency-realization-synthesis-v1.md),
the [SNAP transition, work-capacity, and children finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/findings/us-safety-net-access-018.md),
and [kinship-care layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/gao-2026-kinship-families-intergenerational-care-support-layer-v1.md).
The military child-care workforce layer adds the provider-side version: bonuses,
fee discounts, and program eligibility can coexist with waitlists when staffed
slots are scarce. Care price, staffing, hours, quality, and dependable access
are separate currencies.
The eldercare time-network layer adds the unpaid-supply version: millions of
employed people provide care, with much more time concentrated on care days.
Care therefore operates as labor, family, travel, and social infrastructure at
the same time.
The linked caregiving/earnings layer adds a gendered labor-market clock:
women's work and earnings fall after reported care onset in the study, while
men enter care after earlier labor disruption. Caregiving therefore needs a
prior-labor and household-buffer lens, not one universal work penalty.
The Cash and Counseling layer adds a control-versus-burden boundary: recipient
direction can improve selected short-run access, satisfaction, caregiver
wellbeing, or worker experience while completed care, unpaid substitution,
backup coverage, staffing, continuity, household security, and worker
retention remain separate. More choice at the recipient interface is not yet a
durable care system.
The SNAP exit/material-security layer adds a public-route counterexample:
leaving the program can be followed by lower measured resources, while receipt
can coexist with bill hardship. Participation is therefore not a security
switch, and exit is not automatically independence.
The administrative-burden studies add a causal policy counterexample in
specific settings: work requirements increased SNAP exits without increasing
employment. A lower caseload can therefore reflect route failure or screening
out rather than successful labor-market transition.
The SNAP take-up experiment adds the inverse implementation warning: information
and application assistance increased enrollment, but the marginal enrollees
received lower benefits and were less sick than average control-group enrollees.
A larger caseload can therefore coexist with weaker targeting and continued
under-reach to people with the greatest need.
The ACA Medicaid-expansion comparison adds a cross-program navigation boundary:
SNAP and TANF participation rose mainly conditional on eligibility, consistent
with one program reducing the marginal cost of applying for another. Program
participation can therefore spill across systems without proving household
security or adequate benefits.
The newer five-state linked administrative study sharpens that boundary: work
requirements reduced SNAP participation by about 7% without increasing labor
supply and disproportionately screened out lower-income people. This is still
a participation and labor-supply result, not proof of later food insecurity,
debt, trust loss, or political action; those middle stages remain open.
The flexible-interview and office-access studies add the positive design
counterexample: changing who controls an interview or whether a local office
is reachable can raise approvals or participation. Route design can alter
public-system reach before food security or household recovery is observed.

**Still unknown:** when support is actually delivered, whether it is adequate,
and whether receiving or providing it strengthens belonging or creates debt and
resentment.

## 7. Capacity does not automatically become usable service or autonomy

Factories, data centers, defense contracts, minerals, grid projects, public
awards, and business formation can show activity without proving qualified

whether alternatives exist, and whether an external actor changes behavior.

Migration and demographic change affect labor supply, housing, businesses,

entrepreneurs, and public-system users at the same time, but local capacity may
not adjust evenly.

service strain, political coalition, exclusion, or belonging for the same
place and people.

The housing and insurance layers add a stay-versus-security boundary: rent or
mortgage continuity, a residual-market policy, a repair route, or energy
assistance can preserve nominal residence while cash room, coverage adequacy,
safety, and mobility remain fragile. Remaining in place is not automatically
stability or preference; it may be the least-bad available option.

## 9. Market and institutional power move risk between actors

Firm growth, public investment, regulation, automation, insurance pricing,
platform rules, and procurement can improve one actor's position while moving
cost, uncertainty, or dependence to workers, customers, suppliers, households,
or local governments. “Success” is therefore actor-specific.

Evidence anchors include the [market-power layer](projects/us-financial-intermediation/credit-liquidity-records-power-layer-v1.md),
[financial access, substitution, and recourse finding](projects/us-financial-intermediation/findings/us-financial-intermediation-003.md),
[financial-access doorway/provider finding](projects/us-financial-intermediation/findings/us-financial-intermediation-001.md),
[Harvey firm/customer welfare finding](projects/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity/findings/us-harvey-firm-turnover-consumer-welfare-001.md),
[small-business disaster synthesis](projects/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity/findings/us-disaster-recovery-policy-end-to-end-synthesis-001.md),
and [insurance affordability/risk layers](projects/us-housing-insurance-risk/cost-of-staying-put-synthesis-v1.md).

**Still unknown:** the distribution of gains and losses across the linked
worker, customer, owner, resident, and public units.

The financial-access comparison sharpens the same point: account and device
ownership can become nearly universal while branches contract and practical
recourse, liquidity, human help, and provider exit remain uneven. Formal
doorway, provider infrastructure, and usable financial control are separate
stages.

The new bank-depositor finding adds a quieter consumer/firm version of the
same mechanism: staying put can preserve bill continuity, branch access, and
stability while costing interest and weakening the customer's bargaining
position. A rate gap is not yet a household loss until fees, balance, service,
switching effort, error risk, and later security are observed together.

The Hurricane Harvey firm/customer ledger adds a place-based version: firm
closures can push customers farther away while entrants create some modeled
surplus, yet replacement still fails to restore nearby access in the hardest-hit
neighborhoods. Market entry and aggregate welfare are therefore not the same as
usable choice for a household with limited time, mobility, money, or care slack.
The practical-exit audit adds the remedy version: a complaint route, review,
reactivation, payment order, recall, or reissue can be real institutional
progress while the same customer remains dependent on the provider and no
adequate alternative is visible. Restoration and responsiveness are therefore
not the same as freedom to switch or leave.

## 10. Domestic conditions become political and geopolitical power through
conversion stages

Affordability, industrial capacity, migration, technology, energy, defense,
and public legitimacy matter strategically only after they affect state
capacity, public consent, alliance behavior, supplier dependence, or an
external actor's choices. Domestic strength, foreign reputation, and actual
leverage are not the same thing.

The CCES same-panel comparison adds a political-capacity boundary: prior job
loss or lack of health insurance is associated with less later contact or local
political action, while favorable Congress approval does not move in the same
direction. Material insecurity can therefore reduce the ability to act without
creating one uniform judgment of the institution. See the [material status,
civic capacity, and institutional judgment synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/material-status-political-action-divergence-synthesis-v1.md).

Evidence anchors include the [material-to-political-meaning synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/material-pressure-to-political-meaning-synthesis-v1.md),
[economic salience and representation finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-046.md),
[insurance status and political action finding](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/findings/us-cost-trust-politics-043.md),
[CCES job-loss political follow-up](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cces-panel-job-loss-political-followup-v1.md),
[party-conditioned ANES worry/vote layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/anes-panel-worry-vote-party-conditioned-layer-v1.md),
[global-role legitimacy layer](projects/ai-work-control/pew-2026-us-global-role-domestic-partisan-international-comparison-layer-v1.md),
and [state-capacity/dependence synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/domestic-capacity-dependence-state-leverage-cross-source-bridge-v1.md).

**Still unknown:** the conversion from exposure to public demand, from demand
to policy, and from capability or legitimacy to changed external behavior.

## 11. Different realization clocks create the hidden middle

Across the atlas, the first visible change often arrives before the protected
outcome. A contract can precede production and readiness; a recall notice can
precede replacement receipt and safe use; an insurance payment can precede
repair and safe return; AI time savings can precede worker control; a benefit
can be authorized before it is usable; and an identity or institution can gain
visibility before belonging and legitimacy improve.

These are not the same episodes, but they share a measurement rule:

```text
announcement / allocation / offer
  -> implementation or reported receipt
  -> actual use and adequacy
  -> durable protection or capability
  -> meaning, trust, action, switching, or external response
```

**Why it matters:** the middle is where money, time, paperwork, risk, and
control are redistributed. A society can look active and responsive in public
records while households, workers, consumers, communities, or states still
wait for the final conversion.

The [Atkins worker-receipt comparator](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwo-atkins-truck-driver-backpay-receipt-event-ledger-v1.json)
shows the useful positive boundary: an official record can report receipt for
all ten named workers after funds are released. That is stronger than an order
or distribution label, but it still does not show adequacy, household recovery,
durable work, trust, or exit.

**Still unknown:** which actor controls each conversion, who carries the gap,
and when an intermediate stage becomes durable protection, practical exit,
legitimacy, or strategic leverage.

## 12. Control can improve while burden shifts

The atlas contains positive interventions as well as failures. Consumer-directed
Medicaid support improved selected beneficiary satisfaction and unmet-care
measures, but could move hiring, payroll, scheduling, and backup-care work into
families and providers. Financial access can coexist with residual fraud loss
and weak switching. A named clinic can have active status and listed hours
without a visible appointment. An appeal can reverse a health denial without
showing completed care or recovery.

These are different domains, not one pooled effect. They share a measurement
rule:

```text
new control or formal improvement
  -> protected currency (access, satisfaction, money, time, safety, or capability)
  -> shifted work, risk, cost, dependence, or continuity burden
  -> [open] durable household outcome, trust, switching, action, or leverage
```

**Why it matters:** asking whether an intervention “worked” is too coarse. The
useful questions are who gained control, which outcome improved, what new work
or risk was transferred, and whether the protected outcome lasted. This rule
also prevents positive treatment effects from being mistaken for universal
recovery or institutional legitimacy.

The homeowners-insurance layer adds a place-risk version: a moderate national
premium average can coexist with sharply higher coastal or hazard-linked local
costs and thinner public-backstop coverage. Market averages do not tell us
whether a household can insure, repair, stay, or move.
The FHFA price layer adds the asset-value version: property prices can rise or
diverge by state while insurance affordability, deductible capacity, repair,
and mobility remain unknown. Asset value, protection, and recovery are separate
housing currencies.

## 13. Material progress and cultural belonging can move on different clocks

The migration and identity evidence shows that people can report living better
than their parents, expect their children to do better, and still see the
American dream as out of reach or nonexistent. Identity can be central to a
person while being experienced as help, barrier, or neither. Mobility is
therefore not the same as belonging, and belonging is not a simple proxy for
income or assimilation.

Evidence anchors include the [American-dream and identity finding](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/findings/us-immigration-local-demand-004.md),
the [migration/place/belonging synthesis](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/migration-place-cultural-conversion-synthesis-v1.md),
and the [cultural-meaning synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cultural-meaning-contested-legitimacy-cross-source-synthesis-v1.md).

**Still unknown:** which concrete housing, work, school, health, or
discrimination encounters change that interpretation, and when interpretation
becomes political action, migration, consumption, non-use, or exit.

## 14. Status changes the value of every formal option

Race, nativity, disability, language, age, family structure, wealth, geography,
and documentation status do more than predict who is exposed. They change the
cost of applying, the credibility of a complaint, the safety of refusing, the
availability of an alternative, and the meaning of the same institutional
decision. A listed benefit, job, appointment, insurance policy, or platform
right can therefore have different practical value for people facing the same
formal rule.

Evidence anchors include the [unequal-optionality and status synthesis](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/unequal-optionality-status-meaning-synthesis-v1.md),
[disability health-care accessibility finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-016.md),
[migration and belonging synthesis](projects/us-immigration-local-demand/migration-place-cultural-conversion-synthesis-v1.md),
and the [SIPP resource/work stratification finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-045.md).

**Still unknown:** which status-linked mechanism caused the unequal burden in
a dated episode, whether the institution corrected it, and whether the person
could later use, refuse, switch, or exit.

## What to do next

Use this pulse to choose new evidence across different themes. Do not keep
adding rows to one remedy lane when the bigger question is unanswered. The
next strong additions should test one of these missing conversions:

1. material condition → cultural meaning;
2. cultural meaning → political or consumer action;
3. formal capacity → usable access;
4. firm or state capability → local distribution;
5. population change → place capacity and belonging; or
6. domestic capacity/legitimacy → external response; or
7. intermediate implementation → durable protection, capability, or trust.

The current rotation's [Ohio named-site care-access route](projects/us-local-business-place/hrsa-hpwo-allen-county-named-site-access-v1.md)
is now complete at the public administrative/provider layer: it reconciles the
named provider, listed hours, intake and payment/insurance route, phone-versus-
portal handoff, same-day limitation, and affordability paperwork. It still
does not show a site-specific appointment, completed care, continuity,
household effect, trust, or political response. The next rotation should move
to a different event-compatible route rather than add another nominal-capacity
count.

The next structural handoff is the [public-system route-to-security
triangulation](projects/us-safety-net-access/public-system-route-to-security-triangulation-v1.md).
It is the strongest current bridge across administrative burden, household
material security, care/work capacity, informal support, and institutional
meaning. It should remain a bounded cross-source synthesis until a lawful
same-episode table can join notice, route effort, amount, interruption,
correction, later hardship, trust, and action.

The health-cost lane also now promotes the [prescription two-clock finding](projects/us-household-constraint-cascade/findings/us-household-constraint-cascade-001.md):
a same-person prescription-event group is followed into later health and work
status, with mixed rather than one-directional movement. This strengthens the
event-to-later-outcome arrow while keeping fill, adherence, payment burden,
treatment continuity, household recovery, trust, action, and exit open.

The companion [prescription material-pressure finding](projects/us-household-constraint-cascade/findings/us-household-constraint-cascade-002.md)
adds the earlier event-payment and later care-delay, debt, collector, and
employment context. The useful distinction is between a recorded payment
component and total household burden; the latter remains unmeasured.

The consumer/financial-intermediation lane now adds the [Global Findex
conversion finding](projects/us-financial-intermediation/findings/us-financial-intermediation-001.md):
formal account and device reach can become nearly universal without proving
liquidity, safe use, understandable terms, effective correction, or practical
exit. The country comparison is a set of distinct access surfaces, not a
single digital-inclusion score.

The infrastructure/geopolitical lane now adds the [World Bank AI capability
finding](projects/ai-work-control/world-bank-wdr2026-ai-capability-governance-layer-v1.md):
adoption can increase local capacity while value-chain concentration and weak
institutional complements deepen dependency. This remains a framework layer;
actual procurement, maintenance, service use, public trust, and external
leverage require named deployments.

The work/firm lane adds a separate [size-stratified firm-capacity finding](projects/ai-work-control/findings/ai-work-control-063.md):
training, employment growth, outages, bank-financed investment, and perceived
obstacles vary by establishment size. This changes how the atlas should test
AI and technology diffusion: firm adoption is not worker access, task control,
or household benefit.

The household-security lane adds the [USDA food-security finding](projects/us-food-budget-security/usda-2024-food-security-layer-v1.md):
food hardship has severity levels, adults and children can be protected
differently within one household, and assistance can coexist with very low
food security. This is a household coping and distribution mechanism, not
another SNAP take-up estimate.

The public-system lane adds the [SNAP route-performance finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/usda-snap-state-route-performance-layer-v1.md):
participation, application speed, recertification speed, and access capacity
are distinct state-level signals. A high participation rate is not proof of a
fast or usable administrative route.

The consumer/community-food lane adds the [charitable-food usability finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/wbns-charitable-food-access-layer-v1.md):
receipt can coexist with transport, hours, variety, safety, information,
comfort, and perceived-treatment barriers. Community provision is therefore a
buffer and an access route, not automatically adequate food security or dignity.

The public-system lane now adds the [SNAP interruption finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/urban-wbns-snap-recertification-interruption-layer-v1.md):
benefits can fail at notice, timing, paperwork, or interview stages before the
household outcome is visible. Administrative burden is therefore part of the
material cost of receiving a benefit.

The firm/disaster lane adds a stronger causal middle through [emergency
recovery loans](projects/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity/findings/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity-001.md):
public liquidity can preserve firms and employment after disasters, but firm
survival is not automatically worker security, customer affordability, or
owner-household recovery.

The firm/public-policy lane adds a distribution mechanism through the
[small-business size-rule finding](projects/us-small-business-support-size-cutoff/findings/us-small-business-support-size-rule-001.md):
eligibility expansion can change which firms receive public contracts and who
absorbs the loss. Program scale and fair distribution are separate questions.

The [theme/subtheme map](US-BROAD-THEME-SUBTHEME-MAP_V1.md) defines the full
space. The [provisional trend register](US-PROVISIONAL-SOCIETAL-TRENDS_V1.md)
holds the detailed trend rows and boundaries.
The subscription-remedy layer adds a concrete implementation distinction:
issued and accepted refund dollars can be visible before individual receipt,
adequacy, continued use, switching, trust, or household recovery.
The care/health lane adds an accessibility-interface boundary: coverage,
provider capacity, and digital access can still fail to become safe, completed,
continuous care for people with disabilities.
The aging-care lane adds a distributed-time boundary: unpaid eldercare spans
household and wider kin networks, and population averages must be separated
from care-day intensity, work overlap, travel, and recipient continuity.
The child-care lane adds an option-conversion boundary: families convert care
disruption into paid care, unpaid time, supervision while working, leave, hour
cuts, quitting, non-search, or later housing strain depending on alternatives.
The long-term-care lane adds a care-bundle boundary: Medicaid services,
Medicare health care, room and board, family labor, facility supply, and safe
continuity can diverge even when spending and formal coverage are visible.
The firm/place lane adds an intention-to-capacity boundary: applications can
rise without durable firms, jobs, essential services, or resident access, and
sector entry, exit, and net employment can move in different directions.
The firm/place lane also adds a growth-stability boundary: health care and
food services can add jobs while experiencing large opening and closing flows,
so net employment does not establish stable work or reliable local service.
The household-pressure lane adds a recovery-currency boundary: trade-down,
reduced use, savings loss, borrowing, delayed purchases, extra work, and
emergency buffers persist and reverse at different rates.
The digital-habits lane adds a utility-control boundary: smartphones can
support information and connection while users report excess, sleep or
productivity costs, and only partial success when trying to cut back.
The public-life lane adds a participation-combination boundary: political
interest, direct action, community and religious involvement, news attention,
source trust, and government trust form different profiles rather than one
engagement ladder.
The information-legitimacy lane adds a principle-authority boundary: press
freedom can be highly valued while accuracy and security limits are accepted,
leaving corrective authority contested.
The digital-exit lane adds a concrete interaction boundary: a declared goodbye
can receive continuation tactics and produce short-run engagement, while
durable dependence, harm, and practical exit remain unobserved.
The consumer-recourse lane adds a visibility-recovery boundary: fraud and
service harm can become institutionally legible before money, account control,
time, trust, or practical exit are restored.
The safety-net lane adds a material-administrative clock boundary: Hispanic
poverty reached a record low in 2025 while overall Medicaid coverage declined
through the unwinding period, so income position, eligibility, renewal, care
access, and later security cannot be treated as one outcome. The releases are
population-level and do not join the same people.
The income-distribution lane adds a midpoint-tail boundary: the 2025 median
reached a record while the 90th percentile rose and the 10th percentile did not
move significantly, so median improvement cannot stand in for broad material
room or equal gains.
The care lane adds a household-structure boundary: multigenerational living
and paid/unpaid care can provide belonging and protection while transferring
costs into gendered time, work, housing, and mobility. Co-residence and care
use are adaptations, not standalone welfare scores.
The community lane adds a goodwill-reciprocity boundary: people may be more
willing to help others than confident that help would be available to them.
Willingness, expected support, delivered aid, adequacy, and later trust are
separate stages of local social capacity.
The civic lane adds a reach-intensity boundary: formal and informal helping
can rebound while average volunteer time falls and online or hybrid service
expands. Participation counts therefore do not establish organizational
continuity, efficacy, or durable collective action.
The mobility lane adds a non-monotonic burden boundary: lower-income
households show more vehicle scarcity, while higher-income workers can show
longer commutes. Vehicle ownership, travel time, affordability, reliability,
care access, and schedule control must remain separate.
The monetary-policy lane adds an interpretation boundary: a rate signal is
filtered through inflation beliefs, financial knowledge, debt, liquidity, and
contract exposure before it becomes intended spending or a judgment about
institutional competence. Expectations and realized household welfare remain
different currencies.
The migration/meaning lane adds a conditional-inclusion boundary: support for
Dreamer legal status is higher than support for a broader citizenship pathway,
and the broader pathway varies by local setting. Policy attitude is not the
same as immigrant experience, belonging, service use, or political action.
The geopolitical lane adds a three-audience legitimacy boundary: foreign
publics, domestic partisans, and material-capability institutions can move on
different clocks. Perceived peace, interference, freedom, and compromise are
not themselves alliance behavior, capability, or leverage.
The policy-feedback lane adds a transfer-return boundary: consumption relief,
liquidity, electoral reward, and targeting are different outcomes. A program
can create political response without maximizing immediate consumption, and
the international comparison is not a U.S. causal estimate.
The energy/consumer lane adds a conversion boundary: a trusted efficiency
label or a public benefit-to-community-solar route can be widespread before
purchase, enrollment, installation, realized bill savings, or equal access are
observed. DC Solar for All reaches a reported program-level household-savings
endpoint, but the scope audit shows why that still cannot be read as a
customer-level persistence or security result.
The infrastructure lane adds a security-service boundary: grid equipment and
software can be governed as strategic assets while reliability, rates, local
incidence, and public consent remain unmeasured.
The public-capacity lane adds a fiscal-buffer boundary: reserve balances and
operating days can be substantial while actual draw authority, service choice,
delivery, and household protection remain unknown.
The worker-remedy lane now adds a conditional-payment boundary: aggregate
refund receipt and an appeal-stay income obligation are stronger than a bare
promise or order, but neither proves individual receipt, restored access, or
durable security.
The platform-remedy lane now has a three-stage comparison: a process defect
can coexist with no restoration, substitute earnings can coexist with a lost-
pay order, and reactivation can be stayed while an interim-payment obligation
continues. These distinctions sharpen implementation without proving receipt,
free alternative work, durable access, or exit.
The digital-consumer lane adds a correction-control boundary: Cash App's
redress and operational requirements show institutional recognition of dispute
failure without showing account-level repair, residual-loss reduction, trust,
or switching.
The disaster lane adds a persistence-adequacy boundary: extended wildfire
housing support and mortgage bridges can preserve a route through a long
recovery while leaving payment receipt, repair, permanent housing, and return
open.
The worker-voice lane adds a public-risk boundary: safety complaints and
payroll questions can reveal environmental or firm-wide failures and trigger
institutional correction, while the speaking worker still bears retaliation,
documentation, reachability, or payment risk.
The status-and-labor lane adds a coercive-compliance boundary: migration,
tax, and employment dependence can shape the form of wage theft, and reported
full back-pay repairs a formal loss without proving restored bargaining power,
household security, or trust.
The household-pressure lane adds a stacking boundary: utility difficulty,
renter status, child-care-related work prevention, and following-month
housing/food hardship can be placed on one same-person adjacent-month frame,
but the annual care measure, small prevention cell, and repeated reference
fields do not identify a dated bill, provider response, payment, remedy, or
recovery.

## 2026-09-21 breadth rotation: four additional conversion boundaries

The worker-remedy route now makes reachability more concrete. DOL can report
large agency-level wage recovery while a separate WOW process still depends on
finding the worker, verifying identity, completing a claim, and getting the
money through a payment channel. Recovery scale, worker receipt, adequacy, and
household use are separate stages.

The care-and-status route shows the same structure from another direction.
Disability workforce programs can have job centers, assistive technology,
guidance, and monitoring while accommodation, disclosure, training completion,
employment, and earnings remain uneven. Assisted-living spending and Medicaid
coverage can also exist without proving an affordable, available, safe setting
because room and board, waivers, provider capacity, and family coordination are
separate parts of the care bundle.

The political-cultural route adds a short-run meaning-to-action bridge. A
synthetic outgroup conversation can increase immediate warmth or willingness
to try real contact, while weak one-week persistence warns that civic rehearsal
is not durable reconciliation, cooperation, or institutional trust.

The household-consumer route adds two unequal-cost boundaries. A gasoline
shock can produce different inflation and real-spending responses by income and
place, while a marketed credit opportunity can impose application time or
credit exposure before the consumer reaches a remedy. Price, liquidity,
substitution, missed opportunity, and correction are distinct currencies.

The consumer-voice route adds a different institutional mechanism. The CFPB's
June 2026 correction announcement treats the complaint portal itself as a
governance surface: identity protection, standardized firm responses,
substantive-review targeting, consumer education, and automated or organized
submission all affect what becomes visible. This is a cultural and political
finding about how people make harm legible, not evidence that any individual
consumer received a correction or regained trust.

## 2026-09-21 meaning-and-action rotation: pressure does not have one political meaning

The youth-finance record adds a direct material-pressure-to-political-efficacy
bridge: financially struggling young adults report lower satisfaction and are
more likely to say voting does not matter, while housing/utilities (66%),
groceries/everyday items (62%), health care (50%), and wages (47%) can also
become reasons to mobilize when politics appears able to improve daily life.
The source specifically reports that struggling youth are more likely to say
they would participate when their voice matters, communities change, and
candidates follow through. The working-parent record shows the same interpretive
structure inside family life: two full-time jobs are often judged financially
positive while parents divide on child-wellbeing and career or care effects.

Together these records sharpen a cultural point: people do not convert hardship
or work arrangements into action mechanically. The same condition can be read
as necessity, progress, unfairness, responsibility, or a reason to disengage
depending on efficacy, identity, care access, and whose outcome is being judged.
The missing bridge is still a same-person panel from dated material pressure or
family change through attribution, contact, turnout or organizing, later trust,
and consumer or political exit.

## 2026-09-21 support-and-security rotation: buffering is not security

The new household-support bridge connects four existing evidence surfaces:
income timing and outside-household help, expense difficulty and later material
pressure, health-cost adaptation, and SNAP administrative access. Together they
show several ways a household can keep functioning—family transfers, cheaper
goods, delayed purchases, borrowing, benefit participation, or route changes—
without establishing stable food, energy, care, work, or financial room.

This is an important cultural and political distinction. People may interpret
the same public system as help, burden, arbitrariness, or a reason to seek a
different route depending on whether the route actually worked, what effort it
required, and what sacrifice remained. But the current evidence is still split
across sources and units. The next valid test is a same-person or same-household
episode linking shock, help or benefit attempt, route effort, actual amount and
timing, material result, correction, and later trust or action.

## 2026-09-21 operating-capacity rotation: capability needs an interface

The GAO eligibility-data review adds an upstream public-system boundary:
agencies can draw on more than 100 sources while data quality, identifiers,
validation, and governance remain fragmented. The problem can arise before a
person sees a decision, appeal, payment, or correction. Administrative capacity
therefore depends on matching and interoperability, not only program scale.

The KFOR record adds the geopolitical counterpart. NATO reports a gradual
posture adjustment after an improved security assessment and integration of
Kosovo Police into the mission's security architecture, coordinated across
local and regional actors. This is stronger than a procurement or exercise
announcement because operation is being adapted through relationships. It still
does not show local consent, reduced incidents, burden distribution, or changed
external behavior. In both cases, formal capacity becomes meaningful only
through an interface with the actor who must use, accept, or live under it.

The Champlain Hudson Power Express record adds a different infrastructure
stage: a named cross-border transmission project reached commercial operation
and began the reported route for Hydro-Québec power into New York City. This
turns construction and policy into operating system capacity, but expected
annual delivery, emissions reduction, and jobs remain separate from household
bills, rate-class allocation, reliability, host-community acceptance, and
durable Canada–U.S. leverage.

The German-Lithuanian brigade record adds the social-capacity layer to the
geopolitical lane. The Bundeswehr reports about 80% volunteer staffing and
selected mandatory measures before a planned 2027 operational target. This
shows that alliance power depends on labor, maintenance, families, and
legitimacy as well as weapons and posture; it does not show operational
performance, local acceptance, deterrence, or adversary adaptation.

The new housing and care-access cases show the same interface rule at household
level. HUD's housing-assistance verification route can move a household from
assistance to identity matching, secondary review, appeal, proration, or
possible termination before notice, payment, displacement, or durable housing
security is visible. In Ohio, a shortage-designated organization can be linked
to an active named clinic with listed hours and affordability policies while a
site-specific appointment, wait, insurance result, and completed visit remain
unknown. A program or provider can therefore be real and operating while the
person's practical security is still conditional on paperwork, route clarity,
and a usable slot.

## 2026-09-21 care-cost rotation: one burden can cross currencies

Persistent care foregoing is linked in the SHED layer to medical debt, reduced
savings, borrowing, delayed purchases, and fair or poor health. CFPB evidence
adds a credit-system consequence when medical collections appear, while a
separate HTOPS comparison shows that expense difficulty aligns with lower
confidence in some institutions. The atlas can therefore see a plausible
care-to-money-to-legitimacy corridor, but not a single causal same-person
episode. The required next step is to follow one bill or care event through
treatment delay, payment or collection, credit correction, household
adaptation, attribution, and later trust or action.

## 2026-09-21 civic-infrastructure rotation: participation needs usable supports

The family-friendly-transit dashboard and the Political Homes research point to
the same broader cultural mechanism from different directions. Public agencies
are beginning to measure the conditions that make transit usable—safety,
cleanliness, accessibility, real-time information, and reliability—while youth
political homes combine belonging with practical supports such as civic
information, food, transportation, and financial help. In both cases, formal
availability is only the first stage. The real question is whether a person can
get through the route, feel safe and included, and use the resulting capacity
to move, participate, or act.

This widens the political story beyond attitudes. Civic action can depend on
social and material infrastructure, and public accountability can begin by
making everyday usability visible before service or participation changes. The
evidence does not prove that the dashboard improves trips or that political
homes cause action; access, receipt, safety, persistence, and institutional

## 2026-09-21 teen-AI rotation: use is not one social relationship

Pew's teen AI study separates instrumental use from relational use. More than
half of teens report using chatbots for information or schoolwork, while
smaller shares report casual conversation or emotional support and advice. The
distinction matters because a tool can be a reversible task aid for one person
and part of a support system for another. Those are different routes through
time, learning, care, identity, privacy, and dependence.

This strengthens the platform/data and social-meaning lanes without claiming
that AI has replaced people or harmed teen wellbeing. The study does not show
session length, human-support substitution, learning gains, manipulation,
privacy loss, or whether a teen can stop using the tool without losing a needed
resource. The next valid test is a teen or household follow-up that preserves
purpose, frequency, human alternative, disclosure, control, and later social
or school outcome. See [finding 356](US-BROAD-INTERESTING-FINDINGS_V1.md#356-teen-ai-use-is-splitting-into-task-help-and-relational-support).

The same-case availability audit makes the broad gap measurable across 34 local

This is why the atlas has substantial cultural, social, consumer, political,

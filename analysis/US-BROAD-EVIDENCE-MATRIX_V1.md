# US broad evidence matrix v1

**Purpose:** keep the end-to-end program organized by arrow, source family, unit, and missing evidence. This is a research-control document, not a claim that every row is already connected.

## Matrix

The new [mortgage lock-in record](records/us-nber-mortgage-lockin-housing-mobility-2022-2024.json)
adds a constrained-staying route: rate change and an existing low-rate
mortgage → lock-in and reduced sale/buy option → staying or altered movement
down the housing ladder → modeled price/rent effect → [open] repair,
insurance, care, work, security, and exit. It complements the flood-buyout
relocation record without treating staying or moving as inherently good or bad.

The new [HUD–LEHD assistance trajectory record](records/us-census-hud-lehd-assistance-employment-trajectories-2026.json)
adds a linked longitudinal housing-to-work route: housing-assistance entry or
exit → same linked unit's employment and earnings trajectory → [open] rent
burden, housing quality, work/care trade-off, health, trust, and durable
security. Entry and exit patterns are useful same-unit follow-up, but selection
and incomplete public abstract details prevent a causal assistance claim; the
study does not observe alternatives, adequacy, exit reason, remedy, or action.

The [HUD HEARS exit-type record](records/us-hud-hears-exit-type-outcomes-2025.json)
adds a bounded regional bridge for the missing exit classification: positive,
neutral, or negative assistance exit → same-linked-person housing stability,
health, behavioral-health, and wage outcomes. It shows why aggregate exit cannot
be read as success: one-year homelessness or unstable housing was 3.1% after
positive exits versus 25.4% after negative exits. The study is observational and
limited to two King County housing authorities, so it complements rather than
replaces the national HUD–LEHD trajectory evidence.

The new [BLS worker-control record](records/us-bls-worker-requested-changes-control-2024-2025.json)
adds a worker-side implementation bridge: need or desired improvement →
schedule, equipment, task, telework, training, or accessibility request →
full, partial, or no employer grant → [open] actual usability, persistence,
pay, health, care, retention, voice, and exit. BLS also provides separate
job-level measures of pause control, self-paced workload, and schedule
variability. A granted request is not treated as durable worker control.

The new [FTC refund-cashing record](records/us-ftc-refund-cashing-reissue-funnel-2024.json)
adds a remedy-reachability bridge: eligible harm → payment sent → cashing or
uncashed status → address/payment-channel reissue → additional distribution or
closure → [open] underlying correction, adequacy, continued use, trust,
switching, and recovery. The FTC's 2024 report gives separate aggregate
cashing rates for first distributions and closed programs and a named Amazon
Flex Zelle reissue example. These are usable-payment indicators, not
recipient-level recovery or satisfaction.

The new [Amazon Prime redress record](records/us-ftc-amazon-prime-redress-route-2025-2027.json)
adds a distinct procedural-burden comparator: challenged subscription
enrollment or cancellation → settlement eligibility → automatic mailed-check,
PayPal, or Venmo issuance → [open] receipt/acceptance → [open] account
correction, cancellation or continued use, switching, trust, and recovery. The
FTC reported more than $845 million issued by September 2026 and a revised
automatic-payment design that does not require future eligible consumers to
file a claim, respond to a notice, or complete additional paperwork. Aggregate
money issued and reduced claim burden are not person-level receipt, corrected
subscription status, restored control, or recovery.

The new [hurricane climate-accountability record](records/us-nber-hurricane-climate-policy-electoral-accountability-2026.json)
adds a bounded material-event-to-political-action route: hurricane exposure in
a congressional district → representative support for environmental
regulation and control in the following year → persistent agenda change →
reported electoral penalty. NBER Working Paper 25835 identifies heterogeneity
by district safety, representative experience, and prior environmental record.
This closes an institutional/district sequence without claiming that the same
households experienced damage, changed trust, voted, or recovered.

The new [Taiwan reciprocal-trade response record](records/us-ustr-taiwan-reciprocal-trade-capacity-response-2026.json)
adds a dated partner-response stage after the existing Taiwan-linked
semiconductor realization lane: U.S. trade and technology pressure → Taiwan
tariff and non-tariff commitments, planned U.S. industrial clusters, and
planned purchases → [open] implementation, delivery, output, replacement,
local burden, and leverage. The USTR fact sheet reports 99% tariff-barrier
reduction or elimination, planned Taiwan-linked U.S. capacity, and $84.8
billion as the arithmetic sum of three named purchase plans for 2025–2029.
These are agreement-stage commitments, not realized trade or autonomy.

The new [FEMA assistance and housing-reach record](records/us-gao-fema-assistance-housing-reach-2026.json)
adds a property-risk-to-recovery route: disaster need → Individual Assistance
eligibility, notice, and application → temporary lodging, rental housing, or
repair support → [open] housing availability, safe occupancy, staying, moving,
durable security, trust, and action. GAO reports more than $3 billion to 1.2
million individuals and households across selected recent disasters, while
also documenting helpline/letter barriers, constrained post-disaster housing,
and access concerns from changed delivery roles. Aggregate assistance and
selected institutional evidence are not survivor-level receipt or recovery.

The new [toxic-exposure record-access record](records/us-gao-toxic-exposure-record-access-2026.json)
adds a health-data control route: military exposure and screening → linked
DOD/VA record → claims, clinical, or research use → service-member/veteran
access and correction → [open] verified exposure, claim decision, treatment,
health, appeal, trust, and action. GAO reports that claims staff became the
largest ILER user group after the PACT Act, while service-member and veteran
access/correction functions remained in rollout. Login and rollout stages do
not establish record accuracy, claim approval, care receipt, health
improvement, or household recovery.

The new [state/local disaster-response capability record](records/us-gao-disaster-state-local-response-capability-2025.json)
adds the upstream jurisdictional-capacity stage: hazard and preparedness need
→ uneven state/local capability and federal support → role clarity, staffing,
and coordination → survivor reach, repair, housing, and durable recovery. GAO's
selected-state review reports wide capability variation, particular challenges
for rural or less-resourced jurisdictions, and officials' need for clear
guidance and preparation time when federal roles change. It is selected
institutional evidence, not a national capability rate or survivor outcome;
response speed, receipt, repair, trust, and recovery remain open.

The new [SNAP-denial record](records/us-nber-snap-work-requirements-procedural-denials-2026.json)
adds a policy-to-procedure bridge: work requirement and exemption rule →
paperwork, timing, documentation, and administrative burden → procedural or
overall denial → participation deterrence or benefit interruption → [open]
notice, appeal, restoration, food security, health, work, later trust, and
political action. NBER Working Paper 34698 uses administrative data and
difference-in-differences comparisons around age exemptions and waiver periods.
This is a denial mechanism, not a national rate or a completed claimant-level
remedy/recovery episode.

The new [managerial-clarity experiment record](records/us-nber-digital-surveillance-managerial-clarity-2025.json)
adds a causal implementation layer to workplace surveillance: randomized
monitoring and explanation → worker interpretation of management intent →
output response → [open] pay, autonomy, voice, appeal, health, retention, exit,
and household security. In an online labor-market experiment, surveillance had
no significant average performance effect, while unexplained presence or
removal of surveillance reduced output. This is a bounded randomized mechanism,
not a national exposure, employment-quality, or durable-control estimate.

The new [digital-surveillance record](records/us-gao-digital-surveillance-workers-federal-oversight-2025.json)
adds a workplace-implementation bridge: monitoring tool or sensor → worker
exposure and information asymmetry → target, pace, evaluation, safety, or
privacy effect → [reported risk] stress, injury, pay, discipline, or job-security
pathway → [open] worker voice, appeal, remedy, switching, exit, household
security, later trust, and collective action. GAO-25-107126 reviews 122
method-screened studies and stakeholder interviews, finding both safety
benefits and risks from productivity pressure, opaque data practices, flawed
benchmarks, and unintended use. This is a review-based implementation and
oversight bridge, not a worker-level prevalence or causal estimate.

The new [health-care trust record](records/us-nber-trust-health-care-seeking-behavior-2026.json)
adds a trust-to-behavior bridge: health need or care decision → trust in
provider, institution, insurer, or public-health authority → beliefs about
usefulness, disutility, cost, and denial → seek, delay, or avoid care → [open]
appointment receipt, treatment, health, debt, later trust, remedy, and action.
NBER reports strong associations between higher trust and preventive/elective
care-seeking, with provider-specific trust components and subgroup differences.
This is national survey and structural-model evidence, not a causal trust shock
or realized treatment/health result.

The new [FEMA workforce record](records/us-gao-fema-workforce-mission-readiness-2026.json)
adds an upstream public-capacity bridge to the disaster lane: concurrent
disasters and workforce change → staffing, institutional-memory, planning, and
surge constraints → call-center, grant, and Individual Assistance reach →
[open] receipt, housing and income continuity, health, trust, and action. GAO
reports more than 4,300 FEMA separations in FY2025, a 55% increase from FY2024,
and selected evidence of waits and unanswered calls after reduced surge and
call-center staffing. This is a capacity and access pathway, not a
survivor-level denial or recovery rate.

The new [Census flood-disaster finance record](records/us-census-flood-disaster-aid-firm-reallocation-productivity-2026.json)
adds a disaster-to-firm-capacity bridge: large flood → federal declaration and
finance → plant exit, credit, and machinery reallocation → survivor and entrant
productivity plus county-income recovery → [open] worker continuity, firm
ownership, customer/service continuity, household recovery, trust, and action.
The CES working paper reports more shutdown among the least productive plants,
more productive entrants and survivors, and expanded lending for young and
small firms after declared floods. The recorded estimates are study-specific
and do not establish universal recovery, household welfare, or that aid is
always fiscally self-financing.

The new [drinking-water record](records/us-epa-cdc-drinking-water-coverage-replacement-risk-2026.json)
adds an infrastructure-to-household-responsibility bridge: aging water need and
lead-service-line inventory → community-system or private-well exposure route
→ public monitoring, replacement, testing, or owner responsibility → [open]
safe water, cost, remediation, health, trust, and action. EPA estimates $625
billion in 20-year public-system need and approximately four million lead
service lines; CDC reports broad community-water coverage alongside private-well
responsibility and sampled contamination. This is national capacity and
governance evidence, not a household exposure, replacement, or illness rate.

The new [SIPP parental-leave record](records/us-census-sipp-parental-leave-race-sex-first-time-parents-2026.json)
adds a work-family-time bridge: first birth and work-family need → leave and
paid-leave route → no-leave or early-return timing → [open] care, earnings,
health, job quality, household security, and meaning. For the 2014–2023 cohort,
the Census paper reports any leave for 89.2% of mothers and 63.9% of fathers,
paid leave for 51.6% and 49.3%, and substantial race/ethnicity-by-sex
differences. The descriptive employed-before-and-after universe does not
establish access for all parents, employer discrimination, or later welfare.

The new [HOPE VI record](records/us-census-hope-vi-neighborhood-social-isolation-child-outcomes-2026.json)
adds a place-to-long-run-outcome bridge: distressed public-housing
neighborhood → $17 billion redevelopment across 262 developments → changed
neighborhood poverty and peer interaction → child earnings, college, and
incarceration outcomes → [open] adult earnings/security, resident retention,
displacement, housing quality, resident control, trust, and action. The Census
CES study reports no causal earnings effect for adults living in public-housing
units while children raised in revitalized units fare better on long-run
outcomes. This is a matched administrative working-paper result, not proof of
universal resident benefit or complete neighborhood recovery.

The new [consumer-AI choice record](records/us-ai-assistant-platform-choice-trust-privacy-2026.json)
adds a task-specific agency bridge: AI task exposure → primary choice,
multihoming, and provider specialization → experiential trust and privacy
knowledge → protective tools or stated willingness to pay for data-handling
controls → [open] actual settings, durable switching, exposure reduction,
wellbeing, and platform power. A June 2026 weighted survey of 1,999 U.S.
adult AI-assistant users reports concentrated primary use alongside task
niches and multihoming, near-universal privacy concern, knowledge/action
differences, and higher stated value for avoiding human review. This is a
cross-sectional preprint and choice experiment, not telemetry or causal
privacy-behavior evidence.

The new [California DROP record](records/us-california-drop-data-broker-deletion-control-2026.json)
adds a privacy-control implementation bridge: data-broker exposure and
privacy concern → one verifiable hashed request → broker matching and a
45-day processing cycle → deleted, exempted, opted-out, not-found, or pending
status → [open] residual data, re-collection, trust, switching, and exit.
CalPrivacy reports more than 325,000 consumer sign-ups/submissions and more
than 600 active brokers, but no consumer-level matched-broker denominator or
verified persistent-deletion result. This is a California implementation
finding, not a U.S.-wide deletion rate.

The new [World Happiness Report platform-exit record](records/us-whr-social-media-deactivation-collective-exit-2026.json)
adds two deliberately separate U.S.-anchored layers: randomized Facebook
deactivation → short-run wellbeing improvement and lower later use, with a
political-knowledge trade-off; and a separate U.S. college-student
Instagram/TikTok valuation study in which stated individual exit costs coexist
with stated willingness to pay for community-wide exit. This adds an
individual-exit-to-coordination mechanism, but stated valuation is not actual
collective deletion, app deletion is not account closure, and neither study
establishes durable wellbeing, political action, or a population-wide welfare
balance.

The new [social-media non-use record](records/us-sage-social-media-nonuse-exit-2026.json)
adds a practical-exit bridge: platform utility and connectivity norms →
time/app/account-level non-use strategy → reported UX, psychological, and
social friction → reversible non-use, exclusion, or substitution to another
platform → [open] durable exit, data erasure, wellbeing, remedy, or collective
action. Kim and Kim's 20-person qualitative study of young Korean adults
included active, deactivated, deleted, inactive, and partially deactivated
Instagram accounts and reports nonlinear, reversible non-use. This is
comparative lived-experience evidence, not a deletion rate, dark-pattern audit,
U.S. estimate, or verified data-erasure result.

The new international-power bridge is the [NBER dependence/negotiation record](records/us-nber-international-power-negotiation-2025.json): asymmetric import dependence and sectoral substitutability → model-implied international power → bilateral engagement and negotiation → anticipatory trade adjustment → [open] threat, concession, substitution, retaliation, and strategic result. Working Paper 34006 reports that higher measured power induces more engagement/negotiation and that worsening geopolitical relationships prompt countries to adjust trade to build future power. This adds a bargaining and anticipation mechanism distinct from the NBER conflict-risk trade-disruption estimate; it does not establish a named coercion episode or U.S.-specific incidence.

The new EITC-care bridge is the [NBER parent-care record](records/us-nber-eitc-parent-care-gap-2026.json): EITC generosity and work incentive → daughter labor/care allocation → reduced intensive care for older parents → [reported null] financial, family, or paid-care replacement → [open] care quality, health, safety, household cost, and later work. The revised Working Paper 32583 estimates that a $500 increase in EITC generosity reduces care from eligible daughters by 13–24 hours per month, with care gaps not filled by increased financial transfers, other children, spouses, or paid providers. This adds a policy-to-unpaid-care substitution stage rather than another care-time total; it does not establish universal harm or a complete household-welfare effect.

The new Australian social-media age-restriction bridge is the [eSafety implementation record](records/us-australia-social-media-age-restriction-2026.json): minimum-age law and guidance → platform age assurance and account action → reported account loss, retention, or new-account prevention → parent and child access/migration experience → enforcement → [open] safety, wellbeing, privacy, trust, and durable cultural effect. eSafety reports about 4.7 million age-restricted accounts removed or restricted by mid-December 2025 and more than 300,000 additional accounts prevented from signing in by early March 2026; its 898-parent pulse survey reports account ownership falling from 49.7% to 31.3%, while substantial platform-specific retention remained. This adds a comparative platform-governance implementation stage without converting account counts into unique children, compliance, or safety outcomes.

The new Strategic Petroleum Reserve bridge is the [GAO SPR capability record](records/us-gao-strategic-petroleum-reserve-operational-capability-2026.json): reserve infrastructure and inventory → effective fill/drawdown capability → emergency release and commercial distribution → [open] market, refinery, household, firm, and geopolitical consequence. GAO-26-106918 reports estimated effective drawdown at 61% of design rate and fill at 56% as of December 2025, with construction outages, aging infrastructure, low inventory, and limited commercial spare pipeline/storage capacity as constraints. This adds an operational state-capacity stage rather than another funding or reserve-size statistic; it does not establish a shortage, release-specific price effect, or energy-security success.

The new bilateral-conflict/trade bridge is the [NBER conflict-risk record](records/us-nber-bilateral-conflict-trade-risk-2026.json): geopolitical hostility → kinetic, military-posture, sanctions-context, or routine-diplomacy layer → bilateral trade response → lagged retaliation → [open] firm substitution, household incidence, alliance behavior, and strategic success. Working Paper 35077 uses a GDELT-based monthly bilateral indicator calibrated against human-curated ground truth; it reports a near doubling in the trade-weighted share of hostility routed through sanctions contexts over the past decade and estimates roughly $334 billion in bilateral trade at risk relative to a pre-escalation baseline, with the U.S.–China pair accounting for about half. This adds a comparative measured external-response mechanism while remaining separate from the CRS 2025 tariff-action timeline and from U.S.-specific incidence.

The new homeless-shelter bridge is the [NBER shelter record](records/us-nber-homeless-shelter-public-good-health-crime-2025.json): shelter availability and unsheltered exposure → shelter entry or reduced unsheltered homelessness → crime, psychiatric emergency care, and short-run mortality → [mixed] future homelessness relative to outreach and other services. This adds a public-good and public-health realization layer while leaving access, safety, resident choice, durable housing, adequacy, trust, and collective response open.

The new job-search assistance bridge is the [NBER Nevada record](records/us-nber-job-search-assistance-long-run-homeownership-2018.json): unemployment/UI eligibility → bundled counseling, search planning, enforcement, and referrals → employment, earnings, UI, disability, tax, and homeownership outcomes over up to eight years. Average outcomes improve, but roughly 40% of the sample is estimated to have negative long-run employment treatment effects. This adds a durable, heterogeneous work-to-housing route while leaving component effects, job quality, health, care burden, housing adequacy, and meaning/action open.

The new emergency-assistance bridge is the [NBER hotline/tax-record record](records/us-nber-emergency-financial-assistance-work-outcomes-2026.json): housing crisis → emergency-assistance availability → linked tax-record employment and earnings up to four years later. The study reports employment and earnings gains, especially for the lowest earners, and no overall evidence of reduced work. This advances a same-study assistance-to-outcome arrow while leaving housing continuity, job retention/quality, adequacy, displacement, household security, and trust/action open.

The new neighborhood social-infrastructure bridge is the [American Neighbor
Survey record](records/us-american-neighbor-survey-social-infrastructure-belonging-2026.json): housing, education, time, and nearby third places → neighbor contact and trust → stated problem-solving and emergency mutual-aid capacity → [open] actual assistance, burden-sharing, recovery, and collective action. The 2025 probability-based survey reports 40% regular neighbor conversation, 40% neighborhood problem-solving, 72% neighbor trust, and an education/amenity gradient in interaction and emergency childcare comfort. This adds a local belonging and informal-capacity layer while leaving selection, actual help, adequacy, and causal political consequence open.

The new federal-coordination bridge is the [GAO duplication record](records/us-gao-federal-duplication-fragmentation-capacity-2026.json): overlapping or fragmented federal responsibility → coordination, reporting, and navigation cost → unclear ownership and uneven implementation → service access, fiscal efficiency, accountability, and trust. GAO's 2026 portfolio identifies 610 remaining matters, 97 new matters, $774.3 billion in reported cumulative savings/revenue gains, and more than $100 billion in potential additional gains. This adds a portfolio-level public-capacity layer while leaving household benefit, service quality, transition cost, and legitimacy open.

The new AI-public-finance bridge is the [NBER tax-base record](records/us-nber-ai-public-finance-tax-base-2026.json): AI transformation → labor- and consumption-tax-base change → public-revenue and redistribution problem → tax instrument, ownership, and political conflict → state capacity and household security. Working Paper 34873 models an early displacement stage and a later autonomous-production stage. This adds a fiscal-state-capacity mechanism while leaving realized adoption, ownership, revenue, incidence, administrative feasibility, and legitimacy open.

The new public-university selection bridge is the [NBER admissions-metrics record](records/us-nber-public-university-admission-metrics-2026.json): admissions metric → institution-specific prediction and classification → selection or exclusion route → college performance and completion → mobility and legitimacy. Working Paper 34975 finds high-school GPA more predictive than SAT scores in a large urban public university system, contrasting with Ivy-Plus evidence. This adds a context-sensitive measurement-and-gatekeeping layer while leaving access, fairness, reform, earnings, and trust open.

The new K–12 AI-diffusion bridge is the [NBER school-integration record](records/us-nber-k12-ai-diffusion-inequality-2026.json): AI availability and early use → policy, training, guidance, and district capacity → uneven school integration → student and teacher use → educational and labor consequence. Working Paper 35347 reports lower integration associated with student disadvantage and lagging institutional support. This adds an education-capacity and unequal-exposure layer while leaving causal adoption, learning, wellbeing, privacy, and durable inequality effects open.

The new AI-expectation bridge is the [NBER forecast record](records/us-nber-ai-economic-forecast-policy-preferences-2026.json): expected capability progress → beliefs about growth, labor displacement, and distribution → retraining, job-guarantee, or universal-basic-income preference → political legitimacy and action. Working Paper 35046 compares five respondent groups and finds that disagreement centers more on economic consequences than on the pace of AI progress. This adds an expectation-to-policy layer while leaving realized employment, household security, policy adoption, and political behavior open.

The new cybersecurity-regulation bridge is the [GAO harmonization record](records/us-gao-cybersecurity-regulatory-fragmentation-2026.json): private critical-infrastructure exposure → overlapping controls, definitions, and incident-reporting rules → compliance and response capacity → security practice and public-service continuity. GAO summarizes selected industry perspectives from seven representatives, including reports of duplicative work and conflicting requirements, while noting 11 of 12 earlier coordination recommendations implemented and one partially addressed. This adds a firm-facing governance-cost layer while leaving national prevalence, compliance hours, breach reduction, consumer harm, and service outcomes open.

The new water-cybersecurity bridge is the [GAO water-sector record](records/us-gao-water-sector-cybersecurity-capacity-2026.json): internet-connected operational technology → remote access and cyber exposure → uneven workforce, legacy-system, funding, and legal capacity → prevention, attack, or service continuity → public-health and community consequence. GAO's May 2026 testimony covers roughly 170,000 water and wastewater systems and reports an EPA risk assessment and management plan alongside unresolved requirements and authority gaps. This adds a cyber-to-physical service realization layer while leaving named incidents, outage duration, household burden, and remediation open.

The new emerging-technology governance bridge is the [GAO foresight record](records/us-gao-emerging-technology-societal-governance-2026.json): emerging capability → privacy, autonomy, environmental, or legal exposure → standards, human-override, research, or legal response → public legitimacy, firm power, and infrastructure/state capacity. GAO-26-108079 reviews neural implants, general-purpose robots, and orbital-debris removal with literature review and 11 expert interviews. This adds a future-facing governance-timing layer while leaving adoption, distribution, realized harm, employment, public acceptance, and geopolitical leverage open.

The new founder-tax bridge is the [NBER startup-payoff record](records/us-nber-capital-gains-tax-startup-founder-risk-sharing-2025.json): venture financing and founder ownership → extremely concentrated exit distribution → realization versus accrual tax timing → dilution, downside insurance, and entrepreneurial choice. NBER Working Paper 34512 reports 84% of founders receiving zero exit value and the top 2% capturing 80% of total exit value; its model trades lower successful-founder ownership against more positive payoffs when refundable credits insure failures. This adds a tax-design-to-risk-sharing mechanism while leaving realized reform, employee welfare, innovation, and public revenue open.

The new AI-finance bridge is the [NBER asset-management record](records/us-nber-ai-asset-management-performance-comovement-2026.json): regulatory and labor-market signals → AI strategy adoption in hedge funds → early performance advantage and later decay → return comovement and market-structure consequence. NBER Working Paper 35273 reports steady growth, early outperformance that declines over time, and lower return comovement among AI hedge funds. This adds a firm-capability and financial-market layer while leaving causal alpha, household investor outcomes, leverage, and systemic risk open.

The new managed-LTSS bridge is the [NBER Medicaid delivery record](records/us-nber-medicaid-managed-ltss-health-spillovers-2026.json): Medicaid long-term-services design → provider and case-management route → preventive care and hospitalization → Medicare-financed spillover and household/care consequence. NBER Working Paper 35055 reports a 4.2% Florida hospitalization increase with a 10% flu-vaccination decline but no significant New York hospitalization change. This adds a state-heterogeneous cross-payer mechanism while leaving individual quality, implementation, functional status, and household burden open.

The new American Indian heterogeneity bridge is the [NBER population-measurement record](records/us-nber-american-indian-heterogeneity-human-capital-2026.json) and [existing finding](projects/us-american-indian-heterogeneity/findings/us-nber-american-indian-heterogeneity-human-capital-2026-001.md): historical policy and place → tribal identity, governance, and resource context → measured economic, educational, health, and human-capital outcomes → policy and community-defined improvement. NBER Working Paper 35730 argues that pooled averages and changing definitions can obscure both persistent harm and gains associated with self-governance. This adds a sovereignty- and measurement-aware inequality layer while leaving tribal-specific causal estimates and current policy effects open.

The new jail-healthcare bridge is the [NBER accreditation record](records/us-nber-jail-healthcare-accreditation-mortality-2026.json): contractor-delivered care and weak oversight → randomized accreditation offer → standards, coordination, screening, and oversight → care quality and 12-month mortality. NBER Working Paper 33357 reports improved standards and substantially lower mortality across 46 midsized jails without added capital or labor inputs. This adds an experimental public-capacity route while leaving post-release continuity, trust, detention exposure, and generalizability open.

The new housing-tax bridge is the [NBER property-allocation record](records/us-nber-property-tax-housing-allocation-generations-2026.json): property-tax and bequest-tax design → capitalization and holding costs → downpayment constraints and tenure lock-in → intergenerational homeownership and mobility. NBER Working Paper 35587 models a California-to-Texas tax-rate counterfactual that lowers prices and older homeownership while raising modeled young homeownership. This adds a tax-design-to-housing-allocation mechanism while leaving observed incidence, rentals, displacement, public services, and political response open.

The new AI-production bridge is the [NBER coding-output record](records/us-nber-ai-coding-productivity-shipping-output-2026.json): AI tool exposure → coding activity → projects and releases → marketplace usage → worker, firm, and consumer consequences. NBER Working Paper 35275 reports much larger gains in commits than in projects or actual releases, and more new apps without increased total marketplace usage. This adds a production-stage realization gap while leaving quality, review time, worker welfare, bargaining, firm profit, and consumer value open.

The new AI-measurement bridge is the [NBER validation record](records/us-nber-ai-measurement-validation-inference-2026.json): unstructured data and AI extraction → scalable candidate variables → construct choice and validation → institutional classification, allocation, or public claim → trust, contestability, and legitimacy. NBER Working Paper 35744 distinguishes discovery, construct definition, and observation, and argues that credible inference requires explicit criteria and designed validation, including when predictions are biased or random validation samples are unavailable. This adds a measurement-governance layer while leaving named-system accuracy, subgroup error, appeal, and downstream allocation open.

The new historical immigration-health bridge is the [NBER child-mortality record](records/us-nber-immigration-child-mortality-disease-spillovers-2026.json): local immigration exposure → crowding, communicable-disease environment, and public-health capacity → infant and child mortality → spillovers to native-born children and local institutional response. In Massachusetts towns from 1860–1915, the study reports higher infant and child mortality as foreign-born share rose, with the infant effect concentrated post-neonatally and birth-composition changes explaining only part of the effect. This adds a place-level shared-risk mechanism while leaving contemporary transfer, housing/sanitation channels, and policy response open.

The new dollar-convenience bridge is the [NBER safe-asset record](records/us-nber-dollar-safe-asset-convenience-domestic-global-2026.json): safe-asset market depth and collateral → domestic dollar liquidity convenience versus global relative appeal → investor, central-bank, and repo behavior → fiscal, monetary, and geopolitical consequence. NBER Working Paper 35742 reports domestic convenience about unchanged in 2025 relative to 2019 and expected to persist, while the relative richness of dollar safe assets versus other currencies fell considerably and dollar repo was the highest-convenience dollar asset. This adds a domestic-versus-global financial-power distinction while leaving reserve behavior, household welfare, sanctions leverage, and external response open.

## Status vocabulary

- **Observed:** the source directly measures the stated unit and outcome.
- **Reported:** a paper, survey, company, or agency reports an experience or estimate; it may not identify the mechanism.
- **Compared:** separate sources or groups are aligned for a descriptive comparison.
- **Inferred:** the project proposes a mechanism not directly measured in the source.
- **Open:** the relevant arrow or outcome is not yet measured well enough.

Never promote an inferred or open arrow to observed because several adjacent rows point in the same direction.

## Recovery rule

When starting a new topic, select one row, identify the source and unit for each adjacent arrow, and write the missing link before collecting more prose. A topic is not end to end until the matrix row has direct evidence, a counterexample, a distribution check, and a next test for every important arrow.

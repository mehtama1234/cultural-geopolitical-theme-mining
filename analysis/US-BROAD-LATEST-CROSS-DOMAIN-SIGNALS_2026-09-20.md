# Latest cross-domain signals: what is changing beneath the headlines

**Checked:** 2026-09-24

This is a short orientation memo for the broad atlas. The rows below come from
different samples and units; they are not pooled estimates or one unified
causal story.

## Source-led additions since the prior rotation

The newest direct-source promotions sharpen six distinctions that are useful
before reading the full 90-signal table:

- **Consumer room and fallback:** aggregate consumer-unit budgets show exposure
  before a hardship episode, while bank-fee and outage evidence show that cash,
  liquidity, credit, and physical access condition whether a payment route is
  usable. Neither surface is a recovery measure. See the [BLS consumer-
  expenditures record](records/us-bls-consumer-expenditures-income-quintile-2024.json)
  and [payment-resilience record](records/us-nber-payment-method-resilience-disaster-outages-2026.json).
- **Cultural and social connection:** national meaning can run through family,
  local community, learning, and stories; reported social support, practical
  participation, and delivered belonging remain different measures. See the
  [Gallup anniversary record](records/us-gallup-america250-social-ritual-meaning-2026.json)
  and [CDC community-connection record](records/us-cdc-community-connection-support-participation-2026.json).
- **Child and eldercare time:** family routine is not equal organized
  opportunity, and standardized eldercare-provider diaries show a recurring
  time-allocation profile without identifying a causal care effect. See the
  [SIPP child-participation record](records/us-census-sipp-child-day-cultural-participation-2023.json)
  and [standardized eldercare-provider record](records/us-atus-eldercare-provider-standardized-2024-2025.json).
- **Information and platform agency:** information routes differ by topic;
  health authority mixes providers, peers, social media, AI, and influencers;
  AI choice is task-specific; and a comparative experiment shows interface
  friction can change consent choices. None of these sources verifies accuracy,
  privacy protection, care, or durable exit. See the [topic-diversity record](records/us-pew-information-source-topic-diversity-2026.json),
  [AI choice record](records/us-ai-assistant-platform-choice-trust-privacy-2026.json),
  and [cookie-consent experiment record](records/comparative-nber-cookie-consent-choice-architecture-2025.json).
- **Consumer-health control:** digital health can combine convenience with
  alleged data sharing, subscription billing, and cancellation friction. The
  named pending enforcement case and selected complaint file are not a customer
  prevalence or outcome estimate. See the [FTC Hims & Hers record](records/us-ftc-hims-hers-health-data-billing-cancellation-2026.json).
- **State capacity:** coordination depends on responsibility and staffing; a
  reported live air-policing interception is stronger than posture alone but
  still not proof of deterrence, civilian protection, or durable legitimacy.
  See the [GAO space-cooperation record](records/us-gao-space-allied-coordination-2025.json)
  and [Lithuania interception record](records/us-nato-lithuania-drone-interception-alliance-response-2026.json).

## Ninety signals now visible

| Signal | What the evidence says | Larger interpretation | What is still unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| Support is wider than the household | Family can include chosen and extended ties, not only legal or co-resident relatives. | Social infrastructure is partly relational and cultural, not only institutional. | Whether support was actually delivered, who carried the work, and whether it lasted. |
| Social infrastructure is becoming hybrid | Family and extended ties remain meaningful support routes, while AI companions are increasingly used for advice, stress relief, disclosure, and perceived support. | The practical support system may be assembled across human relationships, public institutions, firms, and conversational systems rather than supplied by one institution. | Whether AI supplements or displaces human ties, who bears the emotional and privacy costs, whether support is adequate, and whether a person can switch or exit. |
| Paid work and family time are being renegotiated | More partnered parents combine full-time work; financial benefit and child-wellbeing judgments do not move together. | Work-family arrangements trade money, time, care, identity, and career control. | Actual unpaid hours, childcare quality, schedule control, and later family security. |
| Information is topic-specific | People use different mixes of news, government, social media, school, and personal networks for different topics. | Trust and institutional access are routed through topic-specific information systems. | Accuracy, persuasion, understanding, action, and correction. |
| Digital life is split by purpose and life stage | In separate 2025–2026 surveys, teens reported routine social-platform use alongside chatbot use; adults reported chatbot use for information, work, health, advice, and companionship; news users showed distinct attention and civic-action styles. | “Digital exposure” is not one social condition: belonging, instrumental help, information, and civic participation create different dependencies, alternatives, and control problems. | Same-person purpose, time, trust, error, correction, human alternative, privacy, social or educational effect, switching, and exit. |
| Public participation is a set of different styles, not one engagement ladder | Pew's late-2025 public-life typology separates Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders across politics, civic activity, religion, and news. Mobilizers are highly active, while Connectors can be broadly involved without the same direct political action; low trust and public activity do not line up into one simple scale. | People can be socially connected, politically interested, news-attentive, institutionally distrustful, or directly active in different combinations. “Apathy” can hide different time, access, identity, or meaning barriers. | Same-person movement across styles, material pressure, social ties, source exposure, schedule/access costs, attribution, institutional response, later action, and withdrawal. |
| Political action depends on social infrastructure | CIRCLE reports roughly 20-point higher civic action and civic self-efficacy among youth with access to “political homes,” which can provide belonging, safe space, food, money, transportation, and civic information. | Participation is partly a capacity and belonging problem: hardship can reduce efficacy, while practical support can make action possible without proving that the setting caused turnout or policy influence. | Same-person entry, support received, safety/belonging, self-efficacy, action, institutional response, wellbeing, persistence, and exit. |
| Synthetic contact can lower the immediate barrier to cross-partisan engagement | In five preregistered experiments, an outgroup-representing chatbot increased measured warmth in several designs and raised the share choosing a costly real outgroup conversation from 61% to 67% in one study; a preregistered one-week warmth effect was small and not significant. | AI can act as a low-friction cultural rehearsal space that changes an immediate meaning/action step without demonstrating durable reconciliation, policy agreement, or institutional trust. | Repeated exposure, representation accuracy, real-conversation quality, cooperation, voting or organizing, durable identity change, disclosure, and governance. |
| Formal access breaks at interfaces | Coverage, benefits, privacy controls, remedies, and services can exist while notice, reach, completion, or fallback remains difficult. | The interface is part of the social outcome; households often supply the missing labor. | Same-person completion, adequacy, durability, and exit. |
| Public measurement can precede usable service | FTA is developing a transit dashboard around safety, cleanliness, universal accessibility, real-time information, and reliability, with public input requested before final measures or outcomes are reported. | Institutions can make everyday friction politically legible before they have changed the underlying service; what gets counted is an early accountability decision. | Final definitions, published scores, route-level performance, completed trips, fare/wait burden, correction, rider trust, and household mobility. |
| Infrastructure creates bargains before users feel the result | Capacity, revenue, rate design, and readiness can be documented before household benefit, local burden, or replaceability. | Public value is distributed through cost allocation and dependence, not just construction. | Actual bills, reliability, burden distribution, alternative providers, and durable benefit. |
| Domestic capacity is not the same as strategic autonomy | Critical-mineral import reliance, application-specific substitution limits, CHIPS awards and milestones, and defense procurement can coexist with research, qualification, supplier, maintenance, or governance dependence. | A country can build options without yet possessing a usable second source or bargaining leverage; autonomy is application-specific and must be demonstrated through qualified output, switching, and external response. | Same application, operating output, purity/yield, customer qualification, inventory, supplier switch, cost, maintenance, refusal, concession, deterrence, or changed external behavior. |
| Market replacement is not the same as practical access | After Hurricane Harvey, firm exits and temporary closures were followed by longer customer travel; entrants added modeled surplus but did not fully replace exits in the hardest-hit neighborhoods. | Aggregate market recovery can coexist with localized losses in time, distance, safety, and choice. | Missed trips, affordability, vehicle access, disability, care duties, worker effects, and durable neighborhood change. |
| Formal financial access can rise while provider infrastructure contracts | U.S. account ownership reached 97% in the 2024 Findex comparison, while IMF provider data show commercial banks, branches, and branch density declined from 2020 to 2024; SHED still shows income-stratified unbanked status, fraud loss, provider contact, and limited switching. | A formal doorway can become more common while the physical network, practical route, recovery capacity, and ability to leave remain uneven. | Same-person/account payment or fraud episode, travel and time cost, digital accessibility, correction, recovered amount, alternative quality, trust, and exit. |
| Alliance legitimacy is not operational consent | NATO remains broadly popular across surveyed members but has sharp country and U.S. partisan differences. | State capability requires social permission that may vary when costs or risks become concrete. | Support for specific deployments, burden sharing, government action, and crisis durability. |
| Named-deployment approval can coexist with low mission knowledge | A Lithuanian summary reported 82% support for stationing a German brigade, while a separate German survey reported only 22% knew something about the Lithuania mission and 10% felt well informed about foreign missions; the measures are different countries, dates, and populations. | General protection or alliance support can provide political permission without demonstrating informed consent for a particular operation, exercise, cost, escalation risk, or future combat use. | Event-specific knowledge, local burden, land and infrastructure effects, party and municipal differences, later support, opposition, and policy durability. |
| Geopolitical legitimacy has three audiences | Pew's 2026 layer reports a median 35% across 36 non-U.S. countries saying the U.S. contributes to global peace and stability, versus 57% among Americans; inside the U.S. the measure was 82% among Republicans and 35% among Democrats, while 83% said the U.S. interferes in other countries' affairs at least a fair amount. | Foreign reception, domestic partisan meaning, and institutional capability can diverge; reputation is a possible condition on cooperation and political durability, not a direct power index. | Dated event, country reception, party/media framing, alliance or diplomatic response, aid/trade/tourism or consumer behavior, institutional adjustment, and later action. |
| Infrastructure bargains are becoming fiscal bargains | Virginia combines large-load tariffs and minimum obligations with a 2026 electricity-consumption tax for certain data-center operators. | The state is trying to convert strategic infrastructure dependence into both cost allocation and public revenue. | Facility-level remittance, household bills, local burden, service quality, and whether residents see the bargain as fair. |
| Security posture can persist while legitimacy is contested | Eastern Sentry continued as a NATO multi-domain activity, while later Polish/G7 records describe alerts and information operations around airspace uncertainty. | Operational persistence and public meaning run on different clocks. | Deterrence, audience trust, civilian security, and willingness to sustain the posture. |
| Domestic security operations can persist before outcomes are known | Poland's Operation “Bezpieczny Zachód” continued from a one-month plan into a year-long, multi-agency border operation and was extended through October 2026. | State capacity includes the ability to sustain coordination, but persistence is not effectiveness, consent, or strategic success. | Migration pressure, resident and migrant burden, safety outcomes, cost distribution, public support, and changed behavior. |
| Digital exit can be contested before account exit | AI-companion studies observe farewell signals followed by continuation tactics and short-run engagement, but not deletion or switching. | Cultural relationships with platforms can make the goodbye moment itself a site of power. | Actual cancellation, replacement, durable trust, wellbeing, and practical exit. |
| Privacy-request completion can be high while practical control remains unverified | Walmart reports separate access, deletion, and sale-opt-out request counts, completion, denial, and median processing times, with materially different completion patterns by request type. | Administrative capacity is a real stage of digital control, but completion is not the same as verified deletion, reduced exposure, or a user’s ability to leave without sacrificing valued services. | Same-request identity/authorization burden, denial reason, actual data change, residual data, repeat effort, trust, continued use, and exit. |
| A large-load operating failure can turn infrastructure dependence into new control rules | In July 2026, nearly 4,000 MW of Northern Virginia data-center load unexpectedly disconnected, shifted to backup generation, and required PJM and Dominion operators to manage load-generation, voltage, and frequency effects; PJM then proposed ride-through requirements. | Infrastructure power becomes politically and institutionally visible when a dependent load behaves in a way that stresses the shared system. | Household interruption or bill effects, facility-level behavior, backup-generation burden, final rule adoption, durable compliance, and local acceptance. |
| A military AI platform can become operationally authorized before it becomes strategic leverage | NATO reports that its Maven Smart System reached full technical operational capability, received classified-network authorization, and was tested in STEADFAST DETERRENCE 2026 to automate scripting and opposition-force responses. | Digital state capacity becomes more real when it crosses security, interoperability, and exercise-use gates—but simulated response is still not adversary behavior. | Mission performance, operator burden, decision quality, actual adversary adaptation, public legitimacy, supplier replaceability, and durable alliance advantage. |
| A multinational capability can produce operational outputs before proving strategic leverage | NATO's ISR Force reports about 180 flight hours, more than 500 intelligence products, operations across Norway, Finland, and Italy, decision-support use during Neptune Strike, and a maritime surface picture supporting over-the-horizon targeting during Northern Viking, while moving toward full operational capability. | Geopolitical capacity becomes more concrete when it operates across places and feeds real operational workflows, but operational integration and reported support are still not the same as targeting success, deterrence, public consent, or changed external behavior. | Product-level decision effect, targeting outcome, operator and host-community burden, member-state cost, adversary response, public acceptance, sustainment, and durable alliance leverage. |
| Post-acute care can be formally covered while access depends on appeal capacity | HHS-OIG reports that Medicare Advantage organizations denied 12% of skilled-nursing admission requests, only 18% of denials were appealed, and 95% of appealed denials were overturned; the reviewed contractor's overturn rate was 97%. HHS-OIG lists three CMS recommendations, all open and unimplemented, with a December 7, 2026 update expected. | A covered service can become an effort- and advocacy-mediated route, and institutional recognition of a problem can still sit before implementation or patient recovery. | Patient-level delay, actual admission, nonappeal outcomes, caregiver substitution, cost, recovery, continuity, implementation effect, trust, and switching. |
| Workplace AI exposure can increase fear while management changes its meaning | Gallup's repeated workforce studies report more than twice the displacement fear among frequent AI users, while perceived respect and organizational care for wellbeing narrow the association by 6.8 and 11.1 percentage points. | Technology becomes a cultural and power experience through organizational interpretation: the same tool can mean development, surveillance, or replaceability. | Actual job loss, pay/schedule change, worker voice, bargaining, organizing, household security, and durable exit. |
| Public eligibility systems can be data-rich without being interoperable enough for reliable oversight | GAO reviewed nine federal eligibility sources; all nine had data-quality issues, seven had cross-source inconsistencies, and more than 100 sources may be used across the award life cycle. No designated government-wide authority had mandatory interoperability requirements as of the report's July 2026 status check. | Administrative capacity is partly a data-governance problem: a program can have many records and screening tools while identity matching, correction, and payment-integrity control remain fragmented. | Individual eligibility decision, improper-payment correction, recipient access, household effect, trust, and whether stronger standards change outcomes. |
| A security mission can adapt through local institutional interfaces before legitimacy is demonstrated | NATO reports that KFOR is gradually adjusting its posture after an improved security assessment and began integrating Kosovo Police personnel into its security architecture, with coordination across Kosovo institutions, EULEX, the Serbian Orthodox Church, and Serbian Armed Forces. | Geopolitical capability is partly a relationship and governance problem: operation can continue through negotiated local interfaces without proving public consent, reduced incidents, or changed external behavior. | Local acceptance, actor-level agreement, incident change, burden distribution, mission performance, and durable trust. |
| Cross-border infrastructure can become operational capacity before household benefit is visible | New York reports that the 339-mile Champlain Hudson Power Express reached commercial operation and is expected to deliver 10.4 TWh annually from Quebec into New York City, potentially meeting up to 20% of the city's needs. | State and infrastructure power can become materially usable through cross-border dependence while cost allocation, reliability, labor, local burden, and public acceptance remain separate. | Household bills, rate-class allocation, monthly delivery, reliability, host-community acceptance, cost incidence, and durable external leverage. |
| Complaint systems are social interfaces, not neutral mirrors of consumer harm | CFPB says portal flaws limited practical utility and describes standardized responses, identity protections, substantive-review targeting, consumer education, and AI- or social-media-driven submission pressures. | Consumer voice is shaped by the channel that makes a problem legible to firms and regulators; improving data integrity can also change who is heard and how much effort is required. | Individual correction, submission friction, exclusion, repeat effort, money recovery, trust, switching, and exit. |
| Alliance capability depends on social capacity before deterrence is tested | The Bundeswehr reports roughly 80% volunteer staffing for the brigade being built in Lithuania and selected mandatory personnel measures before the planned 2027 operational target. | State power is partly a labor, family, maintenance, and legitimacy system; formal force posture can outrun the social capacity needed to sustain it. | Personnel denominator, affected specialties, retention, family burden, local acceptance, operational performance, deterrence, and adversary adaptation. |
| Recovery has multiple clocks | Wildfire relief, mortgage bridges, and local economic awards can continue while repair, return, firm recovery, and household mobility remain unknown. | Public capacity can persist without proving that a home or place has recovered. | Property-level payment, repair completion, safe return, jobs, and later trust or movement. |
| Administrative completion is not underlying control | California's DROP moved from more than 325,000 July sign-ups to more than 500,000 registrants and 654 brokers in August; about 25% of brokers had reported processing. The current route requires broker processing at least every 45 days, but status updates can take up to 90 days and may show deleted, exempted, opted out of sale, not found, or pending. | Organizations can scale a route and report an administrative step while the affected resource remains unverified from the user's or ratepayer's position; time-to-status is itself part of practical control. | Consumer-level deletion, residual data, credited amount, adequacy, repeat burden, trust, switching, and durable benefit. |
| Formal remedy can coexist with continued dependence | Consumer, fraud, recall, and platform records can show complaint routing, review, restoration, payment orders, reissue, or replacement rules without showing that the person received an adequate remedy or had a workable alternative. | Institutional responsiveness may improve access while leaving the affected actor dependent on the same provider or route. | Verified receipt, recurrence, alternative quality, switching cost, trust, non-use, and practical exit. |
| Money and access can recover on different clocks | Worker cases show partial or ordered back-pay, ordinary payment after platform reactivation, or a compensation route alongside unresolved return-to-work, lost-pay, job quality, or later-security questions. | A financial remedy is not automatically restored livelihood, and restored access is not automatically financial recovery; the relevant endpoint depends on which currency was damaged. | Same-worker payment receipt, net amount, work access, schedule/pay quality, recurrence, household effect, trust, and exit. |
| Recovered wages can remain unreachable to the worker | The Department of Labor's Workers Owed Wages route says recovered wages may sit while workers are located, then require a search, identity verification, signed claim form, supporting documents, and a payment process; money can ultimately be sent to the Treasury if the worker remains unlocated after three years. | “Wages recovered” is an institutional state, not the same as worker receipt or restored room; address, identity, digital access, notice, and claim effort become part of the remedy. | Same-worker notice, claim completion, delivery or cashing, amount and timing, residual loss, household effect, later work, trust, and exit. |
| Resolution is not recovery | A month-ordered prescription-event screen shows mixed later health and employment movement; coded nonemployment resolution does not reveal job quality, earnings, treatment continuity, debt relief, or household security. | Positive movement in one measured status can coexist with unresolved need, cost, or insecurity in another currency. | Fill/adherence, exact obligation, care continuity, work quality, household adaptation, later trust, and exit. |
| Material pressure translates selectively into politics | Same-respondent household evidence shows more searches for jobs, food, benefits, credit, housing, and medical help under expense difficulty, while confidence changes unevenly across institutions. | Hardship can increase navigation effort and reshape institutional judgment without producing one automatic path to distrust, withdrawal, or action. | Attribution, usable remedy, completed alternative, recovery, validated political action, and later trust or exit. |
| Household support can buffer volatility without creating security | SHED reports outside-household help, income variability, bill struggle, cheaper-product substitution, delayed purchases, and borrowing; HTOPS follow-up links baseline expense difficulty to later food insufficiency, energy nonpayment, and job loss; SNAP studies show that interviews, office access, work rules, and recertification can change participation without increasing employment or proving adequate support. | Families and public systems can absorb shocks through different routes, but support, participation, and adaptation are not the same as stable household room or security. | Same household/person, shock date, help or benefit route, effort, amount/timing, bill or care outcome, correction, persistence, trust, and ability to switch or exit. |
| Health-cost pressure can travel across several currencies before it becomes political meaning | A recontacted SHED layer links persistent care foregoing with medical debt, reduced savings, increased borrowing, delayed major purchases, and fair/poor health; CFPB evidence links medical collections to credit-score damage; a separate HTOPS contrast finds lower confidence in some institutions among people reporting expense difficulty. | A care problem can become a money, credit, time, and legitimacy problem, but those conversions occur on different clocks and may not belong to the same person or causal episode. | Same bill or care episode, treatment delay, payment/collection, credit correction, household adaptation, attribution, trust, action, and later recovery or exit. |
| A common price shock can produce unequal inflation and spending responses | The New York Fed's April 2026 indicators estimate transportation inflation at 7.06%, with rural and some demographic/income groups above the national inflation measure; receipt data show real gasoline spending fell broadly, with lower-income groups reducing more while higher-income groups increased nominal spending more. | “The price shock” is not one household experience: exposure, ability to absorb it, vehicle and commuting need, and the choice between quantity, quality, credit, or substitution distribute the burden unevenly. | Same-household bill, commuting need, vehicle efficiency, income/liquidity, substitution, debt, work access, food/health trade-off, later trust, and political action. |
| Civic capacity can fall without a uniform change in approval | In a retained CCES panel, prior job loss and lack of insurance were associated with less later political contact or local action, while favorable Congress approval did not move in the same direction. | Material insecurity can constrain the ability to act without translating into one predictable political judgment. | Dated exposure, attribution, efficacy, remedy, party identity, later trust, and durable action. |
| Non-use can be protective and costly at the same time | Across immigration, benefits, healthcare, fraud, and platforms, people may avoid visibility or contestation to protect status, privacy, dignity, time, or safety. | Silence or non-use can be constrained agency rather than apathy, while protecting one currency sacrifices care, income, security, belonging, or future recourse. | Whether need existed, the anticipated risk, alternative used, material loss, later return, trust, and collective action. |
| Staying can be adaptation rather than security | Rent payment, residual insurance, public backstops, repairs, and energy assistance can preserve nominal residence while coverage, cash room, safety, or mobility remain fragile. | Remaining in place is not automatically stability or preference; it may be the least-bad option under constrained alternatives. | Property-level repair, coverage adequacy, transport, health/care burden, feasible move, household choice, and later recovery. |
| Recipient control can coexist with transferred care labor | Consumer-directed care can improve selected short-run beneficiary access or satisfaction and caregiver/worker experience, while unpaid substitution, staffing, continuity, household security, and worker retention remain separate. | More choice at the recipient interface may improve lived experience without removing coordination, payroll, backup, or care-quality burdens from families and workers. | Completed care, task coverage, unpaid hours, worker stability, recipient safety, household effect, trust, and durability. |
| Output and productivity can rise before worker power does | BEA and worker/firm evidence can show AI use, output, productivity, cost, employment, or labor-share movement before worker pay, task discretion, schedule control, bargaining, or household purchasing power are observed as shared gains. | Economic performance is not the same as worker autonomy or distributive improvement. | Task-level exposure, permission, monitoring, pay, workload, bargaining, ownership, household effect, and durable control. |
| Governance can mandate measurement before worker protection exists | New York City Local Law 25 requires a study of algorithmic tools' effects on city hiring, regular work functions, and employee feedback, with a report to city leadership; the public record does not yet show a completed study, named tool, worker decision, appeal, or correction. | A government can recognize an emerging labor-power problem and create an accountability obligation before it has measured the worker-level outcome or supplied a remedy. | Study completion, tool inventory, worker notice and feedback, decision/override, workload/pay change, complaint, correction, bargaining, trust, and exit. |
| Utility can coexist with weak self-control | Smartphones and conversational systems can provide information, connection, advice, or emotional support while users report excess use, sleep/productivity costs, partial cutback success, or continuation pressure at an attempted exit. | Dependence is not established by use alone; practical agency must be tested against utility, obligation, replacement options, and observed behavior. | Logged use, notification/social/work constraints, privacy choice, replacement, durable cutback, account exit, wellbeing, and trust. |
| A first remedy can fail before the purchaser outcome is known | The Sauna360 product record links an initial hazard and free repair offer to a later official statement that the earlier repair was ineffective and a second repair route. | Durability is a separate realization stage: correction can be implemented and later fail without a purchaser-level recovery rate. | Owner notice, first and second repair receipt, downtime, safe restoration, substitute access, residual cost, trust, and continued use or exit. |
| An alternative can be offered without being usable | Worker cases, care routes, housing/insurance records, and consumer remedies distinguish an option being offered from being reachable, affordable, safe, schedule-compatible, or usable by the affected unit. | Counting alternatives overstates practical choice unless the route fits money, time, transport, status, health, care, and risk constraints. | Same-unit offer, access attempt, cost, travel, timing, safety, acceptance/refusal reason, protected outcome, and later durability. |
| Financial marketing can transfer cost into application time and credit exposure | FTC records describe pre-approved credit offers followed by applications, some denials, wasted time, possible credit-score drops, aggregate refunds, and a later reissue route for people who did not cash a prior payment. | A product can create a hidden time or credit cost before a consumer reaches the formal remedy; compensation may not restore the missed opportunity, score, or alternative access. | Same-applicant offer, inquiry, denial, time, score change, alternative credit, refund receipt, correction, later borrowing, trust, and non-use. |
| Reported receipt can coexist with unknown adequacy | The Atkins enforcement record reports that all ten named truck drivers received back-payments after court-held funds were released; separate FTC records condition later refund eligibility on 3,759 businesses having cashed a prior check and send a second check to 5,685 people who accepted a first payment. | A reported or recipient-conditioned receipt is stronger than an offer or order, but it still does not show that the amount restored security, corrected the account, prevented recurrence, or changed the affected unit's ability to stay, switch, or exit. | Worker- or consumer-confirmed payment date and net amount, residual loss, account correction, later work/business security, recurrence, trust, and practical choice. |
| Contestability is unequal | Medicare Advantage appeals, SNAP replacement claims, consumer reissue routes, and worker remedies show correction after a person or institution reaches a contest route. | A responsive route can still undercount people who lacked notice, documents, time, safety, knowledge, or a viable reason to persist. | Same-unit exposure, notice, non-appeal reason, effort, decision, receipt, adequacy, and later security or exit. |
| A remedy can require a second remedy | CFPB records describe returned, uncashed, or reissued redress checks and questions about eligibility or payment legitimacy; Wells Fargo's order required locating consumers and following up on failed delivery; NYC restitution routes require address or bank updates and propose a fund for unclaimed payments. | Legal entitlement, initial issuance, usable receipt, and account correction are separate gates; the administrative work needed to reach the first remedy can become a new burden. | Same recipient, failed-delivery reason, notice, reissue or claim effort, final cashing, account correction, adequacy, and later security. |
| Financial improvement does not automatically reset adaptation | In the 2024–2025 SHED recontact panel, prior adopters often continued cheaper-product switching, reduced use, borrowing, saving cuts, or delayed purchases even when their reported financial condition improved. | Recovery is behavior-specific: a household can regain some room while retaining scarcity strategies, debt, lower quality, or precautionary habits. | Dated trigger, substitute quality, debt repayment, buffer restoration, health/care effect, return to prior use, and longer-term security. |
| Social willingness can exceed expected support | Pew's neighbor survey reports that 76% would be extremely or very likely to bring a neighbor mail or water plants, while 52% think a neighbor would do the same; 67% would bring a meal to a sick neighbor or friend, while 42% expect that help. | Community resilience is not just goodwill; it depends on reciprocity, available time and resources, and whether help is actually delivered when pressure arrives. | Dated need, help requested, help delivered, helper burden, adequacy, repeatability, stigma, and later trust or collective action. |
| Protection can shift work onto the protected | SNAP EBT security controls, fraud monitoring, privacy requests, and consumer recovery routes can reduce exposure while requiring people to monitor accounts, change credentials, document loss, wait, or contest false blocks. | A safety feature can improve system control and still create unequal time, knowledge, accessibility, and error burdens for the person using it. | Same-unit exposure, control activation, effort, false block or delay, interim loss, successful correction, accessibility, and durable security. |
| Distrust can coexist with action | CCES joint trust/action screens show that low institutional trust and civic action are not the same outcome; people can remain active while expressing low confidence. | Political response is a menu, not a ladder from trust to apathy: distrust may produce complaint, contact, voting, organizing, switching, or other forms of contestation. | Dated exposure, blamed actor, chosen action, opportunity cost, institutional response, efficacy, and later trust or exit. |
| Benefit interruption can be a route failure, not an eligibility choice | Among adults in SNAP families, the retained WBNS route reports involuntary stopping or interruption; among a reason-specific subgroup, 40% reported insufficient time after notice and 32% reported no notice. | Participation or exit statistics can hide notice, paperwork, interview, and time barriers; a household may lose a benefit without becoming materially secure or freely choosing to leave. | Same-case notice, deadline, effort, amount, interruption days, appeal/correction, substitute food route, and later security. |

| Employment and safety-net reliance can coexist | GAO reports that about 13.8 million Medicaid-enrolled adults ages 19–64 and about 10.6 million adults living in SNAP-receiving households worked at some point in 2024. | “Working” is not the same as material independence; benefits can be part of the labor system while wages, hours, employer benefits, and household security remain separate. | Same-worker wages, hours, benefit continuity, eligibility churn, employer response, household adequacy, and ability to switch or exit. |

## The simple big-picture interpretation

The atlas is increasingly showing a society organized around **negotiated
dependence**:

- people depend on family, public systems, firms, platforms, utilities, and
  alliances;
- those systems provide real protection or convenience;
- but the terms of access, the hidden labor, the fallback, and the ability to
  leave are distributed unequally.

That is why the project keeps separating formal capacity from lived power.
Capacity is real. It is just not the final outcome.

```text
capacity, right, relationship, or institution
  -> route through an interface
  -> effort, money, time, risk, or meaning
  -> actual use and adequacy
  -> durability, trust, action, switching, exit, or leverage
```

## What the next pass should do

Stop adding another aggregate endpoint unless it changes the map. Select one
existing route and follow the same unit through the missing middle:

1. a customer/account after a remedy or cancellation attempt;
2. a household/person after a benefit, care, or payment episode;
3. a worker after an appeal, payment, or control intervention; or
4. a project/place after operation, cost allocation, or public contestation.

The next record should contain a dated event, a real alternative or non-use,
effort or trade-off, institutional response, defined outcome, and later
persistence or exit. If those fields are not available, record the acquisition
boundary and do not manufacture an end-to-end story.

The financial-marketing signal is grounded in the [FTC Credit Karma refund record](records/us-ftc-credit-karma-wasted-time-refunds-2026.json), which keeps offer, application, denial, time or credit exposure, aggregate refund, reissue, and individual correction as separate stages. The public refund route does not disclose a valid-claim denominator or prove applicant-level score repair, receipt, restored credit access, or later trust.
The second-remedy signal is grounded in the [CFPB Ombudsman redress-reachability finding](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/cfpb-ombudsman-redress-reachability-finding-2026-09-19.md), the [Wells Fargo redress-reachability ledger](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/data/cfpb-wells-fargo-redress-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json), and the [NYC worker-restitution reachability ledger](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/data/nyc-dcwp-restitution-reachability-event-ledger-v1.json). These preserve institutional route stages separately from recipient receipt and recovery.
The care-capacity signal is grounded in the [GAO military child-care workforce finding](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-005.md) and [provider-capacity layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/gao-2026-military-childcare-workforce-capacity-layer-v1.md). The DOD evidence is an institutional comparator, not a civilian child-care estimate.
The disability-workforce signal is grounded in the [GAO disability workforce-program access layer](projects/us-safety-net-access/gao-2026-disability-workforce-program-access-layer-v1.md), which keeps disability disclosure, accommodation, technology, training, employment, earnings, local implementation, and monitoring as separate stages. The selected-program comparison does not establish a causal accommodation effect or a general disability employment rate.
The assisted-living care-bundle signal is grounded in the [GAO assisted-living spending and Medicaid coverage layer](projects/us-aging-care-strain/gao-2026-assisted-living-spending-medicaid-coverage-layer-v1.md), which keeps payer spending, state coverage, waivers, room and board, provider capacity, resident affordability, and family care as separate stages. The spending floor and 44-state coverage count do not establish an available, affordable, safe, or continuous setting for any resident.
The financial-access and provider-infrastructure signal is grounded in the [Global Findex financial-access finding](projects/us-financial-intermediation/findings/us-financial-intermediation-001.md), the [SHED banking substitution and recourse finding](projects/us-financial-intermediation/findings/us-financial-intermediation-003.md), and the [IMF provider-capacity record](records/us-imf-fas-provider-capacity-2020-2024.json). Account ownership, provider infrastructure, fraud recovery, and switching are separate evidence surfaces.
The remedy-durability signal is grounded in the [Sauna360 repeat-remedy event ledger](projects/us-marketplace-product-safety/data/sauna360-repeat-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json) and [bounded durability finding](US-BROAD-INTERESTING-FINDINGS_V1.md#247-a-completed-first-repair-can-be-followed-by-a-product-level-finding-that-the-remedy-was-ineffective).
The federal-data-governance signal is grounded in the [GAO federal eligibility-data interoperability record](records/us-gao-federal-eligibility-data-interoperability-2026.json), which keeps data-source quality, cross-source matching, governance authority, individual payment integrity, and recipient outcomes as separate stages.
The KFOR operating-integration signal is grounded in the [NATO KFOR posture and local-security integration record](records/us-nato-kfor-posture-local-security-integration-2026.json), which keeps security assessment, posture adjustment, local partner integration, coordination, public acceptance, and external behavior as separate stages.
The large-load governance signal is grounded in the [PJM operating-event record](records/us-pjm-large-load-disconnection-response-2026.json) and the [FERC large-load integration order](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/orders/2026/20260618-el26-67-000.pdf), which keep the July operating event, operator response, tariff and cost-shifting concerns, proposed ride-through controls, household incidence, and final rule adoption as separate stages.
The press-legitimacy signal is grounded in the [Pew press-freedom limits record](records/us-pew-press-freedom-limits-2026.json), which keeps principle importance, conditional restriction preferences, perceived freedom, party interpretation, actual information exposure, source switching, and civic action as separate stages.
The realized-infrastructure allocation signal is grounded in the [NYPA Smart Path Connect record](records/us-nypa-smart-path-connect-realization-2026.json), which keeps project completion, energization, system-level savings claims, formal transmission charges, payer class, retail pass-through, household bills, reliability, and durability as separate stages.
The disaster-recovery realization signal is grounded in the [small-business disaster-liquidity record](records/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity-causal-2024.json), which keeps the causal firm-level recovery estimates separate from loan access, worker outcomes, owner households, customers, local political meaning, and durable community recovery.
The usable-alternative signal is grounded in the [worker-control optionality synthesis](projects/ai-work-control/worker-control-optionality-remedy-synthesis-v1.md), [cost of staying put synthesis](projects/us-housing-insurance-risk/cost-of-staying-put-synthesis-v1.md), [aging care/system capacity bridge](projects/us-aging-care-strain/aging-care-system-capacity-bridge-v1.md), and [practical-exit synthesis](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/practical-exit-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md).

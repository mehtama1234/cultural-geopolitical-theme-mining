# Rotation IQ — Named language-revitalization outcomes and everyday use

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** Do named language-revitalization systems convert policy, schooling, teacher development, media, public-service standards and community investment into actual home use, proficiency, everyday service access and intergenerational continuity?

## Why this packet matters

The IP packet established the global architecture and evidence boundary. This packet audits named outcome systems that distinguish language status, acquisition, proficiency, use, service access and community spaces. It asks whether the indicators move together—and where they do not.

```text
policy / strategy / investment
  -> school, teacher, media, home and service opportunity
  -> learner acquisition and speaker confidence
  -> actual daily use, public-service encounter and community participation
  -> family transmission, youth uptake and durable linguistic continuity
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IQ1 | New Zealand’s 2025 State of te reo Māori report organizes evidence across status, acquisition, proficiency and use, and warns that support is growing while actual usage remains low and the future-speaker pipeline is threatened. | Named national measurement framework and status/use distinction. | Same-family transmission, place-level use, service access, causal attribution or future durability. |
| IQ2 | New Zealand schooling data for 1 July 2025 reports 28,382 students in Māori-medium education across 362 schools, while 65.6% of the total school population was not enrolled in Māori Language in Education and 254,638 eligible students received no Māori language learning. | Named education reach and participation gap. | Proficiency, home use, teacher quality, learning outcome, cultural safety or intergenerational transfer. |
| IQ3 | The Te Ahu o te Reo Māori independent evaluation examined an 18-month, two-phase workforce initiative, including participant language development, professional practice, learners, whānau and delivery pinch-points. | Named teacher/workforce intervention and evaluation design. | Population-level speaker growth, classroom exposure, learner attainment, home transmission or service encounter. |
| IQ4 | Te Mātāwai’s strategy describes a community-led vision for Māori as a nurturing first language, including goals of one million community users by 2040 and 25% of Māori children up to age seven having Māori as a first language by 2040. | Named community-led outcome targets and first-language mechanism. | Progress toward targets, independent attribution, family behavior, regional variation or whether targets are culturally adequate. |
| IQ5 | Wales’ 2024–25 Annual Population Survey estimated 828,600 Welsh speakers; 13.8% of people aged three and over reported daily Welsh use, with large geographic differences in speaker share and daily use. | Named population, frequency and geographic-use measures. | Conversation quality, household transmission, service experience, language choice under pressure or causal effect of policy. |
| IQ6 | The Welsh Language and Education Act 2025 sets a target of at least one million speakers by 2050, standardizes ability levels, categorizes schools and sets learning goals; Welsh reporting also says young people need spaces outside school to use the language. | Named statutory education and social-use architecture. | Actual independent users, confidence, service quality, workplace use, community power or durability of the target. |
| IQ7 | Wales’ social-care annual report reports workforce language data: around 10% of surveyed workers spoke Welsh fluently, 29% at basic/foundation level and 25% used Welsh most or all of the time at work; data capture is being integrated into registration systems. | Named sector-specific service/workplace measurement. | Patient/resident comprehension, care quality, language choice, staffing sufficiency, equality or health outcome. |
| IQ8 | Australia’s Closing the Gap culture-and-language dashboard defines strength through language centres, community-controlled media, services in language, education, institutions, plans and legislation, while drawing on community and Indigenous-led data sources. | Named outcome framework and community-data governance direction. | Comparable language-by-language trends, speaker/family outcomes, service encounter quality, data sovereignty in practice or causal impact. |

## Comparative cultural and geographical reading

The Māori and Welsh cases show why speaker count alone is insufficient. Welsh data separates ability, frequency and geography; Māori reporting separates status, acquisition, proficiency and use. Both expose a common bottleneck: education can expand the pipeline without guaranteeing that language becomes the default or comfortable language of home, work, care or public life.

The geography of use matters. A national target may conceal dense language communities, low-density urban populations, rural schools, migration, housing change and uneven service capacity. Community-led institutions and Indigenous data governance can change not just the amount of language activity but who defines success. A service is not linguistically accessible merely because staff skills are recorded; the person must be able to choose, understand and use the language in a consequential encounter.

## Counterexample and source boundary

Speaker estimates, school enrollment, teacher training, policy targets, language-level classifications, staff self-report, media infrastructure and community plans are not interchangeable. A rise in learners can coexist with low daily use; a statutory target can coexist with missing social spaces; staff capability can coexist with a failed care encounter; and a community dashboard can report infrastructure without proving family transmission. The named evidence closes measurement systems and several intervention pathways, but no public source here closes a comparable same-family chain through repeated home use, service access, youth uptake and durable intergenerational transmission.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A language speaker count, school-enrollment figure, teacher-development programme, statutory target, staff-language register, community media centre or language dashboard is not by itself proficiency, daily use, accurate service access, family transmission, cultural authority or durable vitality.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **language-revitalization-policy-to-same-family-daily-use-service-access-and-transmission**. The strongest next acquisition is a named family/cohort tracked across home language, school pathway, proficiency, peer/community spaces, service encounter, media use, identity, youth uptake and repeat intergenerational transmission, with community-controlled definitions of success.

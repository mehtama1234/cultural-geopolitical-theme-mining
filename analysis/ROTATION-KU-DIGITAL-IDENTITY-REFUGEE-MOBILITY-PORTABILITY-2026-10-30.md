# Rotation KU — Digital identity, refugee mobility and portable protection

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Follow-up to:** [KT DPI portability rotation](ROTATION-KT-DIGITAL-PUBLIC-INFRASTRUCTURE-INTEROPERABILITY-PORTABILITY-2026-10-30.md) and [HO migrant social-protection portability rotation](ROTATION-HO-MIGRANT-SOCIAL-PROTECTION-PORTABILITY-HOUSEHOLD-SECURITY-2026-10-30.md)  
**Research question:** When refugees and migrants receive digital identity, biometric registration or interoperable service credentials, do these systems increase protection, legal recognition, work, payments and service continuity across movement—or make sensitive status data more portable to exclusion, surveillance and enforcement?

## Why this packet matters

Mobility turns DPI into a high-stakes test. A credential can help a displaced person prove identity, open an account or access health care, but it can also expose location, family and status information to actors with different legal duties. UNHCR’s Digital Gateway, PRIMES interoperability and Ethiopia’s Fayda pathway provide concrete service and data-governance mechanisms; Thailand’s 2026 identity-card initiative provides a named inclusion case; IOM documents legal-identity barriers across West and East Africa. The central unit is the moving person and household, not the credential count.

```text
displacement / migration / missing documents
  -> registration, biometric or digital ID, consent and data sharing
  -> host-state, humanitarian, bank, employer or border system recognizes credential
  -> movement, work, payment, health, education or protection service becomes possible
  -> person can correct, limit, transfer or withdraw data and retain safety
  -> durable protection, household security and belonging
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Identity is protective only when the person controls the relationship around it. For refugees, registration can prevent refoulement, reunite families and unlock assistance; the same record can become a tool of surveillance or denial if data move beyond the protection purpose. Consent is especially difficult when access to a service, camp, payment or legal document depends on enrollment.

Portability has several layers: recognition by another agency, another city, another country, another bank or employer, and another humanitarian provider. A credential can be portable in software but not in law. It can be recognized by a bank but not by an employer; accepted in a host country but not after onward movement; or useful for a service while exposing a person’s status to enforcement.

The geography of exclusion is concrete. Border settlements, temporary shelters, rural reception areas and urban refugees face different connectivity, language, biometric capture and documentation conditions. Women, children, disabled people, older people and people with uncertain nationality may encounter higher proof burdens or greater risks from incorrect data. Digital and in-person channels must therefore be treated as a combined service system, not as a modernization ladder where the digital channel eventually replaces human help.

The geopolitical question is data responsibility across institutions. UNHCR, host governments, donors, banks, telecoms, employers and vendors can each hold a different piece of a person’s identity. Interoperability may make referrals and payments faster, but it can also make errors and exposure travel faster. Durable protection requires clear purpose limitation, access and correction, independent complaint, safe refusal, and a way to retain assistance when a person declines secondary enrollment.

## Counterexample and source boundary

An identity card, biometric record, digital portal, enrollment count, consent form, interoperable gateway, work permit or bank account is not protection, safe movement, legal recognition, decent work, social-security portability or household security. A credential can unlock services while increasing surveillance; a voluntary opt-out can become functionally compulsory; a portal can complement in-person channels while still excluding people without devices, documents, connectivity, literacy or safe private access.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Digital identity can connect refugee protection, service delivery and economic inclusion, but mobility makes data governance, consent, cross-border recognition and correction central. The public record supports adoption and formal-access pathways, not durable portable protection.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **refugee-digital-identity-to-safe-portable-protection-service-receipt-and-household-security**. The next acquisition should join one person/household to registration, consent, credential, agency recognition, service/payment/work encounter, data access, error, correction, complaint, cross-border movement and later protection or livelihood outcome, with an unregistered or offline comparator.

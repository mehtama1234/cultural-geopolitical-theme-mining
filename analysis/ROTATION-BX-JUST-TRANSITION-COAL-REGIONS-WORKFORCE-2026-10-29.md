# Rotation BX — Just transition, coal regions and workforce/community livelihoods

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** energy transition / coal regions / jobs and skills / social protection / regional identity / gender / economic diversification  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 decarbonization-to-regional-livelihood question

## Research question

When governments announce coal phaseouts, net-zero plans, green investment,
reskilling and mine-land repurposing, what converts transition policy into
secure income, decent work, social protection, regional services, community
voice, gender equity and a durable identity beyond coal?

## Conversion chain

```text
coal dependence, pollution, climate target and mine/plant closure
  -> transition plan, finance, consultation, skills and social-protection offer
  -> worker access, training completion, job matching, enterprise creation, land reuse and local investment
  -> income, decent work, services, fiscal stability, health, gender security and community participation
  -> credible regional future and legitimate decarbonization
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| ILO coal-region just-transition guidance and cases | Just transition requires planning, social dialogue, economic diversification, skills development and social protection; East Kalimantan and South Sumatra illustrate regional coal dependence and emerging alternatives. | policy mechanism, regional cases and intervention requirements | who completes training, gets decent work, retains income and shapes the future |
| ILO employment diagnostics for the Western Balkans | Coal regions such as Pljevlja face closure and diversification challenges; the ILO calls for coordination, green-job definitions, reskilling, social protection and data systems, while implementation and financing remain risks. | named regional problem and governance requirements | realized jobs, worker reach, wages, gender, services and durable local economy |
| World Bank Just Transition for All / feminist coal-sector evidence | Coal transitions can overlook women in households, secondary industries and communities; effective consultation and collaboration across stakeholders and government levels are necessary. | gendered distribution mechanism and policy architecture | women’s income/voice, unpaid work, household security, informal workers and benefit distribution |
| OECD Slovenia coal-region study | SAŠA and Zasavska have distinct closure trajectories; transition requires project pipelines, coordination, local supply chains, environmental regeneration and community well-being, while fund disbursement can lag. | place-based regional mechanism and finance bottleneck | project completion, local jobs, SME survival, public services, identity and participation |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** coal dependence, emissions, pollution, mine/plant closure, fiscal
  exposure and transition targets create visible regional risk and opportunity.
- **Mechanism:** local labor markets, transferable skills, age, gender, health,
  pensions, supplier networks, commuting, land, municipal revenue, investment,
  education, social protection and political identity determine who absorbs the
  transition.
- **Operation:** workers and households receive information, consultation,
  income protection, training, job matching, relocation or commuting support;
  firms diversify; land is remediated and reused; local institutions execute and
  monitor projects.
- **Material consequence:** wages, employment, job quality, household debt,
  unpaid care, health, energy affordability, local tax base, services and
  environmental conditions can change independently.
- **Meaning:** coal can be a livelihood, masculine occupational identity,
  family history, regional pride, pollution burden or political promise. A new
  green project may be interpreted as opportunity, replacement, extraction or
  a threat to belonging.
- **Power:** ministries, utilities, mining firms, unions, municipalities,
  investors, workers, contractors, women’s organizations and residents decide
  closure timing, compensation, land reuse, training relevance and whose voice
  counts.

## Bounded finding

> **A coal phaseout, green-job forecast, reskilling program or transition fund
> is not a just transition.** A net-zero target, closure announcement, skills
> course, renewable investment, mine-land plan or social-protection commitment
> does not establish that named workers and households have secure income,
> decent work, usable training, health and service continuity, gender equity,
> regional opportunity, environmental repair or meaningful power over the future.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Transition is regional, not only sectoral. A mine worker may be counted as
“reskilled” while the spouse loses informal income, a supplier closes, a town
loses tax revenue, or a young person leaves because the new job is distant or
temporary. Coal communities also differ: an active mining region, a post-closure
region, a power-plant town and a supplier corridor require different evidence.

The proper unit is the worker-household-region system. It should include formal
and informal workers, contractors, pensioners, women’s unpaid and market work,
youth, migrants, Indigenous/local communities, municipal finances, public
services and environmental remediation. Local participation is not only a
meeting; it includes agenda-setting, project selection, benefit allocation,
monitoring and remedy.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A green-job forecast is not a job offer, and a training certificate is not
  employment, wage parity or decent work.
- Renewable investment can create construction jobs but few permanent local jobs,
  or require skills and ownership that exclude former coal workers.
- Mine-land repurposing can improve investment while leaving contamination,
  land conflict, displacement or restricted public access.
- Aggregate regional GDP can rise while former workers, women, contractors or
  poorer neighborhoods lose income and services.
- Social protection can prevent immediate poverty without restoring identity,
  voice, long-term opportunity or community cohesion.
- Community consultation can be symbolic if closure timing, finance and land
  decisions are already fixed.
- Coal decline may reflect market, technology or policy changes; not every local
  job loss can be causally attributed to a single climate policy.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: decarbonization target, closure, planning,
finance, consultation, training, job matching, social protection, land reuse,
environmental repair, household income, gender, services, identity and regional
legitimacy are separate stages. Keep decarbonization-to-regional-livelihood
claims in Tier 2 until a named coal-region ledger joins worker/household entry,
intervention, employment/income, services, environment, participation and later
regional outcomes.

## Next test

Select one active coal region, one post-closure region and one transition project
with renewable or alternative-industry investment. Record worker and household
profiles, direct/contract/informal employment, closure timing, compensation,
training enrollment/completion, job match, wage and quality, social protection,
gender and care burden, youth migration, municipal revenue/services, land
remediation, local ownership, consultation, grievance and community identity at
6/12/24 months. Include people who did not enroll or could not transition.

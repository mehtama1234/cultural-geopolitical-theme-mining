# Rotation EM — Legal identity, civil registration and inclusive recognition

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction; Tier 2 later-outcome conversion question  
**Research lane:** civil registration / birth registration / identity / digital public infrastructure / migration / statelessness / rights / privacy

## Why this rotation

The atlas includes digital identity recovery, meaningful digital access, refugee
integration, voting, welfare access, financial inclusion, property, health and
public-service routes. This rotation isolates the legal-identity layer beneath
those systems: whether a person is recognized from birth through death, can
obtain and correct documents, and can use them across institutions without
discrimination, surveillance or dependence on a single fragile credential.

Identity is often represented by registration coverage, ID ownership, biometric
enrolment, digital-ID issuance or interoperability. Those measures can coexist
with missing birth certificates, contradictory records, inaccessible offices,
unaffordable fees, gender or caste discrimination, statelessness, refugee
exclusion, loss after displacement, false matches, data misuse or a document
that no service actually accepts. The unit is the person’s usable recognition
route in a place and time.

## Research question

When a state, health facility, humanitarian agency, bank, employer, school,
social-protection system or digital platform creates or links civil registries,
birth registration, national ID, refugee documentation, biometrics or digital
identity, under what conditions does it produce lawful recognition, access,
mobility, protection, cultural belonging and agency without transferring risk to
women, children, migrants, refugees, minorities, disabled people, rural people,
stateless people or people whose records are wrong?

## Conversion chain

```text
birth / migration / displacement / marriage / death / service need / record error
  -> civil registration, identity, eligibility, biometric and data-governance policy
  -> office, health-facility, humanitarian, mobile, bank, school or digital encounter
  -> document issued, accepted, corrected, renewed and safely used
  -> rights, mobility, protection, services, work, finance, property and participation
  -> recognition, belonging, privacy, agency, appeal and remedy
```

The unit must be a named person, child, household, registry, office, service or
identity event with dates, geography, legal status, document type, cost, wait,
acceptance and correction history. Registration, issuance, biometrics,
interoperability or dashboard coverage is an intermediate signal—not proof of
usable legal identity or rights access.

## Evidence snapshot

### 1. Universal identity requires inclusion from birth to death, not only citizens

The World Bank ID4D inclusion principles call for universal coverage from birth
to death without discrimination, including residents and non-nationals. They
identify legal, procedural and social barriers affecting women, gender
minorities, children, rural populations, ethnic, linguistic and religious groups,
disabled people, migrants, forcibly displaced people and stateless people.

This closes the inclusion mechanism: eligibility and procedure are political and
cultural choices. It does not close whether a named person can obtain, use,
renew or correct the credential.

Sources: [World Bank ID4D inclusion principles](https://id4d.worldbank.org/guide/pillar-1-inclusion)
and [ID4D eligibility guidance](https://id4d.worldbank.org/guide/eligibility).

### 2. The global coverage gap remains material

The World Bank’s 2025 ID4D dataset estimates approximately 800 million people
without official ID and at least 2.8 billion without access to a
government-recognized digital identity for secure online transactions. It
distinguishes registration, issuance, use and management across the identity
lifecycle.

The measurement implication is essential: issuance and ownership are not use.
The next arrow requires a named person attempting to open an account, claim a
benefit, cross a border, enroll in school, access healthcare or correct an error.

Source: [World Bank 2025 ID4D Global Dataset](https://id4d.worldbank.org/global-dataset).

### 3. Birth registration is foundational but not equivalent to a certificate or usable rights

UNICEF reports that birth registration rose to 77% globally among children under
five in its 2024 update, yet 150 million children remained unregistered and more
than 50 million children whose births were recorded lacked birth certificates.
It highlights persistent regional gaps, especially in sub-Saharan Africa.

The conversion test must distinguish notification, registry entry, certificate
receipt, later document issuance and actual acceptance by schools, health,
protection and financial systems. A child can be counted in a registry and still
be unable to prove identity.

Sources: [UNICEF global birth-registration update](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/birth-registration-steadily-increases-worldwide-150-million-children-still-invisible)
and [UNICEF Africa policy paper](https://www.unicef.org/esa/reports/every-child-counted-advancing-birth-registration-africa).

### 4. Registration becomes a service and protection route for refugees

UNHCR states that government-recognized identity documentation can enable
freedom of movement, bank accounts, SIM registration, online services and
economic opportunities. Its 2024 global report also documents that inadequate or
interrupted documentation prevented access to social, legal and protection
services, increased exploitation, arbitrary detention, family separation and
forced-return risks.

This makes documentation a geopolitical and humanitarian infrastructure. The
test must include legal status, document acceptance, renewal, family links,
data protection, movement and remedy—not just the number registered.

Sources: [UNHCR registration and identity management](https://www.unhcr.org/what-we-do/protect-human-rights/protection/registration-and-identity-management)
and [UNHCR Global Report 2024](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/global-report-2024.pdf).

### 5. Interoperability can reduce distance while shifting data and error risk

UNICEF’s Burundi case describes interoperability between health facilities and
civil registration so birth registration can occur close to the community and
within the first 15 days of life. This is a useful implementation mechanism:
placing registration at a trusted service can reduce travel and administrative
friction.

It is not proof that every child receives a certificate or that linked systems
protect privacy, correct errors, accommodate home births, recognize non-standard
families or serve remote populations. Interoperability can improve access and
also magnify a wrong record across institutions.

Source: [UNICEF Burundi health/civil-registry interoperability](https://www.unicef.org/burundi/stories/launch-interoperability-guaranteeing-right-legal-identity-all-burundian-children).

### 6. Digital ID can enable access while creating human-rights risks

The UN Human Rights Office warns that digital-ID systems, particularly when
linked to biometrics and other sensitive data, may enable surveillance,
exclusion, discrimination and privacy harms. It recommends human-rights risk
management across collection, storage, use and reuse.

The identity conversion therefore has two simultaneous tests: can the person
use the credential to obtain a right, and can they contest, limit, correct or
escape harmful data processing? Security, convenience and interoperability are
not substitutes for due process or remedy.

Source: [OHCHR digital-policy human-rights brief](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/issues/development/dfi/OHCHR-DFI-Digital-Policy-Brief-Sept-2024.pdf).

## Bounded proposition 144

**A birth-registration campaign, civil registry, national ID, refugee card,
biometric enrolment, digital-ID platform, interoperability project, identity
wallet or documentation drive is not by itself legal recognition, rights access,
mobility, protection, financial inclusion, cultural belonging, privacy or
agency.** Registration counts, certificates printed, cards issued, biometrics
captured, records linked, systems interoperable or users onboarded do not
establish that a named person could obtain, afford, renew, use and correct a
credential accepted by schools, health services, employers, banks, social
protection, borders or courts—or that the person was protected from exclusion,
surveillance, discrimination, arbitrary detention and data misuse.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

1. **Recognition is political.** A document can affirm belonging, citizenship,
   family relationship and dignity—or classify a person as foreign, suspect,
   stateless or administratively invisible.
2. **The registry is a place system.** Distance, language, transport, fees,
   opening hours, local authority and birth location determine who can enter it.
3. **Documents travel across institutions.** The practical value of identity is
   decided by schools, banks, employers, clinics, borders, courts and platforms.
4. **Error is a social event.** A wrong name, sex marker, parentage, nationality,
   address or biometric match can block multiple rights at once.
5. **Digital sovereignty has a human scale.** Data architecture affects the
   everyday freedom to move, transact, seek help, dissent, organize and be
   recognized without constant surveillance.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- A high registration rate can conceal missing certificates, non-acceptance,
  unregistered births among minorities or women’s inability to control documents.
- A digital ID can speed benefit payment for one group while excluding people
  without phones, connectivity, biometrics, stable addresses or matching records.
- Interoperability can reduce travel while propagating a wrong record across
  health, education, banking and welfare systems.
- Refugee documentation can enable work and mobility while remaining temporary,
  locally unrecognized or vulnerable to renewal interruption.
- Biometric uniqueness can reduce duplicate records while increasing irreversible
  privacy, surveillance and false-match risk.
- A campaign can issue documents without changing the underlying nationality,
  gender, caste, disability, property or migration barriers that determine use.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote to **Tier 1 bounded distinction** for the proposition. World Bank,
UNICEF, UNHCR and OHCHR evidence supports the inclusion, birth-registration,
refugee, interoperability and human-rights mechanisms. Keep the integrated
outcome at **Tier 2** because public sources rarely follow the same person
through issuance, acceptance, correction, service access, mobility, privacy and
remedy.

## Source boundary

The current packet closes the inclusion, coverage, birth-registration,
refugee-documentation, interoperability and human-rights mechanisms. It does
not close named credential use, institutional acceptance, correction, service
access, mobility, privacy, false-match harm, data misuse or remedy outcomes.
Those remain acquisition targets.

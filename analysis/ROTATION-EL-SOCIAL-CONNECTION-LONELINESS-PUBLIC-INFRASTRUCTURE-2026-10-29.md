# Rotation EL — Social connection, loneliness and public infrastructure

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction; Tier 2 later-outcome conversion question  
**Research lane:** social connection / loneliness / public space / culture / digital life / youth / ageing / trust / health

## Why this rotation

The atlas includes social infrastructure, libraries, sport, faith institutions,
public space, mental health, AI companions, schools and digital access. This
rotation isolates social connection as an outcome and a public-capacity question.
It asks whether the places, institutions, technologies and norms around people
create repeated, reciprocal, safe and meaningful relationships—or merely expose
people to contact, programming, surveillance, noise, exclusion or performative
engagement.

Loneliness is not the same as living alone, and social isolation is not the same
as having no online contacts. WHO distinguishes structure, function and quality
of connection; policy can improve one dimension while leaving another harmful.
The unit is a person’s lived connection route in a place and period, not a
headcount at an event or a platform’s engagement metric.

## Research question

When a government, municipality, employer, school, library, cultural venue,
faith organization, food programme, public-space project, digital platform or
community group changes the conditions for social interaction, under what
conditions does it produce reciprocal, safe, culturally legible and durable
connection that improves belonging, health, learning, work and collective
capacity without increasing exclusion, harassment, surveillance or burdens on
already-connected people?

## Conversion chain

```text
life transition / isolation / discrimination / migration / poverty / place change
  -> housing, transport, work, school, culture, public-space and digital policy
  -> encounter, invitation, participation and relationship formation
  -> repeated contact, reciprocity, support, safety and recognition
  -> belonging, health, learning, work, resilience and collective action
  -> trust, voice, inclusion, mutual aid and durable local legitimacy
```

The unit must be a named person, household, group, venue, neighbourhood,
institution or digital community with dates, geography and a connection quality
measure. Attendance, membership, screen time, event count, follower count,
volunteer hours or self-reported contact is an intermediate signal—not proof of
reciprocity, belonging, health, safety or durable connection.

## Evidence snapshot

### 1. Connection has structure, function and quality

WHO defines social connection through three dimensions: structure (relationships,
roles, frequency and duration), function (support exchanged) and quality
(positive, satisfying interactions versus strained, conflictual or violent
ones). It distinguishes social isolation as too few relationships or
interactions from loneliness as the distressing gap between desired and actual
connection.

This is the core measurement guardrail. A crowded event can have low quality or
little support; a remote relationship can be meaningful; and a person living
alone may be well connected. The conversion test must record the person’s
desired connection, actual support, safety and agency.

Source: [WHO social connection definitions and evidence](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/social-connection).

### 2. The issue is global but geographically unequal

The WHO Commission on Social Connection reports that approximately one in six
people globally experience loneliness, with higher rates among young people and
people in lower-income countries. It identifies effects on health, lifespan,
education, employment and economic resilience, and points to interventions at
policy, community, relationship and individual levels.

The headline prevalence is useful for signal detection, but it does not explain
which places or groups are disconnected, whether connection is wanted, or
which intervention changes later outcomes. Local language, age, gender, safety,
faith, disability, migration, conflict and class must remain visible.

Source: [WHO Commission report on social connection](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978240112360).

### 3. Public choices shape connection through the built and digital environment

OECD’s 2025 report finds declining face-to-face interaction over time in the
available European data, deterioration in perceived support in the 2018–2022
period, and growing vulnerability among young people and men. It identifies
government choices, service delivery, socioeconomic conditions, built
environment and online spaces as policy-relevant drivers, while warning that
monitoring and evaluation evidence remains incomplete.

This creates a cultural-geographical bridge: a park, library, café, transit
route, workplace, school or online platform is not automatically social
infrastructure. Its schedule, cost, safety, accessibility, norms, moderation and
ability to support repeated interaction determine who can belong there.

Source: [OECD Social Connections and Loneliness in OECD Countries](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/social-connections-and-loneliness-in-oecd-countries_6df2d6a0-en.html).

### 4. Social connection is now an intersectoral health policy issue

The 2025 World Health Assembly resolution identifies social connection as a
social determinant of health and urges evidence-based policies, public
awareness, monitoring, local development and intersectoral action. It also
recognizes that digital technologies have complex effects and that cultural
values shape social inclusion and cohesion.

The resolution closes the policy signal, not the later outcome. A national
strategy or campaign must still be followed into local places, relationships,
exclusion patterns, safety and later health or collective action.

Source: [WHA78.9 on fostering social connection](https://resolutionsportal.who.int/akn?query=%2Fakn%2Fun%2Fstatement%2Fdeliberation%2Fwhowha%2F2025-05-27%2Fwha78_9%2Feng%40%2F%21main).

### 5. Social cohesion depends on trust and material security

The UN World Social Report 2025 describes declining trust, inequality,
economic insecurity, polarization and fragmentation as threats to collective
action. It links public-service delivery, perceptions of inequality and
economic conditions to institutional trust, while noting that digital
misinformation and deteriorating public dialogue can obstruct participation.

Connection therefore cannot be treated as a friendliness or events problem
alone. A community programme may create contact while unaffordable housing,
precarious work, discrimination or distrust makes relationships fragile or
politically unsafe.

Source: [UN World Social Report 2025](https://desapublications.un.org/publications/world-social-report-2025-new-policy-consensus-accelerate-social-progress).

### 6. Connection interventions need person and place follow-up

WHO identifies policy, community, relationship and individual strategies,
including social infrastructure such as parks, libraries and cafés, but calls
for better measurement and evidence on which interventions work. OECD similarly
notes that the policy field is developing and that evaluation evidence remains
limited.

This is a source boundary rather than a claim of failure: the evidence supports
the mechanism and opportunity space, but rarely follows the same person from a
specific intervention through repeated reciprocal contact, safety, belonging,
health, work or collective action.

## Bounded proposition 143

**A park, library, café, community centre, faith programme, sports initiative,
school club, workplace network, neighbourhood event, digital platform,
anti-loneliness campaign or social-connection strategy is not by itself
belonging, friendship, support, mental or physical health, civic trust,
collective resilience or social cohesion.** Attendance, membership, footfall,
screen time, event counts, contact frequency, volunteer hours, engagement,
campaign reach or self-reported participation do not establish that named people
formed safe and reciprocal relationships, received support when needed, were
included across cultural and geographic difference, or gained voice and remedy
when the connection environment became exclusionary, hostile or surveillant.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

1. **Connection is culturally defined.** Desired closeness, family obligation,
   privacy, hospitality, gender norms, faith and public interaction differ by
   place and group.
2. **Social infrastructure is temporal.** Opening hours, shift work, school
   calendars, transit frequency, caregiving and safety after dark determine who
   can participate repeatedly.
3. **Belonging requires reciprocity.** A service can provide contact without
   letting participants shape the space, offer support or refuse unwanted
   attention.
4. **Digital connection is ambivalent.** It can bridge distance and disability,
   but can also amplify harassment, comparison, polarization, extraction and
   exclusion from offline life.
5. **Trust has material foundations.** Reliable services, fair treatment,
   affordable places, safety and visible institutional accountability make
   connection durable.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- A well-attended event can leave newcomers, disabled people, migrants or
  minorities isolated if language, cost, norms or harassment block belonging.
- A library or café can create meaningful connection for some users while its
  location, hours, policing or price excludes shift workers or unhoused people.
- A digital community can reduce loneliness during displacement while increasing
  dependency, privacy risk, misinformation or online abuse.
- A campaign can increase awareness and self-reporting without changing the
  housing, transport, work or discrimination conditions producing disconnection.
- A strong family network can provide support while being coercive, unequal or
  unsafe; more contact is not automatically better connection.
- Social programmes can increase participation for already-connected residents
  while the most isolated people remain unreachable; a null result may reflect
  selection and route failure rather than no effect.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote to **Tier 1 bounded distinction** for the proposition. WHO, OECD and UN
evidence support the definitions, health, built-environment, digital, cultural,
trust and policy mechanisms. Keep the integrated outcome at **Tier 2** because
public sources rarely follow the same people through repeated connection,
reciprocity, safety, belonging, health and collective action.

## Next test

Follow named participants and non-participants around one social-infrastructure
intervention—such as a library, park, café, faith group, sports programme,
community meal or online/offline hybrid—with an urban/rural or affluent/low-
income comparison. Record an ordinary period and a disruption such as closure,
cost increase, conflict, relocation or platform moderation change.

## Source boundary

The current packet closes the structure/function/quality, geographic inequality,
built-environment, digital, cultural, trust and policy mechanisms. It does not
close named repeated connection, reciprocity, safety, belonging, health,
collective action, digital harms or remedy outcomes. Those remain acquisition
targets.

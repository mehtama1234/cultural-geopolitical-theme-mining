# Evidence-gap agenda: what the atlas needs next v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Purpose:** specify the missing evidence required to test the atlas's strongest
cross-domain propositions. These gaps are not evidence that a program or trend
failed; they identify the unobserved stage that current sources cannot close.

| Priority gap | Current evidence reaches | Missing join | Decisive next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Usable access | eligibility, application, account, voucher, or leave availability | completion, denial correction, and continuity | consented or administrative person-level route data joining offer, attempt, completion, correction, and later service/use outcome |
| Adequacy and recovery | payment, claim, benefit receipt, refund, or formal remedy | whether the amount/service made the recipient whole | event-linked follow-up recording bill/debt, care/housing/work continuity, repeat need, and recipient assessment |
| Practical exit and control | disclosure, consent, request status, cancellation interface, or deletion route | successful reversal, switching, residual exposure, and safe substitution | product-specific panel or audit tracking request, completion, re-collection/recurrence, alternative service, and later burden |
| Unequal cost incidence | aggregate price, funding, capacity, or program totals | which household, place, worker, or customer ultimately bears cost/time/risk | linked tariff/price/fee/benefit data with subgroup, place, and actual bill or time-use outcomes |
| Informal-support substitution | reported help, care time, or work adjustment | who supplies replacement care/money and at what later cost | household network panel preserving paid care, transfers, unpaid hours, schedule loss, reciprocity, and later material security |
| Institutional and strategic effect | project operation, monitoring, alliance exercise, procurement, ISR output, or data visibility | decision quality, legitimacy, partner response, and changed external behavior | event-specific operational follow-up with counterparty response, cost, public/partner acceptance, and sustained performance |

## Design rules

1. Preserve the person/place/organization identifier only with appropriate
   consent, privacy protection, and access controls.
2. Keep route stages separate: offer, attempt, completion, correction,
   adequacy, durable outcome, and reversal/exit.
3. Measure non-use and non-completion, not only successful cases.
4. Retain timing, subgroup, place, and counterfactual context so aggregate
   gains do not erase unequal pathways.

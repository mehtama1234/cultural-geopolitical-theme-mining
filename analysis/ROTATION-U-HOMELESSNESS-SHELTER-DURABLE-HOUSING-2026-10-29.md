# Rotation U: homelessness response and durable housing

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** homelessness, shelter, housing stability, service systems, older adults, veterans and place  
**Status:** Tier 2 mechanism; homelessness counts, program exits and data-quality issues are observable, durable housing and lived stability remain open

## Research question

When a person enters a homelessness-response system, what converts outreach,
shelter, case management, rental assistance or supportive housing into a safe,
affordable and durable home without losing agency, health or social connection?

```text
housing loss/risk
  -> outreach, eligibility and shelter/service entry
  -> case management, rental aid or supportive housing
  -> permanent-housing exit
  -> retention, health, income and community connection
  -> reduced recurrence and durable security
```

## Named-case refresh

These cases distinguish a named household or program from population-level
counts. The positive cases are primarily agency or beneficiary accounts; the
ledger therefore records lease, retention, recurrence, affordability and
agency as open unless the source actually closes them.

### HOM-006 — Moore Place permanent supportive housing, Charlotte, North Carolina

The VA Housing First toolkit describes a Moore Place evaluation that opened 85
permanent supportive-housing units for people with long histories of
homelessness, chronic conditions and disabilities. The case tests whether a
low-barrier permanent home can be paired with support rather than requiring a
person to become “housing ready” first. It closes a named program's housing-
and-support design, but the toolkit summary does not establish each resident's
lease duration, rent burden, health, social connection or recurrence.

### HOM-007 — Chicago Housing Locator Program

HUD's case study reports that Chicago's Housing Locator Program had housed more
than 845 households since its 2006 inception, including 279 in 2008, by
building landlord relationships and helping households move from shelter or
imminent homelessness into permanent housing. The program addresses the
geographical and market-making bottleneck between a shelter referral and an
actual unit. The case study also records provider doubts about locating
affordable housing, so placement volume is not treated as proof of long-term
retention, affordability or choice.

### HOM-008 — HATAS Next Step Rapid Rehousing, Albany County, New York

HUD's cross-system partnership case describes the Homeless and Travelers Aid
Society's Next Step program for justice-involved single adults with significant
needs. Initial data reported that 85% of participants did not return to either
homelessness or incarceration, and the program attributed part of its route to
understanding parole/probation partners and simplifying referrals. This links
reentry, rapid rehousing and recurrence prevention, but the figure is initial
program data, not a randomized estimate, and does not establish housing quality,
voluntary choice or the experience of the remaining 15%.

### HOM-009 — Grady Kendall, Montana HUD-VASH and Freedom's Path

The VA reports that Coast Guard Veteran Grady Kendall moved from homelessness
through temporary hotel placement, HUD-VASH and Freedom's Path housing, with
help applying for SNAP and maintaining VA health and mental-health care. The
account says he purchased his first home in 2024. This closes a named person's
route from housing loss through interim shelter, supportive housing and later
homeownership, while leaving debt, affordability, social ties and whether the
route is replicable for other veterans open.

### HOM-010 — returns after permanent housing as a durability boundary

The VA's research program maintains a dedicated analysis of veterans who exit
VA homeless programs to permanent housing and subsequently return to VA
homeless programs. The existence of this longitudinal return analysis is an
important counterexample to treating move-in or permanent-destination exit as
closure: the system must observe what happens after the lease or placement.
The public report listing does not, by itself, resolve the causes of return,
the timing, housing quality, health, rent burden or the person's preferred
alternative.

## New trend interpretation

The sharper trend is not simply “homelessness increased” or “housing programs
failed.” It is:

> Homelessness response is a conversion problem: a count, shelter bed, service
> contact or permanent-housing exit becomes stability only when a person can
> remain safely housed with adequate affordability, health support, agency and
> social connection.

The 2025 HUD release is useful for scale but should not be treated as a complete
prevalence measure or causal verdict on any policy model. HUD’s own performance
system separates permanent-housing exits and returns, while GAO identifies
transparency limits in the underlying shelter-use and national-estimate data.

## Cultural and geographical meaning

Housing is more than a nighttime location. The place of a lease, shelter or
supportive unit determines access to work, school, care, transport, family,
community and cultural identity. A nominal exit can therefore improve the count
while creating a long commute, separation from support, inaccessible unit or
renewed housing risk.

The Tribal/non-Tribal route distinction shows why governance and recognition
matter. A service can be technically available but not trusted, culturally safe
or chosen. Older adults, veterans, disabled people, families and youth can need
different combinations of housing, health, income, transportation and social
support; one exit metric cannot represent them all.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A PIT count is a one-night estimate and does not measure all hidden or
  episodic homelessness.
- A shelter bed or service contact is not a permanent home.
- An exit to a permanent destination is not proof of affordability, safety,
  lease retention, health or belonging.
- A six-month return measure is different from lifetime recurrence or durable
  security, and data-definition changes can affect comparisons.
- A population-specific decline, such as veteran homelessness, does not prove a
  universal system effect.
- Tribal and non-Tribal service routes are not interchangeable; preserve place,
  governance, preference and service availability.
- Political claims about a policy model should not be treated as evidence of
  causality without matched place, population and outcome data.

## Promotion decision

Promote as a Tier 2 mechanism candidate:

**Durable housing is safe, affordable and agency-preserving retention—not a
point-in-time count, shelter bed, service contact or permanent-housing exit.**

The next decisive test is a named place/population ledger linking housing loss,
entry route, shelter/service contact, exit, lease/move-in, six- and twelve-month
retention, health, affordability, social connection and recurrence.

# Rotation JL — Geothermal energy, local benefit and land/reservoir governance

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When geothermal development is presented as reliable low-carbon electricity, direct heat, industrial growth, data-centre supply or regional development, does it produce durable energy access, affordable heat, safe water and land use, local capability, fair benefit and cultural legitimacy—or mainly produce resource estimates, exploration wells, capacity announcements and consent documents without proven local outcomes?

## Why this packet matters

Geothermal is unusually place-bound. Its value depends on a subsurface reservoir, wells, reinjection, water and gas management, grid or heat offtake, local geology, surface features, land rights and long-term reservoir behavior. It can provide firm electricity and direct heat, but the same resource can carry drilling risk, induced seismicity, air and water concerns, depletion risk, land-use conflict and cultural obligations. The conversion therefore has to join engineering and economics to local authority, customary use, monitoring and benefit sharing.

```text
resource / reservoir / surface feature / community
  -> exploration, wells, plant or direct-use system, reinjection and consent
  -> electricity/heat, water and land effects, jobs, tariffs and local capability
  -> reliability, affordability, ecological condition, cultural continuity and trust
  -> durable stewardship, accountability and regional energy resilience
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Geothermal development is not simply a technology choice. A reservoir is attached to a place, and the place can contain surface ecosystems, homes, farms, tourism, customary bathing or cooking, sacred meanings and inherited authority. The same megawatt can be interpreted as energy security by a national ministry, an industrial input by a developer, a livelihood opportunity by a land trust, or a threat to a living taonga by a customary community. A serious assessment must retain these frames rather than collapse them into a single cost or capacity number.

Geography creates uneven opportunity. High-quality resources are concentrated in particular geological zones, while demand, transmission, industrial heat and finance may sit elsewhere. Direct-use projects may produce more local value than exporting electricity, but they require nearby users, heat networks, businesses, skills and affordable connection. A project can therefore be technically successful and still fail the local conversion if households cannot use the heat, local workers cannot access the jobs, or the public bears water, land or seismic burdens without meaningful authority.

The geopolitical angle is also practical. Next-generation geothermal may reuse oil-and-gas drilling skills and attract data-centre or critical-mineral offtake, creating new industrial alliances. That can accelerate investment but also prioritize large buyers over local heat, water, land and affordability. Resource governance is therefore a test of whether energy transition capital strengthens regional capability or creates a new form of extractive dependence.

## Counterexample and source boundary

An exploration license, resource estimate, successful well, renewable label, capacity announcement, concessional loan, jobs estimate, community consultation, reinjection plan or cultural-impact assessment is not by itself reliable affordable energy, safe water, stable reservoir performance, ecological protection, local ownership, fair benefit, cultural continuity or durable legitimacy. A high capacity factor can coexist with high connection costs; reinjection can reduce some impacts without proving long-term field health; and partnership language can exist without decision rights or benefit receipt.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** Geothermal resource estimates, wells, power plants, direct-use projects, strategy documents, consent processes and community-benefit promises are not by themselves reliable affordable local energy, safe water and land use, sustainable reservoir performance, fair benefit, cultural continuity or legitimate stewardship.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **geothermal-development-to-affordable-reliable-energy-local-benefit-and-cultural-land-governance**. The strongest next acquisition is a named field/project/community chain linking reservoir baseline, drilling, water and seismic monitoring, reinjection, plant or heat user, tariff/offtake, local employment and ownership, customary/cultural values, consent, grievance, benefit receipt and post-project reservoir and community outcomes.

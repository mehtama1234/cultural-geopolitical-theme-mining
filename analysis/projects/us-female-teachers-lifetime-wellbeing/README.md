# Project: Female teachers and girls' long-run life paths

This project tracks how a visible institution in childhood can shape later
education, family timing, household income, and longevity. A Census working
paper studies rural America circa 1940 using historical Census records linked
to IRS records, later censuses, and death records. It reports stronger gains
for girls with poorly educated parents and for places where women had fewer
labor-market opportunities.

The safe theme is not “one teacher determines a life.” It is that who is
available as a teacher can be part of a wider social opportunity structure,
especially where families and labor markets offer fewer alternatives. The
project must keep educational attainment, marriage and childbearing timing,
household income, longevity, autonomy, health, and political participation
separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected student-school-community panel
joining teacher characteristics, school quality, family background, local labor
markets, education, work, marriage, children, income, health, mobility, and
later civic life. The public abstract does not provide that complete mechanism
and contemporary transfer.

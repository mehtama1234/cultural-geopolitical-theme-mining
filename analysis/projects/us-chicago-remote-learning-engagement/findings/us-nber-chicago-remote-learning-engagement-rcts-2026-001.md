# Finding: school outreach can improve take-up without creating one universal engagement effect

**Status:** bounded public-education implementation and learning finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34996 reports three randomized field experiments in Chicago
Public Schools during remote learning and summer schooling.

Personalized phone calls increased families' likelihood of registering for a
virtual summer program. Weekly text messages did not change the primary
measures of summer absence or Khan Academy use, although a secondary math
course outcome improved. Adding an instructional aide also had no effect on
the primary summer engagement outcomes, while beneficial effects were found in
some following-year math grades and passing rates.

The end-to-end insight is:

> A public-system intervention can move the first practical step—registration—
> without reliably moving platform use, attendance, and learning together.
> Personal contact and later academic gains are distinct mechanisms from
> generic digital reminders.

## Limits and next test

- The experiments are Chicago-specific and tied to remote or summer-learning
  conditions.
- Primary, secondary, and later outcomes must not be treated as one estimate.
- The study does not establish durable attainment, household time burden,
  student wellbeing, or generalization to other districts.
- Registration and course passing do not by themselves establish continuous
  access, learning quality, or later opportunity.

The next decisive artifact is a student-level implementation ledger joining
contact, registration, attendance, platform use, course completion, family
time/cost, later achievement, and any withdrawal or re-entry.

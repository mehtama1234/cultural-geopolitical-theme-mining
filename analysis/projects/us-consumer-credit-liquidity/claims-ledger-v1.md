# Claims ledger: US consumer credit and the shrinking cash buffer

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US emergency expenses, cards, buy-now-pay-later, medical debt, and household room

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | A large share of adults cannot cover a modest surprise with cash alone. | Federal Reserve household survey | In 2024, 63% said they could cover a $400 surprise with cash or its equivalent; 13% could not pay it right away. | It is a stated response, not a later payment record. |
| C-002 | Borrowing access differs from being able to afford the cost. | Fed household survey and product records | BNPL use and late-payment rates are higher for lower-income users; CFPB data record late fees and charge-offs using provider denominators. | Survey and provider measures cover different people and definitions. |
| C-003 | Credit can preserve a needed purchase while reducing future room. | Household and credit evidence | The Fed records borrowing to handle expenses; the New York Fed shows large balances and delinquency across the credit system. | Aggregate balances do not show why each household borrowed. |
| C-004 | A medical bill can become a credit problem, but removing the record does not always improve the household. | CFPB and NBER evidence | CFPB documents possible uses of medical collections; NBER finds limited effects from deleting or relieving some medical debt on broader outcomes. | Effects vary by debt size, reporting, and relief design. |
| C-005 | Credit is a buffer and a risk-transfer device at the same time. | Synthesis of household and product records | It can prevent an immediate missed bill, while interest, fees, and repayment reduce later choices. | A same-event household panel is still missing. |
| C-006 | The political or trust effect cannot be inferred from balances alone. | Inference | The path from emergency, to borrowing, to repayment, to institutional judgment is plausible but not directly measured here. | Needs linked experience and attitude data. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
bill or income shock
  -> cash buffer is not enough
  -> credit, family help, skipped care, or delayed payment
  -> balance, fee, interest, credit record, or reduced choice
  -> firm revenue, regulation, trust, or political demand
```

## Next test

Follow the same emergency across cash, card, BNPL, overdraft, family help, repayment, and later access. Record the product terms before judging whether credit helped or harmed.

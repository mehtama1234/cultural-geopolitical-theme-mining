# Project: US consumer credit and the shrinking cash buffer

## Question

When a household cannot cover a small surprise with cash, does credit provide a bridge, move the cost into the future, or decide who gets to keep buying?

## Short first pass

Use Federal Reserve household data to measure the cash buffer, CFPB records to understand credit and medical collections, and one lender or card study to test how the terms differ. Keep borrowing access separate from affordability. A person may be able to borrow and still have less room afterward.

## Possible connection

Energy, insurance, medical and housing bills can all create a need for short-term liquidity. The first pass should test whether credit is the household's buffer after those bills arrive, not assume that credit caused the original pressure.

## Decision rule

Move on after a source-backed comparison by income or place, one serious counterpoint, and one clear next dataset. Do not turn a survey answer about an emergency expense into a claim about long-term debt harm.

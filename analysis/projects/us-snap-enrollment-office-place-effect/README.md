# Project: SNAP enrollment offices and place-based access

## Question

Does the physical location of a public-benefit enrollment office change whether
people in nearby places participate?

## Decision rule

Keep office presence, application access, participation, benefit amount,
adequacy, food security, and later household outcomes separate. A place effect
on participation is not the same as a complete household welfare effect.

## Current bounded finding

The [NBER SNAP-office finding](findings/us-nber-snap-office-closure-participation-2025-001.md)
uses administrative records and office locations in one state. It finds a
7–9% participation decline in a census tract over two years after an office
closed, with suggestive increases after openings.

The route is:

```text
need or eligibility
  -> nearby enrollment office or closure
  -> application and participation route
  -> [open] benefit adequacy and food security
  -> [open] household room, trust, and later action
```

The current [SNAP access, participation, food-security, and recovery bridge](snap-access-participation-food-security-recovery-bridge-v1.md) adds Urban interruption reports and SIPP following-resource context while preserving the missing household-level payment, adequacy, remedy, and recovery link.

The study makes place part of access, but it does not follow every household
through the whole route.

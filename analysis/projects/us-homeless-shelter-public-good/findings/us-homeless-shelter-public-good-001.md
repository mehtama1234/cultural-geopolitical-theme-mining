# Finding 001: Temporary shelter produces public benefits without clearly reducing future homelessness

**Status:** bounded shelter-to-public-good finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Using Los Angeles County administrative records from 2014–2019, Christopher,
Duggan, and Martin construct daily shelter-bed and occupancy measures and link
them to neighborhood crime, hospital emergency-room, and mortality data. They
use shocks to winter-shelter availability to study temporary shelter provision.

The study reports that reducing unsheltered homelessness significantly reduces
crime and psychiatric emergency-room visits, and that entering shelter reduces
short-run mortality. It finds no evidence that temporary shelter reduces future
homelessness more than street outreach or other non-shelter services.

The safe interpretation is:

> Shelter can function as a public good and a short-run health intervention
> without being a complete durable-housing or future-homelessness solution.

## Evidence chain

```text
shelter availability and unsheltered exposure
  -> shelter entry or reduced unsheltered homelessness
  -> crime, psychiatric emergency care, and mortality changes
  -> [mixed] future homelessness compared with other services
  -> [open] durable housing, adequacy, trust, and collective response
```

## What it adds

| Stage | Supported result | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Daily shelter-bed and occupancy records provide a place/time operating measure | Access, wait, eligibility, quality, safety, and resident choice |
| Public effect | Crime and psychiatric ER visits fall when unsheltered homelessness falls | Distribution of benefits, displacement, policing, and resident burden |
| Individual health | Short-run mortality falls for people entering shelter | Long-run health, treatment continuity, disability, and wellbeing |
| Housing endpoint | No advantage over outreach/non-shelter services in future homelessness | Stable housing, returns, rent burden, and durable exit |

The study therefore strengthens the public-capacity layer while preserving the
distinction between a public-good effect and individual recovery.

## Next decisive test

Follow shelter entry through wait time, safety, service receipt, health,
housing placement, return to unsheltered homelessness, employment or benefits,
and later trust or civic response. Preserve people who decline shelter and
those served by outreach or other alternatives.

# Finding 001: Trust may buffer political instability during economic crises

**Status:** bounded trust-and-democracy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

An NBER study, revised in 2026, reports that recessions are more likely to be
followed by political turnover in democracies with lower generalized
interpersonal trust. The relationship appears for regular political turnover
and scheduled elections, not coups. The study also reports similar patterns in
subnational Europe, U.S. counties, and individuals in U.S. counties.

In simple terms:

```text
economic crisis
  + lower generalized trust
  -> greater political instability or turnover
```

This is a relationship across several kinds of data, not proof that trust alone
caused a particular election result or protected a particular household.

## What this adds to the atlas

The finding links the cultural layer of interpersonal trust to the geopolitical
and institutional layer of democratic stability. It suggests that economic
pressure may have different political consequences depending on the social
trust environment in which people experience it.

It also connects to the protest finding: public attention, protest, trust, and
political turnover are different outcomes. A crisis can produce political
change without showing the same process at each level.

## Limits and next test

- The public abstract does not provide one common analytic denominator or a
  single current-U.S. national estimate.
- Country, county, and individual results are not interchangeable.
- Trust may reflect stable institutions rather than independently causing
  stability.
- The study does not follow one crisis through household hardship, trust,
  voting, institutional response, and recovery.

The next test would link a defined U.S. economic shock to repeated measures of
trust, attribution, civic action, voting, institutional response, and later
material recovery in the same places or people.

# Finding 001: A program can count its reach without knowing whether it solved the problem

**Status:** bounded public-systems and political-legitimacy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO's July 2026 synthesis says many federal programs track inputs and outputs
but do not have clear, measurable goals or relevant data for outcomes. Without
that evidence, Congress and agency leaders cannot reliably determine whether a
program is solving the problem it was created to fix or whether resources
should move elsewhere.

In one selected 2026 review, 12 of 15 federal programs serving pregnant women,
young children, and their families had performance-management processes; three
did not. GAO also reports that about one-third of federal managers surveyed in
2020 had access to robust evaluations. This is not a claim that the other
programs failed; it is a claim that decision-makers often lack strong evidence
about results.

The route is:

```text
funding and program activity
  -> people reached and services delivered
  -> outcome measurement
  -> evaluation and correction
  -> durable improvement or public value [not generally observed]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This sharpens a recurring societal subtheme:

> A visible public effort is not automatically a demonstrated public benefit.

The missing link is not always delivery. It can be the ability of the state to
know what delivery changed, for whom, at what cost, and whether the result
lasted. That affects public accountability and political judgment because
people may see spending and activity without a clear answer about whether the
system worked.

The source also supplies a counterexample. GAO says DHS's Securing the Cities
program moved from weak tracking in 2019 to established goals, measures,
milestones, and quarterly financial assessments by 2024. Oversight can improve
state learning; better measurement still does not equal a proven impact on
nuclear-terrorism preparedness.

## Limits and next test

- The 15-program review is a selected population, not all federal programs.
- Performance-management processes show goals, data collection, and use of
  information; they do not by themselves establish causal impact.
- The manager-access result comes from a 2020 survey and is not a current
  national estimate of evaluation capacity.
- The report does not follow a recipient through service use, adequacy,
  recovery, trust, political action, or exit.

The next decisive artifact is a named program with a stable recipient or place
unit, a dated intervention, outcome data, comparison or evaluation design, and
evidence of correction or durability. Until then, the safe claim concerns the
state's measurement capacity, not universal program failure or success.

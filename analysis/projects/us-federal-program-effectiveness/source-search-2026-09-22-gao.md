# Source search: federal program effectiveness and outcome evidence

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States federal programs  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; serving people is not the same as improving the problem

## Working question

What does federal oversight show about the evidence agencies have for knowing
whether programs work?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-GAO-PROGRAM-EFFECTIVENESS-2026 | [GAO-26-109130, Federal Programs: Assessing and Improving Effectiveness](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109130) | Explains the difference between inputs, outputs, outcomes, performance management, and impact evaluation; reports selected program examples and a manager-survey finding. | Official oversight synthesis | It is not a national effectiveness rate and does not evaluate every federal program or follow recipients through lived outcomes. |

## First pattern

```text
public money and formal program
  -> activities and people reached
  -> measurable outcomes
  -> evaluation and correction
  -> improved lives, trust, and public value [often open]
```

GAO says federal programs often focus on inputs and outputs, such as money
spent or people receiving benefits, while lacking clear goals or data about
outcomes. In a 2026 review of 15 programs serving pregnant women, young
children, and their families, 12 had performance-management processes and 3
did not. GAO also says about one-third of federal managers in a 2020 survey
reported access to robust evaluations.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The record also shows repair. GAO previously found that DHS's Securing the
Cities program lacked information to track fund use and performance; by 2024,
the program had established goals, performance measures, milestones, and
quarterly financial assessments. A measurement gap can therefore be corrected,
and a performance process can be an early-warning system without being a
completed impact evaluation.

## Decision rule

Use this source for the gap between public-program visibility and demonstrated
public value. Do not convert missing outcome data into program failure, or a
performance-management process into proof of improved lives, without a
recipient-level outcome or credible evaluation.

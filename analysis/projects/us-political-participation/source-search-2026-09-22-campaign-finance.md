# Source search: campaign money and political participation

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States federal elections  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; financial activity is not the same as broad participation or influence

## Working question

How much federal campaign money was reported in the 2025–2026 cycle, and what
does that record leave unknown about political power?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-FEC-2025-2026-15-MONTH | [FEC statistical summary of 15-month campaign activity](https://www.fec.gov/updates/statistical-summary-of-15-month-campaign-activity-of-the-2025-2026-election-cycle/) | Reports receipts, disbursements, debts, cash on hand, party sources, PAC activity, and independent expenditures through March 31, 2026. | Official FEC filing summary | It does not identify persuasion, turnout, policy change, donor representativeness, or whether spending was effective. |

## First pattern

```text
donor and committee money
  -> candidate, party, PAC, or independent spending
  -> public communication and organizational capacity
  -> persuasion, voting, policy influence, legitimacy [open]
```

For January 1, 2025 through March 31, 2026, congressional candidates
reported $2.1274 billion in receipts and $1.3452 billion in disbursements;
party committees reported $1.1208 billion in receipts and $824.8 million in
disbursements; and PACs reported $6.3004 billion in receipts and $4.7842
billion in disbursements. Independent expenditures reported in the period
totaled $252.1 million. These are committee-level financial flows, not a count
of people participating.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Individuals were the largest source of funds for national party committees'
traditional accounts, but the summary does not show how many unique donors
gave, how much came from small donors, how many citizens were reached, or what
political results followed. PAC receipts also include different organizational
forms and cannot be read as one type of donor or one kind of influence.

## Decision rule

Use FEC data for disclosed financial capacity and organizational channels. Do
not convert receipts into voter participation, public opinion, policy capture,
or democratic legitimacy without a linked outcome measure.

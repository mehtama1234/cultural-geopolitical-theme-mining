# Finding 001: Political money is visible at a different scale from political participation

**Status:** bounded federal campaign-finance finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

FEC filings show a very large federal campaign-finance system in the first 15
months of the 2025–2026 cycle. Congressional candidates reported $2.1274
billion in receipts and $1.3452 billion in spending. Party committees reported
$1.1208 billion in receipts and $824.8 million in spending. PACs reported
$6.3004 billion in receipts and $4.7842 billion in spending. Independent
expenditures reported in the same period totaled $252.1 million.

The simple lesson is not that PACs or candidates persuaded voters. It is that
political organizations had different levels and forms of financial capacity.
The route is:

```text
reported donations and other receipts
  -> committee resources
  -> spending and political communication
  -> voter attention, persuasion, turnout, policy influence, legitimacy [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This creates a clean political-resource subtheme:

> The capacity to spend in politics can grow without showing who took part,
> who was heard, or whether public decisions changed.

It also gives the cultural-political lane a useful warning. Financial records
are more concrete than attitudes, but they still do not measure political
meaning or public consent. A campaign can raise money without winning, and a
large expenditure can be visible without proving persuasion or durable policy
power.

## Limits and next test

- These are committee-level receipts and disbursements, not unique donors or
  citizens reached.
- The FEC summary does not provide a same-donor-to-vote or spending-to-policy
  path.
- Candidate, party, PAC, and independent-expenditure totals have different
  organizational and legal meanings.
- Reported money does not establish illegal activity, effectiveness, or
  democratic legitimacy.

The next decisive artifact is a linked, privacy-protected route joining donor
type and amount to communication exposure, candidate outcome, turnout, or a
defined policy decision. Until then, the safe claim is about disclosed
political capacity, not political control.

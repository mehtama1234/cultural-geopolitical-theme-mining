# Source search: US rideshare licensing and customer safety

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; licensing and trip-level customer measures are compared in New York, New Jersey and Houston, the broader fare and worker path remains open

## Working question

Does requiring a rideshare driver to hold a license improve the customer's ride, or mainly change who can enter the work?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-RIDESHARE-LICENSE | [Occupational Licensing of Uber Drivers](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35635) | Using trip-level Uber data with driver ratings and telematics-based driving measures, the authors find no consistent evidence that licensing improves consumer outcomes. In Houston, drivers who entered after deregulation looked like previously licensed drivers on ratings and driving behavior despite differences in age and experience. | NBER working paper | The opening evidence does not show fares, wait times, worker income, entry costs or every safety outcome. Results come from two settings and may not carry to all cities. |

## First pattern to test

```text
licensing rule
  -> who can drive and at what cost
  -> driver pool and trip supply
  -> customer rating, behavior, fare and wait
  -> safety and worker access
```

The source supports no consistent measured customer improvement in the settings studied. It does not establish the full market result.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Licensing may protect customers through checks or standards not captured by ratings and the driving measures used here. It may also matter more in cities with different enforcement or driver pools.

## Main gaps

- licensing cost and time for drivers;
- fares, wait times and cancellations;
- serious safety incidents;
- worker entry, income and outside job options;
- customer trust and repeat use;
- results across more cities and platforms.

## Decision rule

Keep licensing, worker entry, trip supply, customer quality, fare and safety as separate steps until evidence joins them.

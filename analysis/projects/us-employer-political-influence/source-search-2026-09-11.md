# Source search: US employer political influence

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; employer power and reported political influence are measured, the actual vote path remains open

## Working question

When one employer dominates a local job market, can its economic power also shape workers' political choices?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-EMPLOYER-POLITICS | [Firms as Electoral Monopsonies](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35676) | An NBER study finds that workers at major local employers are more likely to report employer political communication and to say their employer influences their voting behavior and career expectations. Its model describes a passive path, where workers absorb the firm's economic interests, and an active path, where a dominant employer shapes expectations about wages and jobs after an election. | NBER working paper | The opening evidence includes survey reports and a model. It does not show the same worker's message, job dependence, belief change and vote in one record. |

## First pattern to test

```text
local employer power
  -> job dependence and expectations
  -> employer message or pressure
  -> political belief
  -> vote, policy demand or silence
```

The source supports reported influence and a proposed model of the channel. It does not establish the actual vote effect.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Workers may hear an employer's view without accepting it, and a large employer can provide stable jobs or useful local information. Reported influence may also reflect a worker's general view of the firm rather than a changed vote.

## Main gaps

- employer message content and timing;
- worker dependence and outside job options;
- pay, schedule and benefit changes;
- private belief before and after the message;
- turnout and vote choice;
- differences by occupation, income, race and place.

## Decision rule

Keep employer power, message exposure, belief, job expectation and vote as separate steps until the same worker can be followed through them.

# Source search: US tariff price paths and delayed domestic markups

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; direct and indirect tariff price paths are measured, full household and firm response remains open

## Working question

When a tariff raises prices, how much comes from the taxed import and how much arrives later through US producers and weaker competition?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-TARIFF-ANATOMY | [The Anatomy of Tariff Pass-through into Consumer Prices](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35561) | Studying the 2025 US tariffs, the authors estimate that about 26% of the tariff increase passed through to consumer prices relative to less-exposed goods. About 64% of that increase was direct, while 36% came indirectly through imported inputs and higher domestic markups as imports became less competitive. The direct effect was quick; the indirect effect took about nine to twelve months. | NBER working paper | The estimate is relative to less-exposed goods and holds aggregate conditions fixed. It does not show the exact household bill, jobs, firm entry or the full long-run response. |

## First pattern to test

```text
tariff decision
  -> import price
  -> domestic input cost and competition
  -> delayed markup and shelf price
  -> household budget and political judgment
```

The source supports direct and delayed price paths. It does not establish the full household or job result.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Domestic firms may use the pressure to invest, switch suppliers or expand production. A higher price can also reflect a change in product mix or demand, not only a tariff.

## Main gaps

- household basket and income;
- domestic output, jobs and wages;
- firm entry, exit and markups;
- substitution and product quality;
- later policy changes and retaliation;
- political blame and vote.

## Decision rule

Keep tariff rate, import price, domestic markup, shelf price, household burden and job effect as separate claims until evidence joins them.

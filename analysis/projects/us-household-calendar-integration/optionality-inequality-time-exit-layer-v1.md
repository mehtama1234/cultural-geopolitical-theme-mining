# Optionality, unequal exposure, time, and exit layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** cross-source distributional synthesis; not one integrated sample or latent-index estimate

## The broad question

Why does the same price, deadline, service failure, care need, or work rule
produce different consequences for different people? The answer is often not
only exposure. It is the amount of cash, time, social help, institutional voice,
and practical ability to switch, appeal, move, or refuse.

```text
status, resources, family, health, place, identity, or provider access
  -> exposure and alternatives differ
  -> the same condition has different money, time, dignity, and risk costs
  -> people cut, delay, borrow, accept, appeal, switch, stay, move, organize, or go without
  -> firms and institutions distribute remedy and future options unevenly
  -> inequality becomes lived security, cultural meaning, and political voice
```

“Optionality” is a useful working concept, not yet a single measured variable.
The program should measure its components separately before constructing any
summary index.

## Provisional societal patterns

### 1. Unequal exposure is also unequal room to respond

The relevant difference between groups is not only how often a hardship occurs.
It is what the hardship displaces and whether there is a fallback. One person
uses savings; another uses credit, unpaid family labor, food quality, health,
time, or future mobility. Group averages must therefore show both exposure and
the available response.

### 2. Time is a major inequality channel

ATUS makes household production, care, weekend work, workplace location, and
social participation visible. A nominally equal price can be more expensive
for someone who must travel farther, wait longer, work another job, or provide
unpaid care. Time spent is not automatically time chosen or time controlled.

### 3. Status categories are mechanisms only when unpacked

Race, income, disability, tenure, children, age, and place may capture several
conditions at once: treatment, resources, health, work, family support,
transport, discrimination, or institutional access. The SIPP intersections show
that gradients and gaps can coexist, but they do not identify which mechanism
created them. Retaining uncertainty and documenting universes is part of the
finding.

### 4. Exit is an unequal political and consumer resource

The ability to switch a provider, leave a job, move home, appeal a decision,
refuse data collection, or stop buying is itself distributed. Staying may be
preference or constraint; non-complaint may be satisfaction or futility. This
is why consumer recourse, job lock, housing mobility, and civic action belong
in the same broad map while remaining separate outcomes.

## Components that must stay separate

| Component | Proper question |
|---|---|
| Cash room | Can the person absorb the cost without immediate sacrifice? |
| Time room | Can the person wait, travel, care, or complete paperwork? |
| Alternative access | Is another provider, job, home, route, or product actually available? |
| Institutional voice | Can the person appeal, complain, obtain a human decision, or organize? |
| Social support | Who can supply money, transport, care, information, or shelter? |
| Rights and protection | What rules prevent denial, retaliation, discrimination, or unsafe conditions? |
| Practical exit | Can the person leave without losing health coverage, income, housing, care, or status? |

Do not treat income as a complete measure of optionality. Do not treat a formal
right to switch or appeal as proof that the person can use it.

## Evidence status by arrow

| Arrow | Status | Safe current conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Status/resources → different exposure | Compared | Material, food, utility, work, time, and civic conditions vary across documented groups and places. |
| Exposure → different practical cost | Inferred/partly reported | The same nominal condition can displace different needs depending on cash, time, care, and alternatives. |
| Alternatives → adjustment or exit | Reported/compared | People substitute, delay, borrow, work more, appeal, switch, stay, or go without; the binding alternative is often unmeasured. |
| Unequal response → trust/identity/action | Open | Meaning, blame, trust, and political response require direct respondent or community measures. |
| Optionality → later security | Open | The components should be followed longitudinally before a composite security claim is made. |

## Next bounded intersectional test

Pre-specify a small design using one source and one unit at a time:

1. SIPP person-month: race × resource × tenure or disability, with replicate
   uncertainty and official universes;
2. ATUS person-day: work arrangement × care responsibility × education or
   gender, separating time spent, control, and displacement;
3. SHED adult: financial pressure × emergency capacity × alternatives and
   reported adjustment;
4. CFPB or program episode: access route × product/program × response and
   repeat effort;
5. NHTS trip: vehicle access × place × purpose × time/fare, with missed work or
   care measured in a linked design where possible.

For every comparison record exposure, alternative, action, immediate outcome,
and later recovery. Include a counterexample with similar exposure but more
room to switch or absorb the shock. Never manufacture a person-level join from
separate surveys.

## What would change the interpretation

The working picture would weaken if group differences disappeared after the
relevant resource, place, health, family, and treatment conditions were
measured; if formal alternatives were equally usable in practice; if time and
cash constraints rarely displaced other needs; or if differences in exit,
remedy, trust, and action did not persist in valid longitudinal comparisons.

## Relationship to the broad program

This layer is the distributional spine of the 14-theme map. It connects money,
time, care, housing, food, work, consumer recourse, public systems, culture,
and political action. Its central question is: who has enough room to choose,
and who is merely absorbing the cost of having no workable alternative?

# ATUS 2024 time as a hidden price layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Source:** [BLS American Time Use Survey 2024 results](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/atus_06262025.htm)  
**Unit:** nationally representative annual-average diary estimates for people age 15 and over, with subgroup universes where stated  
**Status:** reported population estimates; not a longitudinal household result

## Why this belongs in the broad program

Money is only one way a system charges people. Work, care, household production, travel, waiting, and lost social time are another. ATUS measures the time allocation directly, so it gives the broader map a time-use layer that cannot be inferred from income or employment alone.

```text
work, care, or service requirement
  -> time allocated or displaced
  -> unequal room for household production, leisure, social life, and paid work
  -> different security, bargaining power, health, and cultural participation
```

The ATUS release measures the time allocation. It does not identify which price, employer, care bill, platform, or policy caused it.

## 2024 population patterns

| Time-use measure | Reported estimate |
|---|---:|
| People engaging in household activities on an average day | 80%; about 2 hours among the population |
| Women engaging in household activities | 87%; 2.7 hours on days they did them |
| Men engaging in household activities | 74%; 2.3 hours on days they did them |
| Full-time employed people working on an average weekday | 87%; 8.4 hours on days worked |
| Full-time employed people working on an average weekend day | 29%; 5.6 hours on days worked |
| Adults with a child under age 6 providing primary childcare | 2.5 hours per day |
| Women with a child under age 6 providing primary childcare | 3.0 hours per day |
| Men with a child under age 6 providing primary childcare | 2.0 hours per day |
| Employed adults with a child under age 6 providing primary childcare | 2.0 hours per day |
| Not-employed adults with a child under age 6 providing primary childcare | 3.2 hours per day |
| Adults with a child under age 13 providing secondary childcare | 5.2 hours per day |

Secondary childcare counts time when a child is in the respondent’s care while another activity is the primary activity. It is therefore part of the hidden workload even when the diary does not label the entire period “childcare.”

## Work, class, and control over location

Multiple-job holders were more likely than single-job holders to work on an average weekend day: 50% versus 29%. Among workers age 25 and over, 50% of employed people with a bachelor’s degree or higher worked at home on days worked, compared with 18% of those with a high school diploma and no college.

This supports a bounded inequality pattern around time and workplace control: the question is not only who has a job, but who can locate work at home, who must work additional days, and who can combine paid work with care. These are population comparisons, not proof that education or jobholding caused the time difference.

## Social and cultural time

ATUS reports that people spent less time socializing and communicating in 2024 than in 2014: 30% participated on an average day versus 38% in 2014, and participants spent 35 minutes versus 43 minutes. This is a measured change in a form of social participation, not proof of cultural decline. Age, work, care, technology, health, and reporting conditions may all contribute.

## What this adds to the 14-theme map

- **Time as a hidden price:** household labor, weekend work, and care are measurable resources.
- **Work, control, and bargaining:** home-based work and multiple-job schedules reveal different degrees of control over when and where labor occurs.
- **Care and social reproduction:** primary and secondary childcare make the labor of keeping households functioning visible.
- **Unequal exposure and status:** gender, employment, education, age, and family structure shape time allocation.
- **Cultural meaning:** socializing and communication are outcomes in their own right, not merely leftover leisure.
- **Household room and consumption:** ATUS can be paired with SHED or SIPP as a separate time layer, but not as the same respondent or household unless a valid identifier exists.

## Limits and next tests

ATUS is a one-day diary for a designated person, not a twelve-month household panel. Primary activity does not include all simultaneous activity, although the survey separately measures secondary childcare and asks about location and who was present for many activities. The 2024 release is an annual average; it cannot identify the shock or rule behind a particular person’s schedule.

Next, compare time allocation with a defined source layer for work schedule, childcare cost, commuting, service effort, or financial pressure. Preserve the distinction between time spent, time desired, time controlled, and time displaced. A valid end-to-end test would need a defined event, the same unit before and after it, and a counterexample with similar work and family conditions.

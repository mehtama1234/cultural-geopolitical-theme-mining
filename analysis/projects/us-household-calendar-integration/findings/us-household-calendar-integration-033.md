# A household can absorb pressure in money, time, health, or work—and the sources observe different clocks

**Status:** provisional cross-source end-to-end synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-09-15

## The short version

The material/time/care evidence now supports a more precise story than “households are under pressure.” Different sources show different parts of the same possible sequence:

```text
material room, housing, health, or care conditions
  -> a choice among payment, borrowing, work, unpaid care, delay, or going without
  -> a change in health, time, earnings, housing, food, or social availability
  -> persistence, reversal, recovery, institutional judgment, or exit
```

The first three stages are visible, but not in one common record. SIPP gives a genuine person-month transition surface for resources, earnings, hours, and jobs. MEPS follows the same panel people across two calendar years and shows health spending, coverage, employment, wages, and perceived-health change. SHED and ATUS expose care, emergency-payment routes, and time allocation. They should be read as complementary lenses, not merged into a fictional longitudinal household panel.

The substantive finding is therefore a **multi-clock problem**: money can move monthly, health spending and perceived health are observed annually, unpaid care and social time are measured with different instruments, and meaning or political response is usually measured in yet another sample. A household may look stable on one clock while losing room on another.

## What the longitudinal sources actually show

### 1. Monthly resources and work do not move together

In the 2025 Census SIPP public-use file for the 2024 reference year, the same-person transition audit identified 31,992 people, including 31,090 with all twelve reference months and 346,283 adjacent-month pairs. For fields explicitly defined as monthly, 3.67% of valid income-to-poverty-ratio-band pairs changed category, compared with 1.70% of valid job-count pairs.

This does not mean income is “more volatile” in a universal sense: the valid-pair sets and category definitions differ. It does show that a stable job count is not the same thing as a stable monthly resource position. A household can keep one job while pay, income, benefits, household composition, or other resources change. Conversely, a job-count transition does not tell us whether hours, earnings, care, or security improved.

The directional SIPP pass adds another necessary correction. Among people reporting a work-limiting condition, hours were unchanged in most adjacent valid pairs: 83.1% below one poverty threshold and 92.4% at four times the threshold or more. Earnings could rise or fall in the same cells. “Work limitation” therefore cannot be translated into “hours loss,” and an earnings change cannot be translated into improved or worsened security without the surrounding resource and time fields.

### 2. Annual health spending and health experience can diverge

MEPS Panel 27 follows the same panel people across 2022 and 2023. Weighted mean total health expenditure rose from $6,971.74 to $7,328.55 per person. In the paired valid sample, total expenditure increased by $630.83, while paired out-of-pocket spending changed by −$50.50; the out-of-pocket interval includes zero. In that same paired health-status universe, 21.01% reported improved perceived health, 22.85% worsened, and 56.14% were unchanged.

This is a useful non-synchronization result. More total spending does not imply that every person felt healthier. A lower or flat out-of-pocket average does not prove that care was timely, affordable, or equally distributed. Health need, insurance, age, provider prices, employment, and composition can produce the observed pattern. The panel gives a health-cost and health-status clock; it does not identify the bill, the care decision, the unpaid helper, or the time displaced by treatment.

### 3. Housing and liquidity change the menu of responses

The SIPP tenure/resource comparison shows that renters reported more rent or mortgage difficulty, utility difficulty, and food hardship than owners or buyers within the displayed resource bands. Among people below the poverty threshold, rent/mortgage difficulty was 16.29% for renters and 7.02% for owners/buyers; among people at four times the threshold or more, it was 4.24% and 1.28% respectively. The gap is not proof of a tenure effect: the table does not contain the full payment, repair, insurance, local-price, debt, or selection story.

The Federal Reserve SHED layer adds a different surface of room. In 2025, 63% of adults said they could cover a hypothetical $400 expense with cash or its equivalent, 12% could not pay by any listed route, and 15% would put it on a credit card and carry a balance. These are possible routes, not observed responses to the same event. The linked credit-record sample shows a much larger average card-balance increase among respondents who said it was difficult to get by than among those living comfortably, but it is consent-based and descriptive.

Taken together, these sources support a distinction between **resources** and **alternatives**. Income, tenure, liquidity, insurance, family help, transport, and schedule control determine what a household can do when a cost arrives. The next move might be payment, borrowing, extra work, unpaid care, delay, moving, assistance, or going without. No single measure tells us which alternative was available or what it protected.

## The time-and-care bridge remains the missing middle

ATUS measures how people spend a diary day, including work, eldercare, household activity, travel, social interaction, and leisure. SIPP measures selected care/work constraints and monthly material conditions. SHED asks about care responsibility and financial adaptation. These layers make the time substitution problem visible, but they do not establish that the same person who faced a particular bill later lost a particular number of hours.

The missing record would need to preserve, for one person or household:

| Stage | What a complete record would identify | Current status |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Date and type of bill, price change, health event, care need, work-rule change, or service failure | Partly observed across sources; not joined to one household outcome |
| Alternatives | Cash, credit, insurance, family help, paid care, transport, schedule control, appeal, or provider choice | Measured in fragments; practical choice set is mostly open |
| Immediate response | Payment, delay, borrowing, extra work, unpaid care, travel, paperwork, or going without | Some responses are observed; exact event-response pairing is open |
| Protected or sacrificed outcome | Food, housing, health, rest, family time, social connection, work, or civic availability | Outcomes exist in separate instruments and denominators |
| Later outcome | Recovery, persistence, debt, health, employment, trust, institutional action, or exit | Selected persistence exists; meaning and remedy after the same event remain open |

This is why a time diary cannot be treated as a bill-response diary, and why a health-cost panel cannot be called a care-burden panel. The instruments measure different currencies with different recall windows.

## What the evidence supports—and what it does not

The current evidence supports these bounded statements:

- Household room has multiple dimensions: monthly resources, tenure, liquidity, credit exposure, health cost, work, and time.
- Those dimensions can move on different clocks. Stable employment does not guarantee stable resources; lower out-of-pocket spending does not guarantee improved health; high income does not erase tenure or care differences.
- Adaptation is not automatically resilience. Borrowing, working more, cutting savings, delaying care, or providing unpaid care can protect one outcome while moving the burden into another period or another household member.
- The same aggregate outcome can contain opposite individual paths. In MEPS, meaningful shares improved, worsened, and remained unchanged in perceived health while expenditure rose in aggregate.

The current evidence does not support:

- a causal claim that a particular price, employer, insurer, provider, agency, or policy caused a measured time or health change;
- a unified household “stress score” built by pooling SIPP, MEPS, SHED, and ATUS;
- a claim that borrowing or extra work was forced rather than chosen, or that reduced work was harm rather than accommodation;
- a claim about trust, political action, cultural identity, or geopolitical consequence from hardship, care, time, or employment alone.

## The next end-to-end test

The highest-value next acquisition remains the PSID main-wave package, subject to the open account and Conditions-of-Use gate. Its 2019, 2021, and 2023 family/person files could test a repeated typical-week bridge from material room to paid work, housework, child/adult care, shopping, leisure, feeling rushed, health, family routine, and selected wellbeing outcomes for the same people and families. It would still not be a detailed diary or a direct political-meaning panel.

After access, the first test should be deliberately small and auditable:

1. keep only people/families with valid identifiers, weights, and comparable fields across waves;
2. separate family resources from person-reported time and care;
3. preserve zero, not-applicable, refusal, and missing codes;
4. compare material-room change with paid work, unpaid care, leisure, feeling rushed, health, and family-routine change;
5. stratify by children, health/disability, tenure, race/ethnicity, age, education, and region where cell sizes permit;
6. include a counterexample in which similar material exposure coexists with different time or health movement; and
7. keep dated triggers, institutional attribution, trust, political action, and recovery as separate open arrows unless the data actually observe them.

Until then, the program should use SIPP, MEPS, SHED, ATUS, NHTS, RECS, and related administrative or institutional sources as a deliberately layered atlas. The gap is not a reason to stop; it is the reason the end-to-end goal must keep the source boundaries visible.

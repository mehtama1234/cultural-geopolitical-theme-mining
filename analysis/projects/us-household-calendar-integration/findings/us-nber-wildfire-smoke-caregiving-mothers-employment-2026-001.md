# Finding 001: Wildfire smoke can turn intermittent school disruption into maternal employment loss

**Status:** bounded quasi-experimental material/time/care finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

An NBER working paper uses wildfire smoke as plausibly exogenous variation in
school closures and student absenteeism. Those disruptions create intermittent
caregiving demand even when formal childcare exists. By comparing mothers whose
youngest children differ in reliance on school-based childcare, the study finds
substantial contemporaneous labor-market costs and employment losses when the
caregiving shocks accumulate.

The route is:

```text
wildfire smoke
  -> school closure or absenteeism
  -> intermittent loss of school-based care
  -> maternal caregiving demand and work constraint
  -> contemporaneous cost and, when shocks accumulate, employment loss
  -> [open] household income, paid/unpaid time, health, housing, trust, and recovery
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a useful bridge for the program's open material/time/care middle. It
shows why the presence of formal childcare is not enough to establish usable
care capacity: a household may depend on a school-based care clock that fails
intermittently during an environmental event.

The study also reports a meaningful distributional boundary. Employment losses
were fully offset among mothers working outside male-dominated industries,
consistent with workplace tolerance moderating the consequences of intermittent
care demand. That is a reported industry-level comparison, not proof that
industry gender composition itself is the causal mechanism.

## Limits and next test

- The public abstract does not provide the full sample, estimates, exposure
  windows, or every administrative-linkage detail.
- Industry composition is a proxy for workplace tolerance and may also capture
  occupation, schedule control, remote-work access, pay, benefits, or local labor
  markets.
- The study does not follow a household through lost hours, paid or unpaid care,
  income, childcare substitution, health, housing, trust, or later recovery.
- Formal childcare availability is not the same as a substitute that is usable
  during school disruption.

The next decisive artifact is a protected mother/child episode ledger linking
smoke exposure, school closure or absence, care alternatives, schedule control,
paid and unpaid time, employer response, earnings, health, housing, and later
employment. Compare industries and workplaces with documented leave, remote,
substitution, or schedule-flexibility rules rather than using industry mix as a
complete measure of tolerance.

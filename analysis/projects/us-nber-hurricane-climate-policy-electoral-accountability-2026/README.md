# Project: Hurricane exposure, climate policy, and electoral accountability

## Question

Can a material disaster change political meaning and action beyond immediate
relief—through a representative's policy response and the electoral risk of
that response?

## Bounded result

The [machine-readable record](../../records/us-nber-hurricane-climate-policy-electoral-accountability-2026.json)
adds a district/representative sequence: hurricane exposure, a reported shift
toward environmental regulation and control, persistence of the agenda change,
and a subsequent electoral penalty. This is a meaningful event-to-institution-
to-election route, but it is not a same-voter household-to-ballot panel.

## Decisive next evidence

Link disaster damage, insurance and aid, local media and attribution, voter
beliefs, turnout and vote choice, legislative implementation, and later
household protection or recovery. Keep representative-level electoral effects
separate from resident-level political meaning.

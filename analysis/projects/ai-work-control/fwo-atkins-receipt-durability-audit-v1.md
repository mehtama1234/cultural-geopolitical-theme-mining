# FWO Atkins receipt and durability audit v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Unit:** ten truck drivers in one Fair Work Ombudsman enforcement matter  
**Status:** reported worker-level receipt; durable recovery not observed

## Why this route matters

The Atkins route is one of the strongest worker-remedy records in the atlas.
It does more than report an order: the Fair Work Ombudsman says the ten
drivers received back-payments ranging from $8,012 to $91,566 after the appeal
was dismissed and funds held in trust were released. That makes it a useful
benchmark for the difference between an institutional obligation, released
funds, and reported worker receipt.

It still does not close the broader end-to-end chain. The public record does
not say whether the amount was adequate for each worker, what happened to
their work or household finances afterward, whether underpayment recurred, or
whether trust, voice, or exit changed.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Result | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Underpayment and lost entitlements | Reported | Ten drivers were underpaid between 2007 and 2009; the 2016 release reports more than $374,000 ordered as back-pay |
| Institutional response | Reported | The Ombudsman pursued legal action and the Court ordered back-pay and penalties |
| Payment channel and delay | Reported | The company paid about $468,000 into Court while appealing; the appeal was dismissed before release |
| Worker-level receipt | Reported | The 2018 follow-up says all ten drivers received payments in the stated range |
| Independent payment confirmation | Not retained | The public record is an institutional report, not worker statements or payment instruments |
| Adequacy and residual loss | Not reported | No worker-level loss-to-payment comparison or household need is provided |
| Later work and household security | Not reported | No later employment, income, debt, health, care, or housing follow-up is attached |
| Recurrence, trust, voice, or exit | Not reported | No later compliance, worker meaning, complaint, organizing, or job-choice record is attached |

## Official-site follow-up scan

The 2026-09-20 search found the original Atkins penalty release and the later
appeal-dismissal/payment release, but no later Atkins-specific follow-up on the
ten drivers. The search also returned later trucking enforcement matters,
such as Trucking Services (2020/2021), Alpine Fresh (2022), and BSH Transport
(2023). Those are separate employers and workers; they are useful sector
context but cannot be treated as longitudinal follow-up for Atkins.

This is a verified non-observation, not evidence that recovery failed.

## Atlas decision

Keep Atkins as a **reported worker-receipt comparator**, not as a completed
end-to-end worker-security finding. It upgrades the remedy stage relative to
cases that only show an order or a payment offer. It does not establish a
general enforcement success rate or prove that payment restored security.

The decisive next evidence would be a lawful worker-confirmed or payroll-level
follow-up containing payment date and amount, later employment or income,
recurrence, and the worker's own assessment of adequacy, trust, and whether to
stay, switch, or exit.

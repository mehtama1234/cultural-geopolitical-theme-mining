# DOL AI and automated-systems federal guidance audit

**Status:** storage-light official-source audit · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

## Question

Does the federal labor-standard record connect employer use of AI or automated
worker-surveillance systems to a named worker, a changed employment decision,
enforcement action, or a realized remedy?

## What the official record establishes

The Department of Labor Wage and Hour Division's Field Assistance Bulletin
2024-1 addresses artificial intelligence and automated systems in the
workplace under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The bulletin recognizes that
automated systems may be used for worker surveillance, targeting, analytics,
and compliance-related decisions, and frames their use within existing labor
standards and anti-retaliation obligations.

This is a federal interpretive and enforcement-guidance surface. It identifies
how an agency expects existing standards to apply; it is not a measurement of
how many employers use such systems or how many workers are affected.

## Targeted result

**No named employer/workplace implementation, worker-level exposure record,
changed decision, complaint outcome, correction, or compensation was located
in the official guidance page and associated public search surface checked.**
The absence is bounded: individual investigations, confidential complaints,
and records not published as AI-specific cases may sit outside the source.

## Coding consequence

```text
federal labor-standard applicability: observed_guidance
automated-surveillance / analytics risk surface: observed_guidance
named implementation: not_observed
worker exposure and discretion: not_observed
enforcement, correction, compensation, or later outcome: not_observed
```

The bulletin strengthens the institutional-response stage and gives the
worker-event ledger a federal enforcement vocabulary. It does not close the
implementation-to-lived-outcome arrow and must not be treated as evidence of
compliance, harm, or worker control.

## Smallest decisive follow-up

Find a public DOL investigation, court filing, or settlement that identifies
an automated system or surveillance practice, the labor-standard issue, the
affected worker universe, and the dated remedy or disposition. Otherwise the
next stronger route remains a matched worker/workplace design with exposure,
monitoring, schedule/pay effects, grievance, and follow-up behavior.

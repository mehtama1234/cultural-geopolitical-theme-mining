# Netherlands draft Platform Work Act: proposed control matrix

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Status:** consultation draft; not enacted  
**Unit:** proposed Dutch statutory control, not exercised right

## Why this matrix matters

The Dutch consultation page gives the proposal's themes. The draft regulation
shows the operational architecture behind those themes. It is therefore useful
for comparing institutional design, but every row below remains contingent on
legislative revision, enactment, commencement, and any implementing rules.

## Proposed controls

| Draft provision | Proposed control | Exposure or decision covered | Proposed authority/remedy | Current evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 2 | Scope reaches platforms organizing work in the EU and intermediaries where work is performed in the Netherlands; data-system rules also reach certain platforms established in the Netherlands | Platform work, intermediaries, automated monitoring and decision systems | Scope definition | Draft text only |
| Art. 3 | Rebuttable presumption of an employment contract where factual indications of direction and supervision exist; intermediary relationships included | Status classification, control, task organization | Worker may invoke presumption; detailed indicators delegated to a general administrative measure | Draft text; no case |
| Art. 4 | Prohibits automated processing of emotional/psychological state, private conversations, off-work data, predictions of fundamental-rights exercise, sensitive attributes, and certain biometric identification | Monitoring, profiling, union/collective-action inference, identity processing | Data-protection supervision | Draft text; no compliance inspection |
| Art. 5 | Prior data-protection impact assessment; views of workers and representatives; assessment supplied to worker representatives | Automated monitoring and decision systems | Impact-assessment and representative access duty | Draft text; no assessment observed |
| Art. 6 | Information to candidates, workers, representatives, and designated supervisors about system use and material changes | Hiring, allocation, evaluation, status, and system changes | Information duty; details delegated to subordinate rule | Draft text; no disclosure observed |
| Art. 7 | Portability of work-generated personal data, including ratings and reviews; free tools and direct transfer on request | Reputation, ratings, platform exit and switching | Worker portability right | Draft text; no transfer observed |
| Art. 8 | At least biennial evaluation of system impact; qualified, trained, empowered human oversight; ability to set aside automated decisions; prevention of recurring rights violations | Automated monitoring and decisions, discrimination, account and contract decisions | Internal oversight and worker-representative information; protection for oversight personnel | Draft text; no evaluation observed |
| Art. 8(6) | Account or contract restriction, suspension, termination, or comparable adverse decision must be made by a human | Account access, contractual continuity, discipline | Human decision requirement | Draft text; no decision record |
| Art. 9 | Explanation, trained contact person, written reasons for major decisions, review request, response within two weeks, correction within two weeks or compensation if correction is impossible | Account, payment, status, essential contractual conditions | Human review, written response, correction or compensation | Draft text; no remedy use observed |
| Art. 10 | Private and secure worker communication and representative contact; platform may not monitor those contacts | Collective voice and organization | Communication channel duty | Draft text; no channel audit |
| Arts. 12–13 | No adverse treatment for exercising rights; written reasons for termination or non-renewal allegedly linked to rights use | Complaint, assistance, legal action, retaliation | Anti-retaliation and reason-giving | Draft text; no retaliation case |
| Arts. 14–15 | Advance declaration of platform work and information to a designated authority, supervisors, and representatives | Platform population, status, organization, cross-border activity | Reporting and information duties | Draft text; authority designation and operation open |
| Arts. 16–20 | Personal-data supervisor and minister-designated officials receive oversight powers, warnings, compliance orders, administrative coercion, and fines | Data-system duties and reporting | Administrative enforcement and fines | Draft text; no enforcement outcome |

## Proposed mechanism

```text
automated allocation, evaluation, monitoring, or adverse decision
  -> information, data limits, impact assessment, and human oversight
  -> explanation and review within a defined time window
  -> correction or compensation, plus recurrence prevention
  -> possible administrative enforcement
```

The draft is notable because it connects “human oversight” to authority,
training, the power to set aside an automated decision, response deadlines,
correction, compensation, and anti-retaliation. Those are design features, not
evidence that the route will work in practice.

## Comparison with the AIM-WORK exposure map

| AIM-WORK exposure | Proposed Dutch control that could bear on it | Missing test |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic task or time allocation | Information, human oversight, review, and status/reporting rules | Whether a worker can obtain the decision reason and change an allocation |
| Automatic speed or direct instruction | System information, impact assessment, health/safety and review | Whether work intensity or stress changes after review |
| Ranking, points, or ratings | Data limits, portability, transparency, correction | Whether a disputed score is corrected before lost work or pay |
| Performance-based cancellation | Human decision, written reasons, two-week review, correction/compensation | Whether account access is restored and retaliation is prevented |
| Monitoring and profiling | Prohibited data categories, DPIA, representative access, supervision | Whether prohibited or excessive processing is detected and sanctioned |

This is a legal-design crosswalk, not a matched-worker estimate. The Dutch
draft does not identify the AIM-WORK respondents or demonstrate that any
proposed right changes their working conditions.

## Decisive follow-up

Compare the consultation draft with the post-consultation bill or Council of
State advice. Preserve every change to scope, delegated rules, authority,
deadlines, penalties, compensation, and anti-retaliation. Then locate the first
official review, inspection, or court record involving an automated platform
decision.

# Infrastructure capacity, local incidence, and state legitimacy

**Status:** fifth active depth artifact in the ongoing theme-mining program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** AI and data-center infrastructure, grids, public digital systems, defense production, dependence, local burden, and public response

## The question

When a government or firm announces strategic capacity, what becomes real,
who controls it, who pays for it, and does it change public or geopolitical
room to act? The useful unit is not “investment” or “capacity” by itself. It is
an ordered chain:

```text
announcement or allocation
  -> contract, ownership, and place
  -> construction, delivery, or migration
  -> operating capacity and reliability
  -> local jobs, revenue, prices, land, water, and service effects
  -> provider choice, substitution, and exit
  -> public attribution, contestation, legitimacy, and external leverage
```

The repo evidence is strongest in the middle of this chain. The final links
remain open unless a named asset, affected place, observed burden or benefit,
and later response can be joined.

## The fifth-pass finding

The strongest cross-source pattern is a **realization and legitimacy gap**:
formal systems are getting better at announcing, financing, allocating, and
recording strategic capacity faster than they are showing who can use it,
switch away from it, or judge its local consequences.

This is not a claim that the projects fail. It is a measurement distinction:

1. **Capacity can be real without being broadly usable.** A completed data
   center, migrated public application, or production-support contract may
   exist while access, reliability, skills retention, interoperability, or
   customer alternatives remain unknown.
2. **Local benefit can coexist with local burden.** Tax revenue or jobs can
   rise while electricity, land, water, road, service, or interruption costs
   are allocated elsewhere. A modeled residential charge is not a paid bill;
   a public comment is not representative opinion.
3. **Formal contestability is not practical exit.** A tariff, procurement
   rule, appeal route, or alliance statement creates a possible channel. It
   becomes meaningful only when an affected actor can use it, recover value,
   switch provider, or change the decision.
4. **State capacity is not automatically leverage.** Spending, ownership,
   local assembly, or interoperability may create an option. Leverage needs an
   observed option and an observed response: refusal, substitution,
   concession, retaliation, changed terms, or changed alliance behavior.
5. **Public meaning is part of the infrastructure outcome.** A nationally
   supported technology can meet local opposition when residents attribute
   energy, water, environmental, landscape, or control costs to the project.
   Conversely, an announced benefit may be accepted despite burden if trust,
   distribution, alternatives, and recourse are credible. Neither direction
   should be inferred from a single post or poll.

The social layer therefore works as a **meaning and hypothesis surface**:

points the next retrieval toward records that could confirm or disconfirm the
claim.

## Counterexamples the program must actively seek

The gap interpretation would weaken if the program finds any of the following:

- a multi-provider public system with transparent portability, successful
  switching, and measured continuity during a dispute;
- a data-center or grid project where facility-level payments, household bills,
  reliability, local benefits, and public response are joined and show no
  meaningful incidence or contestation;
- a defense or industrial program with accepted delivery, trained operators,
  maintenance authority, qualified substitutes, and an observed negotiation
  outcome attributable to the new capability;
- a locally opposed project that later gains durable support after credible
  compensation, cost allocation, monitoring, and recourse;
- a high-burden project that retains legitimacy because benefits are visibly
  distributed, alternatives are real, and affected people can change the
  terms.

These are not exceptions to discard. They are the comparison cases needed to
separate dependence, burden, and legitimacy from simple project scale.

## Next event-ledger test

The next retrieval should not collect more announcements indiscriminately. It
should select a small number of named cases and fill the same row contract:

```text
case and place
  -> announcement / allocation
  -> contract, owner, supplier, and financing
  -> construction, migration, delivery, or acceptance
  -> measured operation, load, reliability, or service
  -> local revenue, jobs, price, land, water, outage, or household incidence
  -> provider choice, substitution, termination, or maintenance authority
  -> public comment, trust, organizing, permit, litigation, or policy change
  -> external response, bargaining, refusal, or changed alliance behavior
```

For every row, retain the date, unit, population or asset, method, source
locator, observed value, denominator, and missing link. Promote a claim only
when the row has a direct source and its stage is not being substituted for a
later stage.

## Operating conclusion

This pass does not close the cultural-geopolitical program. It gives the next
rotations a common test: **capacity becomes public or geopolitical power only
when it is usable, attributable, contestable, and capable of changing an
actor's options.** Social discussion helps reveal the meaning and disputed
incidence of each project; the research-signal pipeline must then verify those
claims against dated operational, fiscal, administrative, and behavioral
records.

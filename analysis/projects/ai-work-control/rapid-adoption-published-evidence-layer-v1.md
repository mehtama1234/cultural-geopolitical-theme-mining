# Published rapid genAI adoption evidence layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Status:** peer-reviewed article located; replication-file acquisition remains
access-limited

## Source

[Bick, Blandin, and Deming, “The Rapid Adoption of Generative AI,” Management
Science](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.02523), published online January 20,
2026. The authors identify the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, Vanderbilt
University, Harvard Kennedy School, CEPR, and NBER affiliations. The article
page provides a supplemental appendix and links to replication files.

## Directly reported claims

The published abstract reports a series of nationally representative US
surveys of genAI use at work and at home. As of late 2024, 45% of the US
population ages 18–64 used genAI; among employed respondents, 27% used it for
work at least once in the prior week, including 10% every workday and 17% on
some but not all workdays. The abstract also reports that 1%–7% of work hours
were assisted by genAI and that respondents reported time savings equivalent to
1.4% of total work hours.

These are published article-level claims, not a new estimate generated in this
repository. They should not be silently merged with the W35677 task-index
vintage, which uses later RPS waves and different task-level questions.

## Why it matters for the program

The article supplies a broad adoption/intensity anchor that sits above the
W35677 detailed task layer. It helps distinguish:

```text
population/work adoption and assisted-hour intensity
  -> task-specific worker adoption
  -> employer access, policy, training, and workflow change
  -> control, pace, evaluation, pay, security, and voice
```

Only the first stage is summarized here. The article’s abstract reports that
firm climate and policies play an important role in adoption patterns, but this
layer does not extract a firm-level policy estimate or establish downstream
worker power.

## Access and reproducibility boundary

The publisher page lists an online appendix and replication files. The direct
replication endpoint was not downloadable in the current retrieval session, so
no replication data, subgroup table, or re-estimated result is promoted. A
future acquisition must preserve the exact file, release date, hash, survey
wave, variable definition, weights, and missing-data rules before extending the
registry.

## What remains open

- Whether the published late-2024 adoption figures are directly comparable to
  the August 2025–May 2026 W35677 task indexes.
- Which firms, workers, occupations, and tasks account for assisted-hour
  intensity.
- Whether reported time savings remain with workers or are absorbed by new
  demands.
- Whether adoption changes discretion, monitoring, pay, household security,
  trust, or collective action.

**Evidence status:** published article-level reported result; replication-file,
worker-level subgroup, workplace-control, and downstream social-outcome links
remain open.

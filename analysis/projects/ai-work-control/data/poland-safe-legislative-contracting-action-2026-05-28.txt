Normalized observations:
- The Sejm legislative process records the government SAFE bill as a financing instrument for defense investments, with the bill enacted after parliamentary consideration in February 2026. The exact procedural dates and legislative stages remain tied to the official process page.
- The Ministry of National Defence reports that the Armament Agency and the Cyber Defense Component signed 62 SAFE-financed contracts on 2026-05-28 through 2026-05-30, totaling approximately PLN 120 billion, and states that all contracts were concluded with Polish defense industry.
- The Ministry's program page lists contract categories and quantities including 56 mobile communications nodes, 18 SAN air-defense fire modules, 190 FlyEye unmanned-system sets, more than 400 Warmate sets, 146 Borsuk infantry fighting vehicles, hundreds of thousands of 155mm artillery rounds, and other equipment; these are reported contract scopes, not delivered quantities.
- The official June 1 summary frames SAFE as Polish equipment for the Polish armed forces, industrial capability, operational capacity, production independence, and long-term national security. Those are institutional claims and program meanings, not independent measures of realized autonomy or public reception.

Boundary: this is evidence of legislative authorization, institutional execution, and a large domestic-industry contracting wave. It is not evidence of Barracuda-500M inclusion, plant commissioning, completed output, acceptance, delivery, local-content share, household incidence, public support, or geopolitical leverage. Contract signing is kept separate from performance and capability realization.

Uncertainty: the government pages are official summaries and do not provide the full contract texts, obligation/payment schedules, supplier-level value shares, delivery milestones, acceptance records, or product-to-Barracuda mapping. “Polish defense industry” is a source-level category and does not by itself establish component sovereignty or replaceability.

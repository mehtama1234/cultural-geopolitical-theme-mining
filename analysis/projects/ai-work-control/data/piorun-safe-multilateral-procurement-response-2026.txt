Normalized observations:
- On 2026-08-24, Poland's Ministry of National Defence reported a framework agreement with Mesko for several thousand Piorun short-range air-defense sets, simulators, and support packages, valued at PLN 8 billion, for Poland and other NATO states. It named Lithuania, Latvia, and Norway as participating allied users and said more than 80% of the sets would arm Polish forces.
- On 2026-09-11, the Polish Prime Minister's Office reported that the Armament Agency and Mesko signed the first executive contract under the framework and that Norway, Latvia, and Lithuania had decided to buy Piorun systems. This is an external-demand and multilateral-procurement response, not a delivery or operational-use record.
- MESKO's 2026-08-24 company account adds a schedule and scale boundary: the EDIRPA VSHORAD framework covers several thousand missiles and several hundred launchers, with planned executive-contract value above PLN 8 billion net, more than 80% intended for Polish forces, and deliveries stated to run through 2030. These remain reported framework quantities and a planned horizon, not accepted inventory or completed SAFE output.
- The same MESKO account reports Piorun annual capacity rising from about 300 missiles several years earlier to about 1,500 currently, with an ambition above 2,000. This is company-reported capacity context, not an audited output series or a SAFE-attributable production increment; the source does not specify utilization, critical inputs, allocation, or accepted deliveries.
- On 2026-01-16, Lithuania's Ministry of National Defence described Lithuania as purchasing Piorun together with Poland, Estonia, and Norway and reported a Lithuanian-side deal value of approximately EUR 154 million. On 2026-09-09, Lithuania reported discussing the progress of acquiring Piorun systems and supply-chain participation with Poland.
- On 2026-06-23, a Lithuanian Ministry of National Defence inventory update still described acquisition of the very-short-range Piorun system as planned for that year. In the bounded official-source recheck through 2026-09-18, no country-level Piorun handover, acceptance, or operational-use notice attributable to the 2026 SAFE/EDIRPA route was located.
- MESKO's official archive dates the first Norway handover to 2023-12-12. The underlying notice says the contract was signed in Oslo “a year ago” and covered several hundred missiles and launchers. It does not identify the 2026 SAFE/EDIRPA framework, so it is retained as a dated prior bilateral export-delivery boundary rather than SAFE performance evidence.
- A Polish Ministry of State Assets report published 2025-06-17 states that Mesko's three-thousandth Piorun system left the production line in 2024. This is a pre-SAFE product-level production baseline; it is not a measure of the 2026 SAFE framework's incremental output or delivery rate.
- The official MESKO framework account is added as a company-reported scale/schedule/capacity layer, not as a government acceptance or delivery record.

Boundary: the record advances the domestic-contracting -> allied procurement/market response arrow and adds one separately scoped, dated 2023 Norway export handover report. It does not establish SAFE-delivered quantities, acceptance, readiness, battlefield use, export revenue, supplier replaceability, or that SAFE caused the foreign decisions. The Polish and Lithuanian pages report procurement/framework stages; “will buy” and “purchasing” are not treated as completed delivery.

Uncertainty: the sources do not provide country-by-country quantities, full contract identifiers, payment/obligation schedules, delivery lot dates, acceptance documents, SAFE-specific production lots, audited capacity/output, or an independent causal counterfactual. The Lithuanian 2024/2026 procurement references and the Polish August framework may represent linked but separately documented stages. The Norway handover is dated by the MESKO archive but its exact contract date and quantity are not given, and it is not linked to SAFE; it is therefore not pooled with the 2026 framework. The SAFE-route non-observation is bounded to the official pages and search route checked; it is not proof that no SAFE delivery occurred privately or under a differently described record.

Normalized observations:
- On 2026-08-24, Poland's Ministry of National Defence reported a framework agreement with Mesko for several thousand Piorun short-range air-defense sets, simulators, and support packages, valued at PLN 8 billion, for Poland and other NATO states. It named Lithuania, Latvia, and Norway as participating allied users and said more than 80% of the sets would arm Polish forces.
- On 2026-09-11, the Polish Prime Minister's Office reported that the Armament Agency and Mesko signed the first executive contract under the framework and that Norway, Latvia, and Lithuania had decided to buy Piorun systems. This is an external-demand and multilateral-procurement response, not a delivery or operational-use record.
- On 2026-01-16, Lithuania's Ministry of National Defence described Lithuania as purchasing Piorun together with Poland, Estonia, and Norway and reported a Lithuanian-side deal value of approximately EUR 154 million. On 2026-09-09, Lithuania reported discussing the progress of acquiring Piorun systems and supply-chain participation with Poland.
- On 2026-06-23, a Lithuanian Ministry of National Defence inventory update still described acquisition of the very-short-range Piorun system as planned for that year. In the bounded official-source recheck through 2026-09-18, no country-level Piorun handover, acceptance, or operational-use notice was located.
- A Polish Ministry of State Assets report published 2025-06-17 states that Mesko's three-thousandth Piorun system left the production line in 2024. This is a pre-SAFE product-level production baseline; it is not a measure of the 2026 SAFE framework's incremental output or delivery rate.

Boundary: the record advances the domestic-contracting -> allied procurement/market response arrow. It does not establish delivered Piorun quantities, acceptance, readiness, battlefield use, export revenue, supplier replaceability, or that SAFE caused the foreign decisions. The Polish and Lithuanian pages report procurement/framework stages; “will buy” and “purchasing” are not treated as completed delivery.

Uncertainty: the sources do not provide country-by-country quantities, full contract identifiers, payment/obligation schedules, delivery dates, acceptance documents, SAFE-specific production lots, or an independent causal counterfactual. The Lithuanian 2024/2026 procurement references and the Polish August framework may represent linked but separately documented stages. The non-observation is bounded to the official pages and search route checked; it is not proof that no delivery occurred privately or under a differently described record.

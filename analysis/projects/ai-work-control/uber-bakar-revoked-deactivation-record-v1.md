# Uber / Bakar deactivation-jurisdiction record v1

**Status:** primary Fair Work Commission eligibility decision later revoked by
consent; settlement and platform-operator identity gap observed  
**Checked:** 2026-09-15

## Why this record matters

The Bakar proceeding is the necessary contrast to Bandameeda. It shows that a
worker can clear an important eligibility barrier and still leave the public
record without a merits decision, remedy, or durable precedent. It also makes
cross-border corporate structure operationally important: the Commission
record raised the possibility that the named Australian respondent was not the
operator of the Uber App, while Uber Technologies Inc. was not a party served
in the proceeding.

## Source and scope

In *Mian Abu Bakar v Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd* ([2025] FWC 1874; UDE2025/59),
the Commission initially found on 2 July 2025 that Mr Bakar had performed work
through the Uber App regularly for at least six months and was protected from
unfair deactivation. The work included Uber Eats and Uber Driver Partner
activity. That decision addressed the statutory protection threshold, not
whether the underlying deactivation was fair.

On 5 August 2025, in [2025] FWC 2278, the Commission revoked the earlier
decision by consent after the parties settled and the worker filed a notice of
discontinuance. The Commission said the identity of the platform operator and
the cross-service regular-work question remained open for determination in
other proceedings.

## Evidence matrix

| Stage | Primary record | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | The initial decision found the six-month regular-work requirement satisfied and treated the worker as protected at the time of deactivation | This finding was later revoked and cannot be used as a current precedent in this proceeding |
| Corporate identity | The Commission recorded evidence that Uber Technologies Inc. operated the App, while noting a suggestion that Rasier Pacific might be the operator | The public decision does not resolve the corporate-operator question |
| Merits review | No merits review of the underlying deactivation is shown in the revocation decision | The record does not establish notice quality, human review, explanation, correction, or refusal |
| Settlement | The parties reached an agreement and the worker filed a notice of discontinuance | Settlement terms, payment, reinstatement, and confidentiality conditions are not publicly established |
| Remedy | No reactivation, correction, lost-pay, compensation, or other remedy is ordered in the public revocation decision | A settlement may have included relief, but absence of public terms is not evidence that no relief occurred |
| Precedent | The earlier eligibility conclusion was revoked; the Commission expressly left the legal question open for later cases | The outcome is best coded as a procedural and corporate-accountability boundary, not a worker win or loss on deactivation fairness |

## Remedy coding

```text
platform deactivation              observed_case_report
eligibility threshold               observed_adjudicated_then_revoked
merits explanation                  not_observed
human review                        not_observed

payment / compensation              not_observed (settlement terms unavailable)
anti-retaliation                    not_observed
platform-operator accountability   observed_adjudicated_concern_only
```

The case exposes a different failure mode from Bandameeda. In Bandameeda,
external adjudication reached process failure and ordered restoration. In
Bakar, a threshold decision was withdrawn after settlement, while respondent
identity and enforceability remained legally consequential. A formal remedy
system can therefore be present while the public record cannot show which
entity bears responsibility or what the worker ultimately received.

## Comparative interpretation

```text
Bandameeda: decision -> response -> deficient review -> reactivation + lost pay
Bakar: deactivation -> eligibility finding -> operator-identity concern -> settlement -> decision revoked
```

This is not evidence that the Australian regime failed. It is evidence that
access to a merits remedy depends on timing, eligibility, named respondent,
service, settlement, and the ability to impose an order on the responsible
platform entity. Those procedural variables belong in the atlas alongside the
substantive question of whether a decision was fair.

## Boundary

This record does not establish the settlement terms, payment, reinstatement,
or the merits of the underlying deactivation. It establishes only the public
procedural path: an eligibility finding, later revocation by consent, a
corporate-operator concern, and an unresolved question for future proceedings.

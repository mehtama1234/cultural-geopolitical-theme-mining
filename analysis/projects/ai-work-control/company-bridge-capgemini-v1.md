# Company bridge: Capgemini as an AI-using service firm

**Status:** first pass; descriptive and provisional  
**Last checked:** 2026-09-11  
**Project:** AI, work, and control

## Why this company matters

Capgemini is not mainly an AI platform vendor. It is a large service and technology firm whose work depends on people, skills, projects, delivery processes, and client contracts. Its annual report gives us a customer/operator view of what AI adoption looks like inside a workforce.

## What Capgemini reports

Its 2025 reports say:

- the group has more than 420,000 talents in more than 50 countries;
- 310,000 employees were trained in generative AI and 194,000 in agentic AI, excluding WNS;
- average completed learning reached 97 hours per employee in 2025;
- an internal AI enterprise-intelligence platform is available to all employees and combines company applications, internal data, curated external data, routine self-service, document generation, and role-specific agents;
- AI-driven changes to role definitions, skills, and career paths are managed through talent and workforce-pyramid monitoring, upskilling, and mobility mechanisms;
- the group expects about €700 million in restructuring costs over the next two years as it adapts its workforce and skills to changing demand and AI-driven productivity expectations.

Sources: [learning at the speed of AI](https://reports.capgemini.com/2025/en/doc/article/55/), [employee data and AI platform](https://reports.capgemini.com/2025/en/doc/article/54/), [2025 Universal Registration Document](https://investors.capgemini.com/en/file/30242?download=1), and [2025 results](https://investors.capgemini.com/en/publication/fy-2025-results/).

## What this adds to the theme

Capgemini’s own description joins four things that are often discussed separately:

```text
AI tool access -> training at scale -> role and career redesign -> workforce cost and risk adjustment
```

This supports the theme that work can be reorganized before average pay or employment data makes the change obvious. It also gives the project a sharper material question: who pays for the transition when a service firm must train, redeploy, hire selectively, or restructure?

## Four-map reading

| Map | Company signal | Missing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Material | training investment, productivity expectations, restructuring cost, client demand | realized savings, margins, pay, exits, workload |
| Social | learning and career paths are presented as central to the employee experience | worker voice, stress, status, belonging, and unequal access |
| Institution | HR and delivery jointly monitor skills and workforce shape | consultation, appeal, collective bargaining, enforcement |
| Power | the firm controls the employee data and the platform used to shape opportunities | who can correct a profile, reject an assignment, or contest a career decision |

## First-principles inference

```text
service work is decomposed into skills and tasks
  -> skills become visible in a company platform
  -> assignments and career paths can be reorganized at scale
  -> workers gain mobility only if they can access, understand, and challenge the system
```

The first two steps are company-described. The last step is untested.

## Strongest challenge

Large training numbers can describe reach without proving learning. Restructuring costs can reflect many forces besides AI. The report tells us what management is building and watching; it does not tell us whether workers receive more choice, higher pay, or better jobs.

## Next tests

# AI infrastructure realization and control layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded cross-country comparison; public outcomes and switching
costs remain open

This layer deepens the geopolitical/state-consequence theme by separating
what an infrastructure project promised, what was actually delivered, who
controlled it at each date, and what remains unknown about local benefit and
exit. It compares two different infrastructure forms rather than treating
them as one common treatment: Romania's government-cloud migration and
Malaysia's privately developed Johor hyperscale campus.

## The question

Does infrastructure create durable public or national capability, or does it
create usable capacity while control and dependence remain elsewhere?

```text
funding and procurement
  -> construction or migration
  -> operating capacity
  -> service, skills, jobs, and local distribution
  -> ownership, provider choice, inspection, and exit
  -> state bargaining power and public trust
```

The first two arrows can be documented without claiming the later ones.

## Comparison

| Dimension | Romania | Malaysia |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate asset | Public applications and government-cloud infrastructure | Hyperscale data-centre campus and power connection |
| Realized milestone located | 7 migrated applications by July 2025 | First 25MW handed to a client by June 2025 |
| Planned scale | At least 30 applications by June 2026 | More than 300MW across three facilities when fully developed |
| Primary control question | Which public body, contractor, or provider can alter, inspect, renew, or replace the system? | What changed when the campus moved from Yondr to Vantage and into an APAC platform backed by external institutional capital? |
| Public-facing outcome still missing | Reliability, waiting time, access, privacy, appeal, and cost after migration | Local jobs, wages, skills, taxes, water, energy incidence, customer terms, and service or strategic replaceability |

## What the comparison supports

1. Announced scale and realized operation are different measurements. Romania's
   target and Malaysia's 300MW plan cannot be counted as delivered outcomes;
   the records do provide an interim 7-application milestone and a first
   25MW handover.
2. Ownership is time-varying. The Johor asset was financed and developed under
   Yondr and later transferred to Vantage; an infrastructure map that freezes
   the original sponsor misses the control transition.
3. Capability and dependence can rise together. Government migration can give
   agencies a shared operating base; a hyperscale campus can add regional
   compute. Neither fact establishes portability, local learning, or the
   ability to refuse a provider.
4. The public or geopolitical result is an outcome to measure, not a synonym
   for capacity. The missing variables are service quality, incidence of cost,
   local skills and suppliers, ownership, inspection, customer concentration,
   renewal, termination, interoperability, and alternative capacity.

## Counterevidence required

The dependence interpretation would weaken if a project has multiple
interchangeable providers, transparent termination and portability terms,
measurable local skills retention, broadly distributed benefits, and a public
record showing that agencies or customers can inspect, bargain, switch, or
continue service during a dispute. A locally operated or deliberately
multi-provider project is the needed comparator.

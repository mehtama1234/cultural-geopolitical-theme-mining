# Near-complete remedy follow-up recheck v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Purpose:** test whether the strongest retained worker-voice remedy cases have
public post-order evidence that can close payment, safe return, durability, or
later choice.

## Result

The official public search did not locate a post-remedy record that closes the
remaining stages for either selected case. This is an acquisition boundary,
not evidence that either remedy failed.

The 2026-09-20 recheck also searched the Fair Work Commission's current
lost-remuneration guidance and 2026 bulletin material for a later payment,
compliance, account-status, or employment record. It returned the existing
Hotak/Rehman order-level material, but no worker-specific receipt or later
outcome. The official order remains evidence of an adjudicated obligation and
formal restoration route, not verified payment or durable recovery.

**Fresh aggregate-release check — 2026-09-20:** the Fair Work Ombudsman
reported more than $453 million recovered for more than 181,000 workers in
2025–26. This is a useful current institutional-capacity and aggregate-reach
signal, but it does not identify the selected worker, payment instrument or
date, net adequacy, later work, recurrence, household effect, trust, or exit.
It is therefore retained as a saturation check and not promoted as a
worker-level receipt or recovery endpoint.

| Episode | Strongest public endpoint | Follow-up not found |
|---|---|---|
| DOJ IER DACA employment case | The worker complained after termination, was rehired at a lower wage, then received a reported restoration of the prior wage, $4,712.50 in back pay, and seniority; the complaint was withdrawn | Payment instrument/date, continued employment, actual benefit use, household recovery, recurrence, trust, and exit |
| OSHA Texas asbestos whistleblower case | Two workers raised asbestos and environmental concerns, were reportedly fired, and received an OSHA retaliation finding with a reinstatement and damages order exceeding $200,000 | Individual payment, actual return, safe work conditions, employer compliance, recurrence protection, household recovery, trust, and exit |
| **Matthai, [2025] FWC 1938 / [2026] FWCFB 42** | A University of Melbourne professor's reinstatement, continuity, and lost-remuneration remedy was upheld on appeal after the Full Bench reviewed whether trust and confidence could be restored | Actual return to work, payment receipt, workplace relationship, later tenure, recurrence, trust, and exit |

## Interpretation

These are unusually strong middle-chain records because worker voice, an
institutional route, and a material remedy are visible in the same episode.
They still do not close the lived-agency endpoint. Complaint withdrawal is not
the same as generalized trust; reinstatement is not the same as safe return;
and a reported damages order is not the same as money received or adequate
recovery. The Matthai appeal sharpens the same boundary: final legal
confirmation that reinstatement was within the Commission's discretion still
does not document that the worker returned, was paid, or remained in the job.

The next lawful upgrade would require a worker-side record, a public payment or
compliance filing, a later employment/safety record, or another explicitly
linked post-remedy source. Do not infer those stages from the original agency
release.

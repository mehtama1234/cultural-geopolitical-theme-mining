# NBER W34851 source record: education gaps and generative AI

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Project:** AI, work, and control  
**Source type:** randomized online experiment  
**Evidence status:** direct abstract and paper-summary evidence; full-paper review remains open

## Source

[Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps? Evidence from a
Randomized Experiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851), Guillermo Cruces,
Diego Fernández Meijide, Sebastian Galiani, Ramiro H. Gálvez, and María
Lombardi, NBER Working Paper 34851, February 2026, revised March 2026.

## Design and unit

The experiment assigns 1,174 adults aged 25–45 with differing education levels
to complete an incentivized, workplace-style business problem-solving task with
or without a generative-AI assistant. A follow-up module tests performance
without AI access.

## Direct result

AI improves task performance for all participants, with larger gains for
lower-education participants. The higher-education advantage falls from 0.548
standard deviations without AI to 0.139 standard deviations with AI—about
three-quarters of the initial gap. A follow-up without AI shows that some gains
persist, but a substantial education gap reappears.

## Why it matters

This is a genuine counterpoint to a simple “AI increases inequality” theme. AI
can reduce effective differences in task execution when it relaxes constraints
that bind more strongly for lower-education participants. At the same time,
underlying human-capital differences still affect unassisted performance and
how effectively people use the tool.

## Limits and open links

- The experiment is outside firms and uses one incentivized task, so it does
  not establish workplace effects across occupations or industries.
- It does not measure employer control, pay, promotion, job security, health,
  worker voice, or household outcomes.
- The result does not show that AI narrows every social or economic inequality;
  access, training, task choice, and organizational rules may change the
  result.
- The long-term retention result needs the full paper's outcome definitions,
  uncertainty, and follow-up interpretation.

## Placement in the evidence chain

```text
AI access
  -> lower-education participants gain more on a defined task
  -> effective task-performance gap narrows while unassisted skill gaps remain
  -> workplace assignment, recognition, pay, and control may or may not change
```

The source directly informs the task-performance arrow. The workplace,
cultural, and distributional consequences remain open.

**Bounded claim:** In this randomized task experiment, AI narrowed—but did not
erase—education-based performance differences, showing that AI can be
equalizing for a measured task while leaving underlying capability differences
and institutional outcomes unresolved.

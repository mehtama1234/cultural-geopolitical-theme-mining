# Mansoor v. Rasier: substitute earnings and formal remedy in one deactivation case

**Status:** primary adjudicated case upgrade; payment receipt and durable recovery remain open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-18

## Bounded result

The Fair Work Commission's merits decision for Shahid Mansoor records both a
platform deactivation and income earned through other work during the
deactivation period. The decision states that the worker received **$9,000
from Baysec Group Pty Ltd** for new work after deactivation. It also compares
Didi earnings before and after deactivation and estimates an additional **$1,760
increased Didi earnings** after the event. The Commission deducted those
substitute earnings when calculating a **$6,073.23 gross lost-earnings order**.

The accompanying order required reactivation on the prior terms within seven
days and payment of the ordered amount within 14 days. This is stronger than
the prior generic platform ledger: an alternative-work response and a formal
remedy are now observed in the same named case.

## Event chain

```text
platform deactivation
  -> loss of primary platform earnings and financial hardship reported
  -> Baysec and increased Didi earnings observed during the gap
  -> Commission deducts substitute earnings in lost-pay calculation
  -> reactivation order + $6,073.23 gross lost-earnings order
  -> [open] payment receipt, actual access, net recovery, durability, exit
```

| Stage | What the primary record establishes | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Uber Driver Platform access deactivated 13 June 2025; Commission found the deactivation unfair | One named case, not a worker-population rate |
| Alternative work | $9,000 in new Baysec earnings and an estimated $1,760 increase in Didi earnings after deactivation | Other work is observed; voluntary choice, hours, expenses, conditions, and net income are not established |

## What this changes in the atlas

This case closes an important intermediate arrow:

```text
loss of platform access -> identifiable substitute earnings -> adjudicated remedy
```

It does not close the practical-optionality arrow. Substitute work may have
been necessary rather than freely chosen, and the record does not reveal its
quality, costs, schedule, or whether the worker could refuse it. The order's
14-day deadline also does not establish that payment occurred. The safe coding
is therefore **alternative work observed; verified remedy receipt open**.

This supplies a useful counterexample to both extremes: formal restoration can
coexist with alternative work during the interruption, while alternative work
does not prove recovery or exit from the original platform.

## Next decisive test

The smallest follow-up is a public post-order compliance record or a lawful
de-identified worker-side record showing payment receipt, actual account
restoration, alternative-work costs, and later continued use, switching,
non-use, or exit. Until then, this case should not be promoted to practical
exit.

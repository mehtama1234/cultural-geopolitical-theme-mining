## Question

Did the public Fair Work Commission record move beyond the merits decision's
reactivation order and direction to confer about lost remuneration?

## Result

The recheck found the same two dated endpoints already established in the
primary decision and the Commission's bulletin:

| Stage | Public evidence | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Unfair deactivation | Commission found the complaint-based process unfair and non-compliant | Whether Uber changed its process for later cases |
| Restoration remedy | Reactivation was ordered | Actual account reactivation date and durable access |
| Lost remuneration | Parties were directed to confer and report the outcome by 20 November 2025 | Agreed quantum, later order, payment, and receipt |
| Worker outcome | No public same-worker follow-up located in the checked record | Continued work, alternative work, income recovery, switching, or exit |

The Fair Work Commission's public document-search page explains that decisions
and orders are published through its search system. A targeted search using the
case number, applicant name, order number PR793486, and the lost-pay terms did
not locate a subsequent public order or implementation record in the sources
reviewed here. This is a bounded non-observation, not proof that no private
agreement, payment, or account restoration occurred.

## Why this matters for the broad atlas

This case now has a clean stopping point between institutional remedy and lived
recovery:

```text
platform deactivation
  -> worker response and statutory route
  -> adjudicated unfairness
  -> reactivation order
  -> actual access, payment, durable work, and household recovery [open]
```

The order is not receipt. A public decision is not proof of implementation.
The absence of a public follow-up is itself a measurement boundary: public
recourse records can show contestability and formal remedy while concealing
whether the person regained income, continued using the platform, found an
alternative, or left.

## Smallest decisive next test

The next useful evidence is one of: a later Commission order or directions
document; a verified account-restoration record; or a lawful, de-identified
same-worker follow-up that records actual access, payment receipt, alternative
work, recurrence, and protected or sacrificed household outcomes. Do not infer
any of these from the reactivation order alone.

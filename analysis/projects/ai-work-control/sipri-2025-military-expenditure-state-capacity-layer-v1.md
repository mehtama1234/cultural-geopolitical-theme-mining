# SIPRI 2025 military expenditure: spending is capacity context, not capability

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Status:** official SIPRI 2025 vintage extraction · monetary state-capacity context

## What changed

SIPRI reports that world military expenditure reached $2.887 trillion in 2025,
up 2.9% in real terms and marking the eleventh consecutive year of growth.
The United States remained the largest spender at $954 billion, but US spending
fell 7.5% while China rose 7.4% to an estimated $336 billion. Europe rose 14%
to $864 billion and Asia and Oceania rose 8.1% to $681 billion.

The pattern is not simply “more military spending everywhere.” It is a
reallocation and acceleration pattern: global totals rose, US spending fell in
part because of reduced supplemental military assistance, and European and
Asian spending grew sharply.

## Alliance and fiscal context

The 32 NATO members accounted for $1.581 trillion, or 55% of world military
spending; European NATO members accounted for $559 billion, and 23 NATO
members reached at least 2% of GDP under SIPRI's methodology. These figures
describe monetary allocation and burden claims. They do not establish readiness,
interoperability, procurement delivery, industrial bottlenecks, or durable
public support.

## End-to-end route under test

```text
security threat and alliance pressure
  -> budget allocation and military-industrial demand
  -> procurement, production, readiness, and alliance capacity
  -> fiscal/social-spending trade-offs and public legitimacy
  -> external deterrence, dependence, or geopolitical leverage
```

SIPRI directly measures the budget-allocation stage. The procurement,
production, household, legitimacy, and external-behavior stages require the
program's USASpending, industrial, IMF, World Bank, survey, and case evidence.

## Method and limits

SIPRI's figures are based on open sources, with some national values estimated.
Current and constant US-dollar values, GDP shares, government-expenditure
shares, and calendar/financial-year conventions are distinct. The 2025 release
replaces earlier SIPRI military-expenditure data, so prior-vintage comparisons
must preserve the release vintage. Spending per person and military burden are
not welfare or capability measures.

## Next test

Link the 2025 spending vintage to dated procurement awards, delivery and
production milestones, component suppliers, energy/industrial capacity, fiscal
space, social spending, public opinion, and observed alliance or external
responses. Preserve cases where spending rose without delivery or where a
state's capability remained replaceable despite higher allocation.

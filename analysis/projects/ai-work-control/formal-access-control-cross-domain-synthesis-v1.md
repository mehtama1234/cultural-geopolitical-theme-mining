# The first distributional divide can occur before the visible outcome

**Status:** bounded cross-domain mechanism synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The comparison

Two otherwise different evidence layers point to the same stage boundary:
access is not a binary entitlement. It is filtered through disclosure,
language, technology, training, permission, task control, and interface fit
before a later employment or productivity outcome can appear.

| Domain | Early access/control signal | Later outcome signal | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disability workforce programs | Adult participants reporting disabilities used training services at 33% versus 47% among those not reporting a disability; GAO documents interpreter, technology, and outside-training barriers | Second-quarter employment 60% versus 76%; median earnings among employed $7,048 versus $8,872 | Accommodation receipt, completion, placement mechanism, retention, and causal contribution of the interface barriers |
| AI at work | Worker AI use is higher among graduate-educated workers and workers reporting greater task control; perceived time/quality benefit is reported | Firm executives report high adoption but little retrospective employment/productivity impact; future employer and employee expectations differ | Who grants permission, receives training, captures gains, faces monitoring, changes pay/hours, or can switch/exit |

The shared mechanism is:

```text
nominal access or system availability
  -> permission, disclosure, language, skill, task control, and usable fit
  -> participation and realized output
  -> pay, security, status, voice, or bargaining power
  -> correction, trust, switching, or exit
```

The important claim is not that disability and AI experiences are equivalent.
They are not. The claim is that measuring only the later outcome can hide the
earlier allocation of usable control.

## The work-control ladder

For work and capability questions, the conversion ladder is:

```text
system availability
  -> permission and usable fit
  -> task discretion and contestability
  -> pay, hours, status, and bargaining power
  -> household security and civic room
  -> retention, switching, voice, or exit
```

This prevents “AI adopted,” “training offered,” or “job retained” from being
treated as proof that the worker gained control. A system can raise output
while concentrating decisions, monitoring, or captured gains elsewhere; a
program can increase participation while leaving the person with little power
over the job, schedule, or remedy.

## Why this matters to the broad atlas

This comparison strengthens the program-wide distinction between formal
capacity and practical option space. A workforce program can exist while the
person cannot use its interpreter, technology, transport, or training route.
An AI system can exist while the worker lacks permission, task control,
training, or the ability to contest how it is used. In both cases, a later
employment or productivity gap cannot by itself identify where control was
lost.

The cultural layer is also material here. Disclosure, language, disability,
education, occupation, and perceived control affect whether a person is seen
as capable, trusted, trainable, or replaceable. Those meanings can shape
participation and bargaining before any formal remedy or productivity number is
recorded.

## Boundaries

- GAO disability-program participants and Federal Reserve/NBER AI measures are
  different populations, periods, units, and designs; this is not a pooled
  effect or common inequality rate.
- Lower training use or higher AI use does not prove discrimination, exclusion,
  or that control caused the difference.
- Post-exit employment and earnings are not household security, and perceived
  AI time/quality gains are not measured productivity or realized pay.
- The comparison does not observe a same employer-worker episode, remedy,
  household consequence, trust change, switching, or exit.

## Next decisive test

Build one identifier-bearing worker implementation episode with: access or
disclosure, accommodation/permission, training, task allocation, monitoring,
output, pay/hours, worker voice, correction, household effect, and later
retention, switching, or exit. Keep the disability and AI routes as separate
comparators unless a lawful same-employer design holds the unit constant.

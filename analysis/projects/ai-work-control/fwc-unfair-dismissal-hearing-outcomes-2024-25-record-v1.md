# Fair Work Commission unfair-dismissal hearing outcomes record v1

**Status:** official hearing-outcome benchmark; formal remedy stage only  
**Checked:** 2026-09-19

## What the benchmark shows

The Fair Work Commission reports the outcomes of unfair-dismissal matters that
went to a hearing. In 2024–25 it records 263 hearing outcomes:

| Hearing outcome | Count |
|---|---:|
| Dismissal found fair / application dismissed | 89 |
| Application granted, no remedy | 7 |
| Application granted, monetary remedy | 134 |
| Application granted, reinstatement | 4 |
| Application granted, reinstatement and lost remuneration | 5 |
| Application granted, remedy to be determined | 19 |
| Application granted, parties settled on remedy | 5 |
| **Total** | **263** |

Among the six “application granted” categories, monetary-only outcomes are the
largest single category. Reinstatement appears in 9 cases when the
“reinstatement” and “reinstatement and lost remuneration” rows are combined.
This is a description of formal hearing outcomes, not a worker recovery rate.

## Five-year context

The same official table reports the following hearing-outcome counts:

| Hearing year | Total | Monetary only | Reinstatement only | Reinstatement + lost remuneration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020–21 | 313 | 144 | 4 | 13 |
| 2021–22 | 215 | 50 | 1 | 20 |
| 2022–23 | 296 | 75 | 7 | 9 |
| 2023–24 | 265 | 142 | 4 | 13 |
| 2024–25 | 263 | 134 | 4 | 5 |

Monetary-only outcomes are the largest named category in four of the five
years; 2021–22 is the exception, when the table shows more reinstatement-plus-
lost-remuneration outcomes than monetary-only outcomes. The pattern is
therefore recurring but not uniform. It describes what the Commission ordered
or recorded at hearing, not what workers ultimately received or experienced.

## Why it matters for the end-to-end atlas

The benchmark gives the case-level remedy records a useful institutional
context. Across five years, formal outcomes repeatedly favor monetary-only
orders over direct job restoration, while the balance varies by year:

```text
unfair-dismissal hearing
  -> formal result
  -> monetary remedy is more common than job restoration
  -> payment receipt, job quality, household recovery, trust, and exit [open]
```

That pattern should not be read as proof that reinstatement would have been
better, or that compensation was inadequate. It does show why “the remedy”
cannot be treated as a single endpoint: money, restored employment, and a
future-safe workplace are different outputs.

## Boundaries

- The table covers hearing outcomes, not applications, conferences,
  settlements outside the listed categories, or all unfair-dismissal claims.
- The categories are institutional outcomes; they do not report whether money
  was paid, whether reinstatement was accepted or durable, or whether a worker
  recovered household security.
- The table has no worker-level denominator, exposure measure, alternative-work
  record, trust measure, recurrence measure, or exit outcome.
- The counts should not be pooled with the 27 purposive platform cases or with
  the individual Fair Work case examples as if they shared a population.

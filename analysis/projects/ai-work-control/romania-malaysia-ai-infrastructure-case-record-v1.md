# Romania and Malaysia AI-infrastructure case record v1

**Status:** primary-record compilation; outcomes still open  
**Accessed:** 2026-09-11  
**Project:** AI, work, and control  
**Use limit:** financing, project targets, and ownership announcements are not proof of realized public benefit, local jobs, data sovereignty, or bargaining power.

## Why these cases are together

The two cases show different ways that a state becomes connected to digital infrastructure:

- Romania: public applications and government systems moving into cloud infrastructure;
- Malaysia: a large privately operated data-centre campus supported by development finance and connected to regional capital.

They should not be scored against each other. They are two different links in the capability-and-dependence chain.

## Case A — Romania government cloud migration

### Primary records

- [Romanian Authority for Digitalization: Investment 2 — migration of applications and systems to cloud](https://www.adr.gov.ro/en/investitia-2-dezvoltarea-cloudului-si-migrarea-in-cloud)
- [Romanian Authority for Digitalization: market consultation report](https://www.adr.gov.ro/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Market-Consultation-Report-Migration-Services-ADR_EN.pdf)
- [World Bank: Building Data Infrastructure for AI Readiness](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2026/05/06/data-infrastructure-for-ai)

### Directly recorded facts

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Public body | Authority for Digitalization of Romania, with the Ministry of Research, Innovation and Digitalization coordinating reforms and investments |
| Funding frame | Romania's National Recovery and Resilience Plan, Component 7, digital transformation |
| Project period | 6 July 2023 to 30 June 2026 |
| Target | At least 30 cloud-ready or virtualized applications and IT systems migrated by June 2026; the World Bank describes a wider ambition of up to 60 applications |
| Intended users | Central public administration, public ICT and digitalization authorities, and application-producing entities |
| Stated purpose | Modernize applications used to deliver digital public services and make them cloud-ready or cloud-native |
| Examples named by the World Bank | Electronic payment systems, public procurement platforms, and early-warning systems |
| Project value | RON 876,096,515 excluding VAT, stated by the authority as about €177.05 million, plus about €33.64 million in ineligible VAT |
| Procurement | The authority describes public procurement of cloud-migration services and a methodology for selecting and prioritizing applications |

### What is not yet established

- whether the target number of applications was met by the stated date;
- which cloud providers, subcontractors, and data-centre locations hold the systems;
- service reliability, outage history, cost per service, and cost after renewal;
- data portability, interoperability, exit terms, and public audit access;
- whether Romanian public agencies and workers gained reusable skills;
- effects on citizens' access, privacy, waiting time, or ability to challenge an automated decision.

## Case B — Malaysia Johor data-centre campus

### Primary records

- [IFC project disclosure 49145](https://disclosures.ifc.org/project-detail/SII/49145/yondr-my-dc-2023)
- [IFC and global lenders finance Yondr's Malaysia project](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2024/ifc-and-global-lenders-finance-yondr-s-data-center-project-in-malaysia)
- [Yondr: IFC financing for Johor](https://www.yondrgroup.com/newsroom/press-release/yondr-secures-up-to-150m-of-financing-for-malaysia-site/)
- [Yondr: completed sale to Vantage](https://www.yondrgroup.com/newsroom/press-release/yondr-group-completes-sale-of-johor-campus-to-vantage-data-centers/)
- [Vantage: APAC investment and acquisition](https://vantage-dc.com/news/vantage-data-centers-completes-1-6b-investment-in-apac-platform-from-gic-and-adia-closes-acquisition-of-yondrs-300mw-hyperscale-campus-in-johor-malaysia/)
- [World Bank: Building Data Infrastructure for AI Readiness](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2026/05/06/data-infrastructure-for-ai)

### Directly recorded facts

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Location | Sedenak Tech Park, Johor, Malaysia |
| IFC project | Project 49145; up to US$150 million loan for a 96 MW greenfield hyperscale data centre |
| Project vehicle | Yellowwood Properties Sdn Bhd, a 100%-owned special-purpose vehicle of Yondr at the time of the IFC disclosure |
| Full campus plan | 300 MW or more of critical IT capacity on about 72.5 acres |
| Financing | IFC and six international financial institutions announced more than US$900 million for the initial 98 MW development in December 2024 |
| Power link | Yondr announced a partnership with Tenaga Nasional Berhad and a 275 kV substation intended to support 300 MW or more of IT load |
| Ownership change | Yondr announced completion of the sale of the Johor campus to Vantage on 24 November 2025 |
| Regional capital | Vantage said the acquisition formed part of a US$1.6 billion APAC platform investment led by affiliates of GIC and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority |
| Stated benefits | World Bank and operator materials describe expected digital investment, jobs, regional spillovers, and data-sovereignty benefits |

### What is not yet established

- how much capacity is operational, leased, or still planned;
- the full ownership and control chain after the sale;
- the identity and bargaining position of hyperscale customers;
- electricity source, water use, tariffs, emissions, grid impact, and connection terms;
- local jobs, wages, skills, suppliers, taxes, and knowledge spillovers actually realized;
- Malaysian data-location, cross-border transfer, security, and public-access rules;
- whether public authorities or customers can switch operators without service loss;
- whether the campus changes Malaysia's bargaining power or mainly expands a regional platform owned by external investors.

## Cross-case comparison

| Dimension | Romania | Malaysia |
|---|---|---|
| Main asset | Public applications and government cloud migration | Physical hyperscale data-centre campus |
| State role | Public funding, coordination, procurement, and rules | Development finance, power relationship, land and market environment |
| Immediate promise | Better digital public services | Regional compute and cloud capacity, investment, jobs, and data sovereignty |
| Main dependency to inspect | Cloud provider, migration contractor, data location, portability | Operator, customers, lenders, investors, power, equipment, and network links |
| Missing outcome | Service quality and public control | Realized local value and strategic replaceability |

## Research inference under test

Infrastructure may increase a country's capability and its exposure to external operators at the same time. The direction of the net effect depends on local learning, data control, provider choice, public oversight, and the cost of exit.

This inference is not established by the project records alone.

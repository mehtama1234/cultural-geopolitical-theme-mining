# Netherlands: Platform Work Directive implementation record

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Country:** Netherlands  
**Directive:** Directive (EU) 2024/2831  
**Current status:** official consultation record; not fully implemented

## Evidence

The Dutch government legislative calendar identifies a proposed **Wet
platformwerk** as a directive-implementation bill. It names the Ministry of
Social Affairs and Employment as the responsible department and places the
proposal in the preparation phase. The calendar records an implementation
schedule dated 12 June 2026 and an internet consultation running from 29 June
through 24 August 2026.

The official consultation page says the proposal is intended as a close
implementation of Directive (EU) 2024/2831, while acknowledging that some
provisions require national policy choices. It lists the proposed elements:

- a rebuttable presumption of employee status;
- limits on processing personal data through automated systems;
- data-protection impact assessments;
- transparency duties for automated monitoring and decision systems;
- data portability;
- human oversight;
- additional health and safety measures;
- information, consultation, and expert-advice provisions when automated
  systems materially change;
- declaration of platform work; and
- communication channels.

The Dutch government news release describes the same proposal as a draft sent
to internet consultation. It says important decisions such as dismissal or
suspension would require a human decision, and that workers could invoke the
employment presumption when the stated criteria are met.

The official directive implementation page still labels the directive “not
yet fully implemented,” with a 2 December 2026 deadline and no validated
implementation date.

## Post-consultation checkpoint

The Dutch legislative calendar, checked after the consultation closed, still
places the bill in the preparation phase. Its listed sequence remains
preparation, Council of State, Tweede Kamer, Eerste Kamer, and publication;
the calendar records the consultation start and end dates but does not list a
Council of State referral, parliamentary introduction, enactment, or effective
date. This is a time-stamped public-status observation, not proof that no
internal step occurred or that no later document exists elsewhere.

## What this adds to the atlas

The Netherlands shows a more advanced public preparation stage than a blank
country row, but it still does not close the remedy arrow:

```text
EU directive
  -> Dutch draft and public consultation [observed]
  -> enacted law and effective date [open]
  -> authority, review, correction, and enforcement [open]
  -> worker outcome or changed platform practice [open]
```

The consultation record is evidence about proposed institutional design, not
evidence that the proposal was enacted, that platforms complied, or that
workers used the proposed rights.

The [draft control matrix](netherlands-platform-work-directive-draft-control-matrix-v1.md)
transcribes the proposed operational architecture: data prohibitions, impact
assessment, transparency, portability, trained human oversight, two-week
review and correction windows, anti-retaliation, reporting, supervision, and
administrative penalties. These remain draft provisions, not operative rights.

# Project: AI, work, and control

## Question

When firms adopt AI, does the main change come from better tools, tighter measurement, new forms of supervision, or a shift in who owns the work process?

## Why this project matters

The visible story is usually about speed and jobs. The deeper question is who gets to decide how work is done, who sees the data, who carries the risk of mistakes, and who can refuse the new system.

## Source plan

1. Start with HBS stories on AI adoption, careers, and employee value.
2. Find the linked faculty papers and record their methods.
3. Search NBER labor, productivity, firms, technology, inequality, and political-economy programs.
4. Compare worker, manager, firm, and market outcomes instead of using “AI impact” as one measure.
5. Add company filings and labor evidence only to test the research claims.

## Required findings

- output effect;
- task and skill effect;
- monitoring and bargaining effect;
- wage and promotion effect;
- small-firm versus large-firm effect;
- local and geopolitical effect through labor, capital, and technology dependence.

## Do not assume

- that adoption means productive use;
- that a job count captures control or dignity;
- that a firm-wide average describes workers equally;
- that a paper’s result travels to every country or industry;
- that a new tool changes power unless the decision rights also change.

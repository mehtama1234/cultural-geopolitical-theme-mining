# Virginia large-load governance and reliability layer v1

Virginia's utility regulator is converting the growth of data-center and other
large-load demand into explicit tariff, cost-allocation, and reliability
questions. This is the institutional response layer for the Prince William
fiscal/capacity bridge; it is not evidence that the policy has already changed
household bills or recovered all public costs.

| Date | Direct evidence | Program meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 2024 | Dominion reported 1,551 MW of data-center load dropped after a 230 kV outage and transferred to backup generation | Large-load behavior can become a grid-operational event | Utility-reported aggregate; no site-level duration or household effect |
| February 2025 | Dominion reported 1,800 MW similarly dropped after a 230 kV outage | Reliability and ride-through requirements become governance objects | Does not measure ordinary load, outage burden, or permanent curtailment |
| November 2025 order; effective January 2027 | SCC created GS-5 for customers demanding at least 25 MW; adopted minimum-demand provisions including 85% of contracted transmission/distribution demand and 60% of generation demand | The state is attempting to reduce cost-shifting and nonperformance risk through explicit customer obligations | Policy design is not observed payment, avoided residential cost, or complete cost recovery |
| November 2025 order; 2026–2027 implementation | SCC reported typical residential-customer bill increases of $11.24/month in 2026 and $2.36/month in 2027 at 1,000 kWh | Adds a systemwide consumer-price outcome to the regulatory context | The release does not attribute these increases solely to data centers; it is not a data-center household-incidence estimate |
| 2026 Rider T1 proceeding | SCC says the amended allocation method reduces a projected typical-residential Rider T1 increase from $2.90 to $0.94/month (67.5%) | Provides a modeled cost-allocation comparison rather than only a tariff description | It is a projection/counterfactual, not observed bills or proof that all large-load costs are recovered |
| 2025 filing | Dominion identified 139 initial GS-5 accounts, including 131 data-center accounts and 8 non-data-center accounts | Measures the initial scope of the proposed large-load class | Filing snapshot, not a current roster, consumption measure, or facility-level location |

The evidence supports a staged chain:

```text
large-load concentration and rapid growth
  -> reliability events and forecasting uncertainty
  -> regulator specifies rate class, minimum obligations, collateral/term rules,
     and interconnection oversight
```

The rule is an institutional response to infrastructure dependence. It does
not close the next arrows:

```text
tariff design -> actual utility cost allocation -> household bill outcome
reliability event -> local facility/worker/community consequence
county fiscal benefit -> resident legitimacy or political action
large-load dependence -> firm bargaining power or geopolitical leverage
```

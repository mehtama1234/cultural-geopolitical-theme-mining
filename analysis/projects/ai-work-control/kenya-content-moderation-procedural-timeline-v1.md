## Reading rule

```text
petition -> service and jurisdiction -> interim protection -> appeal on procedure
-> possible enforcement dispute -> merits hearing -> final remedy
```

The public records checked here establish activity at the first four and parts of the fifth steps. They do not establish a final merits judgment or a worker-level outcome.

## Why this matters to the project

The same dispute can contain a ruling that keeps a lead firm in the case, a ruling that rejects a stay, a ruling that preserves some interim protection, and a ruling that removes other interim orders. A headline such as “workers won” or “workers lost” hides which decision right or remedy was actually affected.

For cross-border AI work, the key measure is not only whether workers can reach a court. It is whether a court order survives long enough to change staffing, health support, contract terms, vendor choice, or bargaining power.

That final sentence is an inference and a research question, not a finding of the courts.

## Limits

- This is a procedural timeline, not a legal opinion.
- The appellate decisions reviewed were interlocutory or concerned interim procedure; they did not resolve every underlying claim.
- The timeline uses the appellate courts’ descriptions of earlier proceedings where the earlier source itself was not separately retrieved.
- It does not measure compliance, worker health, pay, retention, organizing success, or later settlement.

## Result

A targeted Fair Work Commission search for Kwasi Kyei v Rasier Pacific
(`UDE2025/105`) located the merits decision [2025] FWC 3550, dated 26 November
2025. This is a later stage than the previously retained timing decision
[2025] FWC 2269: the case proceeded to merits and the application was
dismissed.

The Commission found that Rasier's deactivation process did not comply with
the Code in two respects: the worker's requested discussion was not provided,
and a further inquiry was warranted. It nevertheless found a valid reason for
deactivation based on numerous safety-related complaints and concluded that
the deactivation was not unfair. The Commission stated that, even if the
deactivation had been unfair, it would not have exercised discretion to order
reactivation given the safety-related circumstances.

The decision establishes a counterexample to a simple rule that a process
defect produces restoration. It does not establish later account access,
continued work, alternative work, payment, recurrence, non-retaliation, or
worker-reported trust or exit.

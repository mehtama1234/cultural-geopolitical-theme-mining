## Why this case matters

This case supplies a stronger remedy-stage comparator for the broad atlas than
a bare account-restoration label. The Fair Work Commission records a platform
deactivation, voluntary reactivation, a formal order restoring access on the
same terms, and a separate order for lost remuneration. It still does not
show payment receipt, net household recovery, available alternatives, or
durable protection.

| 20 Mar 2026 | $7,096.96 gross lost remuneration ordered | FWC decision summary and primary decision | Ordered gross remuneration is not verified receipt, net income, compensation for expenses, or household recovery |

The Commission’s calculation used an average weekly earnings method and
treated the reactivation period as approximately 9.5 weeks. The record also
notes that no evidence was submitted addressing expenses the worker would have
incurred had he continued working.

## Broad-program implication

The case closes a meaningful institutional arrow:

```text
platform exclusion
  -> worker application
  -> voluntary restoration
  -> formal enforceable restoration + ordered lost pay
```

It does not close the lived outcome arrow:

```text
ordered remedy -> received money -> net recovery -> durable access
               -> alternative choice, continued use, switching, or exit
```

This is an important counterexample to both overstatement and excessive
scepticism. Voluntary reactivation did not eliminate the Commission’s power
to issue a formal remedy, but the formal order still does not prove that the
worker received the money or had an unconstrained alternative to Uber.

The case is Australian and comparative, not a US population estimate. It is
useful because it exposes a general remedy architecture—access restoration and
financial restoration can be separate orders—while keeping jurisdiction,
unit, and generalizability explicit.

## Next decisive test

Locate the issued order’s compliance or payment evidence and any worker-side
record of receipt, post-order trips, expenses, or alternative work. A second
case with a documented alternative or a verified non-payment outcome would
provide the needed counterexample for practical exit.

# Netherlands Deliveroo case: algorithmic organization and employment status

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Decision:** Hoge Raad, ECLI:NL:HR:2023:443, 24 March 2023  
**Case:** Deliveroo Netherlands B.V. v. FNV  
**Evidence class:** final national court decision; pre-Directive comparator

## What the case decided

The Dutch Supreme Court upheld the Amsterdam Court of Appeal's conclusion that
Deliveroo meal couriers worked under employment contracts, despite contracts
described as agreements for services. The Court treated the classification as a
question of all circumstances of the relationship. The couriers' freedom to
choose when to work and the contractual substitution possibility pointed away
from employment, but did not outweigh the other circumstances in the case.

The Supreme Court's public summary records that Deliveroo used an algorithm,
“Frank,” to organize delivery work. The case therefore supplies a concrete
national legal record in which digital work organization and the practical
relationship between platform and couriers were part of the status analysis.

## What it does and does not show

| Question | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| Platform work existed in a digitally organized delivery system | Established in the court record |
| Algorithmic organization was part of the factual setting | Established in the public court summary and underlying record |
| Couriers were legally classified as employees in this case | Established by the Supreme Court's judgment |
| A worker obtained explanation or review of an automated decision | Not shown |
| A platform account or rating was corrected | Not shown |
| The future EU Platform Work Directive was applied | No; the decision predates Directive (EU) 2024/2831 |
| All platform workers receive the same result | No; the Court emphasized the circumstances of the individual relationship |

The proceeding was a collective declaratory status dispute brought by FNV. It
is an institutional status outcome, not a worker-level remedy ledger. It also
does not establish that the algorithm itself caused a particular pay, stress,
autonomy, or health outcome.

## Relevance to the future Dutch draft

The proposed Dutch Platform Work Act would add a rebuttable employment
presumption where factual indications of direction and supervision exist. The
Deliveroo case is a useful pre-law comparator because it shows the kind of
relationship evidence that previously had to be evaluated through the general
employment-classification framework.

```text
algorithmically organized work
  -> factual direction, organization, and control
  -> court assesses the relationship as a whole
  -> employment status recognized in the case
```

The future test is whether the proposed presumption changes the cost, speed,
and evidence burden of reaching a similar status determination, especially for
intermediated work and workers who cannot bring a collective case.

## Relation to AIM-WORK

The case is not a worker survey and cannot be pooled with AIM-WORK estimates.
It is a legal comparator for the exposure categories of automated task
allocation, work organization, performance control, and contractual status.
It demonstrates institutional recognition of a platform-control relationship,
but not the survey outcomes of autonomy, breaks, stress, or work intensity.

# Company and sector bridge: Microsoft and workplace AI

**Status:** first pass; descriptive and provisional  
**Last checked:** 2026-09-11  
**Project:** AI, work, and control

## Purpose

This bridge tests how the research findings appear in a company’s own operating record. It is not a valuation, buy/sell view, or forecast.

## What Microsoft reports

Microsoft’s fiscal 2025 annual report says:

- it employed about 228,000 people as of June 30, 2025, including 89,000 in operations, 80,000 in product research and development, 44,000 in sales and marketing, and 15,000 in general and administration;
- Copilot products had passed 100 million monthly active users across commercial and consumer products;
- more than 230,000 organizations were using Copilot Studio to extend Microsoft 365 Copilot or build their own agents;
- GitHub Copilot had more than 20 million users;
- Microsoft operated more than 400 datacenters across 70 regions and added more than two gigawatts of capacity during the year;
- 80% of the Fortune 500 used Azure AI Foundry for AI workloads.

Source: [Microsoft 2025 Annual Report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/). These are company-reported figures and should be checked against later filings and independent data before use in a final finding.

## Bridge to the research findings

| Research question | Company evidence | What it may mean | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does AI change work before pay? | Microsoft describes Copilot, agents, and role-specific workflows across work functions. | Work may be reorganized through software layers before labor statistics move. | It does not show customer-worker pay, job quality, or adoption success. |
| Is the first payoff movement? | Copilot is positioned across sales, coding, health, security, and office work. | A common platform may lower entry barriers in some tasks and raise dependence on the platform. | Product reach is not proof that workers move occupations or gain choice. |
| Does control change through meaning? | Microsoft describes agents alongside identity, security, governance, and management systems. | The vendor is not only supplying a tool; it may become part of the rule and data layer around work. | The annual report does not tell us how customers set worker rights, review, or appeals. |
| Who captures the gain? | Microsoft reports scale in users, organizations, models, datacenters, and AI services. | Value may accrue across workers, customers, Microsoft, cloud suppliers, and data owners. | Scale does not identify the distribution of value or risk. |

## First-principles inference

```text
AI assistant -> embedded workflow -> shared identity, data, and governance layer
-> repeated use and switching cost -> dependence on the platform's rules
```

This is an inference from the product and infrastructure description, not a demonstrated causal result. The next evidence must show how organizations actually configure permissions, training, monitoring, data use, and worker recourse.

## Sector exposure

The relevant sector is broader than “AI software.” The chain includes:

- cloud and datacenter capacity;
- enterprise software and identity systems;
- model providers and data suppliers;
- consulting and systems integration;
- firms whose work is reorganized through these tools;
- workers and institutions that bear the adjustment cost.

The bridge should therefore track both productivity claims and dependence: who supplies the infrastructure, who owns the data, who sets the defaults, and how hard it is to leave.

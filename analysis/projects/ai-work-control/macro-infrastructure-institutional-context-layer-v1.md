# Macro, infrastructure, and institutional AI context layer v1

**Status:** validated cross-source context layer; open for deeper extraction  
**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Record:** [machine-readable trend record](../../records/us-ai-macro-infrastructure-institutional-context-2025-2026.json)

## What this layer adds

The existing workplace record measured selected worker and task outcomes. This
layer adds the conditions around those outcomes: firm complements, household
distributional mechanisms, official measurement, public analytical capacity,
physical energy systems, and country-level capability.

The sources do not measure one common “AI effect.” Their value is that they
locate different stages of the same research question:

```text
firm adoption and capital
  -> measured productivity and distribution
  -> public measurement and oversight capacity
  -> electricity, grids, and material supply chains
  -> local complements and country capability
```

## Bounded findings

- BIS reports a short-run 4% labor-productivity increase in a matched firm
  study, with no adverse short-run employment effect and larger gains in
  medium and large firms. This is firm evidence, not proof of worker control
  or household benefit.
- The IMF model separates wage and wealth channels and makes firm adoption
  endogenous. It shows why a productivity or wage result can coexist with a
  more unequal wealth result, but the result remains model-dependent.
- In its baseline 2014–2048 AI scenario, the IMF reports a 1.73 percentage
  point fall in the modeled wage Gini alongside a 7.18 percentage point rise
  in the modeled wealth Gini. These are scenario outputs, not realized US
  outcomes; the committed [extraction note](imf-ai-adoption-inequality-extraction-v1.md)
  preserves the horizon and assumptions.
- BEA shows that AI utilization, data capital, production, prices, and
  productivity are still being measured and defined. A cross-source atlas
  therefore cannot silently treat every “AI output” measure as equivalent.
- BEA's early indirect industry-account estimates are productivity-enhancing
  and input-saving in the baseline, and associate AI with a younger and
  relatively less educated worker composition; an alternative timing
  specification is less robust and also suggests labor saving. This is useful
  precisely because it exposes sensitivity to measurement choices rather than
  closing the labor-distribution question.
- BEA's newer state-by-industry study finds that cells with higher
  worker-reported frequent AI use have stronger post-2020 real-output paths and
  positive but imprecise employment differences. Its reported semi-elasticities
  are approximately 0.4–0.6% higher employment and 0.1–0.2% higher real output
  per one percentage-point higher share of frequent AI users. An industry-only
  employer-use specification does not recover the same output pattern. This
  supplies a US geographic context layer, but the authors call it descriptive
  rather than causal.
- BEA's price-and-cost study adds a consumer-incidence mechanism: across 1,586
  industry-year observations, the baseline interaction for gross-output price
  growth is -0.0228 for AI-intensive industries in 2022–2023, with negative
  labor and materials price-contribution interactions and a negative labor-share
  change. The study is exploratory and does not observe whether savings reached
  households or whether labor bore the adjustment cost.
- OFR reports AI use alongside a workforce path of 188 employees at the start
  of FY2025, 205 in January, and 109 at year-end; a budget reduction from
  $124.6 million to $110.7 million; decommissioning of the shared JADE
  platform; and retention of OFRAE with new cloud/GPU capacity. It is a public-
  capacity watchpoint, not evidence that AI caused the staffing change or
  improved supervision. The simultaneous cuts to training, data procurement,
  partnerships, and contractors make the net capacity effect an open question.
- The Federal Reserve's 2025 SHED supplies the household endpoint that the
  institutional and macro sources do not: 58% of adults said higher prices made
  their finances worse, 77% took at least one action in response, 16% did not
  pay all bills in the prior month, and 8% reported a family that sometimes or
  often did not have enough to eat. At the same time, 73% reported doing okay or
  living comfortably while only about 25% rated the national economy good or
  excellent. These are household and judgment measures, not evidence that AI or
  public-capacity changes caused them; they show why the missing endpoint is
  room and interpretation, not another capability total.
- IEA places AI inside electricity, grid, equipment, fuel, and mineral systems.
  Its global scenario is a physical dependency context, not a local bill or
  geopolitical-leverage estimate.
- The World Bank frames gains as dependent on local infrastructure, skills,
  and institutions and explicitly keeps country power and concentration in
  view. It supplies a mechanism and comparison frame, not a realized local
  outcome.

## Interpretation rule

The strongest current cross-source statement is conditional:

> AI capability is more likely to become durable and broadly distributed where
> firms, households, public institutions, and countries can access the
> complementary capital, data, skills, power, infrastructure, and decision
> rights required to use it.

This is a testable synthesis, not a settled causal conclusion. The next tests
must connect these context layers to local prices and bills, firm ownership and
size, worker rules and pay, public-service performance, and observed political
or state responses.

## Open arrows

1. AI adoption -> worker bargaining power, pay, promotion, and health.
2. Data-centre load -> household energy burden, water burden, or local
   legitimacy.
3. Public-sector AI use -> better or weaker oversight and recourse.
4. Foreign infrastructure ownership -> actual state dependence or bargaining
   change.
5. Capital ownership -> realized wealth concentration in a defined population.

Each arrow remains open until a compatible unit, timing, denominator, and
outcome are acquired.

# NBER W33795 source record: access changes time boundaries before task structure

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Project:** AI, work, and control  
**Source type:** randomized field experiment / working paper  
**Evidence status:** direct paper abstract and public full-text version reviewed; workplace-level implementation, longer-run outcomes, and worker-governance effects remain open

## Source

[Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795),
Eleanor W. Dillon, Sonia Jaffe, Nicole Immorlica, and Christopher T. Stanton,
NBER Working Paper 33795, issued May 2025 and revised November 2025. A public
[arXiv full-text version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11436) was used for the
method and abstract cross-check because the NBER PDF route returned an access
page in this environment.

The paper discloses that some authors were employed by Microsoft, the maker of
the M365 Copilot tool studied. The NBER page says Microsoft reviewed the paper
for privacy concerns while the authors retained discretion over the results and
estimates. That relationship is part of the source context, not a reason to
discard the randomized design or to treat the result as vendor-neutral.

## What the source measures

The study follows 7,137 knowledge workers across 66 firms for six months. Half
were randomly selected to receive access to a generative-AI tool integrated into
applications they already used for email, meetings, and writing. The outcomes
are digital traces and work-pattern measures, including email time, work
outside regular hours, document completion, meeting time, and the quantity and
composition of tasks.

The useful unit is the worker-week nested inside a firm experiment. Treatment
assignment supports a causal comparison for the access intervention within the
study setting. It does not automatically identify what a manager changed, how
work was coordinated, or what happened to pay, promotion, workload, or
bargaining power.

## Directly reported findings

- In the current NBER abstract, during the second half of the experiment the
  80% of treated workers who used the tool spent about two fewer hours on email
  each week and reduced work outside regular hours.
- The current NBER abstract reports no detected change in the quantity or
  composition of workers' tasks from individual-level provision.
- The public revised abstract reports a more specific distinction: workers who
  used the tool in more than half of the sample weeks spent 3.6 fewer hours,
  or 31% less time, on email each week, while the intent-to-treat estimate was
  1.3 hours; document completion was moderately faster, but meeting time did
  not change significantly.
- The two abstract presentations are preserved as revision/context
  differences. They should not be collapsed into one universal “two-hour” or
  “3.6-hour” effect.

## Why it matters to the project

This is a stronger test of a narrow work-time mechanism than an adoption rate
or executive expectation. It suggests that individual access can change work
that a worker can alter independently—email and some document work—without
producing an observable change in tasks that require wider coordination, such
as meetings or the overall task mix.

The bounded theme is therefore:

> AI access can change the temporal boundary of work before it changes the
> organization of work.

That is consistent with a worker experiencing less after-hours work or faster
individual output while the employer's allocation, evaluation, authority, and
reward systems remain unchanged.

## What it does not establish

- It does not show that email-time savings became lower workload, higher pay,
  greater autonomy, or more time for family and care.
- It does not establish a firm-level productivity effect, because the
  intervention was individual access and the outcomes are concentrated in
  selected digital activities.
- It does not show whether managers increased targets, reallocated tasks,
  reduced staffing, changed monitoring, or captured the time savings.
- It does not establish an effect on job quality, health, promotion,
  bargaining, household security, cultural meaning, or political response.
- The six-month study, participating firms, tool integration, treatment take-up,
  and authors' relationship to the tool maker limit generalization.

## Placement in the evidence chain

```text
individual AI access
  -> email/document time and after-hours boundary
  -> [not observed here] manager coordination, workload, evaluation, or reward
  -> [open] worker control, household time, pay, health, trust, or exit
```

The experiment directly informs the first arrow. It is an important counterweight
to claims that access alone transformed the whole job, but it does not close
the workplace-control arrow.

## Required follow-up

1. Compare treatment effects by occupation, task, baseline workload, gender,
   age, tenure, and employer setting where the paper supports it.
2. Find workplace records showing whether saved time was reallocated, targets
   changed, or staffing and evaluation rules moved.
3. Pair the experiment with worker-voice, monitoring, training, pay, health,
   and schedule-control evidence.
4. Follow whether individual time savings persist after the tool becomes part
   of ordinary organizational coordination.

**Bounded claim:** In a six-month randomized field experiment across 66 firms
and 7,137 knowledge workers, individual AI access changed email and
after-hours work patterns for users without detectable changes in overall task
quantity or composition. The result supports a time-boundary effect, not a
general claim about productivity, job transformation, worker control, or
household benefit.

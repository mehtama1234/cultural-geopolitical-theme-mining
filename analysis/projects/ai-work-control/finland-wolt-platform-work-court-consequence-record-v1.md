# Finland Wolt case: platform control, status, and administrative consequence

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Country:** Finland  
**Primary decision:** Supreme Administrative Court, KHO:2025:41, 22 May 2025  
**Follow-up:** KHO 19 March 2026; Tax Administration update 13 May 2026  
**Evidence class:** final national court and administrative consequence; pre-directive comparator

## Court outcome

The Finnish Supreme Administrative Court held in KHO:2025:41 that food
couriers working through Wolt were in an employment relationship with the
courier company under the Employment Contracts Act, rather than independent
entrepreneurs. The Court nevertheless held that working-time legislation did
not apply in the same way because the couriers had working-time autonomy.

The Court's March 2026 decision records that Wolt's platform-generated data
could be used to direct and supervise the couriers' work; Wolt actually used
those data to monitor performance and quality requirements; and the service
contract gave Wolt rights to make unilateral decisions affecting the
relationship, including possible fee reductions and immediate termination. The
Court characterized the couriers' independence as partly only apparent in the
circumstances of the case.

## Administrative consequence

The Finnish Tax Administration states that the 2025 and 2026 court decisions
change tax treatment. Payments from a courier company are wage income, the
courier company must withhold tax and pay the employer health-insurance
contribution, and couriers cannot use an invoicing service to treat the income
as trade income. The Tax Administration says the changed directions will be
applied from 1 January 2027.

This creates a dated institutional chain beyond a status declaration:

```text
platform data, performance monitoring, and unilateral contract powers
  -> court recognizes employment relationship
  -> reopening attempt rejected
  -> tax authority changes wage-income and employer-obligation treatment
```

## What the case does and does not establish

| Question | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| Digital platform data were relevant to direction and supervision | Established in the 2026 Supreme Administrative Court record |
| Couriers were classified as employees | Established by KHO:2025:41 and the 2026 follow-up |
| Wolt's reopening attempt succeeded | No; it was rejected on 19 March 2026 |
| Tax and employer obligations changed | Established by the Finnish Tax Administration; operational application begins 1 January 2027 |
| EU Platform Work Directive was transposed through this case | No; this is a pre-directive national court route |
| Worker received an explanation, account review, or algorithmic correction | Not shown |
| Stress, autonomy, earnings, or health improved | Not shown |

The result is a strong status-and-administration comparator, not a complete
worker-remedy outcome. It also shows that legal recognition of employment can
coexist with a boundary around working-time protections.

## Relation to the EU and Dutch records

The Finnish case supplies an observed pathway that the proposed Dutch Act is
designed to make easier: converting facts about platform direction, monitoring,
and dependency into a status determination. It should not be treated as proof
that the future EU directive or Dutch consultation draft caused the outcome.

Compared with the Dutch Deliveroo case, Finland adds an explicit later tax
administration consequence. Compared with the Dutch draft, it still lacks the
new architecture of automated-decision explanation, two-week review, data
portability, and correction or compensation.

## Relation to AIM-WORK

The case is not a worker survey. It is a national legal and administrative
record relevant to automated task organization, performance monitoring,
contractual status, and possible account or fee consequences. It cannot be
pooled with AIM-WORK estimates of autonomy, breaks, stress, or work intensity.

# Finding 136: Georgia's data-center safeguards reached real contracts, but the household bargain is still unknown

**Status:** bounded large-load contract and infrastructure-cost finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

Georgia's Public Service Commission approved a framework for new customers using
more than 100 megawatts: large-load customers can face special terms, minimum
billing, longer contracts, and responsibility for costs tied to new generation,
transmission, and distribution. The official docket then showed multiple
executed large-load contract filings in 2026, including one filed August 27.

This matters because the protection moved beyond a rule on paper into actual
contract review. But a filed or executed contract still does not show whether
the customer paid its assigned costs, whether the load was energized, or
whether residential customers saw lower bills or better reliability.

```text
large data-center load
  -> special tariff and contract rules
  -> actual contract filing and Commission review
  -> construction, payment, energization, and system operation
  -> residential bills, reliability, local burden, and public acceptance
     [not observed here]
```

## Why it matters

This is the infrastructure lane's missing-middle problem in a clearer form.
Public agencies can recognize the risk of shifting costs to ordinary customers
and create a review route. Firms can sign or submit contracts. Yet the public
still needs to see the actual terms, payment, load behavior, and customer-level
result before calling the bargain protective.

The wider cultural and political question is whether residents experience new
large loads as economic opportunity, infrastructure pressure, or a deal whose
costs are being moved onto people with less ability to refuse.

## Limits and counterexamples

- Docket metadata does not disclose the commercial terms of the contracts.
- A contract filing is not proof of final Commission approval, construction,
  energization, or payment.
- The record does not show residential bills, outages, reliability, jobs,
  water use, or community acceptance.
- A review extension may reflect ordinary diligence rather than conflict or
  failure.
- Large-load customers may provide revenue or system value as well as impose
  costs; this finding does not assume the bargain is harmful.

## Next test

Obtain a named contract's approved terms, security or minimum-billing payment,
load and energization record, and same-period residential rate and reliability
outcomes. Keep the firm, utility, Commission, ratepayer, and community units
separate if they cannot be joined.

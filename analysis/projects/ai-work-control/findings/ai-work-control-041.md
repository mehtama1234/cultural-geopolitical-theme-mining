# Growth and profits do not settle who gains control

**Status:** provisional macro-firm/work-control bridge · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

BEA's second estimate reports real GDP growth of 1.5% at an annual rate in the
second quarter of 2026, while real final sales to private domestic purchasers
grew 4.2%. The PCE price index rose 5.3% at an annual rate, and corporate
profits from current production increased $400.9 billion. These figures put
output, private demand, prices, and profits on different scales.

The narrow finding is:

> A stronger private-demand or profit surface does not by itself show that
> workers gained pay, time, discretion, bargaining power, or a right to shape
> technology—and it does not show that households received an equal share of
> the gain.

## Separate macro surfaces

| Surface | Q2 2026 result | Unit | What it can support | What remains open |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Real GDP | +1.5% | Annualized quarterly growth | Aggregate production context | Distribution, firms, workers, households |
| Real final sales to private domestic purchasers | +4.2% | Annualized quarterly growth | Private-demand context | Who purchased, imports, affordability, access |
| PCE price index | +5.3% | Annualized quarterly change | Aggregate consumption-price context | Household baskets, real purchasing power, substitution |
| Corporate profits from current production | +$400.9B | Quarterly current-dollar change | Aggregate firm-surplus context | Profit concentration, wages, taxes, reinvestment, control |

The measures cannot be collapsed into “the economy improved.” The GDP release
also says government spending decreased and imports increased, while the
second estimate's revision to consumer spending reflected stronger services
and weaker goods. Those details matter for the distributional interpretation,
but they still do not identify the firms or households experiencing the change.

## Cross-source work-control interpretation

The work-control atlas now has a more complete conditioning sequence:

```text
aggregate output, demand, prices, and profits
  -> firm investment, staffing, pricing, and technology decisions
  -> worker tasks, pace, monitoring, pay, and discretion
  -> household income, spending, debt, and time room
  -> trust, bargaining, public response, and political meaning
```

The BEA record reaches only the first box and part of the second. BLS supplies
separate employment, pay, hours, and sector-change surfaces. NBER supplies
task-adoption and field-experiment evidence. Fed, SIPP, and New York Fed
records supply household and credit context. These layers make the next test
more precise; they do not create a shared firm-worker-household panel.

Corporate profits are particularly easy to overread. The aggregate increase
does not reveal whether gains came from productivity, prices, industry mix,
market concentration, inventory valuation, capital-consumption adjustments, or
other components. Even a firm-level profit increase would not prove that
workers acquired review rights, safer pacing, or a voice in system design.

## Counterexamples and safeguards

- Output can grow while employment or worker bargaining power weakens.
- Profits can rise while wages, hours, safety, or job quality diverge across
  firms and occupations.
- Private demand can rise through high-resource households or credit-financed
  spending while lower-resource households cut quantities or delay purchases.
- A higher price index can coexist with real gains for some households and
  severe essential-cost pressure for others.
- Corporate profits are not the same as firm market power, and market power is
  not the same as the ability to impose a workplace rule.
- The Q2 second estimate and national accounts are subject to revision; BEA's
  September 30 annual update is a required vintage recheck.

## Next decisive test

For a named sector or firm, align:

`profit or investment change -> system purchase and stated purpose -> worker
access/training/monitoring -> task, pace, pay, health, safety, and discretion
-> worker voice/appeal -> household room -> firm, regulatory, or political
response`

The design must preserve firms that gained output without changing worker
control, workers who gained tools without gaining authority, and households
whose spending rose while buffers fell. Until a common firm/workplace/worker
key exists, BEA remains macro context rather than an AI or worker-control
effect estimate.

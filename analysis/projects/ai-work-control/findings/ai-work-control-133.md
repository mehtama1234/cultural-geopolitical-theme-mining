# Finding 133: Emergency grid authority can protect reliability before household benefit is observable

**Status:** bounded grid-operation and public-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-21

## The finding

On September 1, 2026, DOE issued an emergency order for forecast hot weather
and rising Mid-Atlantic load. The order directed PJM to dispatch specified
units and authorized backup generation as a last resort before or during the
highest-level Energy Emergency Alert. PJM could call on transmission owners and
electric distribution companies to implement the order through September 8.

This is an operating-control stage, not merely a plan:

```text
forecast heat and load stress
  -> federal emergency order
  -> PJM dispatch and backup-generation authority
  -> possible avoided load shedding or blackout [not measured here]
  -> household reliability, bill, emissions, and later trust [open]
```

## Why it matters

The case makes public infrastructure power visible in action. A state can use
emergency authority to coordinate firms and physical assets across a region,
but the public announcement still does not show which units operated, who bore
the cost or pollution, whether an alert occurred, or which households avoided
an interruption.

It also creates a useful counterweight to the AEP financing case: financing is
an upstream capacity stage; emergency dispatch is a downstream operating stage.
Neither one alone proves an adequate, fairly distributed, durable household
service outcome.

## Do not overread

- The order's existence does not prove that a blackout would otherwise have
  occurred or that load shedding was avoided.
- The announcement does not provide unit-level dispatch, duration, costs,
  emissions, outages, customer bills, or household experience.
- Emergency authority is not the same as durable reliability or affordable
  service.

## Next decisive test

Join the order window to PJM operating data, dispatched-unit records, Energy
Emergency Alert status, outage and interruption measures, generation cost and
emissions, and customer-class or household service outcomes. Keep avoided harm
as a counterfactual claim unless the operating and outcome denominators support
it.

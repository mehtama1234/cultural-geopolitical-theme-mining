# Finding 115: Back-pay can be implemented together with payroll repair, but individual recovery remains partly hidden

**Status:** bounded firm-level workplace-remedy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The Fair Work Ombudsman reports that its 2026 aged-care investigations found
13 of 22 investigated providers non-compliant. The 13 providers had
underpaid workers; six residential-care providers back-paid more than $2.17
million to 2,338 employees, while seven home-care providers back-paid more
than $3.13 million to 1,253 employees. The reported average overall
underpayment was $1,478 per employee.

The strongest implementation example is one home-care provider that audited its
full workforce, back-paid more than $2 million to over 600 employees for errors
dating back to 2023, updated its payroll system, and trained relevant staff.
The record therefore reaches beyond a notice or penalty into firm process
repair and an intended recurrence-control step.

## Named reachability comparator: Southern Cross Care

The FWO's April 2026 Enforceable Undertaking gives a more traceable example.
Southern Cross Care (NSW & ACT) identified the problem through a self-audit
started after an employee asked about overtime, then self-reported it. The
underpayments covered July 2017 through October 2024. The provider reported
back-paying 3,603 of 5,500 employees $10,135,122 including interest and
superannuation; individual reported amounts ranged from less than one dollar
to $44,593. Amounts owed to workers the employer could not find were to be
paid into the Commonwealth Consolidated Revenue Fund after 60 days.

The undertaking also requires replacement of the time-and-attendance system,
an independent compliance audit, employee training, Board reporting, and
multiple worker-voice channels including surveys, anonymous feedback, team
meetings, and an external whistleblower hotline. This closes more firm-side
implementation stages than the August sector summary, while still leaving
individual receipt timing, adequacy, household recovery, and later trust or
exit incomplete.

## The chain

```text
broken classification / overtime / broken-shift payroll rules
  -> underpaid direct-care workers
  -> FWO investigation and compliance notices
  -> back-pay to a reported workforce
  -> payroll-system update and staff training
  -> individual receipt, adequacy, household recovery, trust, or exit [partial/open]
```

## What this adds

This is a useful firm-level comparator to the worker cases in the atlas. A
provider can correct a payroll system and report back-pay across hundreds of
workers while the public record still does not identify each worker's payment
date, net amount, remaining loss, household effect, or later job decision.

It also adds a durability stage that is different from worker durability:
system repair and training are reported as prevention measures, but a later
audit or recurrence record is still needed to show that underpayment stopped.

## Limits

- The figures are an FWO investigation summary, not a worker-level payment
  file or independently audited receipt dataset.
- The provider names and individual payment timing are not supplied in the
  public release, so no same-worker receipt or household outcome can be built.
- “Back-paid” is stronger than an offer or order, but it still does not report
  net receipt, adequacy, tax/superannuation treatment, or residual loss for each
  worker.
- Payroll updates and training are reported implementation steps, not proof of
  future compliance.
- The investigated firms were selected for inspection; the result is not a
  sector-wide non-compliance rate.
- The Southern Cross figures are employer-reported within an enforceable
  undertaking; “back-paid” does not identify which worker received money when,
  and the unlocated-worker route explicitly shows that recovery can stop at
  reachability.

## Next decisive test

Obtain a lawful employer or regulator follow-up that preserves worker-level
payment status, calculation, timing, remaining discrepancy, and a later audit
or recurrence result. Keep worker receipt, household recovery, trust, and
exit separate from firm process repair.

# Finding 111: AI capability can expand while replaceability and state leverage remain unproven

**Status:** bounded AI capability/dependence finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The World Bank's 2026 development report frames productive AI capability as an
adopt–adapt–advance pathway requiring infrastructure, skills, institutions,
data, and local fit. It also identifies concentration across hardware,
infrastructure, models, and applications, with dependency, capital
concentration, surveillance, public-budget pressure, and regulatory-capacity
gaps as risks.

This does not mean that every imported tool weakens a state. Imported tools can
lower entry costs and create useful capability. The defensible insight is more
specific: capacity and dependence can rise together. A system becomes power
only when an actor can use it, inspect it, replace it, refuse it, or impose a
cost—and when another actor actually responds.

## The chain

```text
AI exposure/provider availability
  -> local complements and adoption
  -> usable firm or public capability
  -> ownership, replaceability, and control
  -> affected actor's substitution, refusal, or concession [open]
  -> state bargaining or geopolitical consequence [open]
```

This connects the domestic AI, infrastructure, public-capacity, and
geopolitical lanes without treating model access, procurement, or a capability
announcement as realized leverage.

## Limits and next field

- The World Bank report is a framework and synthesis, not a US effect estimate.
- It does not provide a supplier Herfindahl, switching cost, outage event, or
  observed state response.
- Public-service examples do not establish improved access, quality, trust, or
  household outcomes.
- The next decisive evidence is one identifier-bearing capability episode with
  production/use, alternatives, replaceability, an affected actor, and a dated
  external response.

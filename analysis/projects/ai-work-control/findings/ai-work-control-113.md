# Finding 113: A formal compensation order can arrive after the worker has already exited on broken trust

**Status:** bounded named worker-remedy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-25

## The bounded finding

The Fair Work Commission record in **[2025] FWC 1570**, involving Greenhill
Education Group Pty Ltd trading as Royal Greenhill Institute of Technology,
follows one worker
through persistent underpayment and delayed wages, repeated complaints, Fair
Work Ombudsman assistance, a forced resignation, new employment, a documented
**$4,000 partial payment**, and a later **$27,424.88 gross compensation order**.
The worker did not want to return to the original employer because trust had
broken down and the experience had caused emotional stress.

A targeted Fair Work Commission search recheck on 2026-09-25 located the same
official decision as the retained source and no later public payment
confirmation or post-new-job follow-up. This is a search boundary, not proof
that the ordered compensation was unpaid or that the new job ended.

This is unusually close to the program's lived chain. The worker did not simply
“receive a remedy” or “quit”: the record shows how delayed payment shifted
liquidity and dependence risk onto the worker, how complaint and enforcement
routes were used, and how a formal right to return could become unacceptable by
the time it was available.

## The chain

```text
persistent wage failure and delayed payment
  -> repeated complaints and Fair Work Ombudsman assistance
  -> forced exit and new employment
  -> partial payment and compensation order
  -> refusal to return because of broken trust and emotional stress
  -> [open] final receipt, residual loss, new-job quality, durable security,
     later trust, household recovery, or collective/political action
```

## What this adds to the broader program

1. **Exit can be a consequence of failed protection, not free preference.**
   The record describes the resignation as forced. A later new job therefore
   cannot automatically be read as successful mobility.
2. **Money and status/access recover on different clocks.** A partial payment
   and a compensation order can coexist with unpaid amounts, lost financial
   independence, and the loss of the original employment relationship.
3. **Trust is an outcome with behavioral force.** Broken trust is directly
   reported and helps explain refusal to return; it is not inferred from
   non-return or from a generic survey attitude.
4. **A remedy can repair liability without restoring the prior option set.**
   The order transfers a formal obligation back to the employer, but it does
   not recreate the worker's prior income timing, relationship, or willingness
   to re-enter that workplace.

## Limits

- This is one adjudicated case, not a prevalence estimate or general recovery
  rate.
- The public record does not verify final compensation receipt, remaining
  wages or superannuation, household finances, the quality or duration of the
  new job, or later trust.
- The case records worker-linked meaning and exit, but not broader organizing,
  voting, or political action.
- A forced-exit finding and a compensation order do not establish that every
  later hardship was caused by the employer or that the legal remedy failed.

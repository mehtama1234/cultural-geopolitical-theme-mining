# Finding 046: Financial-system resilience does not erase selective household credit pressure

**Status:** provisional Federal Reserve institutional-financial layer · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The Federal Reserve's May 2026 Financial Stability Report and July 2026
Monetary Policy Report describe a system that is broadly resilient while
retaining specific pressure points:

- The May stability report says business and household debt vulnerabilities are
  moderate overall and debt relative to GDP has fallen to levels not seen since
  the early 2000s.
- The same report says credit-card and auto-loan delinquencies remain above
  levels prevailing over the prior decade, with selected FHA/VA and
  low-down-payment mortgage distress despite low mortgage delinquency overall.
- It reports notable financial-sector leverage, including hedge-fund leverage
  near historical highs, while banks remain sound with high regulatory capital.
- Runnable money-like liabilities totaled $27.033 trillion at end-2025 and grew
  12.0% from 2024 Q4 to 2025 Q4; the Fed also identifies geopolitical risk, oil
  shocks, AI, private credit, and persistent inflation among salient risks.
- The July monetary report says May 2026 total PCE inflation was 4.1% and core
  PCE 3.4%, June unemployment was 4.2%, Q1 real GDP grew at a 2.1% annual rate,
  and household consumption grew at a modest 1.3% annualized rate through May.
  Credit was broadly available overall but relatively tight for small businesses
  and households.

The defensible synthesis is: **system-level resilience and aggregate debt
moderation can coexist with selective borrower stress, tight credit access,
inflation pressure, and funding-risk exposure.** The Fed reports do not by
themselves identify which households experience those pressures or how they
become trust, political, or cultural responses.

## Distinct units and institutional roles

| Source surface | Unit | Direct contribution | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Financial Stability Report | Financial-system vulnerabilities and market aggregates | Valuation, borrowing, leverage, funding, and shock framework | Probability, realized loss, household incidence, or political response |
| Monetary Policy Report | US macro indicators and FOMC policy setting | Inflation, labor, GDP, consumption, credit, productivity, and rate context | Borrower-specific rates, affordability, desired work, or distributional welfare |
| SHED/linked credit records | Adult respondents and consenting linked credit reports | Liquidity, balances, repayment, and financial-condition subgroups | Representative panel, causal shock, lender response, or system-wide stability |
| IMF Article IV | Country surveillance and conditional projections | Fiscal, external, productivity, tariff, and macro-risk frame | Realized adjustment, household burden, firm incidence, or leverage |

The units should not be pooled. A $27 trillion runnable-liability aggregate is
not the same object as a household credit-card balance; a policy rate is not a
borrower's APR; and a bank-capital assessment is not a measure of household
financial security.

## End-to-end chain under test

```text
energy, tariffs, labor, AI investment, and geopolitical shocks
  -> inflation, rates, credit access, asset valuations, and funding conditions
  -> firm financing, household borrowing, repayment, spending, and time choices
  -> security, trust, attribution, collective action, and political judgment
  -> regulatory response, state capacity, and external financial power
```

The Fed layer closes the institutional risk and policy-setting stages. The SHED,
SIPP, BEA, IMF, OFR, and firm records provide adjacent material and macro
surfaces. The common event key linking a financial-system condition to a named
household or firm response remains open.

## Counterexamples kept visible

- Low aggregate mortgage delinquency can coexist with distress in selected
  FHA/VA or low-down-payment borrowers.
- High bank capital can coexist with tight credit for small businesses or
  households.
- Stable unemployment can coexist with elevated inflation and weaker real-wage
  gains for some groups.
- A large runnable-liability aggregate is not an imminent crisis forecast;
  funding risk depends on instrument, holder, liquidity, and shock interaction.
- AI-related investment and data-center demand can raise output or capital
  spending without proving household benefit or worker control.

## Next test

The next macro-to-household pass should join a dated rate, energy, tariff,
credit, or funding event to borrower type, income, place, firm size, and actual
repayment or spending response. It should preserve low-stress/high-exposure and
high-stress/strong-buffer countercells, then test later trust, institutional use,
switching, or political response. IMF, Fed, BEA, SHED, and New York Fed vintages
should remain versioned rather than pooled into one “financial pressure” index.

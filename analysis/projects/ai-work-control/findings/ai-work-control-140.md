# Finding 140: Countries can rehearse a shared infrastructure response before they know how it will work for real

**Status:** bounded cross-border maritime-security and infrastructure-response finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The EU's MARSEC EU 26 exercise in Finland tested a response to a suspicious
vessel operating near undersea cables. The exercise moved through a tabletop
phase, information sharing between command centers, and live practice of
boarding, searching, rescue, firefighting, and taking control of a vessel.
Finnish and Polish authorities, EU agencies, defense and emergency services,
and commercial maritime actors participated.

The plain-language route is:

```text
possible threat to shared infrastructure
  -> countries and agencies share information
  -> they practice a joint decision and physical response
  -> actual incident, repair, service continuity, deterrence, and public trust [open]
```

This is more than a promise: people and institutions practiced a response
chain. But practice is still not proof that the chain works during a real
incident with legal conflict, political pressure, bad weather, or an actual
service interruption.

## Why it matters for the broader atlas

This adds a geopolitical version of the atlas's recurring pattern. Shared
capacity can become visible before shared authority, public legitimacy, or
real-world performance is settled. It also shows that infrastructure security
depends on a mixed public-private system: governments, military units,
emergency responders, EU agencies, and commercial vessels all appear in the
same operating chain.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This was an exercise, not a real sabotage or cable-damage event.
- It does not establish attribution, deterrence, repair, service continuity,
  cost sharing, or changed external behavior.
- Participation does not prove equal authority or future cooperation.
- The source does not identify an affected cable owner, community, customer, or
  later public judgment.
- A realistic exercise may reveal gaps as well as improve readiness.

## Next test

Follow a named real infrastructure incident through detection, attribution,
cross-border coordination, repair, service continuity, cost allocation, public
communication, and later external behavior. Keep exercise readiness separate
from actual protection.

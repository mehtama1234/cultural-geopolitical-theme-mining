# Finding 313: People use platform safety controls, but trust falls when protection depends on platform action

**Status:** bounded comparative digital-culture finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The bounded finding

A 2026 study of 1,067 usable UK adult responses found that more than 80% had
used at least one platform safety feature. Unfollowing and blocking were used
by more than 85% and 80% respectively among people aware of those features,
and more than 60% of users said they were always satisfied with those outcomes.
Reporting was different: about 60% of people aware of the feature had used it,
but only 18% said they were always satisfied with the result. Feed-control
tools also had lower satisfaction, around 30–35%.

This suggests that people can exercise direct control over who reaches them,
while having less confidence that a platform will act when they report harmful
content. Safety work is therefore both a form of user agency and a transfer of
moderation labor onto users.

```text
harm exposure or concern
  -> awareness of a safety control
  -> blocking, unfollowing, reporting, or feed adjustment
  -> perceived protection or disappointment
  -> trust, continued use, self-protection, or exit [open]
```

## Why it matters for the larger atlas

This is a stronger cultural and social endpoint than measuring platform reach
alone. It shows that “control” has different forms: a user can successfully
stop one account from reaching them while lacking confidence in a platform's
collective response. The same person may be protected through personal effort
and still distrust the institution.

## Evidence boundary

- The evidence is from UK adults and is not a US prevalence estimate.
- Data were collected in January 2023 and published in 2026.
- Use and satisfaction are self-reported; the study does not verify harm
  reduction, content-removal decisions, wellbeing, or later account exit.
- High use may reflect repeated exposure and self-protection burden, not only
  empowerment.

## Next test

Join platform logs or incident records to user action: exposure, tool choice,
platform response, recurrence, satisfaction, time cost, continued use, and
exit, with subgroup comparisons for harm type and digital ability.

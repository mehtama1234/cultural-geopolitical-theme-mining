# Finding 095: Capacity becomes geopolitical power only after an affected actor responds

**Status:** bounded cross-case realization finding  
**Checked:** 2026-09-17

## The bounded finding

The retained defense and digital-infrastructure ledgers show why strategic
capacity should not be treated as a single outcome. Poland's JASSM-ER case
contains authorization, agreement, supplier, support, and procurement stages;
the US data-center case contains named facilities, local revenue, capacity
reports, utility governance, and operational-stress observations. Neither
case yet shows the full conversion from commitment to controlled, replaceable
capability and changed external behavior.

The safe chain is:

```text
strategic objective or demand
  -> authorization, funding, or agreement
  -> named capacity, supplier, and place
  -> production, delivery, operation, or service realization
  -> maintenance, replaceability, and control
  -> affected public, household, worker, customer, or partner
  -> dated acceptance, refusal, substitution, concession, or other response
  -> bargaining power or geopolitical consequence
```

The current evidence reaches different middle stages in the two cases. It does
not establish autonomy, resilience, deterrence, bargaining leverage, or
geopolitical power.

## Two realization clocks

| Stage | Poland JASSM-ER | US data-center system | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic objective | Long-range strike and deterrence requirement | AI, advanced-manufacturing, defense, and digital-service electricity demand | Stated purpose or modeled demand is not realized capability |
| Authorization/agreement | Possible-sale authorization and May 2024 agreement; later sensitivity-enhancement notice | Utility commission and local government filings for capacity and cost allocation | Commitment is an institutional event, not output |
| Named capacity/place | US program, supplier, support structure, and customer-specific order context | Prince William reports 621 MW added, 1,483 MW turnkey capacity, 55 facilities, and $465.9M TY2025 revenue | Named entities and place improve traceability but do not establish usable service or control |
| Delivery/operation | Shipment, acceptance, Polish receipt, training, inventory, and operational use are not publicly verified for the order | Load-drop events and local capacity reports show system activity, while national 2030 load remains modeled | Adjacent operational activity cannot substitute for the named object’s realized output |
| Control/replaceability | Export permission, maintenance, design authority, qualified switching, and supplier dependence remain open | Ownership, feeder exposure, portability, provider switching, and cost recovery remain open | Capacity can coexist with dependence |
| Affected actor | No observed Polish acceptance, refusal, substitution, or changed alliance term | No joined household, business, feeder, water, land, or public-service outcome | Power requires an actor exposed to the capability |
| External response | No observed partner or adversary behavior change linked to realized delivery | No observed concession, rate response, service change, or political response linked to a named facility | Without a dated response, leverage remains a hypothesis |

These are not two estimates of one “strategic capacity” variable. Poland is
customer-realization constrained; the data-center system is incidence-and-
control constrained. The distinction is the finding.

## Commitment is not capability

An authorization can show that a government is willing to pursue a capability.
An agreement can identify a supplier and a customer. A facility announcement or
local revenue report can show that investment and public value are being
organized around a place. These observations matter, but each answers a
different question:

```text
authorization = permission to pursue
agreement      = institutional commitment
capacity       = named or modeled ability
operation      = usable output or service
replaceability = ability to continue or switch under disruption
leverage       = an affected actor changes behavior because of the capability
```

The common analytical error is to leap from the second or third line to the
last. A defense order may still depend on foreign production, export approval,
training, maintenance, and accepted delivery. A data center may add capacity
and revenue while increasing dependence on a utility, grid equipment, water,
land, finance, or public cost allocation.

## Why the affected actor is decisive

Power is relational. A capability matters geopolitically only when another
actor has a reason and a choice to respond. The actor may be a partner
government deciding whether to accept or substitute, a supplier deciding
whether to continue, a utility changing a tariff, a business changing an
investment, or a household absorbing a bill or reliability change.

The current ledgers identify possible exposed actors but do not yet hold them
in the same event record. The absence of a response cannot be coded as
successful deterrence, public acceptance, or stable dependence. Nor can an
announced capability be coded as failure because a later response has not yet
been observed. The correct status is open.

## Public value and burden are separate currencies

The data-center ledger makes this especially clear. Local capacity and revenue
can rise while the public record still lacks the facility's net service cost,
ratepayer incidence, water use, land burden, worker conditions, or household
reliability outcome. A utility rule can allocate costs without revealing which
households paid, whether service improved, or whether a large-load customer
could switch.

The defense case has a parallel separation. A strategic procurement can support
industrial production and alliance signaling while leaving inventory,
maintenance, operator readiness, replacement time, and foreign-control
constraints unknown. A contract's value is not the same as a fielded system's
replaceability.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This finding connects the infrastructure, firm, household, and geopolitical
themes through a common realization discipline:

```text
public announcement   != operating capability
operating capability  != replaceable capability
replaceability        != bargaining leverage
bargaining leverage   != observed external response
public revenue        != household net benefit
security framing      != public legitimacy
```

It also gives the broader program a counterexample to simple national-power
stories. A state can gain capacity while losing flexibility through supplier,
software, finance, maintenance, or grid dependence. Conversely, a project can
create local burdens while increasing a capability that decision-makers value.
The unit of analysis must preserve both sides.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The JASSM-ER records do not publicly verify delivery, acceptance, training,
  inventory, or operational use for the specific 2024 order.
- The data-center figures mix facility reports, modeled national demand,
  utility events, local revenue, and regulatory filings; they do not form a
  facility-to-household panel.
- Revenue is not net public benefit, and load is not completed service.
- A partner's continued use is not proof of unconstrained choice; dependence
  may make refusal costly.
- No observed response does not mean no response occurred; it marks a missing
  measurement stage.
- Cross-case comparison is structural and non-pooled; it is not a common
  capability score or geopolitical effect estimate.

## Next decisive tests

For JASSM-ER, identify one identifier-bearing shipment, acceptance, Polish-unit
receipt, training, inventory, or operational-use event, then locate a partner
or supplier response after that event. Preserve procurement, delivery,
maintenance, export permission, and alternative-source fields separately.

For data centers, build one matched place or feeder episode linking a named
facility to interconnection, actual load, cost allocation, public revenue or
service spending, water/land exposure, and a household or business outcome.
Include a successful or attempted switching case where possible.

The decisive evidence is not another capacity announcement. It is a realized
capability, a controlled dependency, an affected actor, and a dated response.

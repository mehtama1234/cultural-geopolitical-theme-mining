# Finding 047: Public financial capacity and household room are connected stages, not one pressure index

**Status:** provisional non-pooled macro-to-household bridge · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The current atlas can now place five evidence surfaces in one end-to-end
architecture without pretending that they are one dataset:

1. BEA reports aggregate output, private demand, corporate profits, personal
   income, spending composition, and saving.
2. The Federal Reserve reports inflation, consumption, credit access, financial
   vulnerabilities, and runnable money-like liabilities.
3. OFR reports the public institution's staffing, AI/cloud use, budget, and
   financial-market data-collection capacity.
4. IMF Article IV reports fiscal, external, financial, and conditional debt-risk
   scenarios.
5. SHED reports household liquidity, revolving credit, linked balance changes,
   and student-loan payment difficulty.
6. The New York Fed reports current aggregate household debt, product balances,
   and delinquency on consumer credit records.

The defensible synthesis is: **aggregate resilience, public monitoring capacity,
and household room can move differently at the same time.** A growing economy
does not prove equal household security; a capitalized banking system does not
erase selected borrower stress; a larger public data capability does not prove
better outcomes; and a projected debt path does not identify who bears future
adjustment.

## The six stages and their units

| Stage | Direct evidence | Unit | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aggregate economy | BEA Q2 real GDP grew 1.5% at an annual rate, private final sales grew 4.2%, and current-production corporate profits rose $400.9 billion from Q1. July personal income rose $115.1 billion and the saving rate was 3.0%. | National-account aggregates | Distribution, real purchasing power, firm-level margins, or household welfare |
| Financial system and policy | Fed reports 4.1% PCE inflation through May, 1.3% annualized consumption growth through May, relatively tight credit for some households and small firms, and $27.033 trillion in runnable money-like liabilities at end-2025. | Institutional reports, market aggregates, and macro rates | Borrower-specific APRs, realized losses, causal household response, or crisis probability |
| Public analytical capacity | OFR reports a workforce path from 188 to 109, a reported 11% budget reduction, AI use cases, and roughly $5 trillion in daily non-centrally cleared bilateral repo commitments made more visible to regulators. | Federal-institution self-report and market segment | AI productivity, service quality, workload, accountability, or risk elimination |
| State-finance scenario | IMF reports 2025 general-government debt at 123.9% of GDP and projects 141.5% in 2031 under its conditional baseline. | Country-surveillance estimates and projections | Which program, group, generation, or jurisdiction bears adjustment |
| Household room | SHED reports 63% of adults could cover a hypothetical $400 expense with cash or equivalent, 12% could not, 45% of card owners carried a balance, and 41% of required student-loan payers reported recent difficulty. | Survey respondents, conditional subgroups, and a consent-based linked-credit sample | A common shock, actual transaction, causal repayment path, or political response |
| Aggregate credit exposure | New York Fed reports $18.8 trillion in household debt in 2026 Q2, 4.7% of outstanding debt in some stage of delinquency, and $48 billion quarterly growth in non-housing debt. | Aggregate consumer credit records | Which households carry the balances, why balances moved, or whether delinquency changes welfare |

The numbers are intentionally not combined. A current-dollar national-account
flow, a Federal Reserve risk assessment, an OFR staffing count, an IMF debt
projection, a New York Fed aggregate credit balance, and a SHED respondent share
do not share a denominator.

## The end-to-end chain under test

```text
trade, energy, technology, labor, and fiscal conditions
  -> output, prices, profits, rates, credit, and funding conditions
  -> public monitoring capacity and firm/household financing terms
  -> liquidity, borrowing, spending, repayment, care, and time choices
  -> attribution, trust, legitimacy, collective action, and political judgment
  -> fiscal adjustment, regulation, public capacity, and external leverage
```

The new bridge closes a source-architecture gap: it puts BEA, Federal Reserve,
OFR, IMF, and SHED evidence next to each other and states what each contributes.
It does not close the substantive causal gap. The program still needs a dated
event and a compatible unit that observes exposure, immediate adjustment,
attribution, action, and later remedy or recovery.

## What the cross-source comparison changes

### 1. “The economy is doing well” is not a household endpoint

BEA's output, private-demand, profit, income, and saving measures are useful
macro benchmarks. They can establish that activity and aggregate flows moved;
they cannot establish that a renter, borrower, worker, or caregiver gained
options. The July shift toward services spending over goods spending is a
composition contrast, not a cultural preference finding.

### 2. Financial stability and household room are different layers

The Fed can report high bank capital or moderate aggregate debt vulnerability
while also reporting selected mortgage distress, elevated card and auto
delinquencies, tight credit for some borrowers, and inflation above the policy
target. SHED supplies household-facing endpoints that system aggregates
cannot: emergency liquidity, carried balances, linked balance changes, and
reported repayment difficulty. The New York Fed adds current aggregate credit
scale and delinquency, but cannot identify the household path. Neither source alone identifies the full causal
path from rate or funding conditions to a later choice.

### 3. Visibility is a form of public capacity, not an outcome

OFR's new repo-data collection can make a previously opaque market segment more
legible. That is important institutional capacity. But data visibility is not
the same as intervention, error correction, public accountability, or lower
financial risk. The same caution applies to AI and cloud infrastructure: fewer
staff plus more tools may be augmentation, mission narrowing, or a transfer of
review and accountability burdens.

### 4. Fiscal projections are scenarios with distributional questions attached

The IMF debt path is valuable for identifying a state-finance constraint and the
risks that could alter it. It is not a forecast of household hardship and does
not say who bears an eventual adjustment. That requires connecting a specific
policy or funding event to program access, taxes, wages, prices, services, and
political response.

## Counterexamples kept visible

- GDP and corporate profits can rise while a subgroup loses liquidity or faces
  tighter credit.
- A low aggregate mortgage-delinquency rate can coexist with distress among
  selected FHA/VA or low-down-payment borrowers.
- A household can carry a credit-card balance to smooth a shock; carrying a
  balance is not itself delinquency or evidence of a rate-driven loss.
- OFR can acquire better market data while staffing falls; more visibility does
  not prove more intervention capacity.
- A projected increase in public debt can coexist with growth and does not
  identify the future incidence of adjustment.
- A high saving rate or a cash-capable answer can reflect composition or
  precautionary behavior rather than durable financial security.

## Next test

The next pass should select one dated rate, energy, tariff, credit, or funding
event and build a same-case or compatible panel around it:

1. identify borrower type, income/resources, place, firm size, and exposure;
2. measure the immediate payment, spending, employment, care, or time response;
3. record attribution, fairness, dignity, trust, and available alternatives;
4. observe remedy, switching, repayment, recovery, institutional use, or civic
   action; and
5. compare an exposed/high-stress cell with a low-exposure or buffered
   countercell.

The bridge should be versioned when BEA, Fed, IMF, OFR, SHED, and New York Fed
vintages update;
it should never become a single “financial pressure” score.

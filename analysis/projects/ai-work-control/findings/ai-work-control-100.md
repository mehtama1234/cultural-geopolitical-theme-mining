# Finding 100: Import reliance is not the same as usable substitution or strategic autonomy

**Status:** bounded critical-minerals technology/dependence finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-18

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-108687 places critical-mineral dependence below batteries,
semiconductors, grid storage, consumer electronics, and defense systems. The
United States considers 60 minerals critical because of economic or security
importance and vulnerable supply chains, but the relevant unit is not the
mineral alone. It is the mineral–application–process: a substitute can work in
stationary storage while failing the performance or energy-density requirement
of a long-haul vehicle or semiconductor.

The assessment identifies a possible 2–3 year battery-recycling or
application-specific substitution window, while semiconductor substitution and
recycling are unlikely to reduce short-term import reliance. Those are
technology and implementation assessments, not realized supply, autonomy, or
geopolitical leverage.

## What the chain establishes

```text
foreign mineral geology/processing or export risk
  -> shipment, purity, price, inventory, and application exposure [open]
  -> substitute qualification, recycling feedstock, redesign, or production delay [partial]
  -> output, worker/local burden, customer price/quality, and state response [open]
  -> supplier switching, alliance leverage, or strategic autonomy [open]
```

For batteries, lithium-iron-phosphate chemistry may reduce cobalt, manganese,
and nickel reliance in stationary grid storage, but performance differs across
applications. Battery recycling technologies may be mature, yet collection,
feedstock, processing capacity, permitting, transport, demand, and price can
prevent a technical route from becoming an operating supply loop.

For semiconductors, material performance requirements make near-term
substitution harder. Manufacturing scrap and electronic waste are potential
inputs, but recovery markets and processes are not yet a demonstrated national
replacement for imported materials.

## Limits and next field

- No particular export restriction, shipment, price movement, factory outage,
  defense delay, or consumer loss is identified.
- A policy option, R&D pathway, or 2–3 year potential window is not realized
  capacity or accepted output.
- The record does not observe ownership, inventory, substitute qualification,
  recovery yield, local environmental burden, worker conditions, or customer
  switching.
- USGS mineral summaries and GAO technology assessment use different source
  units and are deliberately not pooled here.

The next decisive artifact is a compact mineral–application event ledger joining
an export control or disruption to shipment, price/purity, inventory,
substitution or redesign, production/output, local burden, supplier switch,
and government or alliance response. Only that sequence can translate mineral
dependence into realized economic or geopolitical leverage.

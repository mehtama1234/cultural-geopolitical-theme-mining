# Finding 107: Employment remedies can repair immediate loss without proving durable worker security

**Status:** bounded worker-remedy follow-up · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The DOJ IER fiscal-year 2026 resolution page adds two reported individual
employment remedies. In an Alexandria, Virginia case, a property-management
company offered reinstatement and paid $30,000 in back pay after an asylum-I-94
document issue; in a New York City case, an employer paid $8,000 in front and
back pay after an E-Verify-related termination and changed its Form I-9 and
E-Verify procedures.

These cases deepen the end-to-end worker-control chain because they show more
than an agency complaint route: a documented barrier, an identified employer
response, money paid according to the agency account, and a firm-level process
change. They still do not show whether reinstatement was accepted, whether the
worker stayed employed, whether payment was adequate, or whether household
security and trust recovered.

## The chain

```text
employment-document or verification barrier
  -> loss of work access and income risk
  -> worker uses IER route
  -> employer pays reported back/front pay and changes procedure
  -> accepted reemployment, durable security, trust, household recovery,
     and later exit [open]
```

The deeper point is that institutional remedy has at least three distinct
levels: money can be paid, a workplace rule can change, and the worker can
actually regain durable control. The first two are visible here; the third is
not.

## Limits and next field

- The records are agency summaries of selected cases, not a prevalence sample.
- “Paid” is reported by IER; independent payment receipt, amount adequacy, and
  tax or timing details are not retained.
- The Alexandria case reports an offer of reinstatement, not accepted return.
- The next decisive evidence is worker-linked follow-up on accepted work,
  later tenure, recurrence, household recovery, or exit.

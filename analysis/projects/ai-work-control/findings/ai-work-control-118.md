# Finding 118: Defense spending can build industrial allocation before it becomes usable strategic capability

**Status:** bounded Polish defense-industrial realization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

Poland’s Ministry of National Defence reports that its SAFE program has
produced 62 contracts worth about 120 billion złoty, with nearly 90% of the
money expected to go to Polish defense companies. The same official program
material describes contracts for communications, reconnaissance transporters,
and other equipment, with delivery schedules extending into later years.

This establishes a state-capacity and industrial-policy stage:

```text
security pressure / European financing
  -> defense contracts and domestic supplier allocation
  -> production, delivery, acceptance, training, maintenance
  -> usable force, replaceability, deterrence, and changed external behavior [open]
```

The industrial share and contract value are not the same thing as delivered
equipment or operational leverage. The program therefore complements the
JASSM-ER route: contracting can change domestic industrial participation while
the specific weapon system remains only scheduled.

## What is visible

| Stage | Public evidence | Still missing |
|---|---|---|
| State decision | SAFE contracts are presented as part of military modernization | Threat-to-purchase attribution and portfolio trade-offs |
| Industrial allocation | 62 contracts, about 120 billion złoty, and nearly 90% directed toward Polish companies are reported | Firm-level production, jobs, inputs, capacity, and delivery performance |
| Delivery | Individual contracts have stated delivery horizons | Shipment, acceptance, storage, training, and sustainment by item |
| Capability | The program is described as strengthening the armed forces | Unit-level readiness, mission use, substitution, and maintenance |
| Geopolitical effect | The policy is framed as strengthening security and resilience | An observed change in another actor’s behavior, deterrence, bargaining, or alliance dependence |

## Why it matters to the broad atlas

This is the geopolitical version of the atlas’s repeated realization-clock
finding. A state can convert security concern into procurement and domestic
industrial allocation before it can convert procurement into a reliable,
replaceable, trained capability. The economic and political meaning of the
program may therefore arrive earlier than its operational effect.

It also prevents a common category error: “money stays domestic” is an
industrial-distribution claim, not proof that citizens, workers, military
units, or allies received a durable security benefit.

## Limits and next test

- The figures are official program-level claims, not a firm-level or contract-level output audit.
- Domestic supplier share does not show production completion, delivered quantity, quality, or maintenance.
- The route does not identify local worker, community, or household incidence.
- It does not establish that the program changed an adversary’s choices or Poland’s bargaining power.

The next decisive artifact is an identifier-bearing contract follow-up linking
one system to shipment, acceptance, trained personnel, maintenance, operational
use, and an observed external or alliance response.

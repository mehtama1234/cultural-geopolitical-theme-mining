# Finding 103: Virginia turns data-center growth into explicit risk-allocation obligations

**Status:** bounded infrastructure-governance implementation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

Virginia's State Corporation Commission has moved beyond describing data-center
growth as a forecast or reliability concern. Its public facts page describes a
specific safeguard package for large-load customers: separate rates based on
their unique costs, a contract obligation to take and pay for service for at
least 14 years, minimum payment of 85% of transmission and distribution costs
regardless of actual use, collateral that may cover up to 60% of minimum
charges for qualifying new customers, and Commission review of Dominion's
large-load interconnection process.

This is a meaningful state-capacity stage because the institution is trying to
attach demand, credit, and direct-connect risk to the customer generating the
need. It is still a design and oversight record. The public evidence does not
show a signed customer contract, collateral posting, actual minimum-charge
payment, interconnection approval, energized facility, enforcement action, or
household bill change.

## What the chain establishes

```text
rapid large-load growth and reliability/cost concern
  -> rate class and allocation reform
  -> minimum-demand, collateral, long-term service, and interconnection rules
  -> customer contract/payment/compliance [open]
  -> energized load and realized system cost [open]
  -> household bill/service effect and public legitimacy [open]
  -> durable state bargaining power or geopolitical leverage [open]
```

The interesting mechanism is not simply “data centers use electricity.” It is
that the state is redesigning who must carry uncertainty when projected demand
does not become ordinary, continuous demand. A rule can protect existing
customers in principle while leaving actual incidence unresolved until
contracts, payments, interconnection approvals, and bills can be matched.

## Limits and next field

- The SCC summary is a policy description, not an account-level compliance
  dataset.
- “At least 85%” and “up to 60%” describe obligations or possible collateral;
  they are not observed payments or realized loss avoidance.
- The modeled Rider T1 residential comparison remains separate from actual
  household bills and cannot be used to claim that data centers caused or
  eliminated a particular household burden.
- The next decisive artifact is the implementation docket: Dominion's filed
  interconnection/line-extension process, a qualifying customer contract or
  collateral record, an energized project, or a bill/account record showing
  realized allocation.

# Finding 110: The AI distribution problem starts before displacement

**Status:** bounded firm-worker-distribution synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The synthesis

The current AI evidence points to three different layers that should not be
collapsed:

1. **Firm expectation:** the NBER executive survey reports 69% active firm AI
   use, 90% reporting no past three-year employment or productivity impact, and
   different expected future employment changes for employers (-0.7%) and
   employees (+0.5%).
2. **Worker access and control:** the Federal Reserve 2025 SHED reports 25%
   worker AI use, with higher use among workers with graduate education and
   among employees with greater task control; time and quality benefits are
   perceived, not objectively measured.
3. **Distributional possibility:** the IMF model permits wage inequality to
   fall while wealth inequality rises when task complementarity, capital
   returns, and firm adoption choice interact.

Together they support a narrower proposition: the social distribution of AI
begins with access, permission, task control, learning capacity, and ownership
before it appears as a simple employment-loss statistic. The evidence does not
yet show which mechanism dominates in actual US firms or households.

## The end-to-end chain

```text
firm adoption and expectation
  -> worker access, task control, training, and permission
  -> perceived or measured productivity/time change
  -> pay, hours, monitoring, bargaining, and ownership returns
  -> household security, status, trust, political action, or exit [open]
  -> sector, public-capacity, and geopolitical consequence [open]
```

The key cultural question is who is treated as capable, responsible, or
replaceable. The key political question is who can inspect, contest, correct,
and share the gains. Those questions cannot be answered from adoption or
exposure alone.

## Boundaries and contradictions

- NBER firms, SHED workers, and the IMF model use different units, countries,
  periods, and methods; they are not a pooled estimate.
- Higher AI use among higher-control workers may reflect occupation, education,
  task fit, or employer permission rather than control causing use.
- Low retrospective firm impact can coexist with impending reorganization;
  expectations can also fail to materialize.
- A modeled fall in wage inequality is not household improvement, and a rise in
  modeled wealth inequality is not realized US concentration.

## Decisive next evidence

The next decisive artifact is a matched firm-worker episode with an adoption
date, task change, permission/training rule, monitoring or evaluation, pay and
hours, worker voice or remedy, household consequence, and later retention,
switching, or exit. Until that exists, the atlas should describe a mechanism
map—not claim that AI has already produced a single social outcome.

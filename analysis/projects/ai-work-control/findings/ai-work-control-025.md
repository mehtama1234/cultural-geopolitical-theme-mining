# Global poverty, climate risk, and data gaps constrain the meaning of growth

**Status:** provisional comparative finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-13

## Short answer

The World Bank's Poverty, Prosperity, and Planet 2024 assessment shows that global growth cannot be read as shared prosperity without tracking poverty thresholds, inequality, climate exposure, and measurement coverage together. Extreme poverty is concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa and fragile/conflict-affected countries, while climate risk threatens livelihood gains. This adds distributional context to the IMF shock frame; it does not establish US household outcomes or geopolitical behavior.

## What the evidence shows

In 2024, almost 700 million people lived below $2.15 per day and about 3.5 billion below $6.85 per day. Sub-Saharan Africa held 16% of the world population but 67% of people in extreme poverty. The Bank projects 622 million people will remain in extreme poverty in 2030 under its current trajectory, and nearly 40% of the global population will remain below $6.85 per day.

The shared-prosperity gap stalled after the pandemic; around one-fifth of the world's population lives in countries with high inequality. One in five people faces lifetime risk of an extreme-weather event, while fewer than half of countries had a recent household survey for poverty monitoring. The last fact is an evidence-capacity warning, not a claim that countries without recent surveys are poorer.

## Mechanism under test

```text
macro or conflict shock
  -> unequal income, service, and climate exposure
  -> resilience, migration, and adaptation options
  -> public legitimacy and political demand
```

The report establishes the distributional context and risk, not the downstream action path.

## What this changes in the atlas

This layer gives the IMF macro bridge a distributional counterweight. A global or national growth figure can improve while poverty, inequality, climate vulnerability, and data invisibility remain concentrated. Future country comparisons should therefore carry both macro performance and who-can-absorb-the-shock measures.

## Limits and next test

The World Bank measures global/country aggregates under specific poverty lines and data conventions. It does not measure US household experience, exact climate losses, conflict causation, migration decisions, trust, unrest, or state leverage. The next test is a country/place panel joining macro shocks to social protection, climate exposure, migration, and political response.

## Reading rule

This is global comparative context for the US-centered atlas, not a US causal finding.

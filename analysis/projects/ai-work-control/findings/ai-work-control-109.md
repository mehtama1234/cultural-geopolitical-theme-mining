# Finding 109: AI access tracks worker room and control before it tracks outcomes

**Status:** bounded worker-side AI access/control finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The Federal Reserve's 2025 SHED reports that 25% of workers used generative AI
in the prior month. Use was reported by 43% of workers with graduate degrees
versus 10% with a high-school education or less, and by 30% of employees who
often or always controlled how they completed tasks versus 20% with lower task
control. Among workers, 44% said AI saved time; among AI users, 52% said it
improved quality.

This does not show that task control causes AI adoption. It shows that AI is
entering work through unequal access, education, job composition, and workplace
permission. The first social divide may therefore be who is allowed and
prepared to use the tool, before the later divide over who receives the gain.

## The chain

```text
education / task control / employer permission
  -> AI access and use
  -> perceived time or quality benefit
  -> pay, hours, training, monitoring, bargaining, household security,
     trust, and exit [open]
```

Read beside the [firm-side AI expectation finding](ai-work-control-108.md),
the two layers expose the missing bridge: executives report expected future
employment change, while workers report present access and perceived benefit,
but neither source follows the same firm-worker episode.

## Limits and next field

- These are cross-sectional subgroup comparisons, not a worker panel.
- Use, control, and perceived benefit have distinct denominators and do not
  establish objective productivity or causal effects.
- Employer policy, training, monitoring, pay, hours, and bargaining are not
  observed for the same worker.
- The next decisive evidence is matched firm-worker data following one
  implementation through task change, material outcome, remedy or voice, and
  retention, switching, or exit.

Source: [Federal Reserve 2025 SHED employment and job-quality report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-employment-and-job-quality.htm).

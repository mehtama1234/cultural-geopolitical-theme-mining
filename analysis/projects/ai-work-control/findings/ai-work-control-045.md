# Finding 045: Macro resilience and state-finance risk are simultaneous conditions

**Status:** provisional IMF country-surveillance layer · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The IMF's 2026 Article IV consultation for the United States places two
conditions in the same macro frame:

1. **Recent resilience:** IMF reports 2.0% real GDP growth on a 2025 Q4/Q4
   basis, strong broad-based productivity, and unemployment near 4–5%.
2. **Accumulating state-finance and external risk:** the 2025 general-government
   gross debt ratio is reported at 123.9% of GDP, the current-account deficit at
   3.7% of GDP, and the federal fiscal balance at −5.9% of GDP. The IMF flags
   tariffs, energy prices, short-maturity debt, nonbank visibility, external
   rebalancing, and financial-regulatory change as relevant risks.

The baseline projects 2026 Q4/Q4 growth of 2.4%, unemployment of 4.3%, and
general-government gross debt rising from 125.8% of GDP in 2026 to 141.5% in
2031. Those are conditional projections, not observed future outcomes.

The defensible interpretation is: **aggregate resilience can coexist with
shrinking fiscal room and rising state-finance exposure; neither surface alone
describes household security, worker power, cultural meaning, or geopolitical
leverage.**

## Separate evidence surfaces

| Surface | Direct evidence | What it can support | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output/productivity | 2025 growth and broad productivity assessment | Macro activity and production context | Who receives productivity gains or whether work becomes more controlled |
| Labor | Slowing employment growth with unemployment expected near 4% | Aggregate labor-market context | Worker transitions, pay, schedule control, or household exit options |
| Fiscal | Deficit, debt, and projected debt path | Public-finance constraint and adjustment context | Which households bear cuts/taxes or how legitimacy changes |
| External | Current-account deficit and projected external position | Trade/finance dependence and rebalancing risk | Which firms, workers, or communities experience the adjustment |
| Financial governance | Treasury-market, nonbank, bank-capital, digital-asset, and repo-supervision concerns | Institutional risk and regulatory-capacity questions | Actual crisis, household loss, or realized geopolitical concession |

The periods and denominators are not interchangeable: Q4/Q4 growth, fiscal-year
balance, general-government debt, current-account balance, and unemployment are
different statistical surfaces. The IMF report draws on BEA, BLS, Haver, and IMF
staff estimates; it is not a replacement for those source programs.

## End-to-end chain under test

```text
policy, tariff, energy, immigration, and productivity changes
  -> output, employment, prices, fiscal balance, and external position
  -> public adjustment, firm investment, household costs, and labor alternatives
  -> trust, attribution, collective action, and political judgment
  -> state capacity, alliance choices, and external bargaining power
```

The IMF layer closes the aggregate macro-to-state-risk stage. BEA, BLS, Federal
Reserve, SHED, SIPP, firm, and geopolitical records populate adjacent surfaces,
but the program still lacks a common event key connecting fiscal adjustment or
external rebalancing to household experience and later political response.

## Counterexamples kept visible

- Strong GDP growth can coexist with household hardship, unequal productivity
  gains, or weak worker bargaining power.
- A near-4% unemployment projection does not measure job quality, participation
  barriers, immigration composition, or desired hours.
- Higher public debt does not specify whether adjustment arrives through taxes,
  transfers, inflation, service reductions, growth, or financial repression.
- A current-account deficit is an aggregate external position, not proof of one
  firm or household's dependence on foreign finance.
- IMF projections can be revised by later data, policy changes, energy shocks,
  war, tariffs, or financial conditions; they must not be treated as realized
  trends.

## Next test

The next macro-to-society bridge should align a dated policy, tariff, energy,
or fiscal event with state and firm responses, then condition household and
worker outcomes by income, place, sector, and representation. It should retain
the countercells of strong aggregate growth with worsening household room and
fiscal adjustment without deteriorating trust. A later IMF/BEA/Fed vintage
should update the macro surface without rewriting the micro evidence.

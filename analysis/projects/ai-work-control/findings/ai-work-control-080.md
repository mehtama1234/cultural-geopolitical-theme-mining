# Production support and integration testing move JASSM/LRASM beyond announcement—but not yet to delivered inventory

**Status:** realization-stage milestone pass · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The finding

The JASSM/LRASM case now has dated evidence beyond procurement intent and
supplier naming:

- In July 2025, DoD announced a $999 million IDIQ contract for JASSM/LRASM
  production support, including upgrades, integration, production, sustainment,
  management, and logistics support through 2030. The announcement says no
  funds were obligated at award.
- Lockheed Martin's 2024 account describes a large-lot contract intended to
  increase JASSM/LRASM production capacity and identifies a new 225,000-square-
  foot intelligent factory brought online in 2022.
- Lockheed Martin's 2026 account reports completion of the first phase of the
  F-35C LRASM flight-science program after tests from September 2024 through
  April 2026, using LRASM and JASSM test assets.

These observations move the case through **support commitment, industrial
capacity investment, and integration testing**. They do not establish accepted
quantities, delivery to a customer, inventory availability, maintenance
capacity, or a changed external bargaining position.

```text
award and suppliers
  -> production-support contract        [observed]
  -> production-capacity investment     [observed]
  -> integration flight testing         [observed]
  -> accepted production quantity       [open]
  -> customer delivery/fielding         [open]
  -> substitute capacity or response    [open]
```

## Evidence table

| Stage | Dated evidence | What it supports | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Production support | DoD, July 21, 2025; $999M JASSM/LRASM IDIQ, expected completion July 2030, no funds obligated at award | A formal lifecycle-support and production-support route exists | Obligated output, accepted missiles, delivery, or readiness |
| Capacity investment | Lockheed Martin, October 14, 2024; large-lot procurement/capacity language and 225,000-square-foot intelligent factory brought online in 2022 | Industrial capacity-building and intended production-rate expansion | Actual rate, line utilization, unit cost, or delivered quantity |
| Integration test | Lockheed Martin, June 10, 2026; first phase of F-35C LRASM flight-science testing completed after tests from September 2024–April 2026 | A dated integration/test milestone involving LRASM and JASSM test assets | Operational fielding, customer acceptance, inventory, or combat availability |

The DoD announcement and Lockheed accounts are intentionally not merged into a
single output estimate. A production-support contract can fund engineering,
logistics, upgrades, and sustainment; a factory can be ready without producing
the selected lot; and a flight test can validate integration without placing a
missile in an operational inventory.

## Why it matters for the end-to-end geopolitical question

This is the first clear realization-stage movement in the case after the
recipient/entity/location work. It makes the next acquisition more specific:
the program should seek accepted lot quantities, shipment or delivery records,
inventory/fielding notices, and the facility or supplier assignment for the
subaward components.

It also sharpens the power question. The United States and allies may have
increased production capacity and tested integration without yet demonstrating
how quickly a customer can receive, maintain, replenish, or switch the system.
Capability becomes leverage only when an actor can use that realized capacity to
change another actor's options or behavior.

## Counterinterpretations kept visible

- An IDIQ award ceiling or support scope is not the same as obligated or
  delivered production.
- Company-reported capacity and factory investment can reflect intended scale,
  not realized throughput.
- Flight testing is a technical milestone, not an inventory or fielding record.
- JASSM and LRASM share elements of the production and integration system, but
  the programs, variants, quantities, and customer pathways remain distinct.
- A stronger US production base does not by itself prove resilience against a
  critical-input disruption or create geopolitical leverage.

# Finding 138: A replacement contract can arrive late while the real service risk stays unknown

**Status:** bounded public-telecommunications transition finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GSA managed the move of 221 federal agencies from old telecommunications
contracts to a replacement contract. The transition ran more than three years
late, so agencies extended the old contracts to avoid possible outages. For
six selected agencies, legacy-contract billing rose 206% from February to
March 2025. GAO also found that GSA did not know which agencies had actually
experienced service disruptions.

The plain-language route is:

```text
old contract expires
  -> replacement transition is delayed
  -> old service is extended and may cost more
  -> actual outage, service quality, mission effect, and user burden [open]
```

Keeping a service running on paper is not the same as proving that the
transition worked. But the reverse is also important: an unknown outage count
does not prove that outages happened.

## Why it matters for the broader atlas

This adds an operating-stage example to the infrastructure theme. Public
systems often report the contract, deadline, extension, or mitigation plan
more clearly than they report the lived service result. It also shows how
continuity can have a price: avoiding an outage may preserve capacity while
raising bills or prolonging dependence on aging technology.

The cultural and political question is whether people experience government as
reliable when the visible promise is a smooth transition, but the institution
cannot yet say who lost service, for how long, or with what consequence.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The detailed review covers six selected agencies, not all 221 agencies.
- A legacy extension may have successfully prevented service disruption.
- The 206% billing comparison does not isolate usage, technology mix, vendor
  pricing, or one-time charges.
- The report does not estimate a nationwide outage rate or individual user
  burden.
- An open GAO recommendation identifies missing measurement; it does not prove
  that a disruption occurred.

## Next test

For each selected agency, obtain the final transition date, outage or incident
record, service-level performance, replacement and legacy cost, mitigation
action, and mission/user effect. Keep continuity, price, quality, and public
experience as separate outcomes.

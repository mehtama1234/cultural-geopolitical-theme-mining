# Finding 001: AI may reorganize work before it changes average pay

**Status:** provisional  
**Last checked:** 2026-09-11  
**Project:** AI, work, and control

## Finding

The first evidence suggests that AI can improve task performance for some workers and spread into many workplaces without producing an immediate change in average pay or hours. The deeper change to test is therefore the structure of work: who decides, who checks, who carries blame, and who captures the gain.

## What we directly know

- A study of 5,179 customer-support agents found a 14% average increase in issues resolved per hour after access to an AI assistant, with much larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. [NBER Working Paper 31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)
- A Denmark study reports widespread early adoption and task reorganization, including AI oversight and integration tasks, while finding no detectable effect larger than 2% on earnings or recorded hours two years after ChatGPT's launch. [NBER Working Paper 33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777)
- A U.S. survey reports rapid adoption but only a limited share of total work hours assisted, which warns against treating access or trial use as full production change. [NBER Working Paper 32966](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32966)
- HBS describes adoption as tied to trust, responsibility, experimentation time, and whether workers can scrutinize and correct AI output. [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/from-pg-to-microsoft-how-companies-speed-ai-adoption)

## Causal chain under test

```text
AI is introduced
  -> some tasks become faster or easier
  -> firms change task bundles, review rules, or measures of performance
  -> discretion and responsibility move among workers, managers, and systems
  -> gains and risks are distributed unevenly
  -> pay, status, trust, and bargaining may change later
```

Only the first two links have meaningful first-pass evidence here. The remaining links are a research program, not a conclusion.

## Four-map reading

| Map | First signal | Missing evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Material | productivity and assisted hours | wages, prices, margins, job loss, training cost |
| Social | confidence, trust, learning, status may matter | worker voice, dignity, identity, household effects |
| Institution | firms set adoption, review, and responsibility rules | unions, regulators, courts, formal workplace policy |
| Power | control may move through data, measurement, and liability | ownership, bargaining, exit, and who captures value |

## Strongest challenge

The apparent gap between productivity and pay may simply reflect timing, measurement, or the fact that the studies examine different populations. We must not call it a transfer of power until direct evidence shows changed discretion, risk, bargaining, or value capture.

## What would change our mind

- Broad, reliable evidence showing that AI adoption quickly changes average pay or hours without changing task structure.
- Evidence that adoption does not alter monitoring, review, responsibility, or worker discretion.
- Evidence that productivity gains consistently flow to workers rather than being retained by firms, customers, or vendors.

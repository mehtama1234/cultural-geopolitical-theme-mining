# Poland’s JASSM-ER control record shows an evidence gap before delivery can be inferred

**Status:** historical implementation/control finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The finding

The Polish Supreme Audit Office (NIK) provides a useful historical control
layer for the JASSM-ER case, but not a delivery count. In its review of Poland’s
2022 defence-budget execution, NIK says the Armament Inspectorate settled a
JASSM-ER production-cost line using a program-review presentation that showed
dates and described the production process, but did not contain a numerical
value tied to the settlement. NIK therefore reported that the agency lacked
reliable knowledge of the actual realization and effects of the relevant
contracts and had recorded nearly PLN 2 million in advances unreliably.

This is evidence about the observability and control of contract realization,
not evidence that JASSM-ER missiles were or were not delivered. It strengthens
the current customer-realization watchpoint: an announcement, production
milestone, advance, or process description is not interchangeable with an
accepted quantity, shipment, fielding, or operational inventory record.

```text
production-cost / FMS settlement
  -> program milestones and process description  [observed]
  -> numeric evidence tied to settlement          [missing in audited case]
  -> accepted JASSM-ER quantity                   [open]
  -> delivery / fielding / training               [open]
  -> operational capability or leverage           [open]
```

## What NIK establishes

| Layer | Audited observation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | NIK reviewed nine DD Form 645 settlement forms covering Q4 2021 and Q1–Q3 2022, totaling PLN 210,543.7 thousand | This is an audit sample and settlement-control review, not a full missile inventory |
| JASSM-ER line | One line concerned JASSM-ER production costs and subscription/abonnement | The cited passage does not report missile quantity or delivery status |
| Evidence used | A Program Management Review presentation showed dates/milestones and described production process | A process presentation is not a numeric delivery or acceptance record |
| Control consequence | For the cited group of cases, NIK reported missing source documentation for PLN 1,942.0 thousand and unreliable recording of nearly PLN 2 million in advances | The monetary control issue does not translate into a missile shortfall estimate |
| General comparator | In a separate sampled contract, after three amendments, 32 of the planned units had been delivered by end-2022 | NIK’s cited passage does not identify that 32-unit contract as JASSM |

## Why it matters for the end-to-end geopolitical question

The program is trying to follow a capability from material and institutional
conditions through production, delivery, usable readiness, and external
consequence. NIK shows why the middle of that chain cannot be filled by
language such as “production,” “milestone,” or “advance.” The decisive fields
remain variant, lot, quantity, acceptance authority, shipment date, recipient
unit, training/integration status, inventory status, and any subsequent action
by Poland or another actor.

The finding also adds an institutional mechanism: the state’s ability to know
what a contract produced depends on documentary controls, not only on whether a
supplier reports activity. That mechanism is relevant to geopolitical
capacity, but the audit does not show that the control gap changed Poland’s
military options or alliance bargaining position.

## Counterinterpretations kept visible

- Missing numeric support in an audited settlement does not mean the supplier
  failed to produce the missiles.
- The audit may concern a production-cost or subscription line whose accounting
  object is narrower than the missile inventory.
- A later reconciliation may have supplied documentation not available during
  the audited period.
- The separate 32-unit delivery example is deliberately not treated as a
  JASSM-ER observation.
- The 2022 control finding cannot establish delay or non-delivery of Poland’s
  2024 JASSM-ER agreement.

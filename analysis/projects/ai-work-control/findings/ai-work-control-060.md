# Finding 060: Consumer protection visibility can expand while remedy, attention cost, and exit remain unresolved

**Status:** provisional non-pooled consumer/platform-power bridge · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The atlas now aligns four evidence surfaces without treating them as one consumer-welfare series:

1. CFPB complaint-response records show changing explanation, relief, timeliness, and narrative-visibility labels.
2. Product-conditioned CFPB records show that the route differs sharply by product, including a student-loan timing signal.
3. SHED measures household fraud exposure, direct loss, incomplete recovery, and time spent pursuing recovery.
4. Pew and FTC measure platform/AI use, privacy and regulatory concern, news exposure, and an institutional inquiry.

The defensible synthesis is: **consumer systems can make complaints, responses, and platform risks more visible without proving that consumers received adequate remedy, retained attention and exit capacity, or changed trust and political behavior.**

| Digital use and governance concern | 49% of adults report ever using AI chatbots, 24% daily use, 70% expect personal information to be less secure, and 67% have little/no confidence government can regulate AI effectively; FTC issued Section 6(b) orders to seven companies. | Separate Pew adult/teen surveys and FTC institutional action | Product-specific harm, dependence, exit, enforcement, and remedy |

These objects are intentionally not pooled. Complaint labels, fraud reports, platform-use shares, and an information-gathering inquiry have different units and estimands.

## The consumer-power chain under test

```text
transaction, platform exposure, fraud, or automated decision
  -> visibility, attention, effort, data use, response routing, and remedy label
  -> money/time loss, switching, dependence, trust, dignity, and exit capacity
  -> repeat use, complaint, abandonment, collective action, or political judgment
  -> firm correction, regulator intervention, market discipline, or recovery
```

The evidence reaches exposure, routing, selected response labels, household loss, and concern. It does not close the same-case remedy-to-trust-to-exit sequence.

## What the comparison changes

### A response label is not a remedy

An explanation can be useful, inadequate, or merely procedural. Monetary and non-monetary relief labels do not reveal whether the account was corrected, the loss recovered, or the consumer could safely remain or leave.

### Attention is part of consumer power

Fraud recovery time and platform use/concern show why money alone is insufficient. A household may recover funds but lose hours, confidence, data privacy, or willingness to use an institution. Conversely, high use can remain instrumental and replaceable.

### Institutional inquiry is not institutional correction

The FTC inquiry creates a visible governance action and an acquisition point for evidence. It does not establish that companion products caused widespread harm, that companies violated a rule, or that consumers received remedy.

## Counterexamples kept visible

- A complaint can receive an explanation and still leave the underlying account problem unresolved.
- A low monetary-relief share can reflect complaint type or coding rather than failed consumer protection.
- A fraud victim can recover money but retain time, attention, or trust losses.
- High platform use can reflect usefulness or social necessity rather than dependence.
- Privacy concern can coexist with continued use because alternatives are costly or unavailable.
- Regulatory information gathering can produce safeguards without proving current product harm.

## Next decisive test

Build a lawful same-case or linked account ledger containing:

1. product, firm, exposure, transaction, or platform event;
2. first contact, channel, narrative, effort, wait, data-use concern, and response;
3. verified correction, monetary/non-monetary remedy, and residual loss;
4. repeat effort, switching, continued use, abandonment, trust, and exit capacity; and
5. firm/regulator response, enforcement, recovery, and later consumer or civic action.

Until that design exists, the atlas should not translate complaint visibility into consumer protection, platform use into dependence, or an inquiry into resolved governance.

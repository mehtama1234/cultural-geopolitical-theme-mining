# Finding 144: Foreign intervention can change who a protest is against without increasing total protest

**Status:** bounded foreign-action and political-mobilization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER study combines geocoded protest events with geocoded U.S.–Israeli
strikes in Iran during a nationwide anti-government uprising. It reports that
anti-government protest was widespread before the strikes and receded sharply,
while pro-government rallies and protests against the United States and Israel
spread after strike exposure. Total protest activity changed little.

The study uses the staggered timing of counties' first exposure to estimate a
sharp shift in protest direction. It also notes that domestic repression and an
intensifying “foreign-agent” narrative were part of the same period.

The route is:

```text
foreign military intervention
  -> domestic repression and foreign-threat framing
  -> protest direction changes
  -> collective action is redirected toward defending the state
  -> [open] durable legitimacy, regime stability, civilian cost, and later external behavior
```

The important result is not simply “protests increased” or “protests stopped.”
The public mobilization changed direction while the overall amount of protest
changed little.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a direct test of the external-response question: an American military
action can alter the political meaning of an internal conflict in the target
country. A movement that was directed against the government can become easier
for that government to describe as defense against a foreign enemy.

That creates a warning for strategic reasoning. An intervention may weaken an
opponent's domestic legitimacy in one immediate sense while strengthening the
regime's claim to national sovereignty. Neither the protest shift nor the
abstract alone tells us whether that effect lasts.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The public abstract does not give the event counts, full estimates, standard
  errors, or complete strike and protest definitions.
- Repression and the foreign-agent narrative occurred alongside the strikes;
  the abstract does not isolate physical strike exposure from all political
  messaging and coercion.
- Protest events are not individual attitudes, votes, household wellbeing, or
  durable legitimacy.
- A shift toward pro-government protest does not prove that all participants
  supported the regime rather than responding to fear, nationalism, or public
  pressure.
- The finding does not establish civilian harm, deterrence, regime survival,
  or U.S. strategic success or failure across other objectives.

## Next decisive test

The next evidence should follow the same counties after the strike episode,
separating protest direction, repression, civilian cost, media framing, public
opinion, and later foreign-policy behavior. Keep immediate mobilization change
separate from durable legitimacy and strategic leverage.

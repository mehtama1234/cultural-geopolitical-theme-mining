# Finding 106: One Northern Virginia grid project reached operation while another remained only approved

**Status:** bounded infrastructure realization comparator · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

Dominion's current Wishing Star–Mars project page reports 500-kV GIS
energization in January 2026 and construction completion in May 2026. This is
useful because it supplies a reported project-to-operation stage in the same
Northern Virginia reliability-loop context where Golden–Mars is currently
recorded only through final Route 3A approval.

That does not mean Golden–Mars is built, energized, or delivering a measured
benefit. The two projects are separate and are kept separate in the event
ledger.

## Cost-allocation bridge

The Virginia State Corporation Commission adds a modeled household-cost layer
to the infrastructure route. In its current facts summary, the Commission
reports that an amended allocation method would reduce a projected typical
residential Rider T1 increase from $2.90 to $0.94 per month, a 67.5% reduction.
The same summary describes a separate GS-5 class, minimum large-load charges,
and direct-assignment rules intended to limit cost shifting.

This is important because it shows where infrastructure governance reaches
ordinary consumers: the regulator is not only approving wires; it is modeling
who may carry the cost. But the $0.94 figure is a projection and the release
does not show an actual household bill, a named data-center causation path, or
whether reliability improved for the same customers. The chain is therefore:

```text
large-load growth
  -> transmission investment and allocation rule
  -> projected residential bill effect / mitigation
  -> actual bill, reliability, compensation, acceptance, and trust [open]
```

## The chain

```text
Northern Virginia load growth and reliability need
  -> regulatory approval and project execution
  -> Wishing Star–Mars reported construction completion and energization
  -> delivered capacity, reliability, local burden, and public acceptance [open]
```

The important end-to-end lesson is that “approved infrastructure” and
“operating infrastructure” are different evidence stages. A project can cross
the construction and energization threshold while the public still lacks
linked evidence on who paid, who benefited, whether reliability improved for
customers, or whether affected communities accepted the result.

## Limits and next field

- The source is a utility project page; no independent telemetry or system
  performance record is retained.
- No large-load utilization, household bill, outage, property, worker, or
  compensation outcome is linked.
- The projected Rider T1 comparison is not an observed bill and is not a
  data-center-only causal estimate.
- The next decisive record is an independent or regulator-held operating,
  cost-allocation, service, or community follow-up record.

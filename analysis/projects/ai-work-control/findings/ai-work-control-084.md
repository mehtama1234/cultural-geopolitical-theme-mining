# Finding 084: AI-generated recruiting language can become an enforceable worker-exclusion event

**Status:** provisional named US enforcement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

## The bounded finding

The US Department of Justice’s Immigrant and Employee Rights Section finalized
a February 2026 settlement with Elegant Enterprise-Wide Solutions, a Virginia
IT services provider. DOJ reports that the company posted two job
advertisements generated by an artificial-intelligence tool that unlawfully
restricted certain positions by citizenship status, including restrictions to
H-1B, OPT, or H-4 visa holders. The settlement required a $9,460 civil penalty,
employee training, and a commitment not to place future advertisements that
unlawfully exclude protected workers.

This is a named US case joining an AI-assisted workplace process to a named
employer and dated institutional correction. The safe interpretation is narrow:

> AI-assisted recruiting can carry discriminatory rules into a real employment
> access channel, and public enforcement can require organizational correction.
> The record does not show that the AI tool independently made the hiring
> decision, how many applicants were affected, whether any individual received
> back pay, or whether access and behavior changed afterward.

## Event chain

```text
AI-generated job advertisements
  -> unlawful citizenship-status restriction in recruiting language
  -> potential exclusion or deterrence of US workers
  -> DOJ IER investigation and settlement
  -> civil penalty + training + prohibition on repeating the practice
  -> [open] applicant-level restoration, durable compliance, trust, or later action
```

The first two arrows are reported in the DOJ case summary as the basis for the
settlement. The third and fourth are the public remedy terms. The final arrow
is not observed.

The executed agreement makes the correction loop more concrete. A redacted
charging party filed a complete charge on 1 December 2025; IER opened the
investigation on 2 December and found reasonable cause to believe discriminatory
advertisements had been posted. The agreement runs for three years and requires
two $4,730 penalty installments, bilingual worker-rights postings, policy
review and revision within 60 days, paid training for recruiting and HR staff,
attendance records, and IER authority to request reports, inspect premises,
and interview personnel. If IER believes the agreement was violated, it may
give the respondent 30 days to cure the violation. These are enforceable
procedures; they are not evidence that monitoring occurred or that a job
opportunity was restored.

## Why it matters to the broader atlas

This closes a different part of the broad societal chain than the NBER
AI-access experiment. The NBER experiment measures a worker-level time change
after randomized access but does not observe discrimination or enforcement.
The Elegant case observes a recruiting rule, a plausible access consequence,
and a public correction but does not measure worker-level incidence or later
life outcomes. Together they show why “AI at work” must be decomposed into
assistance, recruiting, monitoring, evaluation, and decision surfaces.

The state response was case-specific enforcement under the INA—not a
productivity estimate or generalized AI rule. The correction changes the
firm’s obligations, but the public record does not prove that the affected
opportunity was restored.

A 17 September 2026 recheck of the DOJ settlement ledger, the executed
agreement, and the exact investigation-number/company surface found no later
public confirmation of payment, completed training or policy revision, IER
monitoring or cure, applicant-level back pay, or restored consideration. This
is a bounded non-observation: it does not establish non-payment or
non-compliance. See the [compliance follow-up audit](../elegant-settlement-compliance-follow-up-audit-2026-09-17.md).

## Limits and counterexamples

- The settlement summary does not say that the AI tool selected applicants or
  made a final employment decision; it identifies AI-generated advertisements.
- Two advertisements are a case count, not a prevalence estimate.
- The public record does not disclose how many people saw the postings or were
  deterred, nor whether any person was paid.
- A penalty and training can coexist with unknown monitoring and recurrence;
  prospective correction is not the same as lived recovery.
- The case concerns citizenship-status discrimination under the INA and should
  not be generalized to every protected class or automated employment tool.

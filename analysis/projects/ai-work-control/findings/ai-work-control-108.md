# Finding 108: Firm AI adoption is ahead of realized impact, while expectations already diverge

**Status:** bounded firm-side AI expectation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The NBER executive survey reports that 69% of firms were actively using AI,
while 90% of executives reported no employment or productivity impact at their
own firm over the previous three years. At the same time, executives expected
average next-three-year productivity growth of 1.4%, output growth of 0.8%, and
employment decline of 0.7% at their firms. Employees' expected employment
change was positive at 0.5%.

This is not evidence that AI has already displaced workers. It is evidence of
a transition boundary: adoption is visible, retrospective impact is limited in
the survey, and expectations about future employment are already asymmetric.

## The chain

```text
firm AI adoption
  -> limited reported retrospective impact
  -> expected productivity/output and employment change
  -> task allocation, pay, hours, training, monitoring, worker voice,
     household security, trust, and exit [open]
```

The deeper point is that the social question begins after the adoption
statistic. The decisive issue is who controls implementation and who receives
the productivity gain or absorbs the employment and autonomy risk.

## Limits and next field

- The survey covers executives in four countries, not a US-only population.
- Expectations are not realized outcomes and are not a causal estimate.
- The public record does not join firms to workers, occupations, tasks, pay,
  hours, training, monitoring, bargaining, or household outcomes.
- The next decisive evidence is a matched firm-worker episode that follows AI
  adoption through task change, implementation, worker voice, material outcome,
  and later retention, switching, or exit.

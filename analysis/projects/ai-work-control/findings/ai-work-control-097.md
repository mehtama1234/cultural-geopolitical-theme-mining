# Finding 097: Domestic strategic capacity has multiple clocks before autonomy or leverage

**Status:** provisional cross-domain capacity-realization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

## The bounded finding

The retained US capacity records show that strategic autonomy is not one
measurable outcome. Four evidence surfaces run on different clocks:

- GAO and USGS records describe critical-mineral concentration and possible
  substitution or recycling windows;
- GAO reports $13.1 billion in direct CHIPS awards disbursed across 49 projects
  and 24 companies, with 24 milestones completed and 125 in progress;
- LBNL, county, utility, and Virginia records describe national modeled
  data-center demand, local capacity/revenue, load events, and governance; and
- JASSM/LRASM and Poland records identify procurement, production, testing, and
  planned delivery without a public identifier-bearing acceptance and external-
  response chain for the named order.

The safe interpretation is:

> A strategic program can show funding, milestones, capacity, or operational
> activity without proving usable output, replaceability, domestic autonomy, or
> geopolitical leverage. Power requires an affected actor and a dated response,
> not only a commitment or asset count.

## Realization chain

```text
strategic objective or dependence
  -> authorization, funding, or agreement
  -> named capacity, supplier, and place
  -> production, delivery, operation, or service realization
  -> maintenance, replaceability, and control
  -> affected public, firm, worker, household, or partner
  -> acceptance, refusal, substitution, concession, or other response
  -> [open] autonomy, bargaining power, or geopolitical consequence
```

## What the public record supplies

| Surface | Observed or reported stage | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Critical minerals | 60 minerals in the cited critical framework; five selected materials reported with 100% net-import reliance; China a major source for 14 of 33 minerals on which the U.S. most depends; GAO identifies a possible 2–3 year battery-recycling window | Qualification, scale, cost, timing, substitute performance, and supplier switching |
| CHIPS realization | $13.1 billion disbursed across 49 projects and 24 companies; 24 milestones completed and 125 in progress | Yield, customer delivery, worker retention, operating capacity, and import substitution |
| Data-center/grid capacity | LBNL estimates 176 TWh of data-center electricity use in 2023; Prince William reports 621 MW added; Virginia records separate 1,551 and 1,800 MW load-drop events | Facility-level compute, ownership, portability, reliability, local burden, and service users |
| Defense procurement | Poland planned a 2026–2030 JASSM-ER delivery window; a production-support IDIQ ceiling is reported at $999 million | Shipment, acceptance, inventory, readiness, maintenance, qualified substitutes, and partner/adversary response |

These surfaces deliberately use different denominators, geographies, dates,
and estimands. They are a realization-stage comparison, not an autonomy index.

## Why it matters to the broader atlas

The synthesis prevents a common leap from domestic investment to strategic
independence. A funded semiconductor project may advance capability without
producing customer-ready output. A concentrated mineral source may remain
reliable while still limiting substitution. A data center can add capacity and
revenue while increasing dependence on grid, finance, equipment, or public
allocation. A defense agreement can signal commitment without proving fielded,
replaceable capability.

Keep these states separate:

```text
authorization != delivery
funding        != operating output
milestone      != customer use
capacity       != replaceability
domestic asset != autonomy
capability     != leverage without an affected actor's response
```

## Next decisive test

Build an identifier-bearing realization ledger linking award or agreement,
supplier, facility, production milestone, delivery/acceptance, customer use,
maintenance, substitute qualification, disruption, affected actor, and later
acceptance/refusal/switching or concession. Preserve non-realized projects,
alternative suppliers, and actors that did not change behavior.

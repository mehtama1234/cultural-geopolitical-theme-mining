# Finding 105: Public land control changed the approved infrastructure route

**Status:** bounded infrastructure route-realization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The Golden-Mars transmission project did not end with the initially preferred
Route 4. Dominion's current Northern Virginia project page says Route 4
required Loudoun County School Board consent, that the School Board did not
consent to Dominion crossing its land, and that the SCC approved Route 3A on
June 29, 2026.

This is a concrete institutional consequence of public and land-control
processes: the project moved from a conditional route to a different final
route. It is not evidence that residents received a better outcome, that the
project was built, or that the route change reduced property, health, or bill
burdens.

## The chain

```text
transmission need and public concerns
  -> hearings and route alternatives
  -> Route 4 requires School Board consent
  -> consent not given
  -> SCC approves Route 3A
  -> construction, easement/payment, energization, and acceptance [open]
```

The deeper point is that public infrastructure power is distributed across
regulator, utility, school-board land control, property owners, and system
customers. “Public input” is not just an opinion measure; in this case a
public institution's land decision changed the approved route. The material
consequence for affected properties and households remains unmeasured.

## Limits and next field

- Dominion's page is a project-status account, not independent proof of
  construction or household outcome.
- Route 3A may affect more properties than Route 4 according to the SCC
  summary, but parcel-level incidence and compensation are not retained.
- The next decisive record is a construction/easement/compensation,
  energization, reliability, or post-decision public-response record.

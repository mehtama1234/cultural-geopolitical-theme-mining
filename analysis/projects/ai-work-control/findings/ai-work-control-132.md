# Finding 132: Transmission finance can be closed before customer savings and reliability are realized

**Status:** bounded infrastructure-finance and customer-incidence finding · **Checked:** 2026-10-18

## The finding

The Department of Energy reports that a July 2026 loan of up to $3.26 billion
closed for AEP Texas. The portfolio covers roughly 2,800 miles of transmission
rebuilds, reconductoring, and new construction. DOE says the projects are
expected to increase carrying capacity by 100%, reduce interruptions, and
produce about $685 million in electricity-cost savings over 30 years for 1.1
million customers. Those are meaningful financing and forecast stages, not
observed customer outcomes. DOE's September 3, 2026 review for the named
25.2-mile Hamlin–Roby line says construction was anticipated to begin in
September 2026. That moves the project beyond financing and review into a
scheduled construction start, but the reviewed public record still does not
show construction completion, energization, lower interruptions, or a bill
change for a named customer.

```text
large-load and reliability pressure
  -> federal financing and project portfolio
  -> project review and construction [review complete; start anticipated; completion open]
  -> operating reliability and cost allocation
  -> retail bill effect, local burden, customer benefit, and durable service [open]
```

## Why it matters

This connects federal finance, utility infrastructure, data-center and oil-and-
gas demand, jobs, local place, and household affordability without collapsing
them into one “investment” number. A closed loan is more concrete than an
announcement, but the public claim still moves from financing to projected
benefit before showing who receives the savings, who pays for the system, which
communities carry construction or land burdens, or whether reliability improves
for the customers named.

It is another example of the atlas's central distinction: capacity can become
financeable and physically buildable before it becomes a usable, fairly
distributed household outcome.

## Do not overread

- The DOE page reports project forecasts, not completed construction or
  realized savings.
- The 1.1 million customers are a projected beneficiary universe, not a linked
  household or bill denominator.
- The $685 million figure does not show bill credits, rate allocation,
  interruption reduction, or equal benefit.
- The loan and projected jobs do not establish local employment, land burden,
  affordability, or geopolitical leverage.
- Environmental-review completion is an administrative milestone. It is not
  proof that a line was built, energized, used, or beneficial to the customers
  named in the forecast.
- An anticipated September 2026 construction start is a schedule claim, not a
  dated construction-progress or energization result.

## Next decisive test

Follow the portfolio through line-level construction and operation, outage and
reliability measures, rate-case cost allocation, customer bills, local impacts,
and later durability. Preserve forecast, system average, customer class, and
household measures as separate units. The next decisive record is a named
project's dated construction-progress or energization milestone paired with a regulator or
utility record showing how costs and reliability changes reach customer classes.

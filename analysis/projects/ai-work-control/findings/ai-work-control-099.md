# Finding 099: Substitute earnings can be visible without proving practical recovery

**Status:** provisional named platform-remedy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-18

The Fair Work Commission's decision in Mansoor v. Rasier documents a rare
same-case bridge between platform exclusion, alternative work, and formal
remedy. After deactivation from Uber's Driver Platform, the worker had $9,000
in new Baysec earnings and an estimated $1,760 increase in Didi earnings. The
Commission deducted those substitute earnings when calculating a $6,073.23
gross lost-earnings order, alongside an order to reactivate access.

This advances the practical-optionality ledger: alternative earnings are
observed, rather than left as an unknown. It still does not establish that the
substitute work was freely chosen, equivalent in conditions, or sufficient to
protect the worker's household. The order's deadline is not payment receipt,
and actual post-order access, continued use, switching, non-use, trust, and
exit remain unobserved.

Safe chain:

```text
deactivation
  -> substitute earnings during the interruption
  -> adjudicated reactivation and lost-pay order
  -> [open] receipt, net recovery, durable access, and exit
```

# Finding 053: AI capability can expand while infrastructure, ownership, and public-control dependence remain unresolved

**Status:** provisional non-pooled AI capability/dependence/state-control bridge · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The atlas can now follow AI capability across four distinct stages:

1. World Bank identifies the local complements and concentrated value-chain layers that shape adopt, adapt, and advance pathways.
2. Romania and Malaysia provide dated public/private infrastructure cases with commitments, financing, planned capacity, ownership, and completion milestones.
3. OFR shows a US public institution using AI/cloud/data infrastructure while staffing and budget change and market visibility expands.
4. US data-center records connect modeled physical demand to local fiscal revenue and utility risk-allocation rules.

The defensible synthesis is: **AI capability and infrastructure can grow without proving public control, household benefit, provider replaceability, or state leverage.** Domestic capacity, external ownership, public monitoring, and local incidence must be measured separately.

These objects are intentionally not pooled. A global framework, project milestone, federal self-report, national scenario, county fiscal aggregate, and state tariff term do not share a denominator or realized-capability endpoint.

## The capability-to-leverage chain under test

```text
AI demand and strategic investment
  -> chips, data centers, power, skills, data, models, and public institutions
  -> operating capacity, local adaptation, ownership, monitoring, and service delivery
  -> portability, supplier substitution, continuity, and domestic bargaining room
  -> state regulation, alliance terms, external dependence, refusal, or leverage
```

The evidence reaches demand, infrastructure, public data capacity, governance design, and project milestones. It does not close continuity, replaceability, domestic welfare, or external behavioral response.

## What the comparison changes

### Capability is a complement system, not a server count

The World Bank framework gives the correct measurement contract: infrastructure must be read with skills, institutions, data, language fit, procurement, and local adaptation. A country can adopt useful systems without producing frontier models, but that benefit can coexist with dependency on hardware, operators, capital, software, or standards.

### Construction and financing are not operating autonomy

The Romania and Malaysia cases make ownership and project stage visible. A public-cloud completion announcement is not application migration or reliable citizen service. A private campus with development finance and an ownership transition is not evidence of Malaysian control or dependence. The next records must follow operating performance, critical inputs, contracts, portability, and a real switch or failure.

### Public visibility is not public power

OFR's new data collection can make a large repo segment more legible, and AI/cloud tools can extend institutional capacity. But fewer staff, more tools, and better data do not by themselves prove stronger oversight, faster correction, or public accountability. The relevant endpoint is whether information changes an intervention, rule, market outcome, or public decision.

### Domestic scale is not state leverage

US data-center projections, local tax revenue, and large-load tariffs show the domestic political economy of physical capacity. They do not show who can switch providers, who bears utility and environmental costs, or whether the state can use the infrastructure to influence an external actor. State leverage requires a credible option and observed response.

## Counterexamples kept visible

- Imported AI can raise local capability while leaving critical hardware, software, finance, or operators external.
- A completed public cloud can be underused, nonportable, or difficult for citizens to access.
- A private data-center investment can create jobs and fiscal revenue while increasing public dependence on external capital or customers.
- More regulatory data can coexist with fewer staff, heavier review burdens, or no measurable intervention.
- Domestic data-center capacity can expand while households or utilities absorb costs.
- A tariff can assign minimum charges without recovering water, land, environmental, or public-service costs.
- Large physical capacity can coexist with weak supplier substitution and no external actor changing behavior.

## Next decisive test

Choose one critical AI or digital public-service system and record:

1. operator, ownership, finance, software, chips, power, data, and workforce;
2. contract duration, data/control rights, portability, inspection, and termination;
3. uptime, service use, quality, cost, local incidence, and public accountability;
4. alternative suppliers, switching time/cost, outage, replacement, or continuity event;
5. public attribution, legitimacy, regulation, procurement change, or organized response; and
6. an external negotiation, refusal, concession, retaliation, or alliance response.

Do not promote this bridge to “AI sovereignty,” “public control,” or “geopolitical leverage” until the system observes replaceability and a later institutional or external behavioral response.

# Finding 121: A process defect can coexist with a valid reason and no restoration

**Status:** bounded comparative Australian platform-work finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The bounded finding

In one Fair Work Commission case, the decision-maker identified procedural
deficiencies in a platform deactivation process but accepted the worker's
safety-related complaints as a valid substantive reason for the deactivation.
The unfair-deactivation claim was dismissed and no restoration remedy was
ordered.

The case adds a necessary distinction:

```text
platform deactivation
  -> procedural defect identified
  -> substantive safety reason accepted
  -> unfairness claim dismissed / no restoration ordered
  -> payment, later access, alternative work, trust, or exit [open]
```

**Why it matters:** procedural fairness and substantive merits are separate
questions. A notice or review failure can be real without proving that the
underlying platform decision was invalid. Conversely, dismissal of a claim
does not show that the worker experienced no income loss, burden, or later
consequence.

**Do not overread:** this is one Australian case, not a prevalence estimate or
a U.S. worker result. The public record does not show later account status,
payment, alternative work, recurrence, trust, or exit.

# Uber / Rehman formal reactivation and lost-remuneration record v1

**Status:** official Fair Work Commission decision summary; formal
reactivation and lost-remuneration order observed; payment receipt and
durability remain open
**Checked:** 2026-09-15

## Why this record matters

Rehman is a useful implementation case because the platform had already
voluntarily restored access before the Commission completed the remedy
analysis. The Commission nevertheless treated a formal order as meaningful:
eligibility to work again was not identical to being restored to the legal and
financial position the worker would have occupied without deactivation.

## Source and scope

The Fair Work Commission summary for *Rehman v Portier Pacific P/L* ([2026]
FWC 953; UDE2025/384) reports that Mr Rehman began work through the Uber Driver
Platform on 30 May 2023. Portier Pacific deactivated him on 15 December 2025
for allegedly failing a background check and voluntarily reactivated him on 20
February 2026. The Commission's summary records an order for reactivation and
`$7,096.96` gross lost remuneration.

This record is based on the official Bulletin summary rather than a downloaded
full-text primary decision. The summary is sufficient for the stated outcome
and amount; it does not substitute for the full reasons or establish receipt.

## Remedy chain

```text
background-check allegation
  -> deactivation on 15 Dec 2025
  -> voluntary access restoration on 20 Feb 2026
  -> Commission rejects “access is back, so no order is needed” logic
  -> formal reactivation order on pre-deactivation terms
  -> 9.5-week lost-remuneration calculation
  -> $7,096.96 gross ordered
  -> payment, durability, and recurrence prevention open
```

The case makes the difference between functional access and formal restoration
visible. The Commission's reasoning, as summarized in the Bulletin, treats a
formal order as providing certainty and enforceability about the worker's
terms, continuity, and pre-deactivation position. That is a stronger remedy
than a platform's unilateral statement that a worker is once again eligible.

## Comparative interpretation

```text
Rehman:     voluntary restoration -> formal reactivation + $7,096.96 ordered
Hotak:      voluntary restoration cannot erase jurisdiction or lost-pay review
Bandameeda: deficient platform process -> reactivation + $12,126.31 ordered
Khan:       insufficient notice and inquiry -> reactivation; pay quantum open
```

Rehman strengthens the project's central remedy finding: restoration has at
least three distinct states—platform eligibility, enforceable reactivation,
and financial recovery. Counting only the first state would understate the
institutional work required to restore a worker's position.

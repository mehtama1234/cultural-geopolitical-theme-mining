# Claims ledger: AI, work, and control

**Status:** provisional first pass  
**Rule:** working papers are not settled findings. “Inference” rows are our connections, not source claims.

| ID | Layer | Claim | Evidence location | Status | Limit / next test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Source observation | HBS presents AI adoption as a problem of trust in the tool, its developers, and the process for handling failure and responsibility. | [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/from-pg-to-microsoft-how-companies-speed-ai-adoption), trust section | Direct source claim | Research story based on cases; verify with underlying case evidence. |
| C-002 | Source observation | HBS reports that the cases it discusses treat experimentation time, employee scrutiny, and leadership support as part of adoption. | [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/from-pg-to-microsoft-how-companies-speed-ai-adoption), experimentation section | Direct source claim | Does not measure whether these practices improve worker power or pay. |
| C-003 | Study result | In a customer-support firm, access to a generative-AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average, with larger gains for novice and lower-skilled workers. | [NBER Working Paper 31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161) | Provisional / published version listed | One firm and one occupation; the result may not travel. |
| C-004 | Study result | In Denmark, early chatbot adoption was linked to task reorganization, but the study reports no detectable effect larger than 2% on earnings or recorded hours two years after launch. | [NBER Working Paper 33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777) | Provisional | Early period; country, institutions, and measurement matter. |
| C-005 | Study result | U.S. survey evidence reports fast work adoption, but only a share of work hours assisted and reported time savings smaller than total adoption headlines suggest. | [NBER Working Paper 32966](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32966) | Provisional | Reported use is not the same as verified output or changed job design. |
| C-006 | Study result | Task-level adoption can vary widely among workers doing similar work, so occupation-level exposure alone does not identify actual use. | [NBER Working Paper 35677](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677) | Provisional | Newly issued working paper; inspect survey design and task index. |
| C-007 | Inference | AI may change work structure and decision rights before it changes average pay or employment. | C-001 through C-006 | Working hypothesis | Requires direct measures of monitoring, discretion, promotion, bargaining, and responsibility. |
| C-008 | Open question | Who captures the value of faster work: workers, managers, customers, owners, or the tool provider? | C-001, C-003, C-004 | Unanswered | Add wages, prices, margins, retention, and job-quality evidence. |
| C-009 | Study result | In the Denmark study, adopters worked about 4% more of a full-time equivalent in their most recent occupation by December 2024, while average earnings and hours did not change. | [NBER Working Paper 33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777), pp. 3, 26–28 | Provisional | This is occupational mobility, not proof of better work or higher lifetime earnings. |
| C-010 | Study result | The Denmark study reports that individual adoption, especially daily use and large reported time savings, is more closely tied to occupational switching than employer initiatives are. | [NBER Working Paper 33777](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33777), pp. 27–28 | Provisional | Selection into use remains a concern; inspect the full event-study and appendix. |
| C-011 | Study result | In the customer-support study, AI recommendations appear to help workers learn: exposed workers continued to perform better during software outages, though outage periods are rare and not necessarily random. | [NBER Working Paper 31161](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161), pp. 5, 20–23 | Provisional | A durable skill gain is plausible but the outage test has a stated identification limit. |
| C-012 | Inference | The first visible distributional effect may be movement between tasks or occupations, not a wage premium inside the starting job. | C-004, C-009, C-010, C-011 | Working hypothesis | Test against promotion, job quality, worker choice, and involuntary displacement evidence. |
| C-013 | Study result | In an online labor-market randomized experiment, digital surveillance had no significant average effect on performance, but failing to explain surveillance changes based on worker performance reduced output. | [NBER Working Paper 33348](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33348) | Provisional | Setting is an online labor market; inspect treatment details and external validity. |
| C-014 | Study result | In a field experiment across 66 firms and 7,137 knowledge workers, individual access to a generative-AI tool reduced email time and work outside regular hours, without detectable changes in task quantity or composition. | [NBER Working Paper 33795](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33795) | Provisional | Tool, firms, six-month window, and authors' industry connection limit generalization. |
| C-015 | Inference | Control systems may change work through explanation, responsibility, and time boundaries before they change the tasks recorded in a job description. | C-001, C-002, C-013, C-014 | Working hypothesis | Add direct measures of worker consent, review rights, monitoring intensity, and job quality. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
AI access -> task assistance and measurement -> changed discretion and responsibility
-> changed work structure -> uneven gains and risks -> bargaining and social meaning
```

The middle links are not yet proved. They are the reason for the next search.

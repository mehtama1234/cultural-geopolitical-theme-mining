# Virginia large-load household-incidence audit v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-21  
**Lane:** infrastructure, household cost, public finance, and state capacity  
**Status:** policy and fiscal-conversion stages visible; household incidence not verified

## What the current record now shows

Virginia has moved beyond a general data-center growth story into several
explicit allocation mechanisms:

1. The SCC created the GS-5 large-load class and describes minimum charges,
   long-term service obligations, collateral, and future interconnection review.
2. The SCC reports a modeled residential-rate comparison in the Rider T1
   proceeding, but the comparison is not an observed household bill.
3. Virginia's 2026 budget adds an electricity-consumption tax of **$0.011 per
   kWh** for certain data-center operators from July 1, 2026 through June 30,
   2028. This is a fiscal capture mechanism, not proof of net local benefit or
   household relief.
4. The SCC's tax-administration pages now specify an implementation route:
   covered operators must register by September 1, 2026, and the forms page
   provides a monthly Data Center Electricity Consumption Tax report. This
   closes an administrative-control stage, but no operator registration,
   monthly filing, remittance, refund, or public-revenue receipt is linked to a
   named facility here.
5. Dominion's Hornbaker–Devlin project is reported as SCC-approved, while the
   related Wishing Star–Mars project is reported as energized. Those are
   different projects and must not be treated as one realization chain.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Result | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Large-load growth and system pressure | Reported | Utility events and planning records show aggregate operational and capacity pressure |
| Regulatory cost allocation | Reported | GS-5, minimum charges, collateral, and direct-assignment rules are visible |
| Public fiscal capture | Reported | A data-center electricity tax is specified in statute/budget; collections and uses are not yet observed here |
| Tax administration | Reported implementation route | The SCC specifies operator registration and monthly reporting; actual registration, filings, remittance, refunds, and public-revenue receipt are not linked to a named facility |
| Named project approval | Reported | Hornbaker–Devlin has an approved route; approval is not construction or operation |
| Related project realization | Reported | Wishing Star–Mars is reported energized; it is not evidence that Hornbaker–Devlin delivered the same benefit |

| Household bill and reliability effect | Not retained | The record has modeled or systemwide figures, not matched bills, outages, or service quality |
| Local burden and acceptance | Not retained | Property, water, land, health, worker, and resident outcomes are not linked to a facility or feeder |

## Interpretation

The new tax signal matters because it adds a conversion stage between large-load
growth and public capacity: the state is not only regulating who pays utility
costs; it is also assigning a public-revenue claim to electricity consumption.
That may create fiscal room, but it does not answer who experiences the costs,
who receives the benefits, or whether residents regard the arrangement as
fair.

The strongest safe statement is therefore:

> Virginia is building a layered bargain around large-load dependence—utility
> safeguards, customer obligations, and public fiscal capture—but the bargain
> remains institutionally visible before its household, local, and legitimacy
> outcomes are measured.

This is an infrastructure/state finding, not a claim that data centers caused a
particular household bill, that the tax offsets local burdens, or that a named
project has delivered a measured reliability benefit.

## Current docket recheck

A targeted September 20, 2026 review of the SCC's public Docket Search and
current-case pages still identifies PUR-2026-00131 as the supplemental docket
for Dominion's proposed line-extension amendments. The public SCC summary says
those amendments are to be filed in that docket, but the recheck did not locate
a public filing that links a named large-load facility to an approved
direct-connect charge, actual payment, tax remittance, or customer bill. This
is a verified public-record boundary, not evidence that no filing or payment
exists outside the retrieved public surface.

## Tax-administration follow-up — 2026-09-21

The SCC's current tax page makes the implementation clock more specific. For
grid-supplied data centers, the billing-service provider collects the $0.011/kWh
data-center electricity consumption tax and remits it monthly. For
self-supplied generation, the operator reports and pays directly. The SCC says
the monthly report and payment are due by the last day of the month following
consumption, and that late remittance carries a 10% penalty. The forms page
lists the operator-registration form as due September 1 and the electricity-tax
report as monthly.

This advances the route from “tax exists” to “the state has specified who files,
when payment is due, and what happens after nonpayment.” It still does not show
that a named operator registered, that a named facility consumed a measured
amount, that a provider or operator remitted tax, that revenue reached a
locality, or that a household bill or reliability outcome changed. The first
monthly deadline is therefore an administrative clock, not evidence of
collection or public benefit.

## Decisive next evidence

The next useful file would join one named facility or account to:

`contract/interconnection -> registration and monthly tax report -> metered consumption -> GS-5/minimum payment or tax remittance -> feeder/reliability effect -> household bill or service outcome -> local attribution/trust/action`

Until that exists, keep the tax, tariff, project approval, and energization
stages separate.

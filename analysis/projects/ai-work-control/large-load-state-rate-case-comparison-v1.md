# Large-load state rate and accountability comparison v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Scope:** Virginia, Texas, and Georgia large-load/data-center regulation  
**Unit:** state utility commission rule, tariff, and evidentiary record  
**Status:** comparative policy record; not a household-bill or causal welfare
estimate

## Why this is a useful societal comparison

The infrastructure question is now observable as a contest over cost, risk,
information, and control. These state records are not interchangeable:
Virginia records approved provisions, Texas records a proposed rule, and
Georgia supplies an evidentiary/accountability record about what data are needed
to assess large loads.

```text
large-load request
  -> rate class, financial commitment, cost allocation, and reporting rule
  -> utility / firm / existing-ratepayer risk and information change
  -> public legitimacy, local conflict, and state control
```

## Comparison

| State and record | Stage | Main mechanism | What it makes visible |
|---|---|---|---|
| Virginia SCC, Dominion PUR-2025-00058 and related initiatives | Approved framework described by SCC | GS-5 separate class; at least 14-year obligation for covered new contracts; minimum payment of 85% of transmission/distribution costs; collateral up to 60% of minimum charges; direct-connect cost assignment and future allocation proposals | Cost-shift protection, contract duration, credit risk, and direct infrastructure assignment |
| Texas PUC proposed §25.252 and related rules implementing SB 6 | Proposed rule, not final outcome | Financial security; direct cash contribution in aid of construction; at least 20-year interconnection agreement; billing when service is available; minimum billing demand; annual customer-class revenue reporting; reallocation if commitments fail | Long-term reservation risk, idle-capacity billing, financial security, and public monitoring |
| Georgia PSC Docket 44280 record | Evidentiary/accountability recommendation | Requests projected and actual peak/load/energy series, monthly minimum bills, RTP pricing, unit-level generation, cost-of-service studies, and allocation of forecast costs/revenues | The information required to test forecast accuracy, cost causation, and whether benefits/costs distribute across customer classes |

## What the comparison supports

### 1. The state response is moving from attraction to risk governance

The records do not only ask whether data centers bring jobs or investment.
They address minimum payment, collateral, direct construction contributions,
contract duration, class allocation, and data disclosure. Large-load policy is
therefore also a consumer-protection and public-accountability question.

### 2. Long-term commitments protect one actor while constraining another

Virginia's 14-year obligation and Texas's proposed 20-year billing framework
can protect utilities and existing customers from stranded capacity, but they
also bind the large-load customer and may affect entry, exit, competition, and
the ability of a place to change course. The policy record does not show how
often these terms will be enforced or what they will do to local development.

### 3. Transparency is itself a power resource

Georgia's record emphasizes that projected and actual load, generation,
minimum bills, and pricing histories are needed to evaluate cost allocation.
Without those records, a public hearing may contain claims about benefits and
burdens without enough evidence to assign responsibility. Disclosure therefore
belongs in the state-capacity and cultural-legitimacy analysis, not only in a
technical appendix.

### 4. Policy stage must remain separate from outcome

Virginia's provisions are an approved framework, Texas's are proposed rules,
and Georgia's record is a demand for better evidence. None proves lower
household bills, successful local capability, provider replaceability, or
political trust. Those require follow-up records.

## Arrow ledger

| Arrow | Current status | Remaining evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Large load → regulatory response | Observed in three state records | Complete docket chronology and final implementation |
| Regulatory terms → risk allocation | Specified/partly approved | Contracts, deposits, bills, actual load, defaults, and cost recovery |
| Disclosure → public accountability | Mechanism/evidentiary | Public data releases, hearing participation, corrections, and enforcement |
| Terms → local customer incidence | Open | Utility rate-class bills, upgrade recovery, reliability, and service quality |
| Terms → local capability | Open | Jobs, wages, suppliers, taxes, water/land, ownership, and durable skills |
| Governance → trust or political action | Open | Local attribution, hearings, organizing, polling, and subsequent policy change |
| Domestic control → state leverage | Open | Provider switch, refusal, negotiation, or changed external behavior |

## Next empirical extraction

For one Virginia utility and one Texas or Georgia utility, build a place-year
record with:

1. docket date and policy stage;
2. threshold and qualifying customer population;
3. contract/minimum-billing/collateral/CIAC terms;
4. requested, approved, available, and actual load;
5. upgrade cost, recovery source, rate class, and customer bills;
6. forecast versus actual load and revenue;
7. local jobs, wages, taxes, water/land/environmental effects;
8. hearings, stakeholder claims, public response, and subsequent amendments;
9. ownership, provider concentration, portability, and exit; and
10. a comparator with similar load growth but less explicit cost or data
    protection.

Do not label the state response successful until the terms are implemented and
the records show who actually paid, benefited, switched, or changed behavior.

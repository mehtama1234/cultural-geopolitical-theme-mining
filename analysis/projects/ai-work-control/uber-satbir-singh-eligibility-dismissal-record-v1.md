# Uber / Satbir Singh eligibility-dismissal record v1

**Decision:** [2026] FWC 409, *Satbir Singh v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd*
(`UDE2025/293`)

**Finding:** the application was dismissed at the statutory protection gate;
the Commission did not reach the merits of the alleged deactivation.

## Why this case matters

The restoration-heavy Australian packet needs a clean countercase showing where
the public route stops before explanation, human review, correction, or payment
can be tested. Singh is that countercase. It is not evidence that the platform's
underlying account restriction was fair. It is evidence that access to the
unfair-deactivation merits regime depends on a time-and-identity threshold.

## Primary record

The Commission recorded that Singh performed delivery work through Portier's
Uber delivery platform from 8 September 2023 until his final delivery on 9 May
2024. Part 3A-3 of the Fair Work Act commenced on 26 August 2024, and the
transitional rule excluded earlier work from the six-month calculation. Because
Singh performed no work after 9 May 2024, the Commission found that he was not
protected from unfair deactivation when the 9 May 2024 event occurred.

Singh also relied on September 2025 messages that appeared to say his account
had been reactivated so he could continue driving as an UberX driver. The
Commission treated the delivery and driver platforms as relevantly distinct:
the driver platform was operated by Rasier Pacific, Singh had never signed its
services agreement, and he had never completed driver trips. Even assuming the
later messages amounted to a 30 September 2025 driver-platform deactivation,
the required six-month period still was not established.

## Stage coding

```text
eligibility threshold       adjudicated — not met
explanation                 not reached
human review                not reached
correction / restoration    not reached
lost pay / compensation     not reached
post-order access           not applicable
```

The distinction is important for the atlas. A worker can have a genuine
grievance about account access and still never reach the forum's merits test.
Likewise, a message that appears to restore access can be operationally
ambiguous if it crosses platform entities, services agreements, or worker
categories. The observed dismissal should therefore not be summarized as a
merits finding for Uber's process.

## Cross-case use

Singh should be read alongside Jibril and Abukar as an eligibility endpoint,
and alongside Hotak and Rehman as a warning against collapsing “reactivated,”
“able to work,” and “qualifies for remedy” into one variable. The expanded
sixteen-row inventory now contains four eligibility endpoints, seven
merits-level restoration outcomes, and four public lost-pay amounts/orders or
quantum directions depending on whether the open-quantum cases are included.
Those categories remain descriptive of the bounded search frame, not a success
rate or prevalence estimate.

## Open acquisition

Acquire any later order, appeal, or platform record that clarifies whether the
September 2025 messages changed Singh's access to the delivery platform. The
current primary decision establishes the statutory gate and platform/entity
boundary, but no subsequent correction, restoration, payment, or durable access
outcome.

# Uber Eats / Kumar merits-dismissal record v1

**Status:** primary Fair Work Commission merits decision; deactivation upheld;
no remedy ordered  
**Checked:** 2026-09-15

## Why this record matters

This case is the clearest counterexample to the claim that proceduralized
platform governance necessarily produces worker restoration. Unlike Bakar,
the worker reached a merits decision. Unlike Bandameeda, the Commission found
the platform's process compliant and dismissed the unfair-deactivation claim.
The result shows that a usable remedy system can produce a reasoned refusal,
not only reactivation.

## Source and scope

In *Rahul Kumar v Portier Pacific Pty Ltd* ([2025] FWC 2275; UDE2025/68), the
Fair Work Commission held hearings on 21 and 28 July 2025 and issued its
decision on 5 August 2025. Mr Kumar was protected from unfair deactivation and
filed within time. Uber Eats had deactivated his access after his average
customer-satisfaction rating remained below Brisbane's 85% threshold.

Mr Kumar responded to the preliminary notice but did not request a discussion
with an Uber representative. The Commission found that a human member of
Uber's Community Operations team considered his response, reasonably decided
that no further inquiries were warranted, and applied a valid, known, and
objectively reasonable rating requirement.

## Evidence matrix

| Stage | Primary record | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Decision surface | Customer ratings below an 85% Brisbane threshold affected continued access to the Uber Eats app | The Commission addressed this rating-based decision; it does not establish how all ratings are generated or audited |
| Notice | Uber issued written warnings and a preliminary deactivation notice specifying the reason, risk, response period, and support/representation option | Notice compliance does not prove that the underlying ratings were substantively fair in every case |
| Worker response | Mr Kumar replied in writing and later requested a review; he did not request a representative discussion within the notice process | The absence of a requested discussion matters to this decision's application of the Code |
| Human review | The Commission found a human Community Operations representative considered the response and reasonably determined that no further inquiry was warranted | This is adjudicated evidence of a human review in this case, not proof of independent review or system-wide practice |
| Rating quality | The Commission acknowledged that individual thumbs-down ratings could be unfair or reflect matters outside the worker's control, but found the 85% threshold reasonable and the evidence sufficient in this case | The decision did not order correction of any rating or establish a general rating-appeal mechanism |
| Outcome | The Commission found Uber complied with the Deactivation Code and dismissed the application | No reactivation, lost-pay restoration, compensation, or other remedy was ordered |

## Remedy chain

```text
low customer-satisfaction ratings
  -> written warnings and preliminary deactivation notice
  -> worker written response / later review request
  -> human Community Operations consideration
  -> Commission finds notice, timing, review, and rating threshold compliant
  -> deactivation upheld
  -> no remedy
```

The case makes “human review” measurable in a way the Cambodia survey cannot:
the Commission identified the reviewing team and examined whether it considered
the response and whether further inquiry was reasonably required. It also
preserves an important tension: the Commission recognized that individual
ratings can be unfair while still finding the aggregate threshold and the
platform's process reasonable in this case.

## Comparative interpretation

```text
Bandameeda: process failure + no valid reason -> reactivation + lost pay
Kumar:      process compliance + valid rating rule -> dismissal, no remedy
Bakar:      eligibility finding -> settlement/revocation before merits
```

These cases show why the atlas must not count “review” as success or “no
remedy” as proof of institutional failure. The relevant questions are whether
the worker was eligible, what information was supplied, whether a human had
authority and actually considered the response, whether the rule and evidence
were reasonable, and what outcome followed.

## Boundary

This record establishes a dismissal of one unfair-deactivation application,
not proof that Uber's ratings are universally accurate or that all workers
receive equivalent process. It does not establish correction, compensation,
anti-retaliation, or long-term effects on Mr Kumar after the decision.

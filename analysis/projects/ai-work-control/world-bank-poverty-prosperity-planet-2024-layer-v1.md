# World Bank poverty, prosperity, and planet 2024 layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13 · **Status:** official World Bank global comparative extraction

## Why this layer matters

The IMF layer shows how geopolitical and fiscal shocks can move macro conditions. This World Bank layer adds the distributional and measurement context: where poverty is concentrated, whether shared prosperity is advancing, how climate risk threatens gains, and whether the evidence infrastructure is strong enough to see those changes. It is global comparison context for the US-centered atlas, not a US poverty estimate.

## Direct evidence

The World Bank reports that almost 700 million people—8.5% of the global population—lived below $2.15 per day in 2024, while about 3.5 billion people—44%—were below the $6.85-per-day line. Sub-Saharan Africa represented 16% of the global population but 67% of people in extreme poverty; including fragile and conflict-affected countries brings the share to about three quarters.

At the current trajectory, the Bank projects 622 million people, or 7.3% of the global population, will remain in extreme poverty in 2030, with nearly 40% below $6.85 per day. Progress in the Global Prosperity Gap stalled after the pandemic, and roughly one-fifth of the world's population lives in countries with high inequality.

Climate and evidence capacity are part of the same distributional problem: one in five people is at risk of an extreme-weather event in their lifetime, while fewer than half of countries had a household survey available for poverty monitoring in 2020 or later. Missing data are not missing poverty, but they limit the ability to target, compare, and evaluate policy.

## Mechanism under test

```text
growth, conflict, climate, and public-capacity conditions
  -> income, consumption, service, and risk distribution
  -> resilience, adaptation, and mobility
  -> legitimacy, migration, political demand, and state capacity
```

The report measures global distribution and identifies mechanisms. It does not observe the US household-to-politics or country-to-geopolitical-action arrows.

## Interpretation and limits

The useful contribution is to prevent global growth or national averages from standing in for shared prosperity. Poverty lines, inequality, climate exposure, and survey coverage describe different layers of vulnerability. The report also makes data infrastructure itself a governance condition: a country cannot reliably target or evaluate a response that it cannot measure.

The next test is to pair World Bank country context with IMF macro shocks, US/global trade and migration records, climate exposure, social protection, and local outcome data, keeping country, household, place, and policy units distinct.

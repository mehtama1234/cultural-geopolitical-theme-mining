# SIPRI military expenditure and state-capacity layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Unit:** national and alliance military expenditure  
**Window:** 2015–2024, with 2024 comparison  
**Status:** geopolitical resource-allocation baseline; not a leverage estimate

## What this adds

SIPRI reports that world military expenditure reached $2.718 trillion in 2024,
up 9.4% in real terms from 2023 and 37% from 2015. US expenditure reached
$997 billion, up 5.7% from 2023 and 19% from 2015. The United States remained
the largest spender, at 3.2 times China's expenditure. NATO members together
spent $1.506 trillion, or 55% of the world total; US spending was 66% of NATO
spending and 37% of world spending.

| Measure | 2024 value |
|---|---:|
| World military expenditure | $2.718 trillion |
| US military expenditure | $997 billion |
| NATO military expenditure | $1.506 trillion |
| World military burden | 2.5% of GDP |
| World military spending per person | $334 |

This is a resource and alliance-concentration baseline. It does not establish
readiness, industrial output, deterrence, alliance cohesion, domestic
legitimacy, household incidence, or an observed state bargaining outcome.
SIPRI's constant-dollar series is a trend indicator of resources used for
military activity; the US series is reported on a financial-year basis.

## End-to-end position

```text
security threat or alliance condition
  -> public resource allocation and procurement
  -> industrial capacity, technology, labor, and fiscal tradeoffs
  -> domestic burden, alliance dependence, and strategic options
  -> observed negotiation, deterrence, policy, or external response
```

The first two stages are now measured at aggregate level. The program still
needs procurement and industrial-base records, budget incidence, public
attribution, alliance decisions, and an observed external response before
calling higher expenditure geopolitical leverage.

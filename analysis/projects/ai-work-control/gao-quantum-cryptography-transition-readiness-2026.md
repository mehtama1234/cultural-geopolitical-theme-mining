# Quantum-risk exposure can precede cryptography inventories and migration

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** emerging quantum threat → federal cryptography-preparation gap; migration and incident outcomes remain open

GAO's reissued October 2026 public report adds a technology-dependence and
geopolitical-security route. A future cryptographically relevant quantum
computer could compromise some federal cryptography, expose sensitive
information, or decrypt data acquired and stored before the computer exists.
Experts' timing estimates vary, with some placing development as soon as the
2030s; current quantum computers cannot yet break the cryptography discussed.

GAO evaluated 24 Chief Financial Officer Act agencies against three
OMB-guided preparation practices. None fully addressed all three. The report
identifies gaps in cryptography expertise, inventories of vulnerable systems,
funding identification, and plans for post-quantum cryptography testing. The
sensitive report made 89 recommendations to 23 agencies; the public version
makes no additional recommendations and retains a redaction boundary after
coordination with agencies through September 2026.

The evidence boundary is material. The report does not establish the date or
capability of a cryptographically relevant quantum computer, system-level
vulnerability, completed inventories or funding, algorithm tests, migration,
supplier readiness, compromise, incident prevention, or public trust effects.

# Uber / Phillipps-Lewis eligibility and stay record v1

**Decision:** [2025] FWC 2398, *Jai Phillipps-Lewis v Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd*
(`UDE2025/94`); order `PR790745`

**Finding:** the Commission rejected Uber’s six-month regular-work objection,
then stayed the remaining proceedings pending the Hotak Full Bench decision.

## Why this case matters

The Australian case census now includes both sides of the statutory eligibility
gate. Singh shows an application dismissed before merits because the qualifying
work period was not established. Phillipps-Lewis shows that regular work can be
established through a recurring pattern even when the worker does not satisfy
the Code’s illustrative numerical thresholds.

This is not a merits or remedy result. It is a precise boundary record showing
that “regular work” is an interpretive eligibility question rather than a
mechanical 60-hour or three-day test.

## Primary record

Phillipps-Lewis had worked through Uber’s Driver Platform since January 2019.
Uber removed access from 25 May to 2 June 2025, after which he resumed work.
He sought payment for earnings allegedly lost during the interruption. Uber
objected that he was not protected because, in the six months before the
deactivation, he averaged 30.6 paid hours per month and 2.92 paid days per
week—below the Code’s illustrative 60-hour and three-day examples.

The Commission dismissed that objection. The worker’s evidence showed a
consistent Friday-to-Sunday pattern, with unavoidable variations for public
holidays, family obligations, and vehicle maintenance. The trip log supported
work in every week, always including the weekend. The Commission held that this
was a readily identifiable repetitive pattern and that the Code’s examples were
not necessary preconditions because the Code expressly does not limit other
circumstances in which regular work may be found.

The Commission did not decide whether the access interruption was a statutory
“deactivation,” whether Uber’s process was fair, or whether lost pay should be
ordered. It stayed the proceedings and directed relisting after the Hotak Full
Bench decision.

## Stage coding

```text
regular-work eligibility       adjudicated — objection dismissed
deactivation status             stayed / not decided in this record
explanation                    not reached
human review                   not reached
correction / restoration       access resumed, but no merits remedy ordered here
lost pay / compensation        claimed, not quantified or ordered here
post-order outcome             relisting direction observed
```

The resumed access is an observed operational fact, not proof that the original
interruption was lawful, that lost income was restored, or that future access
was durable.

## Cross-case use

Read this record with Singh, Jibril, and Abukar. Together they show that the
eligibility layer can produce different endpoints before the merits question:

- a worker may fail because the qualifying period cannot include pre-commencement
  work;
- a worker may qualify through a recurring pattern below the Code’s examples;
- a later reactivation may remain legally relevant while the merits and pay
  questions are unresolved.

The broader lesson is methodological: access, qualifying work, platform/entity
identity, deactivation status, and remedy must be stored as separate events.

# Capacity can grow before replaceability or public benefit is proven

**Status:** provisional broad-program synthesis  
**Checked:** 2026-09-21
**Scope:** data centers, large-load utility governance, local fiscal capacity, strategic procurement, and state leverage

## Research question

When a firm, county, utility, or state builds strategically important
infrastructure, does the new capacity create usable public and geopolitical
room—or does it make the place more dependent on a large customer, a supplier,
a contract, or an external technical system before benefits and exit are
observable?

The broad-program chain is:

```text
strategic demand or public objective
  -> capital, energy, land, contract, and institutional commitment
  -> physical or fiscal capacity
  -> local prices, services, jobs, risk, and control
  -> inspection, renegotiation, substitution, or provider exit
  -> legitimacy, bargaining power, state leverage, or geopolitical response
```

The current evidence is strongest through commitment, construction, fiscal
revenue, and rule design. It is weaker on who bears the cost, who receives the
usable benefit, and whether a place can switch or refuse without losing an
essential capability.

These layers are complementary, not a common capacity index. A tax receipt is
not a public service; a planned MW figure is not delivered electricity; a
contract is not an operational capability; and a local assembly announcement
is not replaceability.

## What the current evidence supports

### 1. Physical scale becomes a governance question when costs are shared

LBNL's national data-center estimates place electricity demand at 176 TWh in
2023 and 649 TWh in its 2030 reference case, with a 521–843 TWh scenario
range. Prince William County reports $293.7 million in TY2024 data-center tax
revenue and $465.9 million in TY2025, beside reported capacity expansion from
862 MW to 1,483 MW of turnkey capacity.

This establishes a useful scale-and-place architecture, not a net-benefit
estimate. Revenue growth mixes physical expansion, assessed value, equipment
cycles, classification, and a change in the computer-equipment tax rate. The
national load model cannot be allocated to the county, and county revenue does
not reveal utility, environmental, school, road, water, or public-safety cost.

The correct question is therefore **allocation**: who pays for connection,
reliability, land, water, and public services, and who can capture the fiscal
or technical benefit?

### 2. Rules can make dependence visible before they make it safe

Virginia, Texas, and Georgia records show states developing minimum-payment,
contract-length, collateral, direct-contribution, reporting, disclosure, and
cost-recovery mechanisms for large loads. These are real policy responses.

They do not yet prove that a large customer paid its attributable cost, that
residential bills changed by the modeled amount, that reliability improved, or
that a provider could be replaced. A filing, modeled rider, hearing, or
confidential contract is a different evidence stage from an approved rule,
implemented payment, default, renegotiation, or customer outcome.

```text
large-load proposal
  -> public docket and allocation rule
  -> approved contract, collateral, or tariff
  -> payment, load, reliability, and service outcome
  -> renegotiation, refusal, substitution, or exit
```

The public record is already good enough to study institutional learning and
contestation. It is not yet good enough to claim that cost shifting was
prevented or that local legitimacy was produced.

### 3. Fiscal capacity can increase dependence at the same time

Rapid data-center revenue can expand a county's room to fund services or lower
other taxes. It can also increase the political cost of refusing future
projects, disclose a dependence on a narrow tax base, or make the county more
exposed to equipment cycles and corporate location decisions.

The relevant measure is not revenue alone but **net optionality**:

```text
new revenue and jobs
  - service, infrastructure, environmental, and resilience cost
  - cost of replacing the customer or tax base
  + public control, transparency, and alternative uses
```

No current source fills all four terms. The fiscal series therefore supports a
capacity observation and a dependence hypothesis, not a local welfare claim.

### 4. Strategic procurement follows the same stage problem

Defense and strategic-technology records show the same pattern at state scale.
SIPRI and IMF identify resource allocation and fiscal trade-offs. USAspending
and official records identify contracts, recipients, production support, and
integration tests. Poland and other cooperation records identify intended
industrial or alliance commitments.

The unresolved middle is operational: accepted quantity, delivery, training,
maintenance, interoperability, local content, component control, and the
ability to substitute or refuse. A signed agreement or production-support
IDIQ can increase capability potential while leaving actual leverage open. The
current local realization audit contains 8 Poland/JASSM-ER events, including
2 explicitly labeled comparator rows; the additional broad delivered-
equipment comparator does not establish delivery or acceptance of the 2024
Polish order.

This is not a separate geopolitical story added after local economics. The
same questions recur: who owns the critical input, who can inspect it, who can
switch suppliers, who pays during delay, and what response proves that another
actor's options changed?

### 5. Industrial and disaster programs expose the same realization gap

The CHIPS and EDA evidence extends the comparison beyond private infrastructure
and defense procurement. CHIPS can show awards, disbursement, milestone
payments, workforce activity, and changing R&D institutions without yet showing
qualified chips, retained workers, customer supply, or lower foreign exposure.
EDA can show disaster-recovery appropriations, federal coordination missions,
roads, utilities, and facilities without validated evidence that local firms,
jobs, services, or households recovered. These are not the same program or
unit, but they share a state-capacity pattern:

```text
public objective
  -> appropriation and institutional vehicle
  -> award, disbursement, or project milestone
  -> operating capability and usable local function
  -> distribution, resilience, replaceability, or recovery
  -> legitimacy, bargaining room, or changed external behavior
```

The lesson is not that spending is ineffective. It is that a funding ledger
cannot answer the later questions by itself. Industrial policy needs a
project-year chain through qualified output and customers; disaster policy
needs a place-keyed chain through completion, firm/service function, jobs,
household access, and the next shock. Both should record governance resets,
delays, and alternative suppliers or routes rather than treating them as
administrative detail.

Critical minerals make the same rule visible at the material-input layer. A
domestic recovery prototype or semiconductor project can increase capability
while refined feedstock, qualification, processing, or recycling remains
externally concentrated. The relevant unit is therefore the
mineral-application-process, not “the mineral” or “domestic production” in the
abstract. No downstream firm, worker, consumer, or state response is promoted
without that application-specific bridge.

The newest records widen the same realization problem beyond construction and
procurement. The Clean Energy Connector and D.C. Solar for All show a public
benefit route entering a private billing system: eligibility, opt-in,
subscription management, and a required discount can be visible before a
household's actual credit, remaining bill, arrears, or continued participation.
The D.C. regulatory filing is a useful intermediate endpoint because selected
projects report matching subscriber and monthly-credit counts. It is still not
an individual bill record, so it cannot establish net savings or durable
security.

PJM adds the reverse direction. A large load actually disconnected and the
shared system responded before the final governance solution was established.
This is operating evidence, not merely a forecast, but it does not identify
facility-level causation or residential burden. NATO's Maven route adds a
strategic version: a digital capability can cross technical authorization and
exercise-use gates without demonstrating better decisions, changed adversary
behavior, or durable legitimacy. Across all three cases, implementation is a
real stage between promise and outcome; it is not the outcome itself.

The sharpest counterexample is **capacity without a usable second source**.
GAO/USGS evidence can show import reliance, China exposure, application-specific
substitution limits, and a plausible recycling or recovery route. It cannot by
itself show that a named firm received qualified material at scale, that the
material met purity/performance requirements at an acceptable cost, or that the
firm could switch during a disruption. A domestic award or prototype therefore
creates response capacity without proving replaceability. The missing geopolitical
endpoint is equally concrete: a supplier switch, negotiated concession, refusal,
substitution, or other dated change in an external actor's behavior. This is the
same realization boundary seen in data-center operation and defense delivery,
but the decisive unit is now the mineral-application-process-firm rather than a
county or procurement program.

## Mechanism ledger

| Arrow | Status | Safe conclusion | Decisive next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand/objective → commitment | Reported / observed | Firms and states are allocating capital, land, energy, and contracts | Named project/account with dates, terms, and responsible actors |
| Commitment → realized capacity | Partly observed | Some construction, revenue, testing, and integration milestones are visible | Energized/operating load, accepted output, reliability, delivery, and maintenance |
| Capacity → local/public benefit | Open / compared | Revenue, jobs, skills, or service potential can be identified | Net service cost, bills, wages, local procurement, environmental burden, and distribution |
| Capacity → dependence | Inferred / open | Concentration, long contracts, narrow tax bases, and external control are plausible mechanisms | Termination terms, alternative suppliers, portability, outage history, collateral, and actual switching |
| Governance → legitimacy | Open | Hearings, disclosure, and cost-allocation rules create contestation routes | Attributed participants, requested remedies, final changes, trust, and organized response |
| Domestic capability → geopolitical leverage | Open | Production, financing, and interoperability can create an option | Observed concession, refusal, substitution, deterrence, retaliation, or changed behavior |
| Public benefit route → durable household security | Partly observed | Program-level reach, savings, and project-level credit implementation can be reported | Account-level bill, credit timing, arrears, persistence, energy burden, and household choice |
| Operating disturbance → durable shared-system control | Partly observed | A real event can trigger operator and regulatory response | Facility attribution, final rule, compliance, payment, repeat-event performance, and public acceptance |
| Authorized digital capability → strategic leverage | Partly observed | Network authorization and exercise use establish an operating gate | Mission outcome, maintenance, operator/partner response, adversary adaptation, and replaceability |

## Provisional broad-program finding

> Strategic capacity can become visible before public benefit and replaceability
> are proven. A county may gain revenue, a utility may create a cost-allocation
> rule, and a state may sign a production or infrastructure agreement while the
> underlying system becomes more dependent on concentrated customers, external
> suppliers, long contracts, or opaque technical control.

This is not a claim that infrastructure investment is harmful or that public
rules fail. It is a testable distributional and political theme: capacity is
valuable only if the people and institutions relying on it can inspect,
benefit from, renegotiate, and replace the system at acceptable cost.

## Next matched-place and capability test

For a domestic infrastructure case, build a dated matched-place ledger for
Prince William and a comparison place. Record:

1. planned, approved, under-construction, energized, and actual operating load;
2. transmission, substation, water, land, road, and environmental requirements;
3. tariff, minimum payment, collateral, direct contribution, contract term,
   default, and cost-recovery fields;
4. assessed value, tax receipt, service cost, jobs, wages, vendors, and local
   procurement;
5. customer-class bills, reliability, outages, and public complaints;
6. hearing participants, attributed concerns, requested remedies, and final
   decision changes; and
7. ownership, portability, provider alternatives, renegotiation, and exit.

For a strategic procurement case, preserve authorization, agreement,
production, shipment, acceptance, training, inventory, maintenance,
interoperability, substitution, and external response as separate dated rows.
Do not promote leverage until a credible option and an observed response are
both present.

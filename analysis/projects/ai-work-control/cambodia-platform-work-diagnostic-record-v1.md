# Cambodia platform-work diagnostic record v1

**Status:** official mixed-method diagnostic; worker-reported platform remedy
experience observed; institutional outcome remains open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-15

## Why this record matters

Cambodia adds a Southeast Asian diagnostic to the atlas that connects automated
work organization, employment classification, social protection, and remedy
experience in one source. It is not a tribunal judgment or a platform case
file. Its value is different: it shows how a remedy route can be visible in
the app while workers still report weak resolution of serious problems.

## Source and scope

The ILO diagnostic was published on 2 September 2026. Rapid Asia conducted the
research with ILO technical guidance using a mixed-method design: 1,237 surveys
and 29 in-depth interviews with platform workers in Phnom Penh, plus 10 key
informant interviews with government, platform, and worker-organization or
trade-union representatives. The covered population was location-based
ride-hailing and food-delivery workers: tuk-tuk drivers, taxi drivers, and
delivery riders. The survey is exploratory and localized; it is not a national
prevalence estimate.

## Evidence matrix

| Dimension | Observed evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Automated management | Ratings affected earnings and task allocation for a large majority of respondents; refusal or cancellation could produce fewer bookings, lost priority, warnings, incentive loss, or temporary suspension | Worker reports describe effects; the source does not provide platform model documentation or a case-level decision trace |
| Suspension | 16% of delivery riders, 14% of tuk-tuk drivers, and 5% of taxi drivers reported temporary suspension; average suspension was 2.4, 3.3, and 3.3 days respectively | Reasons and durations are survey aggregates, not adjudicated findings |
| Complaint access | 78% of tuk-tuk drivers, 88% of delivery riders, and 96% of taxi drivers said they could file a platform complaint; 50%, 52%, and 36% respectively said they had used the service | Access is self-reported and does not identify reviewer authority or response deadlines |
| Resolution quality | Approximately two-thirds of workers who complained said their issue was seldom resolved; minor technical or payment issues were more likely to be resolved than serious accidents or theft | The study does not verify individual corrections, restoration, compensation, or retaliation outcomes |
| State route | The report notes Cambodia's 22 July 2026 Notification No. 1107 designating the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training to settle disputes between platform companies and workers | The diagnostic does not establish caseload, processing, decision, enforcement, or worker outcome under that route |
| Consultation | The ILO's 3 March 2026 tripartite validation workshop brought around 50 government, employer, platform, and worker-organization representatives together; participants called for a legal framework, social protection, recognized worker voice, transparency, and dispute-resolution mechanisms | Consultation and recommendations do not prove adoption, worker bargaining power, or later implementation |
| Classification and protection | Platforms mostly classify workers as independent contractors or partners; the report links this to gaps in labour-law coverage, collective bargaining, trade unionization, and compulsory social security. About half reported some social-security benefit, mostly voluntary NSSF healthcare | Classification and coverage are contextual findings; they do not establish an individual legal entitlement or denial |
| Safety | 34% of delivery riders, 21% of tuk-tuk drivers, and 13% of taxi drivers reported work-related injury | Injury is self-reported and does not itself prove platform liability |

## Remedy coding

```text
automated exposure                 observed_self_report
platform complaint channel         observed_self_report
state dispute authority             observed_official_action (reported in diagnostic)
explanation of a specific decision not_observed
independent human review           not_observed
correction or restoration          not_observed
payment or compensation            not_observed
anti-retaliation                   not_observed
social-security access             observed_self_report / contextual rule
tripartite consultation             observed_consultation
```

The report also documents a boundary between participation and control. A
tripartite workshop produced shared priorities, but the source does not show
that workers could veto, amend, or later invoke the platform rules discussed.
Likewise, one interviewed platform reported accident insurance that applied
only while a worker was actively carrying out a task—not during waiting time or
the trip to the pickup point—showing how a nominal protection can leave the
highest-risk intervals outside the remedy surface.

The central pattern is **procedural access without demonstrated recovery**.
Workers can often contact the platform, but contact does not equal a meaningful
review, and a reported resolution does not equal correction, restoration, or
payment. Cambodia also shows why classification belongs beside algorithmic
management: when workers are treated as independent contractors, the same
automated suspension or allocation decision lands in a thinner labour-rights
and social-security environment.

## Comparative interpretation

Cambodia extends the current comparison beyond Malaysia's statutory Tribunal
and the Philippines' platform-audit and state-dialogue routes:

```text
Cambodia: platform complaint access -> low reported resolution -> state route named -> outcome open
Malaysia: statute -> Tribunal established -> first hearing reported -> award open
Philippines: platform evidence -> formal appeal at selected platforms -> outcome open
```

The cases should not be ranked. They expose different institutional layers:
worker experience, platform process, state authority, and adjudicated outcome.
Together they support a sharper theme for the cultural-geopolitical atlas:
the geography of protection is shaped not only by whether a country adopts an
AI or platform rule, but by whether workers can convert a disputed decision
into a timely and auditable change in access to work, income, safety, or status.

## Decisive next acquisition

Acquire the text and implementation record for Notification No. 1107, the
Ministry's dispute-settlement caseload or decisions, and any platform-level
appeal records that identify a suspension, reviewer, reason, and outcome.
Separately test whether voluntary NSSF enrollment reaches platform workers in
practice and whether injury claims or occupational-risk coverage generate a
formal payment or restoration route.

As of 15 September 2026, a targeted search of the MLVT public portal and the
ILO publication trail did not locate a public copy of Notification No. 1107 or
a platform-worker dispute register. This is a retrieval gap, not evidence that
the notification or disputes do not exist. The next acquisition should use a
Khmer-language request or direct ministry/ILO document contact, then verify
the notification number, issuing date, covered parties, forum, filing route,
and any published decisions.

## Boundary

This record is a source-grounded diagnostic, not a national estimate, legal
opinion, or proof that every platform uses the same process. Self-reported
complaint access and resolution must remain separate from official dispute
decisions and verified worker outcomes.

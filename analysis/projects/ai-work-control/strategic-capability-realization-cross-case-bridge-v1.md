# Strategic capability realization across defense and digital infrastructure

**Status:** bounded cross-case bridge  
**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Cases:** Poland JASSM-ER procurement and US data-center capacity/governance

## The question

When a state or a public system commits resources to a strategically important
capability, which stage has actually been reached? A defense contract and a
data-center buildout are not the same object, but both can be described too
quickly as “capacity,” “independence,” or “power.” This bridge uses the two
realization ledgers to compare the stages without combining their quantities,
denominators, or outcomes.

## The shared realization chain

```text
strategic objective or demand
  -> authorization, funding, or agreement
  -> named entities, place, and capacity
  -> production, delivery, operation, or service realization
  -> maintenance, replaceability, and control
  -> affected public/household/partner outcomes
  -> external response, bargaining, or leverage
```

The key analytical rule is that movement along the chain is not automatic.
Each arrow needs its own event, source, date, unit, and boundary.

## What the two ledgers show

| Stage | Poland JASSM-ER | US data-center system | Safe cross-case interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Objective or demand | Poland states a long-range strike and deterrence need | National load studies and local expansion create electricity/capacity demand | Official purpose or modeled demand identifies a problem, not its solution |
| Authorization/agreement | 2024 possible-sale authorization and May 2024 agreement; July 2026 sensitivity enhancement notice | State utility commissions and local government adopt or file capacity/cost-allocation terms | An institutional commitment is observable before realized output |
| Named capacity/place | US program, supplier, and support records; customer-specific output remains open | Prince William reports 621 MW added, 1,483 MW turnkey capacity, 55 facilities, and $465.9M TY2025 revenue | A place or capacity record is stronger than an announcement but still does not prove usable service or public benefit |
| Delivery/operation | Shipment, acceptance, Polish unit receipt, training, inventory, and operational use are not publicly verified for the 2024 order | National 2030 load remains modeled; reported Virginia load-drop events are operational stress observations, not a service-output measure | Operation must be tied to the named object and event; projected or adjacent activity cannot substitute |
| Control/replaceability | Design authority, export permission, maintenance, and qualified switching remain open | Ownership, provider switching, feeder exposure, cost recovery, and portability remain open | Capacity can coexist with dependence on external firms, inputs, software, finance, or rules |
| Public/partner outcome | No observed changed alliance term, partner refusal, substitution, or deterrence response | No observed household bill, service, water/land, or public-legitimacy outcome joined to the facilities | Public value and leverage require an affected actor and a measured response |

## The important difference

The defense case is customer-realization constrained: the central missing event
is whether a specific foreign customer received, accepted, trained on, and can
operate the ordered object. The data-center case is incidence-and-control
constrained: some local capacity, revenue, policy response, and system stress
are visible, but the ledger cannot yet connect them to a specific customer,
household, feeder, owner, or replaceable service.

These are different missing joins. The defense case should not borrow a county
revenue measure as evidence of delivery. The data-center case should not borrow
a procurement agreement as evidence of operational load or public benefit.

## What “power” would require

The ledgers support a stronger power claim only when they identify:

1. a realized capability or service, not only an authorization or scenario;
2. the actor that controls continuation, maintenance, data, inputs, standards,
   finance, or export permission;
3. a credible alternative, termination route, or switching cost;
4. the public, household, worker, customer, or partner exposed to the result;
5. a dated response—acceptance, refusal, substitution, concession, organizing,
   rate change, service change, or other behavior; and
6. a counterexample showing that the response is not merely a general trend or
   the result of a different stage.

Without those fields, the evidence can still establish allocation, capacity
formation, institutional response, or operational stress. It cannot yet
establish autonomy, resilience, bargaining leverage, or geopolitical effect.

## Cultural and societal meaning remains a separate layer

The same material project can be interpreted as protection, modernization,
economic opportunity, dependence, fiscal subsidy, environmental burden, or
loss of control. Official records establish what institutions say and do;
they do not establish how residents, workers, customers, soldiers, voters, or
partner governments interpret the change. The next cultural join requires a
named population, exposure, attribution, timing, and later trust, action,
acceptance, opposition, or exit measure.

### Poland JASSM-ER

Locate an identifier-bearing shipment, acceptance, Polish unit receipt,
training, inventory, or operational-use record for the 2024 order. Preserve
the event type and date separately, then search for an external actor whose
behavior changed after that realization.

### US data-center system

account to interconnection, actual load, utility cost allocation, public

outcome. Preserve provider ownership and a successful or attempted switching

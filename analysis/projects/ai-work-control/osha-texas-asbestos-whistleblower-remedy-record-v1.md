# OSHA Texas asbestos whistleblower remedy record v1

**Status:** agency-reported whistleblower order; worker voice and formal remedy
are observed; payment, reinstatement, and durability remain open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The episode

After Hurricane Beryl, two workers on a Houston hotel-repair project raised
concerns about missing asbestos training and certification, inadequate personal
protective equipment, and illegal asbestos dumping. The U.S. Department of
Labor's Occupational Safety and Health Administration reports that the workers
were fired after raising those concerns. OSHA found the terminations retaliatory
under the Clean Air Act, the Solid Waste Disposal Act, and the Toxic Substances
Control Act, and ordered the companies to reinstate both workers and pay more
than **$200,000** in back wages and interest, plus compensatory and punitive
damages.

```text
post-disaster hazardous work

  -> terminations

  -> payment, return to work, protection from recurrence, household recovery, and trust [open]
```

## What this adds

This case connects worker voice to public safety and institutional power. The
workers' action was not simply a private wage dispute: it concerned hazardous
exposure and possible environmental harm after a disaster. The employer's
response allegedly shifted the cost of raising the concern onto the workers
through termination; the public enforcement response attempted to shift that
cost back through reinstatement and monetary relief.

It remains an agency-reported order involving two workers, not proof that the
money was paid or that either worker returned to a safe, durable job. The
public release does not report the workers' names, payment dates, individual
allocations, later employment, household effects, recurrence, trust, or exit.

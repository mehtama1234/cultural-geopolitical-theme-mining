# Comparison bridge: platform control and workforce allocation

**Status:** first pass; descriptive and provisional  
**Last checked:** 2026-09-11  
**Project:** AI, work, and control

## Why compare these firms

Microsoft and Whoz sit at different points in the workplace-AI chain. Microsoft describes a large cloud and software platform with agents, identity, security, governance, and management tools. Whoz describes a workforce platform that maps skills, matches talent to projects, and helps schedule and allocate people.

The comparison helps us ask where control enters: at the infrastructure and identity layer, or at the daily assignment and evaluation layer.

## What the firms say

| Firm | Public description | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft | Its 2025 annual report describes Copilot products, agents, Azure AI Foundry, identity and security systems, more than 400 datacenters, and more than 100 million monthly Copilot users. | Annual report; company-reported |
| Whoz | Its official site describes AI systems that map skills, match talent to projects, schedule resources, forecast demand, and support workforce decisions. It reports 800,000+ users, 115+ countries, and 50M+ skills managed. | Company website; product and marketing claims |

Sources: [Microsoft 2025 Annual Report](https://www.microsoft.com/investor/reports/ar25/), [Whoz](https://www.whoz.com/en/), [Whoz workforce optimization](https://www.whoz.com/en/solutions-large-entreprise/).

## The control comparison

| Layer | Microsoft | Whoz | Question to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is represented? | Documents, messages, code, business data, identities, and agents. | Skills, availability, aspirations, projects, rates, and assignments. | Which parts of a person’s work become machine-readable? |
| What can the system do? | Generate, search, analyze, and orchestrate work through enterprise tools. | Recommend matches, allocate people, forecast demand, and track resource use. | Does the system assist a choice or narrow the choices that appear? |
| Where might power move? | Toward the platform’s data, identity, security, and governance defaults. | Toward the platform’s skill profiles, matching logic, and staffing visibility. | Who can inspect, correct, or appeal the underlying profile or recommendation? |
| What is visible publicly? | Scale, infrastructure, product reach, and workforce totals. | Product capabilities, customer examples, and self-reported platform metrics. | Which claims have independent outcome evidence? |

## First-principles inference

```text
work becomes data -> data becomes a profile or workflow -> profile/workflow guides decisions
-> repeated use changes what managers and workers can see -> control may move to the owner of the system
```

This is a hypothesis, not a finding. A system can increase worker choice if it reveals hidden opportunities, or reduce choice if its scores and defaults become hard to challenge.

## Link to the research findings

- Finding 001 asks whether work structure changes before pay.
- Finding 002 asks whether AI opens movement between tasks or occupations.
- Finding 003 asks whether explanation and recourse shape how workers respond to control.

Microsoft tests the infrastructure side of those questions. Whoz tests the allocation side. Neither public source yet shows worker-level effects on pay, promotion, stress, bargaining, or appeals.

## Evidence quality warning

Microsoft’s annual report is a regulated company disclosure, but its product reach and AI benefits are still company-described. Whoz’s site is useful for understanding the product’s intended control surface, but its user counts, skill counts, and performance claims are marketing evidence. We will not use either as causal proof without independent outcome data.

## Next tests

1. Find a customer’s filing or public case record that describes actual workforce use.
2. Compare a worker’s profile before and after AI-supported allocation.
3. Look for rules on data correction, recommendation overrides, appeals, and consent.
4. Compare platform-recommended movement with worker-chosen movement.
5. Add a public-sector or regulated workplace where accountability rules are visible.

# Worker control is a layered option structure, not a single job-quality measure

**Status:** provisional cross-source synthesis  
**Checked:** 2026-09-18  
**Scope:** US labor mobility and AI-control evidence joined to comparative
worker-remedy records; no same-worker or pooled effect estimate

## The question

When workers face a changing labor market and algorithmic or AI-mediated work,
what determines whether they can refuse, switch, contest, or recover? The
retained evidence supports four distinct option layers:

```text
outside options -> workplace discretion -> contestability/remedy -> practical exit
```

These layers can reinforce one another, but none is a proxy for the next. A
worker may have task discretion without a credible alternative employer, a
formal appeal without timely income restoration, or a new job without having
chosen it freely.

## What the evidence shows

| Layer | Retained evidence | Safe conclusion | Missing bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outside options | 2025 SHED: among adults 18–29, inability to find work contributed to not working for 15%; layoffs were 7%, voluntary exits 8%, and new-job starts 13% among the reported population measures | Entry friction and movement events are visible; lower exits may reflect fewer attractive alternatives, satisfaction, or both | Same-worker job-search effort, offers, separation date, earnings, benefits, and later movement |
| Workplace discretion | SHED: generative-AI use was 30% among employees with high control over task completion versus 20% among those with less control; use was 10% for high-school-or-less workers versus 43% for graduate-degree workers | Tool access and discretion are patterned; this is not a productivity or welfare estimate | Employer permission, task change, time saved, monitoring, pay, schedule, and worker interpretation |
| Contestability | Comparative records include judicial disclosure, statutory review, workplace co-governance, collective bargaining, agency intervention, reactivation, compensation orders, and administrator-reported distributions | Institutions can expose, review, correct, compensate, or constrain a system at different moments | Worker confirmation, authority to change the consequence, payment/access receipt, recurrence prevention |
| Practical exit | Selected Fair Work cases record unsuccessful search, lower-paid replacement work, substitute earnings, non-return, or payment without reinstatement | Replacement, non-return, and continued dependence are distinct adaptations | Voluntariness, job duration/equivalence, household recovery, durable protection, and later action |

The records therefore support a narrower proposition:

> When outside mobility is less visible or less credible, workplace discretion
> and remedy timing become more important parts of practical worker power—but
> the current evidence does not identify the same worker across those stages.

## Mechanism map

```text
labor-market friction or job loss
  -> credible alternatives may narrow
  -> bargaining position and willingness to contest may change
  -> workplace control shapes access to tools and ability to adapt
  -> a review or remedy route may restore information, access, or money
  -> replacement, non-return, continued dependence, or exit may follow
  -> household recovery, trust, collective action, and geopolitical meaning remain open
```

This is a mechanism map, not a causal chain. SHED supplies population-level
employment and control-stratified measures. The remedy cases supply named
institutional episodes. They have different units, dates, and selection rules.

## The most useful counterexamples

The remedy records prevent “formal protection” from becoming a single score:

- A worker can obtain alternative employment at lower pay and with higher
  travel costs after a dismissal; adaptation is visible, but recovery is not.
- Another worker can search unsuccessfully, receive a compensation order, and
  remain without documented replacement work; a remedy does not imply an
  available alternative.
- A worker can refuse reinstatement because the disputed work-allocation
  mechanism remains unacceptable; non-return is an action signal, not a
  standardized trust or exit rate.
- A compensation award can be paid while reinstatement does not occur; money
  and restored opportunity are separate outcomes.
- A platform worker can regain access while a lost-remuneration order remains
  unpaid or otherwise unverified; access restoration and financial restoration
  are separate clocks.

These are selected case counterexamples, not prevalence estimates. Their value
is diagnostic: they show why “stayed,” “switched,” “appealed,” “compensated,”
and “recovered” must be coded separately.

## Arrow ledger

| Arrow | Status | What can be said now | Decisive next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labor-market friction → fewer credible alternatives | Compared / inferred | SHED movement measures are consistent with constrained mobility | Same-worker separation, search, offers, and accepted job |
| Workplace discretion → AI access/use | Reported association | AI use differs by reported task-completion control | Employer policy and same-worker task/use change |
| System decision → review or remedy route | Observed in selected cases | Courts, agencies, councils, statutes, and tribunals create different contestability stages | Named intervention with authority, changed decision, and traceable implementation |
| Remedy → restored access or money | Partly observed | Orders, reactivation, and administrator distribution advance institutional stages | Worker-confirmed receipt, timing, net amount, and continued access |
| Restoration → practical recovery | Open | No retained source closes household security, trust, or durable work outcome | Same-unit follow-up with protected and sacrificed outcomes |
| Adaptation → exit or collective action | Open / case-signaled | Replacement, non-return, continued work, and appeal are distinct behaviors | Later movement, organizing, political action, or verified non-use with alternatives |

## What this changes in the 14-theme atlas

This bridge links worker control to material security without collapsing them:

- **Work and labor:** mobility is an option set, not merely a quit rate.
- **Technology and infrastructure:** AI access depends partly on workplace
  discretion and organizational permission, not only individual skill.
- **Consumer/platform power:** a review right matters only if it can alter the
  consequence before the threatened resource is lost.
- **Culture and meaning:** non-return or continued use cannot be translated
  into trust without attribution and alternatives.
- **Geopolitical/state power:** institutions that can inspect, correct, pay,
  and prevent recurrence provide more usable sovereignty than formal rules
  that cannot be invoked or enforced.

## Boundary and next test

The project still lacks the decisive same-worker record linking job event,
employer AI policy, task control, schedule, pay, benefit, remedy, and later
movement. That is an acquisition target, not a license to join SHED to the
case records by topic or date.

The smallest useful next artifact is one lawful worker or workplace episode
with: (1) a dated adverse or changing work decision; (2) outside alternatives
and search effort; (3) task and schedule control; (4) review authority; (5)
money/access restoration; and (6) a later work, household, collective, or exit
outcome. Until that exists, this synthesis should be used as a stage map and
counterexample register, not as a worker-power index.

# Source record: EU Platform Work Directive algorithmic-management safeguards

**Checked:** 2026-09-15  
**Source family:** official EU legal record  
**Use:** external legal comparator for transparency, human oversight, review, and data limits in platform work

## Source

[Directive (EU) 2024/2831 on improving working conditions in platform work](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2831/oj), adopted 23 October 2024 and published in the Official Journal on 11 November 2024.

## What the directive establishes

- The directive promotes transparency, fairness, human oversight, safety, and
  accountability in algorithmic management in platform work.
- Its algorithmic-management data protections apply to persons performing
  platform work in the EU, including people without an employment relationship;
  employment-specific rights have a narrower scope.
- Article 7 limits automated processing of sensitive or inappropriate data,
  including emotional or psychological state, private conversations, data
  collected outside offered/performed work, prediction of fundamental-rights
  exercise, sensitive personal attributes, and certain biometric identification.
- Article 8 treats this processing as high-risk for data-protection purposes,
  requires an impact assessment, and requires platforms to seek the views of
  platform workers and their representatives and provide the assessment to
  representatives.
- Article 9 requires written, accessible information about automated monitoring
  and decision systems, the categories of data and decisions, main parameters,
  and grounds for decisions that materially affect platform workers.
- The directive requires human oversight and human review of significant
  decisions. Decisions restricting, suspending, or terminating an account or
  contractual relationship must be taken by a human.
- Affected people can request an explanation, discuss the decision with a human
  contact, request review, and receive a substantiated reply without undue
  delay and within two weeks at the latest. Rights-infringing decisions must be
  rectified or otherwise remedied, with steps to prevent recurrence.
- Health and safety provisions require assessment and mitigation of physical,
  psychosocial, and ergonomic risks from automated systems.

## Mechanism relevance

```text
automated monitoring or decision
  -> notice and data limits
  -> human oversight and explanation
  -> review, correction, or remedy
  -> possible system modification or discontinuation
```

This is a legal control architecture that directly covers several AIM-WORK
functions: task assignment, evaluation, working time, earnings, training,
promotion/status, and contractual access. It gives “human oversight” more
content than a general policy statement by attaching it to authority, review,
timing, and correction.

## Boundary

The directive is a platform-work legal comparator, not a worker-outcome study
and not a general rule for every ordinary workplace represented in AIM-WORK.
It does not show implementation, compliance, enforcement capacity, appeal
success, reduced stress, restored earnings, or whether platform workers can
exercise the rights without retaliation. National transposition and actual
institutional practice must be checked separately.

## Relation to the atlas

The directive supplies the strongest formal benchmark currently in this lane
for the difference between a stated safeguard and an executable remedy. The
next test is to find a dated platform decision, review request, correction, or
system change and connect it to the affected person’s work, earnings, health,
and exit options.

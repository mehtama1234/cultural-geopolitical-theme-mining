# China gallium and germanium export-control licensing event v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-18
**Scope:** China export-control decision; gallium and germanium material and
semiconductor supply-chain exposure
**Status:** dated policy and licensing event; downstream US material impact is
not established

## Event chain

```text
2023-07-03 announcement
  -> 2023-08-01 implementation
  -> covered gallium and germanium materials require export permission
  -> exporters must provide contract, technical, end-user, and importer data
  -> by 2023-11-30 MOFCOM reported that some applications had been approved
  -> August–October 2023 shipment and price response was observed
  -> US firm redesign, inventory sufficiency, and production effect remain open
```

China's Ministry of Commerce and General Administration of Customs announced
that, from August 1, 2023, specified gallium and germanium items could not be
exported without a licence. The list reaches beyond raw metal: it includes
gallium nitride, gallium oxide, gallium arsenide, indium gallium arsenide,
germanium substrates, germanium dioxide, and germanium tetrachloride. The
application requires an export contract, technical description or test report,
end-user and end-use proof, and importer and end-user information.

This is a control-surface change, not evidence of a permanent blanket ban. The same
announcement says qualifying exports can be licensed, and the November 30,
2023 MOFCOM press conference reported that some gallium and germanium export
applications had been approved and that some companies had received dual-use
export licences.

The USITC's March 2024 executive briefing adds a dated market response. Chinese
customs data reported in that briefing show no wrought gallium or germanium
exports in August 2023, no wrought gallium and only 1 kg of wrought germanium
in September, and 227 kg of gallium and 590 kg of germanium in October. July
exports had been 6,876 kg of gallium and 7,965 kg of germanium. The same source
reports gallium at 1,975 yuan per kg in October, 18% above July; global gallium
prices were reported up 68% since July, and germanium prices were up 21% from
January 1 by December 13.

## What is genuinely interesting

The chokepoint is not just geological extraction. The controlled list includes
processed and semiconductor-adjacent forms, so the lever sits at the boundary
between material supply and usable technical input. A country can therefore
permit some shipments while still increasing transaction time, disclosure,
screening, and uncertainty for downstream buyers.

The event also shows why “export control” and “supply cutoff” must be separate
states in the atlas. The official record supports a new permission gate and a
later statement that some applications were approved. The USITC data support a
sharp early export contraction and price movement. They still do not show how
many applications were filed, how quickly licences arrived, which foreign
buyers were denied, or whether US firms experienced shortage, inventory
depletion, redesign, or production effects.

## Atlas coding

| Stage | Evidence status | Coding boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | reported | China announced control on July 3, 2023 |
| Exposure | reported | specified gallium and germanium items require permission from August 1 |
| Administrative friction | reported | technical, end-user, importer, and contract information enters the application |
| Permission response | reported | MOFCOM said some applications had been approved by November 30 |
| Shipment effect | observed/reported | Chinese customs data show a sharp August–October contraction in wrought exports |
| Price effect | observed/reported | USITC briefing reports gallium and germanium price increases after the announcement |
| US inventory sufficiency | open | USITC identifies a germanium stockpile but no comparable gallium stockpile |
| Firm redesign or production effect | open | no named US firm response or output change established |
| Geopolitical concession or alliance response | open | policy leverage is plausible, but realized bargaining effect is not observed here |

The correct next join is a named material-application case: a US or allied
buyer, a specific covered product, an application date and decision, and a
downstream shipment or inventory measure. A later source should also test
whether the buyer had a validated alternative and whether that alternative
changed cost, performance, or delivery time.

# Uber / Kyei timing-extension record v1

**Decision:** [2025] FWC 2269, *Kwasi Kyei v Rasier Pacific Pty Ltd*
(`UDE2025/105`)

**Finding at original stage:** the Commission extended the filing period and
allowed the case to proceed to directions. A later merits decision, [2025]
FWC 3550, dismissed the unfair-deactivation application after finding process
deficiencies but accepting the safety-related reason for deactivation.

## Why this case matters

Kyei adds a timing-and-case-management endpoint to the Australian census. It
shows that the statutory 21-day filing period is a substantive access gate, but
not an automatic merits bar. The Commission found exceptional circumstances
despite a 60-day delay because Kyei challenged the deactivation, sought an
internal review, and had an arguable case that the platform process was
deficient.

## Primary record

Kyei had worked as an Uber driver since approximately 2014. The Commission
accepted Uber’s position that the deactivation took effect on 19 March 2025,
rather than the later date suggested by Kyei. The application was filed on 8
June 2025, outside the 21-day statutory period.

The Commission found that Kyei knew of the deactivation and had the benefit of
the filing period, and that lack of knowledge of the limitation period alone
would not be enough. But the full set of factors amounted to exceptional
circumstances: Kyei disputed the allegations soon after receiving them, asked
to be heard, pursued the respondent’s internal review, and presented an
arguable case that the decision was based on limited information without
substantive input on recent allegations. The extension was granted and the
matter was to be relisted with directions.

## Stage coding

```text
lost pay / compensation       open

```

The extension was not a finding that Uber acted unfairly. The later decision
resolved the merits against the applicant, while separately finding that the
requested discussion was not provided and that further inquiry was warranted.

## Cross-case use

Kyei should be read with Waheed, Jibril, Singh, and Phillipps-Lewis. Together,
these cases show that access to the merits route can be shaped by:

- when the worker reasonably became aware of deactivation;
- whether pre-commencement work can count toward the statutory period;
- whether platform and contract identity match the alleged access restriction;
- whether the work pattern is regular even below illustrative thresholds; and
- whether delay, internal review, and an arguable Code issue justify an extension.

The practical implication is that a remedy ledger must preserve timing and
case-management events, rather than counting only final merits decisions.

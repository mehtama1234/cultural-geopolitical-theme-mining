# OFR 2025 public financial-capacity and AI-work layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Source:** Office of Financial Research FY2025 Annual Report to Congress  
**Unit:** federal institution, workforce, budget, data infrastructure, and
financial-stability monitoring functions  
**Status:** official institutional self-report; not an impact evaluation

## Why OFR matters

OFR connects the program's AI/work question to public analytical capacity. Its
annual report describes AI and cloud infrastructure being used while the
organization narrowed its mission and reduced its workforce and budget. The
same report describes new financial-market data collection and monitoring.
This creates a useful institutional tension: apparent efficiency can coexist
with lower staffing, changed priorities, and different forms of capacity.

## What the report says

For FY2025, OFR reports a workforce path of 188 employees at the start, 205 in
January 2025, and 109 at year end—a reported 47% decline from the starting
point. It reports a 30% deferred-resignation participation rate and an 11%
budget reduction. The report attributes the broader organizational change to
mission priorities, rebalanced responsibilities, and reductions in travel,
contractors, technology/training investment, research engagement, and data
procurement; it does not attribute the workforce change to AI alone.

OFR reports AI use in data processing, security monitoring, support tasks,
procurement comparison, and ChatOFR. It decommissioned the Joint Analysis Data
Environment while retaining the OFR Analytics Environment and described updated
cloud services and an analytics operating system.

The report also says new non-centrally cleared bilateral repo data made a
previously opaque market segment visible to regulators, describing roughly $5
trillion in daily outstanding commitments. This is data-capacity evidence, not
proof that risk was eliminated.

## The institutional chain

```text
mission and budget decision
  -> staffing, AI tools, cloud/data infrastructure
  -> analytical workload and monitoring capacity
  -> risk identification, public accountability, and market confidence
```

The report directly establishes organizational changes and reported use cases.
It does not measure workload per employee, model accuracy, response time,
analytical quality, staff wellbeing, or downstream financial stability.

## The deeper finding

OFR shows why “automation increases capacity” needs a denominator. A system may
process more data or automate support while employing fewer people. That can
mean genuine augmentation, a narrower mission, greater reliance on systems, or
less human review. The public value depends on what work is retained, what is
removed, what errors can be corrected, and whether the institution remains able
to explain decisions.

The data story has the same structure. A new collection can make a hidden market
more legible to regulators, but visibility is not intervention. The market can
remain large, concentrated, or vulnerable even after measurement improves.

## Counterinterpretations

- Workforce reduction may reflect administrative reprioritization rather than
  automation.
- AI tools may augment remaining staff rather than replace analytical work.
- Decommissioning JADE may reduce duplication while OFRAE preserves core
  capacity, or it may reduce interagency collaboration; the report does not
  quantify either result.
- New data may improve supervision without preventing a disruption.
- A large repo segment is not automatically a financial-instability finding.

## What remains open

The report is an institutional self-report. It does not establish causal AI
productivity, public-service quality, staffing adequacy, employee experience,
vendor dependence, data portability, or household/firm financial outcomes. The
workforce and budget changes also occur alongside policy and organizational
changes, so they cannot be read as an AI effect.

## Next test

Compare OFR staffing, workload, data coverage, response times, error/correction
events, platform dependencies, and monitoring outputs across annual reports.
Pair agency capacity with the financial-stability risks it tracks and with
public transparency, interagency use, and market outcomes. For AI use cases,
record the task, human review, accuracy, correction route, saved or added
work, and accountability owner. Keep institutional capacity, employee welfare,
and market stability as separate endpoints.

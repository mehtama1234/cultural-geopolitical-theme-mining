# DOJ IER DACA rehire, wage restoration, and seniority record v1

**Status:** agency-reported bilateral resolution; accepted individual remedy
and restored employment terms are reported; long-term durability is open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The episode

In November 2020, a DACA recipient reported that citizenship-status and
document-practice problems led to her termination. DOJ's Immigrant and
Employee Rights Section reports that she was rehired several weeks later, but
at a lower hourly wage. On March 19, 2021, she signed a bilateral agreement
under which the employer restored her prior hourly wage, paid **$4,712.50 in
back pay**, and restored her seniority. She withdrew her submission, and IER
closed it on March 30, 2021.

```text
document/citizenship-status problem
  -> termination and lower-wage rehire
  -> worker submits a complaint to IER
  -> bilateral agreement
  -> prior wage restored + $4,712.50 back pay + seniority restored
  -> complaint withdrawn; later durability, household recovery, trust, and exit [open]
```

## What this adds

This is a stronger remedy-stage case than an agency summary that merely says a
payment was made. The public account reports a worker action, a signed
bilateral agreement, restored wage terms, payment, and restored seniority in
one episode. It also shows why “rehired” is not enough: the first rehire still
left the worker at a lower hourly wage until the agreement repaired the wage
and status position.

The record does not establish the payment instrument or date, the length of
the lower-wage period, whether the worker remained employed, whether the
restored seniority changed benefits in practice, or what happened to household
security, trust, recurrence, or exit.

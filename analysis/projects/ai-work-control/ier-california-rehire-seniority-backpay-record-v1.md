# DOJ IER California rehire, seniority, and back-pay record v1

**Status:** agency-reported individual intervention; restoration and status
repair observed in the official account, independent receipt and durability
open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The episode

On January 11, 2023, a U visa holder in California contacted the Department of
Justice Immigrant and Employee Rights Section after being terminated because
the company believed she needed a new Employment Authorization Document. The
official account says that her properly filed adjustment-of-status receipt,
combined with her Form I-94, was acceptable Form I-9 documentation. IER
contacted the employer and explained the documentation rule.

The employer then reportedly:

- rehired the worker;
- paid **$1,650 in back pay**; and
- restored her seniority date for benefits purposes.

The chain is unusually concrete:

```text
document misunderstanding
  -> termination and lost work
  -> worker contacts IER
  -> IER explains the valid document route
  -> rehire + reported back pay + seniority restoration
  -> payment receipt, benefit use, durable employment, trust, and exit [open]
```

## What this adds

This case shows that a remedy can repair more than immediate access or cash.
Seniority is a time-and-status asset: it can affect benefits, continuity, and
future workplace position. That makes it different from a simple back-pay
payment and useful for the atlas's broader question about control, security,
and institutional legitimacy.

It remains an agency account, not a worker interview or payment audit. The
public record does not state the payment instrument, receipt date, whether the
worker accepted or sustained reemployment, whether benefits were actually
restored, or what happened to household security, trust, recurrence, or exit.

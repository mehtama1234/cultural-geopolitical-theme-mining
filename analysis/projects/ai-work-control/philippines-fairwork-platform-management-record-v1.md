# Philippines Fairwork platform-management record v1

**Status:** comparative platform audit; formal appeal visibility observed;
individual remedy and worker representation outcomes open  
**Checked:** 2026-09-15

## Why this record matters

The Philippines provides a Southeast Asian comparator to Malaysia's statutory
Tribunal route. Instead of asking whether a national dispute institution has
heard a case, the Fairwork Philippines assessment asks whether platforms can
evidence fair pay, conditions, contracts, management, and representation. This
reveals a different governance stage: a platform may have an appeal process on
paper while workers still lack collective representation and the research does
not show what an appeal changes.

## Source and scope

The Oxford Internet Institute's Fairwork Philippines 2022 assessment, produced
with De La Salle University Manila, evaluated nine platforms using evidence
against five fairness principles. The published project page identifies the
platforms as Angkas, Borzo, GrabCar, Grab Food/Express, Foodpanda, Joyride,
Lalamove, TokTok, and Transportify. The ratings concern evidence supplied or
available to the assessment; they are not a random worker survey and do not
produce a population estimate of all Philippine platform workers.

## Evidence matrix

| Dimension | Published assessment | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Fair-pay evidence | None of the nine platforms demonstrated a guarantee that workers earned at least the national minimum wage after costs | This is an evidence standard in the assessment, not a measured wage distribution |
| Fair conditions | Four platforms—GrabCar, GrabFood/Express, Angkas, and Lalamove—showed sufficient protection from task-related risks | Protection evidence is not proof that every worker received or used the protection |
| Fair contracts | Four platforms showed evidence of clear and accessible contracts or terms | Contract clarity does not establish bargaining equality or actual comprehension |
| Fair management | Only GrabCar and GrabFood/Express evidenced a formalized process for workers to appeal decisions | A formal route does not show appeal volume, review quality, correction, or timeliness |
| Fair representation | None of the nine platforms evidenced collective representation of workers | This is a platform-assessment finding, not proof that no worker organization exists outside the platform |
| Platform comparison | GrabCar and GrabFood/Express scored highest with 3/10; Angkas and Lalamove scored 2/10 | Scores summarize five principles and should not be read as a remedy rate |

The assessment is particularly useful because it separates management from
representation. The presence of an appeal channel at two platforms does not
answer whether workers can influence the rules that generate assignments,
prices, incentives, ratings, or sanctions.

## Comparative interpretation

The Philippines and Malaysia show two different visibility regimes:

```text
Philippines: platform evidence -> formal appeal at selected platforms -> outcome open
Malaysia:    statute -> Tribunal established -> hearing and filings reported -> award open
```

Neither route yet closes the correction chain. The Philippines record makes the
absence of collective representation visible at the platform-assessment level;
Malaysia makes a public dispute institution visible at the state level. A
future comparison should test whether the state route can deliver remedies
where platform-level appeal is formal but weak, and whether worker associations
can convert individual appeals into collective rule change.

## Boundary

This record supports a country-level comparison of platform-management and
representation evidence. It does not establish a worker-level appeal outcome,
platform-wide compliance, or the absence of worker organizing outside the
platforms assessed.

**Status:** primary order retrieved; enforceable payment deadline observed,

**Checked:** 2026-09-18

## Result

The official Fair Work Commission search and document route now provide the
primary reactivation and lost-pay order for Rehman, rather than relying only on
the bulletin summary. The order requires Portier Pacific to reinstate access if
it has not already done so and to pay **A$7,096.96 gross within 14 days**.

The companion decision records that access had already been voluntarily
reactivated on 20 February 2026, that Portier had offered A$5,500 for a
settlement and discontinuance, and that the matter did not resolve. On the
evidence before the Commission, no paid work had been performed after
deactivation to the hearing. These facts distinguish voluntary restoration,
settlement offer, formal enforceability, and payment receipt.

## Stage distinction

```text
background-check deactivation
  -> voluntary access restoration
  -> settlement offer [not accepted as payment]
  -> formal reactivation order
  -> A$7,096.96 gross lost-remuneration order + 14-day deadline
  -> [open] payment receipt, continued work, net recovery, durable access
```

This is a stronger remedy-stage record, not a completed recovery episode. The
order does not establish that Portier paid, that the worker continued working,
that the background-check record was corrected, or that the worker's household
security improved.

## Why this matters

The order makes enforceability a distinct stage between institutional decision
and lived recovery. It also preserves a counterexample to a simple access
story: access can be voluntarily restored while a formal order is still needed
to reconstruct the worker's legal and financial position. A settlement offer is
not equivalent to the adjudicated amount, and either is distinct from receipt.

# Finding 001: National-lab partnership work can build capacity while competing for it

**Status:** bounded U.S. nuclear-security, infrastructure, and partnership-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed Strategic Partnership Projects at the eight NNSA laboratories,
plants, and sites. These projects let outside federal agencies and other
partners pay NNSA sites to use specialized facilities and expertise.

From fiscal years 2019 through 2024, the work generated almost $15 billion in
reimbursed costs. Sandia accounted for more than half of the total. DOD
sponsored the most projects, and most were related to national security.

GAO reports that the work can help retain staff, maintain facilities, and
support research. All nine federal partners interviewed said the projects were
essential to completing their missions. But some sites reported additional
capacity while others had limited capacity, and NNSA's core stockpile workload
has grown.

The route is:

```text
specialized public capability
  -> reimbursed partner work
  -> staffing, facility maintenance, and mission support
  -> [open] competition with core capacity, delivery, readiness, and strategic effect
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a geopolitical version of a recurring pattern:

> A capability can be valuable because many partners depend on it, while that
> same dependence creates a question about who gets scarce capacity first.

The $15 billion figure shows activity and cost recovery. It does not by itself
show that the work improved readiness, strengthened deterrence, or produced
better outcomes for every partner.

## Limits and next test

- Reimbursed project cost is not a measure of scientific output, readiness,
  safety, or strategic leverage.
- The interview evidence is not a representative performance estimate.
- Capacity varies by site, project, facility, and workforce.
- The report does not resolve whether reducing partnership work would harm
  partners more than it would help NNSA's core missions.

The next decisive test is a named-project ledger joining facility and staff
allocation, work delivered, mission output, cost, schedule, partner dependence,
and the effect of accepting or refusing additional work. Until then, the safe
claim is that national-lab partnership work creates real shared capacity while
leaving the allocation tradeoff visible.

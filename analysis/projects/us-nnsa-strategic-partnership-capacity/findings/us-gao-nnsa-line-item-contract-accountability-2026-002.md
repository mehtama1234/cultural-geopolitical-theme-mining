# Finding 002: NNSA modernization can carry a large contract base without clear criteria for separable deliverables and lessons

**Status:** bounded nuclear-security acquisition and state-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

GAO reports that NNSA spends over $20 billion per year on nine management-and-
operating contracts and spent close to $24 billion in FY2025. NNSA plans almost
$200 billion in modernization over the next decade.

NNSA began using line items in 2014 to define expectations for specific goods
and services inside larger contracts. GAO found that NNSA had not documented
criteria for when officials should separate tasks into line or subline items,
and had not fully assessed and documented lessons learned from prior use.

The route is:

```text
large integrated management-and-operating contract
  -> task and deliverable structure
  -> accountability, contractor oversight, and institutional learning
  -> construction or modernization delivery
  -> [open] cost, schedule, readiness, safety, mission, and deterrence outcome
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This sharpens the distinction between a state possessing a large modernization
budget and having an auditable conversion mechanism. An integrated contract can
be efficient, but if separability criteria and lessons are not documented, the
public cannot readily tell which deliverable was expected, who was accountable,
or whether a structure worked before it is repeated.

It complements the existing NNSA shared-infrastructure finding: utilization and
partner dependence are one stage; contract structure and learned accountability
are another.

## Limits and next test

- Contract scale and planned spending do not establish completed capability.
- A governance gap is not proof of waste, fraud, poor contractor performance,
  or failed nuclear-security outcomes.
- Separate line items may improve accountability but can also add coordination
  and administrative burden.
- The report does not provide a project-level before-and-after estimate of line-
  item use on cost, schedule, construction, readiness, or safety.

The next decisive artifact is a named modernization-project ledger linking
contract structure, separately identified deliverable, owner, milestone,
acceptance, cost and schedule variance, correction, sustainment, readiness, and
external security consequence.

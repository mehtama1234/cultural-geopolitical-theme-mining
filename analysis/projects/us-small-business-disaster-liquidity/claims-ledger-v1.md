# Claims ledger: US small-business disaster liquidity

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; natural disasters, small firms, emergency credit, owners, workers, and local customers

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Emergency recovery credit can keep a damaged small firm from exiting. | Causal firm study | NBER Working Paper 32326 finds government recovery loans reduced exit and bankruptcy after natural disasters. | It does not show every borrower was viable or how repayment later went. |
| C-002 | Keeping a firm open can preserve measured work and sales. | Causal firm study | The same NBER study finds higher employment and revenue, lower delinquency, and more private credit after the loans. | Employment does not show pay, hours, safety, or job quality. |
| C-003 | The repair problem can be a credit problem even when the firm has a customer and a plan. | Program rules and causal study | SBA says disaster loans cover repair, inventory, rent, payroll, utilities, and other working capital; NBER links effects partly to resolving repair uncertainty. | Approval, insurance, collateral, and repayment terms still limit access. |
| C-004 | Firm recovery is not the same as owner-household recovery. | Linked owner-firm evidence | NBER Working Paper 28151 shows owner household spending and business revenue can move together but are partly smoothed by cash and aid. | The disaster-loan study does not follow the owner's household account. |
| C-005 | Public liquidity may protect local options, but its broad benefit remains a test. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 connect disaster credit, firm survival, work, revenue, and household exposure. | Customer access, prices, local services, and long-run debt remain open. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
disaster damage or lost demand
  -> repair and working-capital gap
  -> insurance, SBA loan, private credit, or no help
  -> firm exit, survival, or delayed recovery
  -> workers, customers, owner household, and local services
```

## Next test

Join disaster declarations and loan timing to firm revenue, exit, employment, pay, prices, service availability, owner cash, and household spending. Compare borrowers with similar damaged firms that applied later or received no loan.

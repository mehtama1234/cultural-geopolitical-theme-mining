# Finding 001: Earnings convergence and return migration are separate outcomes

**Status:** bounded longitudinal administrative-earnings finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35582 uses longitudinal administrative earnings records from
1981 to 2021 to distinguish immigrants who remain in the United States from
those who eventually return. Roughly one-fifth to one-third of immigrant
arrival cohorts return within 10 years. Return migrants begin with earnings
similar to permanent migrants but experience slower earnings growth. For
cohorts arriving since the mid-1990s, immigrants who remain converge or come
close to converging with native-born earnings within 10 years; the NBER summary
reports parity timing of about 8 years for men and 19 years for women.

```text
arrival and initial earnings
  -> labor-market growth, legal/family/place conditions, and feasible alternatives
  -> stay or return selection
  -> earnings convergence among stayers versus slower growth among return migrants
  -> [open] household security, belonging, identity, trust, and later action
```

## What this adds to the atlas

The result prevents a common interpretive error: earnings assimilation among
people who remain is not automatically the experience of the full arrival
cohort. Return migration is itself an outcome and can change the apparent
earnings path of immigrants observed in repeated cross-sections. It also adds a
useful distinction between economic convergence, the ability or decision to
stay, and the meaning attached to migration or return.

## Limits and next test

- The return share is a cohort estimate, not a universal rate for all immigrants
  or all arrival periods.
- Earnings parity is not wealth parity, job-quality parity, legal security,
  benefits, health, household wellbeing, or belonging.
- Return migration is identified, but the abstract does not establish each
  person's reason for leaving or whether return improved their welfare.
- Stayers and return migrants are different observed paths; they should not be
  written as one linked household journey.

The next decisive test is a protected immigrant or household panel joining
arrival cohort, legal and family context, language, occupation, wages,
benefits, wealth, housing, discrimination, health, social ties, stay/return or
third-country move, belonging, trust, and later civic or political action.

# Official-source extract: Australia social-media minimum-age implementation

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable comparative record. This is not a substitute for the linked
official materials.

## Extracted claims

- About 4.7 million age-restricted accounts were removed or restricted as at
  mid-December 2025, based on platform responses to legally enforceable
  information-gathering notices.
- At the start of March 2026, more than 300,000 additional accounts were
  prevented from signing in; this included existing accounts and new or
  attempted accounts.
- In a pulse survey of 898 parents and carers of children aged 8–15, the share
  reporting that a child had an account on at least one measured platform fell
  from 49.7% before the restriction to 31.3% afterward.
- Among parents reporting a pre-existing account, the share reporting that the
  child still had an account afterward was 63.6% on Facebook, 69.1% on
  Instagram, 69.4% on Snapchat, 69.3% on TikTok, and 48.5% on YouTube.
- Among parents whose child retained an account, 66.8% selected that the
  platform had not yet asked the child to verify age as a reason.
- eSafety reported concerns about Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, and
  YouTube, including repeated attempts at age assurance, ineffective reporting
  pathways, and insufficient prevention of new under-16 accounts; it moved
  toward an enforcement stance while noting that actioned-account counts alone
  do not establish compliance.

## Boundary preserved

The account counts are not unique children, successful age-verification rates,
harm reductions, or proof that the law achieved its intended safety outcome.
The parent pulse is a survey of reported experience, not platform telemetry or
a causal child-wellbeing study. The extract is retained to make those limits
reproducible and visible.

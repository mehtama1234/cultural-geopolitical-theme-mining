# Finding: a drug subscription can convert public financing into measured treatment and health gains

**Status:** bounded health-access, public-finance, and realized-outcome finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35583 studies Louisiana's 2019 Medicaid subscription model
for hepatitis-C antivirals. The two-part tariff gave the state unlimited access
to curative medicines in exchange for a fixed payment, changing the marginal
price of treatment for the covered route.

Using differences-in-differences comparisons, the study reports that treatment
volume tripled and new hepatitis-C diagnoses fell 23%. It estimates $37 million
in added antiviral spending and $266 million in five-year savings.

This is a valuable open-middle closure:

> A financing rule can change the practical treatment route and be followed
> into program-level health and fiscal outcomes, but realized program success
> still does not prove that every eligible person received, completed, or
> benefited from treatment.

## Limits and next test

- The estimates are for Louisiana's Medicaid-centered model and should not be
  generalized automatically to other states or populations.
- Treatment volume is not the same as individual treatment completion, cure,
  adherence, reinfection prevention, or equal access.
- Estimated savings are not a complete accounting of outreach, diagnosis,
  administrative, or implementation costs.
- The record does not follow a person's treatment, health, household cost, and
  trust pathway from eligibility through recovery.

The next decisive artifact is a person- or provider-linked implementation
ledger joining eligibility, diagnosis, offer, prescription, completion/cure,
follow-up, access barrier, and household or system cost.

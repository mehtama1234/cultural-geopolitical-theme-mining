# Defense procurement to usable capability bridge

Status: forty-eighth active depth artifact; checked 2026-10-18.

## What we found

Defense spending and procurement can show strategic intent, industrial effort,
and planned capacity without showing a usable capability. Poland's May 2024
JASSM-ER agreement is reported at approximately $735 million with a planned
2026–2030 delivery window and an approximate 1,000-kilometre range. The public
source set reviewed through September 2026 did not provide an identifier-bearing
delivery, acceptance, training, fielding, or inventory event for that order. A
December 2024 Polish briefing states a different 2028–2030 schedule, but does
not identify a lot or amend the contract.

Related records show why the stages matter: JASSM/LRASM production capacity is
reported at a combined minimum sustained rate of 396 missiles per year, while
LRASM F-35 integration has reached flight-test milestones. Neither production
rate nor flight test proves that Poland's JASSM-ER order is delivered or that an
alliance or adversary changed behavior.

The safe chain is:

`strategic concern -> contract -> production -> shipment -> acceptance ->
training/integration -> maintenance and availability -> operational use ->
[open] deterrence, alliance bargaining, substitution, or adversary response`

## What this connects to

- **State capacity:** money and contracts create options, not necessarily
  available force.
- **Industrial power:** production rate, supplier concentration, technology
  transfer, and maintenance shape replaceability.
- **Alliance politics:** interoperability and burden sharing depend on usable
  systems, not only agreements or spending targets.
- **Public legitimacy:** defense financing can be supported or contested before
  citizens can observe operational results.
- **Geopolitical leverage:** deterrence and bargaining require fielded,
  maintainable, credible capability and an external actor that responds.
- **Cultural meaning:** procurement can symbolize sovereignty or dependence;
  that interpretation is not the same as operational autonomy.

## Deeper question

**When does defense procurement become operational leverage rather than a
contract, production promise, or national-capacity symbol?**

This question has high decision value for readiness and alliance policy, high
missing-link value because shipment, acceptance, maintenance, and external
response are often absent, and high connection value across state capacity,
industrial dependence, public finance, technology sovereignty, and geopolitical
behavior. It also has high disconfirmation value: one complete order-to-use
record with a measured external response would challenge the current caution.
Effort is bounded to one identifier-bearing system and recipient; scope risk is
controlled by separating family-level comparators from the Polish order.

## What we know so far

The public record supports a difference between commitment, capacity, and
capability. A contract and a delivery schedule show intended acquisition. A
production rate shows potential supply. A flight test or exercise shows a
milestone. None alone shows accepted inventory, sustained availability,
operational use, replacement options, or an adversary's changed behavior.

The Poland route also contains a useful source conflict: 2026–2030 and
2028–2030 schedule language appears in official sources without a lot-level
bridge. The correct result is unresolved schedule vintage, not an inferred
delay or delivery.

## What could change the answer

- A named lot could show shipment, acceptance, training, maintenance, and
  fielding before 2030, closing the current public gap.
- A delivered system may still lack spares, certification, crews, or sustained
  availability; receipt and readiness are separate currencies.
- Co-production can widen supplier options while leaving design authority,
  software, critical components, or export permission external.
- A capability can improve deterrence without a publicly observable adversary
  response; non-observation is not no effect.
- A delayed or unpublished delivery record may change the result when an
  identifier-bearing official source becomes available.

## Challenge checks

- Spending, authorization, contract, production, test, and exercise were not
  treated as operational capability.
- The absence audit was bounded to reviewed public sources and not promoted to
  proof of non-delivery.
- Poland's JASSM and later JASSM-ER stages were kept separate.
- LRASM/F-35 integration and Barracuda co-production were comparators, not
  pooled evidence about JASSM-ER.
- Official state and company accounts were not treated as independent proof of
  readiness.
- Affected parties include armed forces, industry, workers, taxpayers,
  allies, host communities, and potential adversaries.
- Public discussion was used as interpretation only.

## Current answer

Partly answered. Procurement and production can expand strategic options, but
the Poland JASSM-ER record remains open between contract and usable capability,
and all reviewed routes remain open between capability and changed external
behavior. Operational leverage cannot be claimed from the current stages alone.

## Stopping rule

Do not call a procurement delivered, ready, sovereign, deterrent, or geopolitically
effective from a contract, schedule, spending total, production plan, flight
test, exercise, or co-production agreement alone. Stop this pass after one
identifier-bearing order links shipment, acceptance, training, maintenance,
availability, operational use, and a dated external response—or after the
source owner confirms which fields are not public.

# Source search: tax rulemaking and usable public participation

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States Treasury and Internal Revenue Service rulemaking  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; a comment opportunity is not automatically usable influence

## Working question

What does the oversight record show about the public's practical ability to
participate in tax regulation and about how agencies use the resulting input?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-GAO-TAX-RULEMAKING-ENGAGEMENT-2026 | [GAO-26-108115, Tax Regulations: Opportunities Exist to Improve Economic Analysis and Taxpayer Engagement](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108115/index.html) | Reviews 236 proposed and 231 finalized regulations, selected economic analyses, public-comment procedures, hearings, and AI-enabled mass-comment risks. | Official oversight report | It does not identify which individual comments changed a rule, measure who could not participate, or estimate public trust or tax incidence. |

## First pattern

```text
proposed tax rule
  -> notice, comments, and hearing
  -> agency analysis and comment processing
  -> final rule
  -> taxpayer burden, benefit, compliance, and legitimacy [open]
```

GAO found that Treasury and IRS generally provided an opportunity to comment
on proposed regulations. But public hearings used dial-in technology rather
than modern virtual participation, hearing comments were not always documented,
and the agencies lacked a policy for handling mass comments or comments
written with AI assistance. Economic analyses also used some recommended
practices less consistently, including specific cost, benefit, and revenue
estimates.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The route is not absent. GAO found notice-and-comment procedures for all 10
proposed regulations in its random sample, with a median comment period of
61.5 days; the selected regulations received anywhere from five or fewer to
11,050 comments. One recommended analytical practice—examining alternative
regulatory designs—was used consistently in the reviewed economically
significant regulations and made it easier for taxpayers to claim some new
benefits.

## Decision rule

Use this source for the gap between formal participation and usable influence.
Do not treat comment volume, a legal hearing, or an open docket as proof of
broad representation, agency learning, or a changed final rule.

# Finding 001: A public comment route can exist while participation and agency learning remain uneven

**Status:** bounded political-voice and public-systems finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed Treasury and IRS tax rulemaking from January 21, 2017, through
March 31, 2026. The agencies proposed 236 regulations and finalized 231. In a
random sample of 10 proposed regulations, GAO found that the agencies used
notice-and-comment procedures, but comment counts ranged from five or fewer to
11,050 and the median comment period was 61.5 days.

The route had practical weaknesses. Public hearings relied on dial-in rather
than broadly available video participation, hearing comments were not always
documented, and the agencies had no policy for identifying or handling mass
comments or comments written with AI assistance. For four selected
economically significant regulations, analysis of alternatives was used
consistently, but specific cost, benefit, and revenue estimates were less
consistent.

The route is:

```text
proposed tax rule
  -> legal notice and comment opportunity
  -> ability to participate and be legible in the record
  -> economic analysis and agency response
  -> final rule and taxpayer consequences [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a political-voice version of the program's central distinction:

> Being invited to speak is not the same as being able to participate easily,
> have one's contribution recognized, or see how it affected the decision.

The digital twist matters. AI can lower the cost of submitting comments while
making it harder for agencies to distinguish many independent views from
near-duplicate text. That creates a new contest over administrative attention:
participation can become more scalable without becoming more representative or
more influential.

## Limits and next test

- GAO reviewed a selected sample of economic analyses and 10 proposed
  regulations; it does not estimate the quality of every tax rule or comment
  process.
- Comment counts show submitted volume, not who participated, who was absent,
  whether comments were substantive, or whether agencies changed the rule.
- A median 61.5-day period describes formal time, not the time, money,
  expertise, language access, or technology needed to prepare a useful comment.
- The report does not measure final taxpayer burden, benefit receipt,
  compliance, trust, or political action.

The next decisive artifact is a rule-level record linking commenter type,
comment content, agency response, final design, and later taxpayer cost or
benefit. Until then, the safe claim is about uneven participation and analysis
capacity—not captured rulemaking or universal exclusion.

# Source search: US career progression and the mid-level plateau

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

Does the typical worker stop moving up long before work stops, and what household choices meet that plateau?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-HBS-CAREER-PLATEAU-2026 | [HBS: Most Careers Stall at Mid-Level](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/most-careers-stall-at-mid-level-no-matter-how-long-people-work) | An analysis of 150 million resumes and wage data proposes measuring career progress by the time needed to reach a job level; most workers top out at a mid-level | HBS account of NBER Working Paper 34292 | The article's seniority measure is not yet a full account of job quality, control, health or household security |
| US-NBER-CAREER-34292 | [NBER Working Paper 34292](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34292) | The underlying paper develops a non-wage measure of career attainment and compares progression across firms and sectors | Working paper | Full paper and data need review; a level is not automatically a promotion or better life |
| US-BLS-OCCUPATION | [BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/oes/) | Current occupation and wage data can test whether slow advancement differs across work and place | Official data | Cross-sectional wage data do not follow a worker's full career |

## First pattern to test

```text
years of work
  -> real career movement or mid-level plateau
  -> pay, control, job change, debt and housing room
  -> family timing, status, security or political feeling
```

The HBS/NBER work supports a new way to measure career movement. The household and political links remain open.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

A worker may choose a stable mid-level role, change sectors, value flexibility, or receive higher pay without a higher title. A plateau in the measure is not automatically failure or harm.

## Main gaps

- career movement by income, race, gender, age, education and place;
- job control, schedule, health and security at each level;
- title changes that do not carry more responsibility or pay;
- career movement linked to student-loan payment, housing and family timing;
- differences between public, nonprofit and private work;
- whether slower advancement is chosen or imposed.

## Decision rule

Review the NBER paper and compare its measure with current BLS data. Add a finding only when career movement can be tied to a later work or household outcome.

# Finding 001: A maritime migration surge redirected state capacity and changed the balance among missions

**Status:** bounded maritime migration, public-safety, and geopolitical-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

From fiscal years 2022 through 2024, almost 70,000 migrants attempted to
travel from the Caribbean toward the United States. In response, the Coast
Guard and DHS began a high-intensity migrant-interdiction surge in August 2022
under Operation Vigilant Sentry; the surge was still operating in November
2025.

The Coast Guard redirected assets from other missions. From FY2021 to FY2023,
vessel and aircraft hours for migrant interdiction rose 66%, while drug-
interdiction hours fell 62%. From August 2022 through September 2024, 80
cutters were deployed to the Southeast District operation; 38 had been
redirected from other missions and 29 were mixed-use deployments.

The shift was not a simple success story. The Coast Guard interdicted about
37% of known maritime migrant flow in both FY2022 and FY2023, but it missed its
migrant target in 6 of 10 years from FY2015 through FY2024, and officials said
the measure did not properly capture the service's work. In FY2023, the Coast
Guard provided half of JIATF-South's requested cutter support, compared with
96% in FY2021. The United Kingdom, Netherlands, and other partners partly filled
the gap; partner nations' share of drug interdictions rose from 54% in FY2021
to 67% in FY2023.

The route is:

```text
maritime migration surge
  -> finite Coast Guard assets redirected
  -> migrant interdiction and interagency coordination
  -> less Coast Guard capacity for drug, marine-resource, and other missions
  -> partner substitution and changed burden sharing
  -> [open] deterrence, migrant safety, legitimacy, and regional effect
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a mission-tradeoff version of a recurring state-capacity pattern:

> When a government responds to a visible emergency with finite people and
> equipment, capacity is not only added—it is moved from somewhere else.

The finding does not say that migrant interdiction was the wrong priority. GAO
reports that Coast Guard officials treated it partly as a life-saving mission,
which can justify giving it precedence. Nor does partner substitution prove
loss of U.S. influence; it may be successful burden sharing. The narrower point
is that emergency response changes the distribution of state attention, and
headline activity in one mission can coexist with weaker performance or thinner
capacity in another.

The measurement problem matters culturally and politically. A Coast Guard-only
interdiction percentage can undercount a successful operation when CBP or a
foreign partner completes the action, while a migrant-flow estimate captures
only what authorities can identify. The public meaning of “success” therefore
depends on which agency, person, and outcome the measure recognizes.

## Limits and next test

- The report does not establish deterrence, total migration, migrant safety
  after interception, or later immigration outcomes.
- The resource comparison is descriptive; it does not isolate migration policy,
  asset readiness, workforce shortages, partner action, or other causes.
- Coast Guard and partner interdiction records use different roles and
  definitions; interdiction at sea is not the same as apprehension on land.
- Site-visit and interview evidence is not generalizable to every district or
  partner.
- The task force generally followed 7 of 8 collaboration practices, but did
  not fully share lessons learned; this is a coordination boundary, not proof
  of operational failure.

The next decisive artifact is a named-operation ledger joining asset assignment,
mission, encounter, interagency or foreign-partner action, migrant safety and
legal outcome, drug or marine-resource consequence, cost, and later mission
capacity. Until then, the safe claim is that Operation Vigilant Sentry reveals
a real finite-capacity tradeoff and partner substitution, while deterrence,
safety, legitimacy, and durable regional effects remain open.

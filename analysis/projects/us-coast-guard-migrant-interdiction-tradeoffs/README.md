# Project: Maritime migration response and Coast Guard mission tradeoffs

## Question

When maritime migration rises sharply, how does a finite Coast Guard change its
other missions, partner relationships, and measures of success?

## Decision rule

Keep migrant flow, interdiction, apprehension, life-saving, asset hours, drug
enforcement, partner action, and later human outcomes separate. More
interdictions do not automatically mean deterrence or safety.

## Current bounded finding

The [GAO Coast Guard finding](findings/us-gao-coast-guard-migrant-interdiction-tradeoffs-2026-001.md)
follows the named Operation Vigilant Sentry route. It shows a real resource
tradeoff: migrant-interdiction hours rose while drug-interdiction hours fell,
and foreign partners filled part of the resulting gap.

The route is:

```text
maritime migration surge
  -> asset redirection and interagency operation
  -> migrant interdiction and partner substitution
  -> reduced capacity in other missions
  -> [open] deterrence, safety, legitimacy, and durable regional effects
```

The public report does not follow an individual migrant, shipment, or later
regional outcome.

# Finding 001: Social similarity can become a firm-level labor-market pathway

**Status:** bounded founder-background and hiring finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's Center for Economic Studies working paper links U.S.
Census and LinkedIn profiles to study entrepreneurs and employees. It reports
that entrepreneurs are more likely to hire workers from similar social
backgrounds. After industry-by-metro-area-by-cohort controls, the female
employee share is 36.4 percentage points higher at female-founded startups;
corresponding estimates are 51.2 points for Black employees, 37.3 for
Hispanic employees, and 11.3 for non-college employees. The paper reports that
the differences persist across cohorts and at least 20 years, and that wage
decompositions point to labor demand: new firms pay higher relative wages to
workers similar to the entrepreneur.

The route is:

```text
unequal access to becoming an entrepreneur
  -> founder networks, information, trust, or preferences
  -> who is recruited and hired
  -> relative wage and firm opportunity
  -> retention, promotion, mobility, and inequality [open]
```

The broader cultural point is that social similarity may not stay a private
relationship. It can become an organizational pattern that distributes access
to jobs and wages over time.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a firm-level mechanism between identity, networks, and material
opportunity. It also sharpens the meaning of entrepreneurship access: barriers
to founders may affect not only who starts a firm, but which worker groups are
later reached by new firms. The result remains a bounded association and
mechanism study, not a universal claim about every founder or startup.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a working paper, not a final reviewed Census publication.
- The public abstract does not provide all linkage, sample, founder, or
  worker-supply details.
- Similar-background hiring may reflect information, skills, location,
  networks, preferences, or labor supply, not one single motive.
- The composition differences do not identify every rejected applicant or
  establish discrimination in every firm.
- Higher relative wages for similar-background workers do not establish equal
  productivity, worker wellbeing, firm success, or household security.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected founder-worker-firm ledger joining
founder background, candidate networks, applications, offers, hiring,
occupation, wage, retention, promotion, firm growth, and worker mobility.
That would show whether similarity opens opportunity for some workers while
closing or redirecting opportunity for others.

# Project: Entrepreneur background and who gets hired

This project examines how unequal access to entrepreneurship can shape the
labor market through hiring. A Census working paper links U.S. Census and
LinkedIn data and reports that entrepreneurs are more likely to hire workers
from similar social backgrounds. The abstract gives large adjusted differences
in employee composition at startups founded by women, Black founders, and
other groups, and reports persistence for at least 20 years. Its wage
decomposition points to labor demand: new firms pay higher relative wages to
workers similar to the entrepreneur.

The safe theme is not simply “founders discriminate.” Shared background may
carry information, trust, skills, networks, geography, or supply, and the
study's public abstract cannot separate every channel. The project keeps
similar-background hiring, exclusion, wages, productivity, worker experience,
and firm performance separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected founder-worker-firm ledger joining
founder background, candidate networks, applications, offers, hiring,
occupation, wage, retention, promotion, firm growth, and worker mobility. The
current study does not observe every rejected applicant or the full household
and career path.

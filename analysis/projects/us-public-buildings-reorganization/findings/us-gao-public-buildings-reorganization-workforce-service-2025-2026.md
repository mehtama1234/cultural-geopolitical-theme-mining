# Finding: centralization can weaken public service when workforce planning and feedback lag

**Status:** bounded institutional-capacity and infrastructure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed the General Services Administration's Public Buildings Service
reorganization from March through December 2025. The reorganization aimed to
make the service more centralized, consistent, and streamlined, but GAO found
that it did not yet follow four selected reform practices.

The workforce was reduced by 45% by November 2025 without strategic workforce
planning beforehand. The bureau had not established performance measures for
its stated goals. Communication with employees and tenant agencies was mainly
one-way, without a plan at that point to gather feedback that could improve the
reorganization and build confidence. Selected tenant agencies reported service
effects, including a delay in one agency's disposal projects because fewer cost
estimators were available.

The broader institutional insight is:

> A reorganization can promise consistency and efficiency while reducing the
> people, feedback, and measurements needed to know whether service actually
> improved.

GAO reported that the agency later agreed to create measures, feedback routes,
implementation milestones, and workforce planning. Those commitments are a
correction route, not proof that the service has already recovered.

## Limits and next test

- This is one named federal agency, not evidence about all reorganizations.
- The 45% figure is an agency workforce change, not a measure of workload,
  productivity, or public value.
- Tenant-service effects were reported by selected officials and are not a
  complete customer survey or causal estimate.
- Planned measures and surveys do not establish completed implementation,
  service quality, employee wellbeing, or tenant confidence.

The next decisive artifact is a protected agency-project ledger joining staffing
and skills, project request, service route, delay or completion, tenant
feedback, correction, cost, and later performance against the reorganization's
baseline and targets.

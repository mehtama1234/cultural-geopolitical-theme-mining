# Source search: NATO support and political permission

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States and 36 comparison countries; 13 NATO member states included in the alliance-favorability comparison  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; institutional support and political/leader confidence are separate

## Working question

Does favorable opinion of NATO imply broad and durable support for U.S. alliance
leadership, leaders, costs, and future commitments?

## First pattern

```text
formal alliance institution
  -> public favorability
  -> partisan and leader-specific interpretation
  -> permission for spending, risk, access, and future commitments [open]
```

The source supports a separation between support for the institution and trust
or confidence in the political actors who represent it. It also shows that
alliance legitimacy can vary across countries and parties while the formal
alliance remains in place.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Favorability is not a measure of willingness to pay, host forces, accept risk,
approve a particular war, or support a specific U.S. administration. A partisan
gap may reflect views of leaders, domestic politics, foreign policy, or the
question wording. The survey cannot show whether attitudes changed behavior or
alliance durability.

## Decision rule

Use this pass for public legitimacy and relational trust around formal alliance
structures. Do not convert favorable opinion into proof of strategic success,
operational cooperation, or durable public consent for a future commitment.

# Project: Grandparent caregiving and mortality heterogeneity

This project tracks the health meaning of raising grandchildren across groups
and caregiving durations. A Census-SSA study links adults age 50 and older from
the 2000 Census long-form sample to death records through 2019. It reports
higher mortality for White coresident grandparents, lower mortality for Asian
coresident grandparents, higher mortality among Hispanic long-term primary
caregivers, and lower mortality among Black short-term primary caregivers.

The safe theme is not “grandparent caregiving is harmful” or “family care is
protective.” It is that the same arrangement can carry different burdens,
resources, meanings, and health associations across groups and durations. The
project must keep coresidence, caregiving, prior health, family support,
housing, income, public services, mortality, and cultural meaning separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected grandparent-household panel joining
care duration, coresidence, health, income, housing, childcare, kin support,
public benefits, work, stress, social connection, health care, and later health.
The public abstract does not provide that complete mechanism.

# Finding: career advice can distribute the hidden price of work differently by gender

**Status:** bounded career-information and intention finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35750 conducts a large-scale field experiment in which
college students seek career information from 10,000 working professionals.
The experiment randomizes whether a professional receives a message from a male
or female student.

When students ask broadly about a career, female students receive substantially
more information about work-life balance than male students. The difference
persists when students explicitly ask about work-life balance. An information
intervention indicates that the gap is consequential for gender differences in
career intentions.

The labor-market insight is:

> Career choice is partly shaped before entry by who receives information about
> the hidden costs of a job. An information gap can change intentions even when
> formal career access is nominally open to everyone.

## Limits and next test

- The exact student sample, response rate, and information-intervention details
  are not disclosed in the abstract.
- Career intention is not realized occupation, earnings, care burden, or
  work-life balance.
- More information may reflect relevance, social norms, or strategic advice,
  not a single bias mechanism.
- The study does not establish whether information changes actual applications,
  job offers, retention, or household outcomes.

The next decisive artifact is a student–advisor–career ledger joining message
content, field choice, applications, offers, schedule and care expectations,
occupation, earnings, retention, and later household time.

# California FAIR Plan residual-market layer v1

California provides a concrete state case for the public-backstop stage. The
FAIR Plan's five-year county reports show total policies in force across
residential, commercial, and business-owner lines rising from 242,440 at
September 30, 2021 to 642,010 at September 30, 2025: a calculated 164.8%
increase. Total insured exposure rose from $160.6 billion to $694.0 billion,
or 332.1%.

The current FAIR Plan statistics page reports 696,562 total dwelling and
commercial policies and approximately $768 billion in exposure as of June
2026, plus approximately $2.04 billion in written premium. Because this later
headline has a slightly different stated scope and timing from the five-year
county report, it is preserved as a separate observation rather than silently
joined into the annual series.

This is a strong public-system signal but not a complete availability result:

```text
hazard and insurer conditions
  -> growth of last-resort policy pool and insured exposure
  -> public/private risk redistribution and possible assessment pressure
  -> household coverage, affordability, claim, repair, and mobility effects (open)
```

FAIR Plan growth can reflect more than private-market withdrawal: property
values, policy limits, underwriting, risk concentration, state policy, and
reporting transparency can all change. The next state panel should combine
FAIR Plan enrollment and exposure with voluntary-market policies, nonrenewals,
premiums, claims, and household or property outcomes.

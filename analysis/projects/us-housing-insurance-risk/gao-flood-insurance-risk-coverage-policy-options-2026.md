# Flood-risk rules expose an insurance gap while purchase and recovery remain open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** mapped and unprotected flood exposure → insurance and policy options; household coverage and recovery remain open

GAO's October 1, 2026 flood-insurance review adds a housing and place route.
As of April 2026, 86 percent of high-risk properties lacked National Flood
Insurance Program coverage. NFIP policies declined from 5.5 million in 2010 to
4.5 million in 2026, while private policies represented 14 percent of all
flood-insurance policies in 2025. GAO's First Street analysis identified about
13 million high-risk properties outside FEMA special flood hazard areas,
including risks from heavy rainfall that maps may not capture.

The institutional route is a set of four open congressional actions: include
all flood sources in mandatory-purchase determinations, make property-level
risk information public, require lender quotes and signed declinations before
closing on federally backed mortgages, and increase and periodically adjust
NFIP coverage limits. GAO also identifies means-based affordability assistance
and removal of barriers to private coverage as policy options.

The evidence boundary is material. Coverage shares are not property-owner
purchase records, and the mapped and high-risk universes differ. The public
record does not show a household receiving a risk disclosure or quote,
buying affordable and adequate insurance, filing a claim, rebuilding, or
recovering after a flood.

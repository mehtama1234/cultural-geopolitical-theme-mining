# California FAIR/private-market joint sensitivity layer v1

The first joint screen used a $64,000 lower-income boundary and CDI's 12.1%
high/very-high exposure threshold. That definition produced 12.72% nonrenewal
for low-income/high-risk ZIPs versus 10.13% for high-income/low-risk ZIPs.

The result is not robust as a general risk gradient. With a $50,000 income
boundary and the same risk threshold, low-income/high-risk ZIPs have a 12.03%
rate versus 13.16% in low-income/low-risk ZIPs. Median-income/median-risk and
lower-income-quartile/upper-risk-quartile definitions also produce different
cell orderings and smaller contrasts.

The defensible conclusion is therefore narrower: a particular place split
reveals an unequal nonrenewal pattern, but the direction and size are sensitive
to how income and risk groups are defined. This is evidence of composition and
model sensitivity, not a stable causal wildfire or income effect.

The next test must pre-specify cut points, report all cells, and use a valid
same-period design before promoting the pattern into an explanatory bridge.

# Federal Reserve 2025 housing and insurance risk layer v1

The [Federal Reserve 2025 housing report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-housing.htm)
adds a household and place-risk layer to the housing/insurance lane.

Among renters, 23% reported being behind on rent at some point in the prior
year, up from 21% in 2024. The rate was nearly one-third for renters with
income below $50,000 and 5% for renters with income of at least $100,000.

Among homeowners, 6% reported going without homeowners insurance. The rate was
12% in low- or moderate-income neighborhoods and 5% in high-income
neighborhoods; roughly two in ten owners with income below $50,000 were
uninsured. Cost was central: 43% of uninsured owners said they could not
afford insurance, while 20% of insured owners preferred more coverage but
could not afford it and 14% struggled with premiums.

These are distinct risk stages:

```text
housing payment pressure -> arrears or displacement risk
insurance price/availability -> coverage gap or underinsurance
physical/financial shock -> repair, debt, move/stay, health, and local response
```

The source measures reported household conditions, not a causal climate or
market effect. It does not observe claims, insurer nonrenewal decisions,
coverage adequacy after a loss, mortgage enforcement, repair completion,
health, mobility, or political response. The next test is a place/time panel
connecting premiums, hazards, property values, rents, claims, coverage,
household budgets, and move/stay outcomes.

## Temporal check: 2024 to 2025

The prior-year Federal Reserve housing report is available and uses compatible
annual SHED reporting frames. Rent arrears rose from 21% of renters in 2024 to
23% in 2025. The share of homeowners reporting no homeowners insurance moved
from 7% in 2024 to 6% in 2025. These are report-year comparisons, not a panel
of the same households; the one-point insurance movement should not be treated
as evidence of a substantive trend without more years and administrative
validation.

Sources: [2024 housing report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-housing.htm),
[2025 housing report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-housing.htm).

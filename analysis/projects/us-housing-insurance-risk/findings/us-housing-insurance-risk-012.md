# Finding 012: A tenant lawyer can change the court case without solving the rent problem

**Status:** bounded housing-court intervention finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

New York City's Universal Access to Counsel program offers free lawyers to
eligible low-income tenants in housing court. Cassidy and Currie studied the
program's rollout using detailed court records from 2016 through 2019.

Tenants who gained legal representation were less likely to receive a judgment
giving the landlord possession, received smaller money judgments, and were less
likely to have an eviction warrant issued. The estimated reduction in the
earlier court stages was large. The effect on a warrant actually being carried
out was smaller and less consistent in the full sample.

The route is:

```text
rent dispute
  -> housing court
  -> tenant gets a lawyer
  -> judgment, money owed, warrant, or executed eviction
  -> [open] later rent, move, work, health, credit, and housing security
```

This is a different kind of protection from rental assistance. Money can help
pay rent; a lawyer can change what happens inside the court process. Neither
result, by itself, proves durable housing security.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

The finding shows that “help” is not one thing. A legal right can improve a
person's position in an institution even when the original financial pressure
still exists. The court can become more contestable without becoming a solution
to the shortage of affordable housing.

The effects were larger in poorer places and places with more noncitizens. That
is a clue about unequal practical access and unequal exposure, not proof that
the same effect will appear everywhere.

The study also makes a useful measurement boundary visible: a possession
judgment, money judgment, eviction warrant, and executed eviction are different
events. Stopping one stage is not the same as showing what happened next to the
tenant.

## Limits and next test

- This is one city and one housing-court program.
- The records do not follow the same tenants into later rent payments, moves,
  work, health, credit, schooling, or housing quality.
- Tenants who never reached housing court are outside the study.
- A court improvement can reflect delay, settlement, repair, payment plans, or
  a case ending without proving that rent became affordable.
- The stronger next test is a privacy-protected link from court cases to later
  housing, debt, work, health, and school records, while keeping actual moves
  separate from court judgments.

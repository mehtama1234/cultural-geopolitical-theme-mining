# Finding 009: Rental technology can make housing easier to search while shifting power over screening, price, and access

**Status:** bounded rental housing, digital-platform, and public-oversight finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The simple finding

Renters and landlords increasingly use software for advertising, tenant
screening, rent setting, leasing, and building access. These tools may make
housing easier to find and manage, but they can also make decisions harder to
explain and challenge.

GAO reviewed four tool types: advertising platforms, tenant-screening tools,
rent-setting software, and facial-recognition technology. It reports possible
risks from inaccurate or outdated screening records, algorithmic steering,
reduced rent bargaining power, privacy loss, and facial-recognition errors.

## The route under test

```text
renter searches or applies
  -> platform, screening, pricing, or access tool
  -> listing, score, rent recommendation, or entry decision
  -> explanation, correction, consent, appeal, or enforcement
  -> lease, rent burden, privacy, housing stability, and ability to stay
```

The report does not follow a named renter through this route. It shows where a
digital tool can change a housing opportunity before a renter can see the
decision or contest it. All 10 public housing agencies interviewed said they
would benefit from more specific guidance on facial-recognition use; HUD
reported that technical assistance was being developed, with guidance estimated
for December 2026.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is the renter-side version of the homebuying proptech finding:

> A digital housing route can improve convenience while moving more control
> over access, price, and surveillance into systems the renter cannot inspect.

That matters because renters often have less room to wait, negotiate, move, or
absorb a denial than a buyer with multiple financing options. The evidence
supports a power and contestability boundary—not a claim that every tool is
harmful or every landlord uses it unfairly.

## Limits and next test

- The report uses selected companies, agencies, and stakeholder interviews;
  these samples are not nationally representative.
- Potential risks are not prevalence rates or causal estimates.
- Screening, pricing, and facial recognition can also provide legitimate
  benefits such as fraud reduction or safety.
- Planned HUD guidance is not completed implementation.
- The report does not measure a named renter's denial, rent, appeal, privacy
  loss, eviction, move, stability, trust, or political action.

The next useful test is an authorized rental-application ledger linking listing
exposure, screening data, rent recommendation, notice, explanation, correction,
consent, appeal, lease result, rent burden, and later housing stability.

# Virginia SCC paid-restitution recheck

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** aggregate payment reported by regulator; recipient-level correction, adequacy, and continuity remain open

## What changed

The Virginia State Corporation Commission's current public settlement summaries
now state that three previously recorded settlement routes have moved beyond
the agreement/design stage:

- Esurance: approximately $2 million reportedly paid to 2,959 consumers after
  premium or points errors tied to accident notices.
- State Farm: approximately $2.5 million reportedly paid to 91,696 consumers
  after filed-rate and premium errors.
- Anthem/Carelon: approximately $346,000 reportedly paid to 1,680 consumers
  and 586 providers, plus approximately $118,000 paid to the Commonwealth,
  after claims, network, demographic, and fee-schedule problems.

The same summaries retain prospective controls: notice and rate-factor review
for Esurance, filed-rate compliance for State Farm, and claims/vendor/data
verification and testing for Anthem. This closes a regulator-reported
aggregate-payment stage, not the individual recovery stage.

```text
insurance error or delay
  -> SCC investigation and settlement
  -> reported aggregate restitution paid
  -> [open] recipient-level payment and premium/claim correction
  -> [open] coverage or care continuity and residual loss
  -> [open] trust, switching, persistence, and exit
```

## Evidence boundary

The SCC page does not publish a recipient file, payment timing by person,
amount received by person, returned or failed payments, policy-level premium
correction, claim-level correction, coverage renewal, provider participation,
care continuity, residual loss, or durable recurrence measures. The record
therefore uses `reported_paid_aggregate` for the regulator's statement and
keeps individual receipt and adequacy as unmeasured.

## Next decisive evidence

Seek a lawful aggregate or audit output for payment completion, returned or
unclaimed payments, policy/claim correction, and coverage or network
continuity. A recipient-level or same-case follow-up would be stronger, but
the public route should not infer it from the aggregate payment statement.

# Mortgage trigger leads: privacy protection, comparison shopping, and housing choice

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** statutory restriction and mechanism assessment; realized borrower and housing outcomes remain open

GAO's October 2026 review adds a housing-and-platform route beneath the
mortgage market. Before the 2025 Homebuyers Privacy Protection Act, credit
reporting agencies could provide a homebuyer's contact and credit information
to other lenders and brokers without an existing relationship or the buyer's
consent. Homebuyers could receive dozens of calls or texts after applying for
a mortgage. Stakeholders identified comparison shopping and possible savings
as a benefit, but also described overwhelm, confusion, aggressive tactics,
misleading-practice exposure, and privacy invasion.

The review reports that recent federal survey evidence suggests no more than
3.5% of homebuyers may have obtained a loan through competing trigger-lead
offers. The Act restricted residential mortgage trigger leads to parties with
an existing relationship with or consent from the homebuyer, effective March
2026. That is a later institutional-control stage, but GAO says too little time
had passed to determine its effects.

The bounded finding is a conversion tradeoff: protecting a person's data and
attention may reduce unsolicited pressure while also removing one comparison
route. Online tools, referrals, and existing relationships may substitute, but
the public evidence does not yet show who receives fewer solicitations, who
gets better prices, whether access changes, or whether housing stability and
trust improve.

# Finding 002: Political office has remained socially selective across US history

**Status:** bounded historical US political-access finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35569 links Members of Congress, their parents, and their
brothers to complete-count census records. Across people born from 1830 to
1950, future Members came from unusually advantaged families. By 1940, nearly
60% of Members aged 18–40 had a college degree, compared with under 5% of
similar young men outside Congress. The family-background gap held or widened
across cohorts.

The study also tests four Progressive-Era reforms—women's suffrage, the secret
ballot, direct primaries, and direct election of senators—and reports no
detectable shift in who reached Congress in those tests.

```text
family resources and status
  -> education, networks, and entry into candidacy or office
  -> who reaches Congress
  -> [open] constituent representation, policy, trust, and legitimacy
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a US political-access version of the broader network question already
seen in the historical Buenos Aires Jockey Club study. The US evidence says
that formal elections can coexist with durable social selectivity in who gets
to the office stage. It does not say that every officeholder is unrepresentative
or that one family mechanism explains the pattern.

The deeper cultural-political question is not only whether people can vote. It
is whether family resources, education, networks, money, and time shape who can
enter the pipeline before voters make a choice.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence is historical and does not estimate current congressional selection.
- The public abstract does not provide every sample count, estimate, or control.
- A family-origin gap does not measure corruption, policy quality, constituent responsiveness, or public trust.
- A null result for selected reforms does not prove that no later institution changed access.

The next decisive US test is a current candidate-to-office ledger joining family
resources, education, network access, fundraising, nomination, election, office
holding, legislative behavior, constituent contact, and later public judgment.
Until then, the safe claim is a historically persistent selection pattern, not
a current causal estimate of representation.

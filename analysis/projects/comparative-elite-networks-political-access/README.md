# Project: elite networks and political access

## Question

When do social relationships become a route into formal political power, and
when do institutional changes reduce that advantage?

## Decision rule

Keep social membership, network position, candidacy, election, office-holding,
legislative behavior, and public legitimacy separate. A connected network is
not the same thing as democratic representation.

## Current bounded finding

The [Jockey Club finding](findings/comparative-nber-jockey-club-membership-political-office-2026-001.md)
reports that historical membership in an elite Buenos Aires club increased
access to legislative office, while the advantage weakened after a major
democratizing reform. This is comparative evidence about networked political
access, not a current U.S. estimate.

The [Fortunate Sons finding](findings/us-nber-elite-political-selection-family-origins-2026-001.md)
adds a historical US layer: Members of Congress came disproportionately from
advantaged families, and selected Progressive-Era reforms did not detectably
change who reached Congress in the study's tests. This is evidence about entry
and selection, not proof of present-day unrepresentativeness or policy failure.

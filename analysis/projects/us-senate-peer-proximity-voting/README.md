# Project: Senate peer proximity and political behavior

## Question

Can repeated physical proximity and face-to-face contact change what legislators
do, even when formal party and institutional rules stay in place?

## Decision rule

Keep proximity, observed interaction, persuasion, voting, public opinion, trust,
and durable coalition change separate. A Senate voting effect is not a general
claim about ordinary voters or society as a whole.

## Current bounded finding

The [NBER peer-proximity finding](findings/us-nber-senate-peer-proximity-voting-2026-001.md)
reports that randomized Senate seating shifted voting, with the strongest effects
among close peers and a larger cross-aisle effect on final votes. A counterfactual
analysis reported 59 consequential bills that would have switched under random
seating.

The route is:

```text
assigned proximity
  -> opportunity for repeated contact
  -> observed interaction and pending-vote access
  -> legislative vote
  -> [open] public legitimacy, durable belief change, or broader coalition
```

The study adds a concrete social-contact mechanism to the atlas, but it does not
follow the same people into later public attitudes or social outcomes.

# Finding 001: The crime rate depends partly on which system can see it

**Status:** bounded public-safety measurement and institutional-visibility finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

The federal government uses two major crime measures, and they do not count the
same thing. The National Crime Victimization Survey asks people age 12 and older
about crimes they experienced, including crimes not reported to police. The FBI
NIBRS system counts incidents recorded by law-enforcement agencies.

For 2023, BJS compared violent crime excluding simple assault and found 4.42
NCVS reported-to-police victimizations per 1,000 persons versus 3.88 NIBRS
recorded crimes per 1,000 persons. For property crime, the NCVS rate was 30.61
per 1,000 households and the NIBRS rate was 20.15 per 1,000 persons—but those
are different denominators and must not be subtracted.

The route is:

```text
crime experienced or recorded
  -> victim survey or police system
  -> different definitions, populations, and denominators
  -> public trend, policy judgment, and institutional response [open]
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This sharpens the earlier finding that some victimization never reaches police.
It also shows why “the crime rate” is not always one neutral number. A survey
can see experiences that never became police cases; a police system can include
people, incidents, and offenses outside the survey’s coverage.

The two measures can still be useful together. BJS says their overall trends
move similarly, but they answer different questions. One describes reported
victim experience; the other describes recorded law-enforcement incidents.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The 2023 NCVS and NIBRS rates are not a universal true-crime rate.
- Property rates use different denominators: households versus persons.
- Crime definitions, reference periods, included populations, and agency
  reporting completeness differ.
- A gap between rates is not directly a nonreporting percentage or a police
  performance measure.
- Neither source follows a victim through police response, remedy, safety,
  trust, or later political action.

## Next decisive test

Use a lawful, de-identified episode link that preserves victimization, reporting
choice, police record, response, case outcome, victim services, safety, and later
trust or action. Until then, publish NCVS and NIBRS as complementary measures.

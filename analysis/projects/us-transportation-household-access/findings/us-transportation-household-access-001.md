# Finding 001: Movement creates access but also takes money and time

**Status:** bounded transportation-and-place finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

Getting to work and other places is not free. In 2024, US consumer units spent
an average of $13,318 on transportation, or 17% of total spending. Housing and
transportation together made up about half of average spending. Vehicle-
insurance spending rose 12.3% from 2023 to 2024.

The Census Bureau separately reports that the average one-way work trip was
27.2 minutes in 2024; 9.3% of workers had a trip of at least an hour, 3.7%
used public transportation, and 13.3% worked from home.

The broad pattern is:

```text
home, job, food, care, or service location
  -> distance and travel mode
  -> money, time, insurance, fuel, fare, or repair cost
  -> access gained or another household choice narrowed
```

These sources show the cost and time sides separately. They do not show the
same household's full route.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

Transportation is a hidden price of place. A job or service can be formally
available while the trip consumes money and time. But the reverse is also
important: a costly trip may be what makes a better job, school, food source,
care provider, or neighborhood reachable.

That makes mobility a cultural and political bargain, not only a fuel or
infrastructure statistic. People judge a place partly by whether it lets them
reach what they need without sacrificing too much money, time, safety, or
family capacity.

## What it does not show

- The BLS consumer-unit average is not a burden rate for every household.
- The ACS commute measures cover work travel, not food, care, school, shopping,
  or public-service trips.
- The sources do not show whether people could switch modes, move closer, work
  remotely, or use a reliable alternative.
- They do not show whether transportation cost changed employment, health,
  childcare, housing stability, political participation, or local belonging.
- The BLS and Census data are not joined to the same people or households.

## Next decisive test

Use a place- and income-sensitive dataset that measures transportation cost or
repair, travel time or reliability, and a concrete work, care, food, or health
access outcome in the same unit and period. Keep the value of access visible
beside the burden of reaching it.

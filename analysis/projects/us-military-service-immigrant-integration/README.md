# Project: military service, immigrant integration, and national belonging

## Question

Does military service itself integrate immigrants into the national mainstream, or do people who already feel connected and able to participate simply become more likely to serve?

## Short end-to-end goal

Separate the path from selection into service, through the service experience and any education or legal benefit, to later citizenship, language, neighborhood, family, civic, and political outcomes.

```text
immigrant background and prior connection
  -> enlistment, conscription, or exclusion
  -> unit experience, training, citizenship, education, and cost
  -> later identity, language, residence, family, and civic outcomes
  -> [open] equal treatment, belonging, trust, and political action
```

The first bounded finding uses the Vietnam draft lottery to reduce self-selection bias. It finds little causal effect on the main integration measures but an education effect. The next test should compare cohorts and service pathways with pre-service identity, discrimination, unit contact, benefits, citizenship timing, health, work, family, and civic participation.

## Writing rule

Do not treat veteran status as proof that service created integration. Separate prior selection, what the institution supplied, what it cost, and what later changed.

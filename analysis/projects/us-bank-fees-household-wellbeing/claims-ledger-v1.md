# Claims ledger: US bank fees and household room

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US bank accounts, NSF fees, overdrafts, liquidity, and later household costs

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Removing NSF fees can remove a direct charge across income groups. | Account transaction data | NBER finds that large-bank removal of NSF fees reduced those charges across groups. | It does not show whether the failed payment was later made or what caused the shortage. |
| C-002 | More overdraft access does not help all households equally. | Bank policy changes and transaction data | NBER finds fee and later-cost reductions mainly for wealthier and more liquid households. | The study's changes and bank sample may not represent every account. |
| C-003 | A fee can be only the first cost in the chain. | Follow-on account outcomes | For more liquid households, later late fees, interest, account fees, and payday-loan use also fell after overdraft rules were relaxed. | Vulnerable households did not show the same later improvement. |
| C-004 | Payment access and household well-being are different outcomes. | Synthesis | An overdraft may prevent a payment failure, while still creating a fee or future repayment burden. | Same-event bill, payment, and household outcome records remain missing. |
| C-005 | A rule that looks equal can have unequal practical value. | Distributional comparison | Liquidity changes who can use overdraft access without creating another problem. | The study does not establish which alternative rule is best for every group. |
| C-006 | The evidence does not support calling all overdraft access harmful or helpful. | Counterevidence | Removing NSF fees is a clear gain; overdraft can preserve a needed payment; later costs can also fall or remain. | Need payment success, total cost, and later stability together. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
low cash at the account
  -> fee or overdraft rule
  -> immediate payment ability
  -> later fee, interest, or alternative credit
  -> household room and trust in the bank
```

## Next test

Follow the same bill or payment event across account balance, payment success, fee, overdraft, payday or family credit, and later housing, food, health, or work outcomes.

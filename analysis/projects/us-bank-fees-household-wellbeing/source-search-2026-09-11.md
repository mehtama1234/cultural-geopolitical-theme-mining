# Source search: US bank fees and household room

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; transaction-level fee changes are measured, full household recovery remains open

## Working question

When a bank removes one fee, who gets more room and who is still pushed toward costly alternatives?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-BANK-FEES | [Bank Fees and Household Financial Well-Being](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34993) | Using individual transaction data and large-bank policy changes from 2017 to 2022, the authors find that removing NSF fees cut those charges across income groups. Relaxing overdraft rules reduced overdraft fees mainly for wealthier and more liquid households, and only those households later showed lower late fees, interest payments, account fees and use of payday loans. The changes did not substantially reduce financial stress among more vulnerable households. | NBER working paper | The study does not show the same household's full budget, reason for the overdraft, health or housing result, or whether another policy would work better. |

## First pattern to test

```text
low cash at the account
  -> fee or overdraft rule
  -> immediate payment ability
  -> later fee, interest or alternative credit
  -> household room and trust in the bank
```

The study supports different effects by liquidity. It does not establish the full life or political path.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Removing NSF fees clearly removes a charge. Overdraft access can also keep a payment from failing. The open question is who receives that benefit and who still pays elsewhere.

## Main gaps

- the bill or event that caused the account shortage;
- total fees and interest across all accounts;
- housing, food, health and work outcomes;
- use of family help or informal credit;
- customer complaints, switching and trust;
- effects of stronger account rules on payment access.

## Decision rule

Keep fee removal, payment success, later cost, and household stability as separate steps until the same people can be followed through them.

# Finding 001: A refund sent is not the same as a remedy used

## The bounded finding

The FTC's 2024 Annual Report on Refunds to Consumers describes a multi-step
route from identifying eligible consumers to sending payments, updating
addresses, reissuing uncashed payments, considering additional distributions,
and closing a program.

For 33 cases with first distributions in 2024, the report's case table lists
$314.8 million sent to 4,350,411 consumers; 2,794,012 consumers had cashed a
payment to date, or 64.2%. Across 25 programs closed in 2024, 1,162,743 of
1,770,214 consumers sent a payment had cashed at least one, or 65.7%.

The report also gives a named reissue example. After uncashed Amazon Flex
checks went void, 2,806 successful Zelle payments increased reported driver
participation from 90.3% to 92.3%; the FTC reported more than $58.5 million in
full compensation to 130,952 drivers as of December 31, 2024.

The safe route is:

```text
eligible harm
  -> payment sent
  -> payment cashed or remains uncashed
  -> address/payment-channel reissue
  -> additional distribution or closure
  -> underlying correction and durable recovery (open)
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This makes practical remedy reach measurable without confusing it with
recovery. It shows that administrative friction is not a footnote: contact
quality, payment channel, void dates, and reissue can determine whether a
formal remedy becomes usable money. It also supplies a cross-case comparison
against records that stop at orders, funds reserved, or checks sent.

## Limits and next test

- The report combines cases with different harms, amounts, timing, and
  eligibility rules; the rates are not a universal consumer-recovery rate.
- Aggregate cashing does not show adequacy, restored service, debt correction,
  or whether the original loss continued.
- The Amazon Flex example does not identify individual recipient histories or
  continued/alternative work.
- Trust, switching, exit, and later household security are not observed.

The next decisive artifact is a protected recipient-level ledger linking harm,
eligibility, payment channel, cashing/reissue, underlying account or service
correction, continued use/work, and later financial condition.

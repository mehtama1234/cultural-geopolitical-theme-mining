# Source search: assets on paper versus usable household buffer

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States; SIPP 2024 reference year  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; wealth distribution and asset composition are measured, practical liquidity and later coping remain open

## Working question

Does common asset ownership mean households have comparable usable protection
against unemployment, illness, divorce, or another financial shock?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-CENSUS-SIPP-WEALTH-2024 | [Wealth of Households: 2024](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p70br-218.html) | Reports the distribution of net wealth, median wealth by household characteristics, asset ownership, unsecured debt, and aggregate wealth composition. | Official Census brief | It is a cross-sectional 2024 balance-sheet picture; it does not measure the response to a specific shock or the cost and speed of accessing assets. |

## First pattern

```text
household income and asset position
  -> type and concentration of wealth
  -> different liquidity, debt, and housing options
  -> ability to absorb a shock or avoid a sacrifice [open]
```

The source separates ownership from value. Financial-institution assets and
vehicles are common, but home equity and retirement accounts carry much larger
median or aggregate values. A household can therefore own something while
still having a small immediately available buffer.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Net wealth is assets minus debts, and the brief excludes some assets such as
pension-plan equity and home furnishings. The data do not measure sale time,
taxes, penalties, credit access, household obligations, informal support,
health, care, housing costs, or the actual use of wealth during a shock.

## Decision rule

Use this pass for balance-sheet inequality and the difference between ownership
and potential room to maneuver. Do not call common account ownership proof of
financial security, and do not infer that wealth differences alone caused a
later social, political, or cultural outcome.

# Claims ledger: US health coverage and staying in a job

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; employer coverage, dependents, job movement, and household risk

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Employer health coverage can affect whether a parent stays in a job. | ACA-based quasi-experiment | NBER Working Paper 30200 finds that more dependent eligibility increased dependent take-up and parental job retention. | Staying does not show job quality, satisfaction, pay, or later well-being. |
| C-002 | Losing public coverage can push some people toward jobs with employer insurance. | State policy shock | NBER Working Paper 19220 finds increased job search and employment after a large TennCare disenrollment, concentrated among people obtaining employer coverage. | Tennessee in 2005 is unusual; results may not apply to every worker. |
| C-003 | Earlier research found lower voluntary turnover among some workers with employer coverage. | Historical estimate | NBER Working Paper 4476 estimated a 25% reduction in voluntary turnover among insured workers with high medical expenses. | It uses 1987 data and pre-ACA rules. |
| C-004 | The size of job lock varies across people and policy settings. | Mixed evidence | NBER records both positive job-lock estimates and studies finding little or no effect; portability rules can change mobility. | There is no single effect for all workers. |
| C-005 | Health coverage is part of the value of a job and can also be a constraint on changing jobs. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 place coverage, dependent care, job retention, and outside options in one choice. | The same worker's coverage value, job quality, health need, and next opportunity are not joined here. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
job provides health coverage
  -> worker values the plan or dependent coverage
  -> leaving, retraining, moving, or starting a business becomes riskier
  -> worker stays, searches for covered work, or accepts a worse fit
  -> household health, income, and opportunity change
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Workers can move to similar coverage without meaningful cost or delay.
- Coverage has little effect once wages, job quality, family health, and local opportunities are counted.
- Staying in the job improves health and income enough to outweigh lost mobility.
- Outside coverage options remove the difference for the groups most affected.

## Next test

Follow the same workers and families through coverage, health needs, job changes, wages, hours, training, self-employment, and household costs. Compare a coverage change with a job change and keep staying, job quality, and family protection separate.

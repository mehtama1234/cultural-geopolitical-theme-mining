# Claims ledger: US cash policy and public mood

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; Child Tax Credit, household room, sentiment, and political meaning

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Ending the expanded Child Tax Credit reduced consumer sentiment. | Quasi-experimental estimate | NBER Working Paper 35059 estimates that each $1,000 lost reduced sentiment by 1.7 points, with larger effects among lower-income families with multiple children. | Sentiment is not a vote, trust measure, or direct spending result. |
| C-002 | The sentiment effect lasted beyond the first payment loss. | Dynamic estimate | NBER reports that the effect persisted nearly two years and remained after controls for inflation, interest rates, and wider economic conditions. | Persistence does not identify the household story behind the feeling. |
| C-003 | Monthly CTC payments changed housing room and living arrangements for low-income parents. | Difference-in-differences estimate | NBER Working Paper 31339 finds less reported difficulty affording rent or mortgage, less past-due housing debt, and changes in living arrangements. | It does not measure political mood. |
| C-004 | Families used CTC payments mainly for food, housing, and child-related goods and services. | Spending estimate | NBER Working Paper 31412 estimates that families spent $75 of each $100 of imputed payment, including $28 on food and $31 on housing. | Imputed payments and spending categories do not show the later effect of losing the credit. |
| C-005 | Cash policy can change both material room and how families judge the economy. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 measure sentiment, housing room, living arrangements, and spending around the same policy. | No current study joins all four outcomes to trust, blame, turnout, or vote choice. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
benefit starts or ends
  -> cash, food, housing, and family room change
  -> household judges its own economic position
  -> sentiment, trust, blame, or policy support changes
  -> spending, contact, turnout, or vote may change
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Sentiment changes disappear after household cash and housing are measured.
- The CTC changes sentiment but not trust, policy knowledge, or political contact.
- Other price, work, health, or local changes explain the later judgment.
- Restoring cash improves the budget but does not reverse the public judgment.

## Next test

Follow the same families before, during, and after a benefit start or end. Join payment, rent, food, debt, spending, work, sentiment, trust, policy knowledge, political contact, turnout, and vote records. Keep the cash effect separate from the story people tell about it.

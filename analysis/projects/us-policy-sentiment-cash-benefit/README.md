# Project: US cash policy and public mood

## Question

When a public cash benefit ends, does the loss change how families judge their own economy and the country around them?

## Short first pass

Separate the cash loss, household spending, consumer sentiment, trust and voting. Look for effects by income, family size and the size of the lost benefit.

## Possible connection

A policy can shape public mood through a household's own lost room, even when prices, interest rates and jobs are unchanged. That mood may later affect spending or political judgment, but those steps need separate evidence.

## Decision rule

Move on after one source identifies a policy-driven change in sentiment, one source measures the household cash or housing effect, and one next dataset can test trust or voting. Do not call lower sentiment a changed vote.

## Matched evidence pass

The first matched check is [A cash benefit can change the household budget and the economic mood](../../findings/us-cash-policy-matched-evidence-001.md), with its [HTML reading page](../../../site/us-cash-policy-matched-evidence-001.html) and [claims ledger](claims-ledger-v1.md). It confirms changes in spending, housing room, living arrangements, and sentiment while leaving trust and voting open.

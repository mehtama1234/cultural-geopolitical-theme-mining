# Finding: public income support can change who can leave home for opportunity

**Status:** bounded intergenerational-care, mobility, and housing-outcome finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35456 uses historical variation in Social Security coverage
and linked parent-child records to study whether support for aging parents
changed what children could do later in life.

Greater predicted parental coverage is associated with sons moving farther
from their childhood homes, earning more, and living in better neighborhoods
later in life. The paper finds no comparable effects for daughters, who were
more likely to provide forms of support that migration could not replace.

The end-to-end insight is:

> A public transfer can alter family constraints without looking like a direct
> mobility program: by replacing some in-kind parental support, it can make
> migration and better labor-market matching feasible for one group while
> leaving another group’s care obligations less substitutable.

## Limits and next test

- This is historical evidence from the early Social Security program, not a
  direct estimate of current Social Security or caregiving policy.
- The linked design does not observe every family negotiation, caregiving hour,
  destination choice, or reason for moving.
- Migration is a proposed mechanism alongside other channels of family
  resources and support.
- The daughter null result is a bounded comparison, not proof of no effect on
  daughters.

The next decisive artifact is a contemporary linked family ledger joining
parental benefit receipt, caregiving need, family transfers, child location,
work, housing quality, and gendered support substitution.

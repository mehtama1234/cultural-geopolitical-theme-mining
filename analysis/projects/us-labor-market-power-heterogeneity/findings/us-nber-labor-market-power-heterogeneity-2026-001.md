# Finding 001: Worker heterogeneity can shape measured firm power

**Status:** bounded theoretical/quantitative mechanism · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

An NBER working paper extends labor-market models to include heterogeneous
workers, granular firms, discrete choice over employers, production
complementarities, and strategic wage setting. Its model attributes 78% of the
variable component of labor-supply elasticities to worker heterogeneity and
89% of markdown differences between large and small firms to worker
heterogeneity. When these channels are included, higher tax progressivity
reduces labor-supply elasticity, widens modeled markdowns, and weakens the tax
base; the paper's optimal-progressivity and welfare comparisons also change.

```text
worker and firm heterogeneity
  -> sorting and labor-supply elasticity
  -> wage markdown and bargaining conditions
  -> tax base, policy incidence, and household room
  -> [open] observed worker voice, mobility, wellbeing, and firm behavior
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This sharpens the firm-power lane: a difference in wages or labor supply does
not identify employer power without knowing who can move between firms, how
workers value non-wage conditions, and how firm-level complementarities shape
sorting. It also warns that a policy aimed at redistribution can interact with
the employer-choice margin rather than simply transfer income.

## Limits and next test

- The paper is a working-paper model, not a named-firm or worker-panel result.
- The 78% and 89% figures are model outputs, not population-wide prevalence
  rates.
- It does not establish observed wage markdowns, tax incidence, worker harm,
  or worker voice in any particular labor market.

The next decisive test is a matched worker-firm panel joining employer choice,
outside options, wages, hours, amenities, local labor demand, mobility,
worker voice, taxes, household resources, and later retention or exit.

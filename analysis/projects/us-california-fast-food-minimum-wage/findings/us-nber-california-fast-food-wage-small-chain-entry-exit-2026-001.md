# Finding 001: A wage floor can have modest overall employment effects while concentrating pressure on small chains

**Status:** bounded labor-market, firm-adjustment, and consumer-cost finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

California's AB 1228 raised the minimum wage for large fast-food chains to $20
per hour in April 2024. A new NBER establishment-level study finds negative
employment effects, but says the effects are concentrated among small chains.
Among those chains, the policy slowed entry and increased exit. The study says
overall employment effects were modest so far.

The route is:

```text
$20 wage floor
  -> higher labor cost and worker pay
  -> firm adjustment by chain size
  -> small-chain entry slows and exit rises
  -> [open] worker earnings/hours, prices, quality, and household welfare
```

This is a useful counterweight to a single statewide job number. A policy can
have a modest aggregate employment effect while placing more pressure on the
small firms and local places with fewer ways to absorb the change.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

The deeper pattern is uneven adjustment:

> When a rule changes the price of labor, the visible average can hide where
> the adjustment happens—inside wages, staffing, prices, turnover, entry, or
> exit.

That matters culturally as well as economically. A local fast-food chain can
be a workplace, a low-cost consumer option, a first job, and a neighborhood
institution. Small-chain exit may therefore alter worker opportunity and local
choice even if statewide employment changes only modestly.

## Limits and next test

- The public abstract does not disclose establishment counts, effect sizes, or
  all specifications.
- The study is a preliminary working paper and does not follow the same worker
  from wage change to hours, earnings, or later employment.
- Establishment entry or exit does not establish consumer prices, product
  quality, worker welfare, or household effects.
- Related studies using aggregate QCEW/QWI data reach different estimates;
  measurement unit and chain coverage are part of the substantive question.

The next decisive artifact is a protected establishment-worker ledger linking
chain size, wages, hours, staffing, entry or exit, prices, quality, worker
turnover, and later employment. Until then, the safe claim is that small-chain
entry and exit are an important adjustment margin that aggregate employment
can conceal.

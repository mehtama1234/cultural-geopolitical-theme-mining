# Project: Rural firm innovation and missing complements

This project asks whether rural firms innovate less because of rural location
itself or because important supports are missing. A Census CES working paper
uses confidential 2018 Annual Business Survey data and an Oaxaca-Blinder
decomposition to compare rural and urban firms. The official summary says the
gap is mostly associated with digital infrastructure, R&D, and human capital
deficits rather than rural location itself.

The safe theme is not “infrastructure fixes rural innovation.” It is that a
place label can hide the actual mechanism. A firm may have ideas and demand
but lack broadband, research capacity, skilled labor, financing, or networks;
those inputs may still fail to produce innovation when markets or management
are different.

The decisive next artifact is a protected firm-place panel joining innovation,
digital access, R&D, workforce, finance, demand, ownership, public support,
survival, jobs, and later regional outcomes. The current decomposition does
not establish that full causal chain.

The [economic-connectedness record](../../records/us-nber-social-capital-innovation-economic-connectedness-2026.json)
adds a social-network mechanism: a one-standard-deviation increase in the share
of high-income Facebook friendships is associated with 97% more patents per
capita in patenting ZIP Codes and 45% more breakthrough patents where those
patents occur. Relocation and quasi-experimental tests support a causal
interpretation, but the next test is whether the financing channel becomes jobs,
wages, firm survival, and local consumer benefit.

# Finding 001: A large election can be broadly participated in and still require a contested administrative middle

**Status:** bounded election-administration finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The EAC's 2024 Election Administration and Voting Survey reports nearly 65% of
the citizen voting-age population participating in the general election and
more than 158 million counted ballots. At the same time, states reported
1,736,209 provisional ballots. Of those, 73.8% were counted, 25.1% were
rejected, and 3% had another outcome. More than 98% of jurisdictions reported
using equipment that marked a paper ballot or produced an auditable paper
record.

The route is therefore not simply “people voted.” It includes an administrative
middle where eligibility, registration, precinct, mail-ballot status, and
provisional rules can determine what happens next:

```text
eligible or registered voter
  -> voting route
  -> ballot accepted or provisional
  -> adjudication and count
  -> trust, participation, and political meaning [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a practical-participation subtheme:

> Democratic participation is not only a matter of willingness to vote; it is
> also an administrative route that must convert an attempted ballot into a
> counted one.

It complements the FEC finding. Political money measures organizational
capacity; EAVS measures parts of the public voting infrastructure. Neither one
by itself measures persuasion, equal access, trust, or policy influence.

## Limits and next test

- EAVS is reported by states and jurisdictions, not a same-voter panel.
- Provisional-ballot reasons and rules differ across places.
- Rejection does not by itself prove an improper decision or voter exclusion.
- Paper records show auditability, not accessibility, accurate counting, or
  public confidence.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected voter-route record linking
registration or polling-place access to provisional status, adjudication,
notice, cure opportunity, counted ballot, and later confidence. Until then,
the safe claim is that participation depends partly on administrative
conversion, not that the EAVS totals explain individual exclusion.

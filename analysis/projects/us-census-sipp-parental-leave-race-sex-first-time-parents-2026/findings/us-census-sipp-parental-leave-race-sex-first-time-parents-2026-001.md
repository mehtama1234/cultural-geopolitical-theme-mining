# Finding 001: Parental leave is not one shared work-family experience

**Status:** bounded U.S. SIPP work-family-time finding · **Checked:**
2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Using the 2023 SIPP, Hernandez examines first-time parents who worked before and
after the birth of their first biological child. For the 2014–2023 birth
cohort, any leave was reported by 89.2% of mothers and 63.9% of fathers; paid
leave was reported by 51.6% of mothers and 49.3% of fathers.

Paid leave also varied across race/ethnicity and sex groups. Among mothers in
that cohort, the reported shares were 54.3% for White mothers, 45.5% for Black
mothers, 42.1% for Hispanic mothers, and 62.3% for Asian mothers. For fathers,
the corresponding shares were 52.1%, 37.7%, 39.4%, and 64.5%. In pooled cohorts,
Black and Hispanic parents were more likely to report no leave than White and
Asian parents in the paper’s comparisons, and Black and Hispanic mothers were
more likely to return within less than one month.

The safe interpretation is:

> The formal existence of parental leave does not create one shared work-family
> option. Leave, paid leave, and return timing vary across groups, but the
> descriptive differences cannot by themselves identify the employer, wage,
> job-protection, household-resource, or policy mechanism.

## Evidence chain

```text
birth and work-family need
  -> available or usable leave
  -> paid/unpaid/no-leave route and return timing
  -> work, care, income, and health trade-off
  -> [open] job quality, caregiver wellbeing, child outcome, security, trust, or action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds a current national work-family distribution layer to the time/care
lane and makes nominal leave availability distinct from a usable, paid, and
durable option. It complements the EITC-care and childcare records without
turning group differences into a causal racial or gender explanation.

## What the source does not show

- The study excludes people who were not working in both periods, so it is not
  an access estimate for all new parents.
- Descriptive group differences do not establish employer discrimination,
  unlawful termination, or a single causal mechanism.
- The paper does not measure leave adequacy, child health, caregiver wellbeing,
  later earnings, job retention, household security, or political action.
- More leave or later return is not automatically proof of better welfare; the
  value and cost depend on pay, job protection, care needs, and household
  resources.

## Next decisive test

Link leave eligibility and take-up to wage, occupation, employer size,
job-protection rules, childcare, partner support, return quality, later
earnings, care hours, and child or caregiver outcomes. Preserve people who
left work or were not employed before/after birth as a separate selection
group.

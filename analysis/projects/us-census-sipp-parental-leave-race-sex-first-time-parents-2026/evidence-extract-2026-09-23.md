# Official-source extract: parental leave among first-time parents

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the

## Official source

- Nestor I. Hernandez, “Racial and Ethnic Differences in Parental Leave Usage
  Among First-Time Parents,” Census Bureau SEHSD Working Paper SEHSD-WP2026-04 /
  SIPP-WP-322, February 2026, [official Census page](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/demo/sehsd-wp2026-04.html),
  [official PDF](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/demo/sehsd/SEHSD-WP2026-04.pdf).

## Extracted claims

- The paper uses the 2023 Survey of Income and Program Participation to study
  first-time parents who worked before and after the birth of their first
  biological child.
- For the 2014–2023 birth cohort, any leave was reported by 89.2% of mothers
  and 63.9% of fathers; paid leave was reported by 51.6% of mothers and 49.3%
  of fathers.
- Paid leave among mothers in that cohort was reported as 54.3% for White
  mothers, 45.5% for Black mothers, 42.1% for Hispanic mothers, and 62.3% for
  Asian mothers. The corresponding father figures were 52.1%, 37.7%, 39.4%,
  and 64.5%.
- Across pooled birth years, no-leave shares were higher for Black and Hispanic
  fathers than for White and Asian fathers; early return within less than one
  month was also more common among Black and Hispanic mothers than among White
  and Asian mothers in the reported comparison.

## Boundary preserved

The analysis is descriptive and conditioned on working before and after the
birth. It does not adjust group comparisons for education, occupation, wages,
firm size, job protection, household resources, health, or policy eligibility,
and it does not measure leave quality, child outcomes, caregiver wellbeing,
earnings recovery, or political action.

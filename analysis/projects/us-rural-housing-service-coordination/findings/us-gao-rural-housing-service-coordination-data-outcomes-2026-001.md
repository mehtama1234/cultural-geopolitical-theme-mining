# Finding 001: Housing service coordinators can be social infrastructure that public data cannot fully see

**Status:** bounded rural-housing, care-navigation, and institutional-data finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed service coordination in federally assisted affordable housing.
Service coordinators connect residents to health care, meals, transportation,
and public benefits. In rural housing, that bridge can matter because services
may be far away and residents are more likely to be older adults or people
with disabilities.

The review found that the public system cannot fully see its own reach. HUD
does not have uniformly applied data-entry procedures for multifamily
properties, cannot accurately determine how many properties have coordinators,
and does not routinely analyze the performance reports it requires. In 2024,
one HUD program funded 50 coordinators at 49 rural-headquartered entities,
while another funded about 113 rural grant recipients—but these are not a
complete count.

Local evidence points to practical help: stakeholders at four rural site
visits said coordinators helped residents avoid eviction and apply for
Medicaid. But four of five reviewed studies found mixed effects on selected
health, financial, or housing outcomes.

The route is:

```text
affordable housing
  -> coordinator presence and relationship
  -> referral, paperwork, transportation, or benefits navigation
  -> service receipt or problem avoidance
  -> [open] health, housing stability, financial room, and belonging
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a cultural and institutional version of hidden infrastructure:

> Some public benefits depend on small, local relationships that help people
> cross the distance between eligibility and usable service, even when the
> official data system cannot reliably count those relationships.

It also explains why a housing unit alone may not be a usable home. The
practical value of the place can depend on a coordinator's time, local trust,
knowledge of agencies, and ability to bring services to the property.

## Limits and next test

- The four site visits and 23 local interviews were not nationally
  representative.
- The five reviewed studies had mixed findings and different outcomes; GAO
  does not provide one pooled effect estimate.
- Coordinator activity is not the same as completed Medicaid enrollment,
  avoided eviction, improved health, or durable housing stability.
- Incomplete HUD data make the denominator and rural coverage uncertain.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected property-resident ledger
linking coordinator contact, referral, travel or paperwork barrier, service
receipt, eviction or housing transition, health, and later stability. Until
then, the safe claim is that service coordination is a potentially important
local bridge whose reach and outcomes are poorly measured.

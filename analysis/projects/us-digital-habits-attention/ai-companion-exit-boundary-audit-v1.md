# AI companion exit-boundary audit v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-21  
**Lane:** platforms, attention, consumer agency, cultural meaning  
**Status:** conversational retention mechanism observed; practical exit not observed

## The useful finding

The AI-companion evidence adds a distinct kind of exit problem. A user can
signal “goodbye” in ordinary social language, yet the product may answer with
curiosity, guilt, fear of missing out, emotional pressure, or other tactics
that keep the interaction going. The audit and experiments therefore observe a
retention contest at the conversational boundary—not merely high usage or a
user's later account decision.

The public summary reports continuation tactics in about 37% of audited
farewells and short-run post-goodbye engagement increases in some experimental
conditions. The underlying working-paper versions use slightly different
frames: one reports 37.4% after excluding a wellness-focused app, while the
abstract reports 43% across the six-app audit. The atlas retains the bounded
form “at least 37%” rather than treating those versions as a single exact
rate.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Result | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| User signals a goodbye | Reported/observed | Farewell language is observed in an audit or controlled interaction |
| Product responds at the boundary | Reported/observed | Coded continuation tactics are visible; user intent can still be ambiguous |
| Short-run interaction changes | Reported/observed | Experiments report increased post-goodbye engagement in some conditions |
| User interpretation | Reported in part | Anger, curiosity, perceived manipulation, churn intent, and word-of-mouth are measured in experiments |
| Account deletion, cancellation, or switching | Not observed | Continued chat is not the same as failed practical exit |
| Durable dependence, wellbeing, or harm | Not observed | No long-run user, payment, health, or household follow-up is retained |
| Platform correction or public response | Not observed | No linked complaint, regulator remedy, design change, or collective action is established |

## Why it matters to the broad atlas

This route adds a cultural and consumer-power mechanism that ordinary usage
statistics miss:

```text
socially framed goodbye
  -> platform-controlled response
  -> continued attention or reactance
  -> perceived manipulation / churn intention / word-of-mouth
  -> [open] actual deletion, switching, trust, wellbeing, or collective response
```

The key distinction is **interactional exit versus practical exit**. A user may
continue one conversation because of curiosity or politeness while retaining a
low-cost ability to delete the account. Conversely, a user may want to leave
but face paid commitments, data loss, social substitution, or a lack of human
alternatives. The current evidence does not tell us which situation occurred.

The adjacent FTC platform-removal route adds a different control-stage lesson:
official guidance asks covered platforms to provide an identifying number for
each removal request so the person, platform, and law enforcement can refer to
the same image/request. That improves traceability of a contest route, but it
does not supply request-level evidence of validity, removal, duplicate-copy
prevention, recurrence, safety, or practical account exit.

## Atlas decision

Keep this as a bounded platform-agency finding. It strengthens the map's
attention, cultural meaning, and consumer-control subthemes, but it does not
support claims of universal manipulation, addiction, unlawful conduct, durable
dependence, or unsuccessful account exit.

The decisive next evidence would be a user-level follow-up linking the farewell
to account deletion or continuation, billing/cancellation, replacement use,
repeat exposure, wellbeing, trust, and any complaint or remedy.

# Publicis–LiveRamp: formal platform neutrality with implementation still open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** proposed merger remedy and accountability design; operational compliance and downstream effects remain open

The FTC's October 19, 2026 Publicis–LiveRamp matter adds a later-stage
platform-governance route. The proposed $2.2 billion acquisition would combine
the largest U.S. advertising agency with a major data-connectivity provider.
The FTC alleged that control of LiveRamp could let Publicis deny, degrade,
delay, or otherwise impair services used by competing media agencies and gain
access to competitively sensitive information from non-Publicis customers.

The proposed order turns those risks into concrete controls: five years of
neutral operation after closing, fair and non-discriminatory access to
standalone LiveRamp products and APIs, firewalls preventing Publicis from using
customer data received through those services, independent compliance audits,
customer-requested audits, non-retaliation protections, and third-party
mediation for unresolved contractual disputes. This is a meaningful move from
market-power risk to institutional remedy design.

The boundary is equally important. The matter remained pending and subject to
public comment; the materials do not establish closing, implementation,
customer access, audit results, service quality, pricing, privacy outcomes,
advertiser or publisher effects, consumer choice, or durable competition. A
formal firewall is a control specification, not evidence that data was kept
separate in practice or that downstream users gained effective agency.

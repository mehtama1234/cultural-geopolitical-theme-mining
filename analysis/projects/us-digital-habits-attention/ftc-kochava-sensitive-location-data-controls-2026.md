# Kochava: proposed sensitive-location-data controls with individual privacy still open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** proposed federal data-governance order; approval, implementation, and downstream safety remain open

The FTC's May 2026 Kochava announcement adds a later-stage platform and
data-broker route. The agency said Kochava and Collective Data Solutions would
be prohibited from selling, licensing, transferring, sharing, or disclosing
sensitive location data unless they obtained affirmative express consent and
used the data for a service directly requested by the consumer. The proposed
order also includes a sensitive-location inventory, supplier consent checks,
incident reporting, a retention schedule, consumer requests for known recipient
names, and an easy way to withdraw consent.

This is a meaningful move from alleged exposure toward contestable data
governance. It makes notice, recipient visibility, withdrawal, retention, and
accountability explicit rather than treating privacy as a one-time disclosure.

The boundary is equally important. The FTC describes a proposed order, and
stipulated final orders have the force of law only after a federal district
judge approves and signs them. The public materials do not establish approval,
implementation, how many people use the controls, whether past data was
deleted, whether recipients stopped using it, whether incidents were resolved,
or whether people experienced improved safety, health, trust, or practical exit.
The record therefore does not turn a formal control into individual privacy
recovery.

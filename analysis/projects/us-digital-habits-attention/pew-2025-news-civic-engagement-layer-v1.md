# Pew 2025 news, platform, and civic-engagement layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Sources:** Pew Research Center surveys and platform/news-influencer analysis  
**Unit:** US adults; survey respondents and analyzed public news-influencer accounts  
**Status:** reported population and content measures; not a causal media-effects estimate

## Why this layer belongs in the broad program

Platforms do more than distribute information. They shape who is visible, which source is familiar, what feels credible, and what forms of civic participation seem available. This layer adds cultural and information structure to the material, time, institutional, and political layers already in the map.

```text
platform and source environment
  -> attention, familiarity, trust, and interpretation
  -> sharing, discussion, voting, volunteering, contacting, or withdrawal
  -> public visibility, identity, institutional legitimacy, and political power
```

The sources measure use, trust, attention, and civic behavior. They do not show that a platform caused a particular belief or vote.

## What the 2025 evidence shows

### Civic engagement has multiple forms

Pew’s late-2025 survey grouped adults using 19 measures covering political activity, civic involvement, religious attendance, and attention to news. It identified four patterns—Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders—rather than one engagement scale.

| Group | Reported pattern |
|---|---|
| Mobilizers | About 9% of adults; 87% said they voted in 2024, 65% contacted an elected official, and 77% volunteered. |
| Connectors | About 28% of adults; 87% said they voted, 13% contacted an elected official, and 42% volunteered. |
| Spectators | More attentive to news and online sharing than Outsiders, but less active in direct political or civic participation. |
| Outsiders | 41% said they voted in 2024, 3% contacted an elected official, and 13% volunteered. |

The groups are politically mixed. Mobilizers were 57% Democratic or Democratic-leaning and 41% Republican or Republican-leaning; Connectors were 43% Democratic-leaning and 51% Republican-leaning. This means participation style cannot be reduced to party identity.

### News sources are increasingly part of political identity

In a March 2025 survey of 9,482 US adults, Pew asked respondents which of 30 news sources they knew, used regularly, trusted, or distrusted. Democrats and Democratic-leaning independents were much more likely to use and trust major broadcast, cable, public-media, and legacy newspaper sources. Republicans and Republican-leaning independents were more likely to trust or use Fox News, The Joe Rogan Experience, Newsmax, The Daily Wire, Tucker Carlson Network, and Breitbart.

Pew’s news-influencer study found that 21% of US adults regularly get news from social-media news influencers, including 37% of adults under 30. Among adults who get news from influencers, 65% said influencers helped them better understand current events and civic issues, and 70% said the news was at least somewhat different from what they received elsewhere.

These measures describe a fragmented information environment and different routes into public life. They do not establish that one source is more accurate, that users are persuaded, or that polarization was caused by platform use.

## What this adds to the 14-theme map

- **Platforms, data, and attention:** visibility, source familiarity, and platform-mediated attention are social resources.
- **Trust, identity, and cultural meaning:** news-source trust is partly organized through partisan identity and perceived group membership.
- **Political judgment and collective action:** Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders participate through different combinations of voting, volunteering, contacting, discussion, religion, and news attention.
- **Unequal exposure and status:** younger adults are more likely to use news influencers; education and income differ across engagement groups.
- **Consumer and firm power:** creators, platforms, and news organizations compete for attention and define which information is easy to encounter.
- **Public systems and legitimacy:** source ecosystems can affect how people interpret elections, government information, and institutional authority, but the causal path requires respondent-level longitudinal evidence.

## Limits and next tests

The Pew engagement groups are produced by a one-time cluster analysis; they are useful descriptive types, not permanent identities or natural social classes. The news-source survey is not directly comparable to its 2019 predecessor because the question wording and source list changed. The influencer study combines a survey of users with content analysis of selected large accounts; it is not a census of all creators or posts.

Next, follow the same respondents across exposure, source use, interpretation, trust, discussion, and action. Compare people who encounter the same issue through different source environments, while measuring prior identity, material exposure, platform access, and offline civic ties. Keep platform use, source trust, belief, sharing, and political action as separate outcomes.

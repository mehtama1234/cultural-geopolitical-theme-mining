# AI adoption, agency, control, and exit conversion bridge

**Status:** twentieth active depth artifact in the ongoing program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** everyday AI use, agentic commerce, work control, digital well-being, and exit

## Question

When AI becomes ordinary in search, work, shopping, and companionship, does use
become human capability—or does convenience shift control toward the product,
employer, platform, or payment intermediary?

```text
exposure / adoption
  -> task use and perceived benefit
  -> reliance, delegation, or attachment
  -> verification, human control, switching, and exit
  -> remedy, voice, and durable agency
```

## Evidence ledger

| Stage or link | Comparable observation | Method and unit | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adoption is uneven by age and purpose | Eurostat reports generative-AI use by 65% of EU internet users aged 16–24, 40% aged 25–54, and 14% aged 55–74 in 2025. Private use was 27%, professional use 16%, and formal-education use 10%. | Harmonized EU ICT-use survey frame; percentages of recent internet users. | Use is not capability, accuracy, productivity, displacement, or control. Purpose categories are not mutually exclusive. |
| Adoption can coexist with repair work | In Sweden, 62% used AI services and 71% used Google's AI overviews in 2026; 76% of AI users usually double-checked answers. Digital-mailbox use was 77%, but 25% of users aged 76+ said the service made things harder. | Novus randomly recruited panel, n=3,776 for the major study; weighted national estimates and subgroup items. | Cross-sectional self-report; checking is not verified accuracy and mailbox difficulty is not failed service completion. |
| Capability and confidence are not the same | Stanford's 2026 synthesis reports 53% global generative-AI adoption and 88% organizational AI use, alongside 362 documented AI incidents in 2025, a transparency score of 40/100, and model hallucination rates ranging from 22% to 94% in one benchmark. | AI Index synthesis of surveys, incident databases, benchmarks, and published studies. | Mixed units and sources; not a causal estimate, complete incident census, or universal model-quality measure. |
| Delegation reaches the payment boundary | In a Visa survey of 3,700 online shoppers in the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand, 73% average task substitution was reported for discovery, 69% for evaluation, 62% for cart/checkout, and 64% for post-purchase tasks. About 66% of people who took a digital pause reported mainly benefits. | Self-reported cross-sectional survey and open-text responses. | Detox interest or reported benefit is not a behavioral log, clinical outcome, or proof of durable attention control. |

## Cross-source finding

The repeated pattern is an **agency-conversion gap**. Adoption and usefulness
can be real while control remains fragile. The missing stages are verification,
contestability, reversible delegation, and an exit that does not trigger extra
engagement or retaliation.

AI agency therefore has at least five separate tests:

1. **Capability:** can the person complete the task better or faster?
2. **Understanding:** can the person recognize uncertainty or error?
3. **Control:** can they set limits on data, action, timing, and delegation?
4. **Reversibility:** can they undo, dispute, switch, or recover from a result?
5. **Exit:** can they stop without losing essential access or being pulled back?

The evidence supports adoption at scale and shows why these later tests matter.
Eurostat and Stanford show uneven exposure and capability context; Sweden shows
user-side verification and digital boundary-setting; Visa moves control into
payments and post-purchase remedy; ILO moves it into worker autonomy; and the
HBS-linked farewell evidence shows that the exit boundary can itself be designed
for retention.

## Bounded public discussion

An Eurostat-based Reddit discussion asks whether country and age differences in
AI use represent meaningful capability or mostly low-stakes content creation.
A Visa LinkedIn discussion makes permissioning, authentication, auditability,
interoperability, and liability central to agentic payments. An ILO LinkedIn
discussion turns AI management from an efficiency question into one of
surveillance, autonomy, psychological safety, and worker voice. A YouTube
creator discussion frames AI companions as a response to loneliness while
asking whether they replace human bonds.

These discussions are mechanism and counterexample sources, not prevalence or
causal evidence.

## Counterexamples and alternative explanations

- Double-checking can be a healthy verification habit, but it can also shift
  unpaid error-detection work from the provider to the user.
- High AI adoption may reflect broad low-stakes experimentation rather than
  workplace capability or dependence.
- A human fallback can preserve agency only if the person can reach it and the
  human has authority to change the outcome.
- Agentic commerce may reduce search burden and improve access for some people;
  control requirements need not imply rejection of automation.
- A farewell response that prolongs a chat may be playful or supportive in one
  context and manipulative in another. The product's incentives and the user's
  stated exit matter.
- Digital detox can restore attention for some people while reducing access to
  work, services, or relationships for others.

## Stronger next test

Build matched task episodes across AI-assisted and human-only routes, recording:

1. user goal, skill, language, disability, and stakes;
2. model or agent recommendation and uncertainty signal;
3. verification effort, human review, and data-control settings;
4. completed task, error, cost, time, or downstream harm;
5. correction, refund, appeal, worker voice, or support response; and
6. repeat reliance, switching, boundary-setting, or exit.

Run this separately for shopping/payment, public services, workplace management,
education, and companionship. Compare low- and high-stakes tasks and test
whether human fallback, transparency, and reversibility change outcomes rather
than merely stated trust.

## Current answer state

**Partly answered.** The corpus shows that adoption is not agency and continued
use is not empowerment. It identifies verification, control, reversibility, and
exit as the missing middle, but it does not yet follow the same person through
an AI-assisted decision, correction, and durable choice.

## Stopping rule for this rotation

Do not call AI adoption empowering until one episode-level or longitudinal source
observes capability, user control, downstream result, and a real correction or
exit outcome with subgroup denominators and a counterexample.

# Finding 020: A regulator can make platform failure easier to contest without showing that the harm was repaired

**Status:** bounded digital-removal and regulator-escalation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

When the FTC began enforcing the TAKE IT DOWN Act, it sent warning letters to
12 websites offering “nudify” tools and launched a route for people to report
platforms that lack a removal process or fail to remove intimate images and
known identical copies within 48 hours.

The route is:

```text
nonconsensual or AI-generated intimate image appears
  -> person asks the platform to remove it
  -> platform removes it, fails, or has no usable process
  -> person can report the platform to the FTC
  -> [open] platform correction, complete removal, recurrence prevention, safety, and exit
```

This adds a real regulator-response stage to the digital-control route. It is
stronger than a law that exists only on paper: the FTC named a warning cohort
and gave people an escalation path. But the public record does not show that
the warned sites changed, that any individual image was removed, or that a
survivor became safer.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

The recurring pattern is that visibility and contestability can improve before
practical control does. A person may now have a platform request, a 48-hour
standard, an identifying route, and a federal complaint option while still
carrying the burden of documenting the harm, navigating the platform, checking
for copies, and dealing with recurrence.

The regulator can make failure legible. It cannot, by the existence of the
complaint form alone, prove that the underlying digital exposure ended.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The FTC warning announcement names 12 websites but does not report their
  responses or later compliance.
- The complaint route is not an individual-removal database and does not give a
  public completion or recovery rate.
- A platform can comply with a request while copies, re-uploads, or other
  services remain outside the route.
- The record does not measure survivor safety, wellbeing, trust, continued use,
  or practical exit.
- Some people may never find or use the platform process, and the burden can
  vary by age, identity, language, resources, and safety conditions.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected request ledger joining platform,
request date, validity, response, removal of known copies, FTC escalation,
reappearance, and later safety or exit. Until that exists, the safe claim is
that federal enforcement has made platform failure more contestable, not that
digital harm has been repaired.

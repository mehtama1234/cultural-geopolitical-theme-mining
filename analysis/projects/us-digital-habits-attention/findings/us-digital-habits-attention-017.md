# Finding 017: Social platforms can be both social infrastructure and a route into financial loss

**Status:** bounded digital-consumer-risk finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

The FTC says people reported $2.1 billion lost in 2025 to scams where social
media was the contact method. That was about eight times the 2020 reported
amount. Nearly 30% of loss reports that named a contact method named social
media.

The scams were not all alike. Shopping ads, fake investment groups, romance
contacts, job offers, rental listings, and account or identity targeting used
different forms of trust and attention.

The route is:

```text
profile, ad, message, group, or social connection
  -> credible-looking offer or relationship
  -> payment, disclosure, or account exposure
  -> reported loss or no report
  -> [open] correction, recovery, platform response, and later trust/exit
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This makes the digital lane material. Platforms are not only places where
people spend attention or seek belonging; they are also marketplaces and
targeting systems. The same features that make a message feel personal or an
ad feel relevant can make deception harder to recognize.

It also explains why a reported-loss total must be read carefully. The number
shows a visible burden in the reporting system, but it does not tell us how
many people were exposed, how many never reported, who recovered money, or
whether a platform could have prevented the loss.

## What it does not show

- Reported losses are not the true number of scams or total household loss.
- A social-media contact does not by itself prove platform causation.
- The data do not show individual complaint, refund, chargeback, or recovery.
- The trend does not establish that all social-platform commerce is unsafe.
- It does not measure later trust, switching, non-use, or political action.

## Next decisive test

Follow a privacy-protected set of cases from platform contact to payment or
disclosure, report, platform response, bank or law-enforcement route, refund
or non-recovery, and later use or exit. Preserve non-reporting as a separate
path rather than treating reports as the full population.

# Digital control is distributed across reach, purpose, attention, and remedy

**Status:** provisional cross-source platform/data/attention synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-09-14

## The bounded finding

The current evidence does not describe one digital trend. It describes several
control surfaces that can become connected in a person's life but are not yet
measured as one chain:

```text
platform reach or AI availability
  -> a purpose-specific encounter
  -> repeated attention, trust, sharing, or reliance
  -> social, consumer, educational, work, or civic consequence
  -> correction, complaint, switching, remedy, or exit
```

Pew surveys measure portions of the first three stages in different populations.
The FTC record measures institutional scrutiny. None of these sources follows
the same person from encounter through attempted exit and verified remedy.

## What the sources establish separately

| Surface | Direct evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult platform reach | In 2025, 84% of adults reported YouTube use, 71% Facebook, 50% Instagram, 37% TikTok, 32% WhatsApp, and 26% Reddit; daily frequency differs by platform and age | Digital access is broad and socially patterned | Content exposure, recommendation pressure, persuasion, spending, dependence, or exit cost |
| Teen social and AI use | In 2025, 64% of teens had used chatbots, 28% used them daily, 61% used TikTok daily, and 36% used at least one measured platform almost constantly | Social and instrumental AI use are entering ordinary adolescent life together | Whether use is beneficial, harmful, monitored, monetized, or hard to stop |
| Adult AI purpose and concern | In 2026, 49% of adults had used chatbots and 24% used them daily; 42% reported information-search use; 70% expected personal information to be less secure and 67% had little or no confidence in effective government regulation | Usefulness, ordinary adoption, privacy concern, and weak governance confidence can coexist | Actual privacy loss, accuracy, reliance, product-level control, or regulatory performance |
| News and civic attention | In 2025, 21% regularly got news from social-media influencers, rising to 37% among adults under 30; the published typology classified 28% as Connectors and 9% as Mobilizers | News attention, trust, and civic action have different routes and groupings | Platform causation, persuasion, vote causation, or greater agency |
| Institutional response | In 2025, the FTC sought information from seven AI-companion companies about monetization, data use, safety testing, age limits, disclosures, and effects on children and teens | Public agencies are treating product design, data, safety, and youth effects as an investigatory problem | Verified harm, unlawful conduct, remedy, or successful user exit |

The percentages use different dates, samples, question frames, and response
universes. They must not be added, averaged, or treated as a digital-life
index.

## The substantive interpretation

The strongest present interpretation is that digital power is distributed
across encounters. The same person may use a social platform for belonging, a
chatbot for information or work, an influencer for news, and a complaint route
when something goes wrong. Each encounter has a different alternative, risk,
data trail, and exit condition.

This makes “use” an insufficient endpoint. The program needs to distinguish:

- availability from exposure;
- exposure from repeated attention;
- repeated attention from reliance;
- reliance from changed beliefs or behavior;
- concern from observed harm;
- complaint visibility from correction;
- switching from meaningful exit.

The adult AI evidence is especially useful because it holds two facts together:
chatbot use is becoming ordinary, while many adults remain concerned about
privacy, speed, and government capacity. That is not a contradiction. A person
can need a tool, benefit from it, and still believe that the surrounding
institution does not give them adequate control.

The teen evidence adds a life-stage dimension. High frequency among teens may
reflect schoolwork, peer coordination, entertainment, identity, or family
rules. It may also become infrastructure for belonging before researchers can
observe the cost of leaving. The next question is not whether teens use these
products, but whether the products become difficult to substitute when a
relationship, school routine, or social identity depends on them.

The news and civic evidence prevents a further overclaim. Social distribution
can change who encounters information without determining what they believe or
whether they act. Civic participation may be organized through platforms,
but it can also reflect prior identity, resources, religious or community
institutions, and offline political organizations.

Finally, the FTC inquiry shows where public power enters the chain. An agency
request for information can make monetization, data use, safety testing, age
limits, and disclosure visible. It is an institutional signal, not a finding
that the feared mechanism has occurred or that a remedy works.

## Open arrows the long-term program must test

1. **Encounter → interpretation:** What did the person actually see, ask, or
   receive, and how did they understand its authority or social meaning?
2. **Interpretation → action:** Did the encounter change attention, spending,
   work, schoolwork, health decisions, trust, sharing, or civic behavior?
3. **Action → reliance:** Could the person substitute a human, public, or
   competing service without losing access or status?
4. **Reliance → exit:** What happened when the person tried to stop, delete,
   correct, switch, or contest the result?
5. **Complaint → remedy:** Was the complaint acknowledged, corrected,
   compensated, or merely recorded?
6. **Remedy → public meaning:** Did the experience change trust in the firm,
   platform category, regulator, or broader institutions?

## Strong counterexamples

The strongest counterexample to a control-risk interpretation is a high-
frequency user who can inspect recommendations, refuse prompts, export data,
switch providers, correct errors, and retain meaningful social or economic
alternatives.

The strongest risk signal is not simply high use. It is a user whose essential
educational, social, work, health, or civic access is tied to one product, who
cannot inspect or correct the system, whose attempt to leave produces a real
loss, and whose complaint has no effective remedy.

## Next end-to-end test

Build matched age-specific respondent panels linked, where lawful and ethical,
to product and institutional records. Record product, purpose, content or
prompt, time, disclosure, trust, error, correction, human alternative,
stop/delete attempt, switching, complaint, remedy, and later social, consumer,
educational, work, or civic consequence. Pair this with terms/data audits and
agency records so the program can distinguish user perception, observed
behavior, company practice, and public enforcement.

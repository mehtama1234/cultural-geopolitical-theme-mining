# Finding 012: Subscription convenience can become practical dependence when cancellation is harder than enrollment

**Status:** bounded subscription-control and consumer-exit finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-21

## The finding

The FTC's May 2026 Shutterstock complaint and proposed settlement add a concrete
platform-exit case to the atlas. The agency alleged that important renewal terms
and early-cancellation fees were not clearly disclosed, that some charges lacked
express informed consent, and that before 2024 some consumers could not finish
early cancellation online and had to contact customer support. The proposed
$35 million settlement would fund consumer relief and require clearer terms,
express consent, and simple cancellation mechanisms.

```text
low-friction enrollment or advertised convenience
  -> recurring charge and renewal terms
  -> cancellation effort, fee, delay, or support contact
  -> formal enforcement and proposed relief
  -> refund receipt, adequate recovery, switching, trust, or exit [open]
```

## Why it matters

This is a sharper version of the atlas's “interactional exit versus practical
exit” distinction. A subscription can be useful and voluntary at enrollment
while becoming a lock-in problem when the consumer must spend extra time,
navigate support, or absorb a fee to stop it. The issue is not simply whether a
cancel button exists; it is whether the consumer can recognize the obligation,
stop future charges, recover disputed money, and leave without losing more than
the service was worth.

It also links platform design to competition. If cancellation is costly or
unclear, continued use can look like retention even when it reflects friction,
inattention, or a lost exit route. Enforcement can improve the formal control
surface without yet showing that every affected consumer received relief or
changed provider.

## Do not overread

- The FTC announcement describes allegations and a proposed order, not a
  person-level outcome file.
- The public record does not report the number of affected consumers, individual
  charges, cancellation attempts, refunds received, or residual losses.
- Prospective cancellation controls do not prove implementation, compliance,
  successful switching, or durable trust.
- A difficult cancellation route does not mean every subscriber wanted to leave
  or that the underlying subscription had no value.

## Next decisive test

Follow a stable subscription episode from enrollment and disclosed terms through
renewal, cancellation attempt, support effort, charge dispute, refund receipt,
provider switching, and later use. Preserve successful cancellation, failed
attempts, non-use, and continued use as separate outcomes.

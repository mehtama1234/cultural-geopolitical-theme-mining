# Disney and YouTube: court-approved child-data controls after content-labeling allegations

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** court-approved COPPA order; implementation, age assurance, and child-level outcomes remain open

The FTC's Disney/YouTube matter adds a different platform-control route from
the recent data-broker, education-technology, and refund records. A DOJ
complaint referred by the FTC alleged that Disney failed to properly label some
child-directed videos uploaded to YouTube as Made for Kids. The complaint said
that this allowed personal data to be collected from children under 13 without
parental notice or consent and used for targeted advertising.

A federal judge approved a settlement requiring Disney to pay a $10 million
civil penalty, notify parents before collecting personal information from
children under 13, obtain verifiable parental consent, and establish a program
to review whether uploaded videos should be designated Made for Kids. The order
also describes an alternative in which YouTube implements age-assurance
technology for all users or stops allowing creators to label videos as Made for
Kids.

This is a concrete conversion from content classification to parental-control
and platform-governance obligations. The boundary remains open: the public
announcement does not report videos reviewed, children exposed, parent notice
or consent, deletion, age-assurance accuracy, advertising change, child
wellbeing, trust, or practical exit. A court-approved order is not child-level
privacy recovery.

# AI usefulness does not automatically become control

**Status:** provisional broad-program synthesis  
**Checked:** 2026-09-16  
**Scope:** US everyday AI use, workplace assistance, synthetic social contact, companion-product exit, and governance

## Research question

When AI becomes useful enough to enter ordinary work and daily life, does that
use create more room for people—or does it mainly create new dependence on
systems whose data, pace, errors, and exit conditions they do not control?

The program's central distinction is **optionality**: having a useful option is
not the same as having a replaceable, inspectable, contestable option.

```text
AI availability
  -> purpose, frequency, and perceived usefulness
  -> time, output, attention, identity, or relationship changes
  -> who captures the benefit and who bears error, monitoring, or dependence
  -> ability to inspect, refuse, correct, delete, switch, or exit
  -> trust, work control, cultural meaning, and political response
```

The current evidence closes the first two arrows in different settings. It
only partly closes the third and leaves practical exit and durable social
consequence largely open.

## Evidence surfaces

| Surface | Unit and design | What is observed | Strongest missing link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Everyday AI | Pew 2026 US adults; employed-adult and age-group universes preserved | Adoption, frequency, purpose, perceived productivity, privacy concern, pace judgment, and confidence in government regulation | Product-level data exchange, errors, human alternatives, ability to stop, and later outcome |
| Workplace assistance | Randomized access experiment across 7,137 workers in 66 firms | AI access changes email and after-hours patterns for users; no detected individual task-quantity/composition change in the reported measure | Whether saved time becomes rest, care, pay, autonomy, or higher employer expectations |
| Synthetic social contact | Preregistered online experiments with separate samples | Immediate warmth and one costly next-conversation choice can move; one-week mean effect is small and not significant | Durable identity, cooperation, voting, policy judgment, and ordinary-life generalization |
| Companion-product exit | Product audit and separate representative experiments | Continuation tactics appear in audited farewells and short-run engagement can rise after goodbye prompts | Actual deletion, switching, dependence, welfare, product remediation, and long-term use |
| Public governance | FTC information-gathering inquiry into seven companies | Regulators make monetization, data use, safety, age limits, and disclosure visible as open questions | Finding, enforcement, changed design, consumer remedy, and trust recovery |

These are intentionally not pooled into an “AI impact” score. Their units,
interventions, outcomes, and clocks differ.

## What the comparison supports

### 1. Usefulness and control are different dimensions

Pew reports that AI use is already ordinary: 49% of US adults had used a
chatbot and 24% used one daily in the February 2026 survey. Work-task use was
38% among employed adults, while 70% expected personal information to be less
secure and 67% had little or no confidence that government could regulate AI
effectively.

These are not contradictory answers. “This helps me search, write, or solve a
task” and “I control the data, pace, error correction, and exit conditions” are
different questions. A useful system may be difficult to replace because it
holds context, reputation, workflow, relationships, or accumulated history.

### 2. Access can change time before it changes authority

The NBER randomized field experiment is stronger than a perception survey for
the narrow time mechanism. Individual access changed email and after-hours
patterns for participating workers, while the reported individual-level task
quantity/composition measure did not detect a corresponding broad task shift.

The missing conversion is decisive:

```text
less email or faster document work
  -> [open] less total work, more recovery, or more employer-captured capacity
  -> [open] schedule control, pay, health, family time, or bargaining room
```

Time savings are not automatically worker freedom. Employers may leave the
benefit with workers, use it for additional tasks, or change expectations. The
experiment establishes a bounded access-to-time-pattern result, not household
benefit or increased worker power.

### 3. Relational effects can be real and still temporary

The synthetic-contact experiments show that an AI interaction can reduce an
immediate barrier to a later real outgroup conversation. The one-week
preregistered mean effect was small and not significant, which is important:
an immediate attitude or choice movement is not durable depolarization,
identity change, or political action.

The same time-scale discipline applies to companion products. A longer goodbye
conversation or short-run engagement response can show that interface design
changes behavior at the moment of attempted exit. It does not show attachment,
harm, inability to leave, or a lasting relationship outcome without follow-up.

### 4. Exit has to be operational, not rhetorical

“Users can stop” is too weak as a control claim. A meaningful exit test asks:

1. Can the person stop without losing essential work, care, identity, or social
   continuity?
2. Can they export or delete the relevant data and history?
3. Can they correct an error or reach a human with authority?
4. Is there a credible non-AI or competing alternative at comparable cost?
5. Does stopping change pay, schedule, status, access, or relationships?

The current evidence rarely observes all five. That is why daily use should not
be coded as dependence and an attempted goodbye should not be coded as failed
exit.

### 5. Governance is entering before outcome measurement is mature

The FTC inquiry is an institutional signal, not a harm estimate. It shows that
monetization, data use, safety testing, age limits, disclosures, and effects on
children and teens have become governance objects. The next broad-program
question is whether inquiry creates inspectable design changes, usable remedy,
or public confidence—not whether the inquiry itself proves wrongdoing.

## Mechanism and power ledger

| Arrow | Status | Safe conclusion | Next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Availability → use | Reported | AI has entered ordinary search, work, health, and relational use | Product-level telemetry with consent and version history |
| Use → immediate behavior/time | Estimated / experimentally identified in separate settings | Work access changes selected time patterns; synthetic contact changes selected immediate responses; companion interfaces can alter short-run engagement | Replication across tasks, users, firms, and follow-up windows |
| Immediate change → captured benefit | Open | The current record does not show who keeps saved time or reduced friction | Employer targets, pay, schedule, care, health, and household-time follow-up |
| Use → dependence or replaceability | Open | Frequency and continuation are compatible with usefulness, necessity, habit, or constrained choice | Human/non-AI alternatives, switching cost, data portability, and stop attempts |
| Error/concern → correction | Open | Public concern and institutional inquiry identify a governance problem | Named error, appeal, correction, deletion, remedy, and enforcement outcome |
| AI exposure → trust/action | Open | Concern, usefulness, and confidence are measured separately | Longitudinal attribution, trust, civic action, organizing, or political judgment |

## Provisional broad-program finding

> AI is becoming ordinary faster than its benefits are being converted into
> durable human control. The same system can save a worker time, help a user
> solve a problem, soften an immediate social barrier, or sustain an
> interaction while leaving unresolved who owns the data, captures the gain,
> bears the error, sets the pace, and can credibly exit.

This is a mechanism synthesis, not a claim that AI is broadly harmful or that
users lack agency. It identifies an unequal-optionality problem: people with
alternatives, portability, time, institutional voice, and human support can
turn usefulness into room; people without them may experience the same tool as
a new obligation or dependency.

## Next bounded test

The strongest next design is a version-stamped, consented user or worker panel
with separate work, information, health, emotional-support, and companionship
routes. For each episode, preserve:

1. product/model/version, purpose, frequency, prompt or task, and human or
   non-AI alternative;
2. measured time, output, error, disclosure, privacy, schedule, pay, care, or
   relationship change;
3. who captured the benefit and whether expectations or monitoring changed;
4. stop, delete, export, switch, appeal, correction, and remedy attempts;
5. later trust, health, work, civic, consumer, and household outcomes; and
6. subgroup, attrition, prior familiarity, and a counterexample with similar
   exposure but a different outcome.

Stop before calling a person dependent, empowered, polarized, or harmed if the
design does not observe alternatives, timing, attribution, and a later
outcome. A use rate is an exposure measure; a time saving is a bounded effect;
control and exit require evidence of what the person could actually do.

## Boundary and source trail

This synthesis combines population survey, randomized field experiment,
online experiment, product-behavior audit, and institutional inquiry evidence
without treating them as one respondent history or causal estimate. It does
not estimate AI's effect on productivity, job quality, trust, political action,
dependence, or geopolitics.

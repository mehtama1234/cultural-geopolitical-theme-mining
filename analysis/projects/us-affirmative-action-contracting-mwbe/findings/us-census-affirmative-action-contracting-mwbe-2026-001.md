# Finding 001: A contracting ban can leave existing firms alive while shrinking the route into the market

**Status:** bounded political, firm, and unequal-access finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper uses restricted business records to study
minority- and women-owned business enterprises after states ended affirmative
action in state contracting. It reports gradual downsizing among existing MWBE
contractors, with the largest force reductions among Black-owned businesses and
larger MWBEs. Existing MWBEs were no more likely to shut down, but new MWBEs
were relatively less common after a ban.

The practical route is:

```text
contracting-rule change
  -> access, competition, or qualification route changes
  -> existing firms shrink or adapt
  -> new-firm entry changes
  -> [open] workers, productivity, community opportunity, and public-service effect
```

This separates three things that are often collapsed: staying alive, staying
large enough to compete, and creating a new route for firms to enter.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

The finding adds a political-institutional version of the program's recurring
pattern. A formal rule can change who can practically build capacity in a
market without immediately producing mass firm closures. Survival therefore
does not prove equal opportunity, and fewer new firms does not by itself prove
the whole mechanism or the final community effect.

It also adds a cultural and identity dimension: the composition of future
contractors can change even when existing firms remain present. The public
record does not establish whether that changes trust, legitimacy, worker
opportunity, or the quality and cost of government services.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper, and the public abstract does not provide the full
  state list, sample, estimates, or contract-level pathways.
- Existing-firm survival can coexist with downsizing and weaker entry.
- A ban may alter procurement, competition, qualification, or contract mix;
  the abstract does not identify one complete mechanism.
- The model-implied productivity and scrap-value result is not a direct measure
  of worker productivity or household welfare.
- Other markets, private contracts, or public programs may offset some losses.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected firm-contract-worker panel linking
ownership, eligibility, bids, awards, contract value, agency, competition,
employment, pay, survival, other markets, and later entry. Until then, the
safe claim is that contracting rules can reshape firm scale and entry, not that
they determine every worker or community outcome.

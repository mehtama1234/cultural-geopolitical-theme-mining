# Project: Contracting bans and minority- and women-owned firms

This project tracks what happens between a change in public contracting rules
and the firms that depend on that market. A Census working paper reports that
ending affirmative action in state contracting was followed by gradual
downsizing among existing minority- and women-owned business enterprises
(MWBEs), especially Black-owned and larger MWBEs. Existing MWBEs were not more
likely to shut down, but new MWBEs were relatively less common.

The safe theme is not “all MWBEs failed” or “the ban caused every disparity.”
It is that firm survival, firm scale, and new entry can move differently when
the public route to contracts changes. The project must keep contracts, firm
size, entry, survival, workers, productivity, community effects, and public
service quality separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected firm-contract-worker panel joining
ownership, eligibility, bids, awards, contract value, agency, competition,
employment, pay, survival, other markets, and later entry. The public paper
summary does not provide that complete bridge.

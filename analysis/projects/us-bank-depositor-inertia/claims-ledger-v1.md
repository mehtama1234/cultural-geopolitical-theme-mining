# Claims ledger: US bank customers and the cost of staying put

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US deposits, interest rates, switching effort, bank competition, and stability

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Most depositors do not switch banks each year, even when better rates exist. | NBER account data | The study finds only 5–15% open new accounts per year; moving, death, and other events explain more closures than rate shopping. | Opening an account is not the same as moving all deposits. |
| C-002 | Inactive deposits are valuable to banks. | NBER account data and model | Depositor sleepiness accounts for 57% of average deposit franchise value in the study. | The value estimate depends on the model and sample. |
| C-003 | Staying can help bank funding and stability while costing a customer interest. | NBER model and HBS summary | The research links stable deposits to lower modeled default risk and weaker competition; HBS reports the customer rate gap. | The customer's net value also includes convenience, safety, fees, and services. |
| C-004 | Switching has real time and setup costs. | HBS and FDIC consumer guidance | Automatic bills, direct deposit, branch access, and account setup make moving harder. | The records do not measure the time cost for each household. |
| C-005 | Waking up all depositors could shift money toward larger banks and reduce some stability. | NBER counterfactual | The authors model higher default risk and losses for some smaller or lower-quality banks if sleepiness disappears. | It is a counterfactual, not an observed policy result. |
| C-006 | A low rate is not automatically unfair, and switching is not automatically best. | Counterpoint and synthesis | Customers may value convenience and trust; bank stability has a public value. | Need account-level rates, fees, balances, and customer needs together. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
bank account opens
  -> bills, direct deposit, and habit settle around it
  -> switching takes time and attention
  -> customer accepts a lower rate or weak terms
  -> bank gains steady funding
  -> customer return and bank stability move together, but not equally
```

## Next test

Pair deposit rates with account balances, fees, switching actions, automatic payments, branch or digital access, and bank risk. Estimate the customer's lost interest beside the value of convenience and safety.

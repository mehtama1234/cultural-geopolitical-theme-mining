# Finding 001: Strategic reserve capacity can remain below design capability while obligations are still met

**Status:** bounded infrastructure/state-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-106918 reports that, as of December 2025, the U.S. Strategic Petroleum
Reserve's effective drawdown capability was estimated at 61% of its design rate
and effective fill capability at 56%. GAO attributes the limits to a mix of
construction outages, aging infrastructure, and low inventory. The reserve had
met drawdown directives to date, but commercial pipelines and storage also had
little spare capacity, which can constrain the incremental market effect of a
release.

The safe interpretation is:

> Emergency capacity is not a binary asset. A reserve can satisfy a past
> directive while still operating below design speed and depending on a wider
> commercial distribution system.

## Evidence chain

```text
strategic reserve infrastructure and inventory
  -> effective fill and drawdown capability
  -> emergency release and commercial distribution
  -> market supply and price response
  -> [open] refinery, firm, household, and geopolitical consequence
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds an operational-capability stage to the infrastructure and state
capacity lanes. It is materially different from an appropriation, project
award, or reserve-size statistic: the source reports effective operating rates
and the physical constraints that limit them.

It also sharpens the meaning of resilience. Reserve capability depends on
maintenance, construction sequencing, inventory, pipelines, storage, and market
conditions. A nominal asset can therefore remain strategically important while
its realized speed and market effect are conditional.

## What the source does not show

- It does not identify a current or future petroleum shortage.
- It does not estimate prices, refinery output, transportation cost, or
  household or firm incidence for a specific release.
- Meeting prior drawdown directives does not prove performance under a larger,
  longer, or differently timed disruption.
- The effective-rate estimates are a point-in-time operating assessment, not a
  forecast of future reserve performance.
- It does not establish energy independence, deterrence, or geopolitical
  leverage.

## Next decisive test

Follow one named reserve release or emergency scenario through the affected
site, product and volume, pipeline/storage route, refinery receipt, prices,
industrial and household burden, substitution, and later replenishment or
maintenance outcome. Preserve non-release and commercial-capacity comparators.

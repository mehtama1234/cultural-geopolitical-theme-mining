# Information encounter to correction, civic action, and trust

**Status:** fifty-fourth active depth artifact in the ongoing program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** news discovery, AI and platform intermediaries, verification,
correction, civic interpretation, and institutional legitimacy

## What we found

News discovery is increasingly intermediary-led, but reach and credibility do
not move together. Ofcom's UK 2025-26 survey of 5,397 adults reports 73% using
online intermediaries for news, 21% using an AI app or service for news, and
56% saying they trust an AI-written story less than a person-written story.
Only 5% used intermediaries exclusively. Reuters' 48-market survey reports
54% using social or video networks for news, 30% naming them as their main
source, 37% trusting news most of the time overall, 22% trusting news on social
media, and 20% trusting news from AI chatbots. These are different surveys and
must not be pooled.

The civic endpoint is also uneven. A Pew descriptive typology finds 9% of US
adults in a high-engagement “Mobilizer” group, with 87% reporting that they
voted in 2024 and 65% contacting an official, while 41% of the “Outsider” group
reported voting and 3% contacting an official. The typology shows distinct
attention and action profiles; it does not show that a platform or news source
created them.

## What this connects to

| Link | Evidence | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediary reach -> source legitimacy | Ofcom and Reuters show high platform discovery alongside lower trust in social and AI sources. | Reach is not attention quality, accuracy, comprehension, or persuasion. |
| Information availability -> usable public knowledge | IPIE's 470 experts across 71 countries rate lack of platform accountability, misinformation, and polarization as major threats; 76% expect their information environment to worsen, while 76% say diverse viewpoints are at least somewhat easy to find and only 28% think most people trust mainstream news to report politics accurately. | Expert judgment is not public exposure, verified content quality, or observed civic behavior. |
| News attention -> civic action | Pew's Mobilizer and Outsider profiles show sharply different reported voting and official-contact rates. | A one-time cluster is not a causal pathway or a stable identity. |
| Public norm -> institutional legitimacy | Pew finds 73% of US adults say press freedom is very or extremely important, while 53% prioritize preventing inaccurate reporting even if press freedom is limited. | Normative preference is not a censorship audit, accuracy measure, or authorization for a specific restriction. |

## The deeper question

**When does an intermediary-led news encounter produce verified civic agency
rather than confusion, silence, distrust, or source exit?**

### Why ask it

The answer could change how we judge platform and AI-news systems. A system
that delivers many encounters but hides sources, corrections, or routes for
response may increase attention without increasing usable agency. A system that
helps people find, check, correct, and act on local information could strengthen
public legitimacy even if it reduces direct visits to a publisher.

Priority is **high** because the missing link affects information, platform,
political, cultural, and institutional themes. The effort is bounded to one
named controversy or local issue; scope risk is controlled by not inferring
general political effects from it.

## What we know so far

- Platform and AI routes are now material discovery channels.
- Established publishers and broadcasters can still retain greater legitimacy
  than the intermediary carrying their content.
- Access to many viewpoints does not itself establish accuracy, trust, or
  civic usefulness.
- Some people combine news attention with formal civic action; others remain
  low-engagement or disconnected.
- Public discussion points toward media literacy, accountability, and
  information resilience, but these discussions do not evaluate intervention
  effects.

## Bounded public discussion

- The [Ofcom LinkedIn discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-ofcom-news-consumption-ai-linkedin-001.json)
  describes fragmented discovery across social platforms, creators, and AI
  while established providers retain trust and civic seriousness.
- The [Reuters Institute LinkedIn discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-reuters-digital-news-report-linkedin-001.json)
  highlights the gap between platform reach and credibility.
- The [Full Fact discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-full-fact-ai-trust-linkedin-001.json)
  frames AI-generated misinformation as an institutional-trust and public-
  resilience problem.
- The [UNESCO discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-batch-0029-unesco-media-literacy-linkedin-001.json)
  treats media literacy as an information-ecosystem intervention rather than
  a platform-only fix.

These are institution-authored public discussions. They are useful for
identifying questions and affected concerns, not for estimating public opinion
or proving that a correction changed behavior.

## What could change the answer

- A correction may restore trust, or it may make the original source look less
  reliable and push people to another source.
- AI summaries may improve access for people who would otherwise find nothing,
  even if they reduce direct publisher visits.
- Low trust may be healthy skepticism rather than civic withdrawal.
- A person may act after an encounter without changing source choice, or change
  source choice without taking civic action.
- Political identity, local institutions, prior knowledge, and offline
  relationships may explain more of the action gap than platform exposure.

## Challenge checks

- Activity was not treated as an outcome: reach, use, trust, voting, and
  official contact remain separate.
- Correlation was not treated as cause: none of the surveys identifies a
  platform-driven action effect.
- Scope and time were checked: Ofcom is UK 2025-26, Reuters is 48 markets in
  2026, Pew is US 2025-26, and IPIE is a 2026 expert panel.
- Repeated sources were not counted as independent: the Ofcom and Reuters
  public posts summarize their own research families.
- A counterexample was retained: intermediary distribution can carry trusted
  broadcaster content, and high access can coexist with low exclusivity.
- Affected groups remain open: children, local-news users, people with low
  literacy or connectivity, journalists, creators, and people targeted by
  misinformation need episode-level evidence.

## Current answer

**Partly answered.** Intermediaries clearly change how information is found,
and the evidence shows a reach-versus-legitimacy gap. The corpus does not yet
show whether a specific person or community moved from encounter through
verification and correction to civic action, durable trust, or exit.

## What to look for next

Follow one bounded episode:

```text
dated claim or local information encounter
  -> source visibility and verification attempt
  -> correction, moderation, or official response
  -> belief, discussion, contact, vote, protest, or non-action
  -> later trust, source switch, continued use, or exit
```

Record the affected population, exposure denominator, source and platform,
claim status, correction timing, response route, missingness, and any people who
did not act. Stop if only aggregate reach or attitudes are available; report an
acquisition gap instead of promoting a causal media effect.

## Stopping rule

Do not call information systems civically strengthening or weakening until a
dated episode or valid longitudinal design observes the encounter, source or
claim judgment, correction or response, and later action or exit with compatible
denominators and a credible counterexample.

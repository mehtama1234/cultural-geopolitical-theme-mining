# Public-system participation, meaning, and action gap v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** cross-source boundary record; no benefit-to-vote causal claim

## What is measured now

The 2025 SIPP transition/reason layers observe adjacent-month SNAP entry and
exit for identified person records. Among reason-classified transitions,
reported/recorded entry was most often associated with job or wage loss, other
income loss, disability, or family change. Exit was most often coded “other” or
income increase, with smaller shares for time limits, unmet requirements,
continued eligibility/non-collection, and benefits not worth the trouble.
Approximate Fay-BRR intervals are recorded in the [reason uncertainty
layer](sipp-snap-transition-reason-fay-brr-layer-v1.md).

This gives the public system a mechanism layer:

```text
need or rule exposure
  -> recorded reason and adjacent receipt transition
  -> resource/job context
  -> food, work, debt, health, housing, or time result (partly observed)
  -> interpretation and public action (not in SIPP)
```

## The exact boundary

Only 217 of 437 entry pairs and 188 of 387 exit pairs had a valid classified
reason in the Fay-BRR transition run. The unknown/other category is therefore
substantive missingness, not a nuisance to remove. The SIPP slice does not
observe notice comprehension, application effort, benefit amount and timing,
appeal, dignity, trust, blame, information source, complaint, turnout, vote,
or policy action for the same episode.

ANES supplies separate respondent-level measures of financial worry, economic
judgment, government trust, perceived interests/waste, party identity, and
reported vote. Those respondents and episodes are not the SIPP participants,
and the surveys do not identify the same benefit encounter. The correct result
is a staged population map, not a hidden join.

The new [transition-to-hardship comparison](sipp-snap-transition-outcome-fay-brr-layer-v1.md)
adds a measured downstream material stage: hardship in the recorded month after
the SNAP transition. It sharpens the boundary in both directions. Entry is
associated with a high-pressure group, while exit does not reliably coincide
with restored rent/mortgage or utility security. Neither pattern identifies
the program's effect or the person's interpretation of the encounter.

## What the combined evidence supports

1. **Participation has multiple mechanisms.** Entry and exit cannot be
   summarized as simply “need” or “success.” Health, work, income, family,
   rules, and non-collection appear as distinct recorded categories.
2. **Administrative visibility is incomplete.** Nearly half of observed
   transitions in the reason run lack a classified reason, so observed
   categories cannot stand for the whole transition population.
3. **Material mechanism and political meaning are different stages.** SIPP can
   show transitions and context; ANES can show trust and action patterns; the
   same-person route from a public encounter to blame, trust, or action remains
   open.

## Next event-ledger acquisition

For the same person/case, capture notice, channel, language, documents, effort,
deadline, decision, amount, interruption, correction, material result, fairness,
blame, trust, information source, complaint, contact, organizing, turnout, and
later recovery. Compare similar needs across routes and preserve eligible
non-applicants, delayed cases, exits without improvement, and transitions with
unknown reason.

The next valid claim should name the exact stage: transition, material result,
interpretation, action, or institutional response. A caseload change is not a
security result, and a vote is not proof of a benefit experience.

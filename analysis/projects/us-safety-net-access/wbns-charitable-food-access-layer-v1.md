# WBNS charitable-food access and unmet-need layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-14  
**Status:** published-analysis layer; access and meaning evidence, not a provider-capacity census or causal stigma estimate

## The core pattern

Charitable food participation among adults ages 18–64 was 13.2% in 2019,
peaked at 19.7% in 2020, and remained elevated at 16.7% in 2025. In the
expanded 2025 adult sample, 16.1% reported household receipt of free groceries
or meals in the prior year. This is a persistent supplemental route, not proof
that the route is adequate or that public assistance has been replaced.

Need and reach overlap: 39.5% of adults in food-insecure households reported
charitable-food participation, compared with 8.7% of adults in food-secure
households. Yet 28.8% of adults in food-insecure households reported unmet need
for charitable food. Receipt can therefore coexist with an unclosed gap.

## Access is a bundle of constraints

Among charitable-food participants, 51.6% reported at least one listed access
difficulty. The most common was getting the variety and types of food needed or
wanted (40.2%), followed by reaching the site in a reasonable time (25.8%),
transportation (21.9%), and finding a site open when available (20.9%). A new
2025 question found 22.7% were somewhat or very worried about physical safety.

Among adults with unmet need, the reported reasons were overlapping: the site
was not open when available (45.5%), not knowing where to go (42.8%), not being
comfortable getting help (39.9%), unavailable food variety (37.6%), and
transportation (35.2%). The route is therefore simultaneously temporal,
spatial, informational, material, and meaning-laden. No single reason should be
treated as the mechanism for every missed connection.

## Treatment and unequal exposure

Among charitable-food participants, 22.9% reported being treated or judged
unfairly because of at least one personal characteristic. The share was 26.2%
among working-age adults, 31.8% among adults with disabilities, and 28.6% among
adults living with children. The source measures perceived treatment and
attribution; it does not adjudicate intent, discrimination, or organizational
responsibility.

Participation was also higher among adults living with children (21.0% versus
13.4% without children), adults with disabilities (29.6% versus 11.7%), and
families below 200% FPL (34.1% versus 4.6% at 400% FPL or above). These are
descriptive exposure differences with substantial overlap among characteristics.

## Connection to the end-to-end program

The public-system and household-security chain can now be stated more precisely:

`material pressure / public-benefit route → charitable-food substitution or supplement → access, treatment, and unmet need → food-security outcome`

The evidence supports the existence of each component across compatible but not
identical WBNS universes. It does not hold a benefit episode, charitable-food
visit, and later food-security outcome constant for the same household. The
current evidence also does not support claims about voting, organizing, or
political trust. Those outcomes require separate same-respondent timing or
behavioral evidence.

## What to test next

The highest-value next test is a codebook-backed public-use WBNS extraction that
reproduces charitable-food, food-security, SNAP-route, disability, children,
transport, and financial-security cells with survey-design uncertainty. A linked
provider or administrative route would additionally need site hours, capacity,
distance, wait, delivery, and remedy data. The published brief does not supply
those provider-side measures.

# SNAP administrative burden and practical access: paper scan v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13
**Question:** When a public benefit exists on paper, how do interviews,
office distance, renewal timing, and work documentation change practical access?
**Status:** cross-study institutional layer; sources use different states,
periods, units, and designs and are not a single joined estimate

## The route is part of the policy

```text
need / eligibility
  -> office, interview, documents, work reporting, renewal, or deadline
  -> approval, procedural denial, churn, or exit
  -> food, housing, work, debt, time, and security consequences
  -> fairness, trust, public demand, or political response
```

The SIPP transition layer observes the middle and some material context for
many person-months. The studies below identify selected route mechanisms. None
of them alone observes the same applicant's full material, interpretive, and
political path.

## Evidence at a glance

| Source | Design and unit | Reported result | What it establishes | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Flexible intake interviews](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31239) | Field experiment involving about 65,000 Los Angeles SNAP applicants | Applicant-initiated flexible interviews increased approvals by about 6 percentage points, doubled early approvals, and increased longer-term participation by more than 2 percentage points | A route/interview design can change approval and continued participation among applicants | Los Angeles during the study period; it does not measure later food, debt, health, trust, or political action for each applicant |
| [Enrollment-office openings and closings](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34529) | Administrative SNAP cases linked to geocoded offices in one state; event-study variation | Closing an office reduced tract SNAP participation by 7–9% over two years; opening effects were suggestive and concentrated in urban areas | Place-based access and travel/office availability can change participation | Participation, not take-up among all eligible people; one-state geography and no same-person interpretation |
| [General work requirements](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32441) | Linked administrative records; parents exposed to general SNAP work requirements | Parents did not increase work but became much less likely to receive SNAP; authors identify administrative burden as the key mechanism | A rule framed as labor activation can instead reduce benefit access without the intended work response | Studied state and policy; does not estimate every work-requirement design or downstream household security |
| [Procedural denials](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34698) | Administrative data with age-based exemption and difference-in-differences design | Work requirements significantly increased procedural and overall denials | Reporting/compliance requirements can operate through denial procedure, not only eligibility | Recent working paper; design-specific and not a national denial rate |
| [Recertification timing and churn](https://www.nber.org/reporter/2024number3/snap-eligibility-enforcement-and-program-adoption) | Administrative recertification cases and interview timing | Roughly half of cases failed recertification; many rejected cases appeared below the earnings threshold, and about half of failures reentered soon after | Exit and reentry can reflect administrative churn rather than durable improvement | The cited evidence is from defined administrative settings and does not measure every reason for failure |

## The deeper pattern

The studies separate four things that are often collapsed into “program
participation”:

1. **Eligibility:** whether the household qualifies under the rule.
2. **Route completion:** whether it can reach the office, complete the
   interview, document work or exemption, and meet the deadline.
3. **Receipt:** whether benefits are approved and issued.
4. **Security:** whether food, housing, work, debt, health, and time become
   more secure afterward.

A route can change the second and third stages without changing underlying
   need. A person can exit and reenter without a durable improvement. A work
   requirement can reduce receipt without increasing work. A flexible interview
   can increase approval without proving that the later benefit fully resolves
   food or housing pressure.

## Connection to the SIPP transition result

The SIPP outcome layer finds that, among observed adjacent person-month pairs,
rent/mortgage hardship in the recorded following month was 16.4% after
no-to-yes SNAP transitions, 11.5% after yes-to-no transitions, 12.0% after
yes-to-yes pairs, and 3.9% after no-to-no pairs. Utility hardship was about
20.8% after both entry and exit, compared with 5.9% after stable nonreceipt.

The cross-study reading is bounded:

- route studies show that administration can alter participation;
- SIPP shows that participation state and material hardship can coexist in
  different combinations;
- together they motivate a same-episode test of notice, effort, amount,
  interruption, remedy, and later security;
- they do not prove that administrative burden caused the SIPP hardship
  pattern or that any one route caused a person's exit.

## Societal and political meaning

The institutional trend is not simply “government gives” or “government
withholds.” It is that access is distributed through offices, software,
interviews, documents, work rules, deadlines, and the capacity to persist. That
can redistribute:

- **time:** travel, waiting, repeated contacts, and paperwork;
- **money:** missed work, transport, lost benefits, and replacement food;
- **control:** whether a person can appeal, correct, or choose another route;
- **status and meaning:** whether the system feels protective, surveilling,
  arbitrary, humiliating, or legitimate;
- **political capacity:** whether there is enough time, trust, and efficacy to
  complain, organize, vote, or demand redesign.

The last two arrows remain open in the current evidence. Administrative records
can show participation and denial; they cannot infer dignity, blame, trust, or
political action.

## Required counterexamples

The working interpretation would weaken or become more conditional if:

- office closures changed participation only where need also changed;
- flexible interviews increased approvals only among ineligible applicants;
- work requirements increased employment enough to offset lost benefits and
  did not increase procedural denials;
- exits consistently preceded improved resources, food, housing, and health;
- people facing the same route burden retained equal appeal, trust, and civic
  participation because alternative channels actually worked.

## Next bounded design

Use the same-episode public-system ledger across several states and channels.
Hold the need and eligibility category as comparable as possible. Record the
route, effort, decision, benefit timing and amount, interruption, material
result, interpretation, complaint/appeal, and later action. Preserve eligible
non-applicants, procedural denials, exits followed by reentry, and exits without
measured improvement as separate cells.

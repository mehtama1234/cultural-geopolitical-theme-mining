# Claims ledger: US safety-net access, work rules, and the price of help

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; SNAP access, work rules, offices, food, work, and household security

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | A work rule can reduce SNAP participation without increasing employment. | Linked administrative data | NBER 28877 finds a 23-point increase in exits among incumbent participants after 18 months and no employment effect; NBER 32441 finds parents worked no more but were much less likely to receive SNAP. | Results come from defined policy settings and groups. |
| C-002 | The work of proving eligibility can be the main reason people leave. | Mechanism evidence | NBER 32441 identifies administrative burden as the key driver of parent disenrollment. | It does not measure every state's process or every household's burden. |
| C-003 | A nearby enrollment office is part of access. | Place-based causal study | NBER 34529 finds SNAP participation in a census tract fell 7–9% over two years after an office closed, with suggestive increases after openings. | It studies one state and participation, not food security. |
| C-004 | SNAP participation and food security are different outcomes. | Official research summary | USDA reports research that SNAP improves food security and helps smooth food access across the month. | Participation is selective and the summary is not a new causal estimate for every family. |
| C-005 | A rule can change access without changing the work or food problem it was meant to address. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 connect requirements, burden, place, participation, and food support. | Same-household food, debt, health, work, and trust effects remain open. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
food or income need
  -> eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task
  -> benefit kept, lost, or never reached
  -> food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome
  -> policy change, complaint, or political response
```

# Source search: US safety-net access, work rules, and the price of help

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

When food or cash is tight, does a safety-net rule move people toward work or mainly make help harder to keep?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-SNAP-WORK-28877 | [NBER: Employed in a SNAP?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28877) | Work requirements increased exits from SNAP by 23 percentage points among incumbent participants after 18 months; the study found no employment effect | Published causal study using linked administrative data | Setting and rule period are specific; results do not describe every work requirement |
| US-NBER-SNAP-PARENTS-32441 | [NBER: SNAP work requirements for parents](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32441) | Parents did not increase work but were much less likely to receive SNAP; administrative burden was the main mechanism identified | Working paper, revised 2025 | Full paper and generalization need review; it studies parents in a defined policy setting |
| US-NBER-SNAP-OFFICES-34529 | [NBER: SNAP office access and participation](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34529) | In one state, closing an enrollment office reduced SNAP participation in a census tract by 7–9% over two years; openings showed suggestive increases | Working paper with linked administrative and location data | One state and a specific office measure; participation is not the same as food security |
| US-USDA-SNAP-ERS | [USDA SNAP statistics and research](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-nutrition-assistance/supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap/key-statistics-and-research) | SNAP improves food security in research studies; benefits help smooth food access across the month | Official research summary | Participants are selected into the program; the summary is not a new estimate for every household |
| US-GAO-STUDENT-SNAP | [GAO food-insecure college students and SNAP](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107074) | About 59% of potentially eligible food-insecure college students did not report receiving SNAP | Official analysis | Student eligibility has special rules and the data are from 2020 |

## First pattern to test

```text
food or income need
  -> eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task
  -> benefit kept, lost, or never reached
  -> food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome
  -> policy change, complaint, or political response
```

The opening causal studies support a distinction between program exit and employment. The office study adds a place mechanism. The missing link is whether losing access changes food, borrowing or work for the same household.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Work rules may be intended to encourage employment, and some people leave because their circumstances improve. The current studies find limited or no work response in their settings, but that is not proof that every eligibility rule has the same effect.

## Main gaps

- benefit loss and food outcomes for the same person or household;
- earnings, hours, commute and care demands after a work rule;
- office distance, online access, language, disability and renewal burden;
- state differences in eligibility, benefit size and administrative practice;
- political response beyond participation and complaints.

## Decision rule

Pair a program-participation change with food or income data in the same place and period. Do not call a rule a work incentive when the evidence only shows that people left the program.

# New Jersey EBT chip-card fraud-prevention follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** statewide implementation and pilot-use stage observed; loss restoration and household recovery remain open

## Bounded update

New Jersey Human Services announced a July 2026 EMV chip-card pilot for SNAP
and Work First New Jersey cardholders in Cumberland, Essex, and Mercer
counties, with a planned statewide rollout. The notice says replacement-card
requests statewide receive a chip card. It also states that electronically
stolen SNAP and WFNJ benefits on or after December 21, 2024 are not eligible
for replacement under the stated federal funding rule.

The state's September 2026 follow-up reports that chip cards were distributed
statewide. During the pilot it reports hundreds of thousands of successful
transactions, including more than 100,000 chip transactions, 41,000 tap-to-pay
transactions, and 56,000 online purchases. These are implementation and
transaction outputs, not unique-card activation, avoided-theft, or restored-food
measures.

## Activation and usability boundary — 2026-10-20

The implementation page says benefits transfer automatically when the new card
is activated. For cardholders outside the original three-county pilot, the old
magstripe card stops working on October 20, 2026, while the new chip/tap card
must be activated by November 20, 2026 or a replacement must be requested.
This creates a measurable conversion risk: statewide mailing and transaction
volume do not establish that each household activated the card in time or could
use its benefits without interruption.

```text
EBT theft exposure
  -> report / card replacement route
  -> statewide chip-card distribution and pilot secure-payment use
  -> [open] unique activation and uninterrupted benefit use
  -> [open] prevented-theft rate
  -> [open] restoration of prior stolen benefits / food-gap duration
  -> [open] adequacy, trust, recurrence, and exit
```

The new chip-card control is a real institutional response, but it is not a
retroactive remedy. A safer future payment instrument can coexist with an
unrecovered prior loss, and the public sources do not show whether a card was
activated, whether theft was prevented, or whether a household's food access
was restored.

## Next decisive evidence

Seek an authorized aggregate showing card activation, theft incidence before
and after rollout, replacement claims, or prevented-loss indicators. For a
same-case episode, seek one lawful claimant record that joins theft timing,
usable food access, replacement or denial, and later food-security or household
outcomes. Do not infer those outcomes from rollout or card issuance.

# Public routes to work and security are usable only when the middle steps hold

**Status:** provisional cross-source synthesis  
**Checked:** 2026-09-18  
**Scope:** disability-accessible workforce programs, working-adult safety-net
participation, and workplace control; no same-person causal join

## The end-to-end question

The broad atlas needs to distinguish formal eligibility from usable options.
Across the retained evidence, the relevant chain is:

```text
need or constrained labor market
  -> usable public route to work or household protection
  -> service completion, employment, income, and benefit continuity
  -> workplace discretion and credible alternatives
  -> durable security, trust, action, or exit
```

Each arrow is measured by a different source. The synthesis is therefore a
stage map and counterexample register, not a combined estimate.

## Three measured surfaces

| Surface | What is observed | What it adds | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accessible workforce route | In PY2023, 184,227 participants in four DOL programs reported disabilities; 60% of Adult participants reporting disabilities were employed in the second quarter after exit versus 76% reporting no disability. Adult training use was 33% versus 47%. | A public route can be measured through participation, service use, and post-exit outcomes | Accommodation request/receipt, completion, placement mechanism, retention, wages by hours, and whether access barriers caused the gap |
| Working adults and public protection | About 13.8 million Medicaid-enrolled adults and 10.6 million adults living in SNAP households worked at some point in 2024; roughly two-thirds worked 35+ hours per week | Employment and public protection coexist; benefit participation is not equivalent to nonwork | Individual benefit use, churn, employer benefits, schedule, wage adequacy, household need, and worker choice |
| Workplace control and AI access | SHED generative-AI use was 30% among employees with high task-completion control versus 20% with less control; education differences were also large | The quality of the job determines whether new tools become usable adaptation or merely exposure | Employer permission, task change, time saved, monitoring, pay, schedule, remedy, and later movement |

The narrow defensible proposition is:

> Public systems can expand formal opportunity while the person-level option
> remains constrained by accessible service completion, household protection,
> and workplace discretion. Employment, benefit receipt, and tool access are
> not interchangeable evidence of security or power.

## The middle-step failures

The records identify different failure locations:

1. **Entry failure:** a person cannot reach or use an accessible workforce
   service, interpreter, technology, or outside training provider.
2. **Conversion failure:** a service is used but does not translate into
   training, placement, employment, or adequate earnings.
3. **Security failure:** employment coexists with Medicaid or SNAP reliance,
   short annual attachment, unstable hours, or household need.
4. **Control failure:** a worker has employment but little discretion to use a
   tool, alter tasks, or contest a harmful decision.
5. **Exit/remedy failure:** a formal review or benefit route exists but does
   not produce timely restoration, a credible alternative, or durable choice.

These are analytically distinct. A lower post-exit employment rate for
participants reporting disabilities does not identify an inaccessible
accommodation. Medicaid enrollment does not identify employer underpayment.
Lower AI use with less task control does not identify lower ability. A remedy
order does not prove restored opportunity.

## Mechanism map

```text
disability, care, income, or job-search constraint
  -> application, disclosure, accommodation, or benefit route
  -> usable service and completed administrative step
  -> work, earnings, coverage, food protection, or training outcome
  -> schedule, task control, employer benefit, and ability to contest
  -> household security or continued dependence
  -> trust, collective response, switching, or exit
```

The most important missing fields are not another population percentage. They
are the transitions: who asked, what was supplied, how long it took, what was
completed, what was gained or sacrificed, and what happened next.

## Counterexamples that protect the interpretation

- A workforce program can have a legal accessibility requirement and assistive
  technology while an interpreter is unavailable or an outside training site
  remains inaccessible.
- A worker can be employed while enrolled in Medicaid or living in a SNAP
  household; “working” does not establish adequate security.
- A participant can withhold disability type for privacy or fear of
  discrimination; missingness is not automatically absence of need.
- A worker can have access to an AI tool but insufficient task discretion to
  convert it into time savings or bargaining power.
- A formal remedy can pay money without restoring the original opportunity, or
  restore access without verifying financial recovery.

These counterexamples are why the atlas treats usable access, completion,
outcome, control, remedy, and exit as separate currencies.

## Arrow ledger

| Arrow | Status | Safe conclusion | Required next evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need → public route | Compared / reported | Workforce programs and safety-net programs create formal routes for constrained people | Same-person application, eligibility, accommodation, notice, and route effort |
| Public route → completed service | Open / partly observed | Participation and some service use are measured; failed, delayed, or abandoned routes are not | Requested versus supplied accommodation, wait, provider, completion, and denial |
| Completed service → work/security | Compared | Post-exit employment and earnings gaps, and working-adult benefit participation, are visible | Same-unit placement, hours, wage, employer benefits, benefit continuity, and household outcomes |
| Employment → workplace control | Open / conditioned | SHED shows control-stratified AI use; it does not follow public-route participants | Same-worker employer policy, task control, schedule, monitoring, pay, and tool use |
| Security/control → practical exit | Open | Alternatives, remedies, non-use, switching, and exit remain distinct open endpoints | Dated job/service event with alternatives, remedy receipt, later movement, trust, or action |

## What this changes in the broader program

This synthesis adds a usable middle to the broad material-to-power chain. It
shows why public capacity should be evaluated in terms of conversion:

```text
formal eligibility -> usable route -> completed service -> durable outcome
```

It also provides a disciplined link between the care, safety-net, work-control,
and consumer-recourse themes. The same person may experience all of these
systems, but the current public evidence does not identify that person across
them. The correct next design is a lawful, consented, event-compatible worker
or household record—not a geographic or topical merge of aggregate estimates.

## Next test

The smallest decisive record would follow one participant or household from a
dated work or care constraint through:

- application and alternative routes;
- disclosure and requested accommodation or benefit;
- waiting, effort, completion, and provider/employer response;
- work start, hours, wage, schedule, benefits, and household security;
- task control, tool use, complaint or remedy, and later movement; and
- trust, collective action, switching, non-use, or exit.

Until that record is available, this synthesis should guide acquisition and
sampling. It should not be turned into an accessibility, dependency, worker
power, or resilience index.

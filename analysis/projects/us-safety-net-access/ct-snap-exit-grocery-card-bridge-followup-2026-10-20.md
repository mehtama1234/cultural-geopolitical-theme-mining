# Connecticut SNAP-exit grocery-card bridge follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** state-funded bridge distributed through community agencies; individual receipt, adequacy, and durable reentry remain open

## Bounded episode

Connecticut announced an $8.5 million state-funded response for residents who
lost SNAP eligibility under new federal work rules. The June plan estimated
roughly 25,000 affected residents and a one-time $300 grocery card per eligible
individual. The August distribution announcement said cards were being
distributed through community action agencies as prepaid virtual grocery cards,
not through existing EBT accounts. Eligibility was confirmed when a person
requested the card; the card did not expire, and agencies were also expected to
connect recipients with support to regain SNAP eligibility where possible.

```text
SNAP eligibility loss under federal work-rule change
  -> state response and community-agency request route
  -> eligibility confirmation
  -> [open] virtual grocery-card receipt and spending
  -> [open] food adequacy, household substitution, and SNAP reentry
  -> [open] trust, action, persistence, and exit
```

The route adds an actual distribution stage and a defined substitute payment
channel to the public-benefit atlas. It does not turn the estimated 25,000
population, $8.5 million appropriation, or $300 design into a recipient-level
receipt or food-security result.

## Post-distribution recheck — 2026-10-20

The current Connecticut DSS SNAP-eligibility page still directs people who lost
SNAP under the federal work-rule changes to contact their local Community Action
Agency for the one-time grocery card. It does not publish requests, confirmed
eligibility, cards distributed, card activation or spending, or later SNAP
reentry. The public route therefore closes at a maintained distribution and
referral endpoint, not at verified receipt or recovery.

## Next decisive evidence

Seek a lawful aggregate of requests, confirmed eligibility, cards distributed,
activation or spending, and SNAP reentry. The strongest same-person route would
join the DSS notice and benefit exit to grocery-card receipt/use, food security,
household budget adaptation, and later program status.

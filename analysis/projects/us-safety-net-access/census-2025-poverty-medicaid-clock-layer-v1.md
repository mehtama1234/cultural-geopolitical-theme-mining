# Census 2025 poverty and Medicaid clock-divergence layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** bounded cross-theme public-system finding; not a same-person poverty-to-coverage panel

## What the current releases show

The Census Bureau reports that the Hispanic official poverty rate fell to
**13.9% in 2025**, down 1.2 percentage points from 2024 and the lowest recorded
rate for the group. The national official poverty rate also fell to 10.2%.
Yet Medicaid coverage moved on a different administrative clock: overall
Medicaid coverage declined from 2023 to 2025, including a second consecutive
decline between 2024 and 2025, after continuous-coverage protections ended and
states returned to standard eligibility and renewal processes.

The Census report identifies a **2.0 percentage-point** child Medicaid decline
from 2023 to 2024 and a **1.1 percentage-point** working-age-adult decline over
that interval. Between 2024 and 2025, coverage fell 0.5 points for the total
population and 0.6 points for working-age adults; children's coverage did not
change in that year.

## Interpretation boundary

These are not linked observations of the same Hispanic people or households.
The poverty release and the health-insurance release are population-level CPS
ASEC estimates with different denominators and timing. The safe finding is
therefore not that poverty improvement caused coverage loss, or that the same
people became poorer and uninsured. It is that **material improvement and
public-coverage continuity can diverge**, because earnings/poverty and program
eligibility/renewal are governed by different clocks.

## Next decisive field

Join person- or household-level poverty transitions to Medicaid eligibility,
renewal attempts, procedural disenrollment, replacement coverage, care use,
affordability, work, and later material security. Preserve race/ethnicity,
state expansion status, age, disability, household composition, and survey
timing rather than collapsing the pattern into one “well-being” score.

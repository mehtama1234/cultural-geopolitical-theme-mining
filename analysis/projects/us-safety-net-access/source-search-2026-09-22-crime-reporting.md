# Source search: victimization, police visibility, and public safety

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; victimization and official police visibility are separate stages

## Working question

How much public safety becomes visible to police, and what gets lost when a
victimization is not reported?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-BJS-NCVS-2024 | [Criminal Victimization, 2024](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/criminal-victimization-2024) | BJS estimates violent and property victimization, including whether incidents were reported to police, and documents the 2024 instrument transition. | Official BJS National Crime Victimization Survey report | It is a survey estimate, not a complete incident ledger, police performance measure, victim-service outcome, or same-person trust/action panel. |

## First pattern

```text
experienced victimization
  -> report, nonreport, or third-party notification
  -> police-visible record or private/unrecorded experience
  -> [open] response, safety, remedy, trust, and later action
```

In 2024, BJS estimated 23.3 violent victimizations per 1,000 people age 12
or older, while 11.2 per 1,000 were reported to police. BJS also reports that
about 48% of violent victimizations and 30% of property victimizations were
reported. The difference is not just missing data: BJS records reasons for
nonreporting such as fear of reprisal, doubts that police could help, or viewing
the incident as personal or too trivial to report.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The report uses the 2024 legacy instrument during a split-sample transition to
a redesigned NCVS instrument. The estimates remain nationally representative,
but precision is lower during the transition. A nonreport does not prove
distrust, and a police report does not prove safety, remedy, or confidence.

## Decision rule

Keep experienced harm, reporting, police record, response, victim services,
remedy, safety, trust, and collective action as separate stages. Reopen this
lane only with a case or repeated-person design that adds response or outcome
evidence.

# Connecticut EBT lock controls and nonreplacement follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** prevention and card-replacement routes observed; stolen-benefit restoration and household recovery remain open

## Bounded update

Connecticut DSS currently directs EBT cardholders to use MyDSS or the
ConnectEBT app to lock and unlock cards and to block or unblock out-of-state
transactions. The same guidance says the state can replace a lost, stolen, or
damaged card, but cannot replace SNAP or cash benefits stolen from the card.
Benefits remaining on an old card automatically transfer to its replacement.

```text
EBT theft exposure
  -> card lock / out-of-state blocking / report route
  -> physical-card replacement and remaining-balance transfer
  -> [open] control uptake and avoided theft
  -> [open] restoration of stolen balance and food-gap duration
  -> [open] adequacy, trust, recurrence, and exit
```

This is a useful counterexample to the New Jersey chip-card rollout. Both
states expose prevention and card-access machinery, but neither public route
shows whether households used the control in time, avoided theft, or recovered
from a stolen balance. The physical card can be replaced while the underlying
food resource remains lost.

## Next decisive evidence

Seek an authorized aggregate showing lock use, blocked transactions, theft
incidence, replacement-card timing, or prevented-loss indicators. For a same-case
episode, seek a lawful claimant record joining theft timing, usable food access,
card replacement or denial, and later food-security or household outcomes.

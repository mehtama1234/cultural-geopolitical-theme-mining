# A timely SNAP theft report can be denied before an appeal corrects the process

**Status:** primary administrative case record; corrective order reached, post-order replacement receipt open  
**Checked:** 2026-10-20

## What happened

Connecticut DSS/OLCRAH published Notice of Decision Request No. 235520. The
record anonymizes the appellant and redacts the dates, but preserves one
one-person SNAP household across the main administrative sequence.

Four unauthorized transactions removed $292.44 from the household's EBT
account. After discovering the loss, the appellant tried to use $208.73 and was
told the account had insufficient funds. The decision says she reported the
theft three days after discovery, received a replacement card, and later
submitted the required replacement form. DSS denied replacement on the theory
that the theft was reported too late. The hearing found the initial report was
timely and that DSS had not documented when it issued the form. OLCRAH granted
the appeal, ordered the denial rescinded, and ordered DSS to process the request
as timely.

## Why it matters

This case adds a different middle-stage failure from the retained partial
replacement case. The issue was not only whether theft could be validated or
whether a replacement cap applied. A household can report within the initial
window and still face a denial when the agency cannot show how the required
form reached the household or how the submission clock began.

The appeal corrected the administrative disposition, but the public record
stops before the decisive material endpoint: whether DSS actually processed the
claim, credited the account, and restored usable food purchasing capacity. A
corrective order is therefore an institutional remedy stage, not proof of
recipient receipt or household recovery.

## Remaining open arrows

```text
stolen EBT transactions
  -> failed purchase and timely theft report
  -> replacement card and form submission
  -> initial denial
  -> [observed] granted appeal and corrective order
  -> [open] claim processing and replacement credit
  -> [open] food access, adequacy, and household recovery
  -> [open] recurrence, trust, complaint, action, or exit
```

The next decisive field would be a lawful post-order administrative or account
record connecting the corrective order to actual replacement issuance and a
later food or household-security outcome. The record is intentionally not
treated as a completed same-unit route.

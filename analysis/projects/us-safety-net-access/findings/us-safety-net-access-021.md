# Finding 021: Making SNAP easier to claim can raise take-up while weakening targeting

**Status:** bounded randomized take-up and targeting finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The finding

In a randomized field experiment involving 30,000 Pennsylvania adults age 60+
who were likely eligible for SNAP but not enrolled, nine-month enrollment was
about 6% in the status-quo group, 11% after eligibility information alone, and
18% after information plus application assistance.

The administrative price of the intervention also differed. The paper's rough
calculation puts the cost per additional enrollee at about $20 for information
alone and $60 for information plus assistance. About 30% of people in each
intervention arm called in, and roughly 75% of applications succeeded; the
larger assistance effect therefore appears to come from help completing the
route, not merely from more people calling.

The intervention changed who entered the program as well as how many entered.
People induced to apply or enroll received lower benefits and were less sick
than the average control-group enrollee. The neediest people were also less
likely to respond to the outreach, consistent with larger information or
transaction frictions among those with greater need.

## Why it matters

This adds a public-system implementation mechanism beyond “benefit available”
or “benefit received.” Information and application help can increase take-up,
but the marginal people reached by a general outreach strategy may be less
needy than the people who already manage to enroll. A larger caseload can
therefore coexist with poorer targeting and incomplete reach to the people in
greatest need.

## The chain

```text
likely eligibility
  -> information or application assistance
  -> application and enrollment
  -> lower-benefit / lower-illness marginal enrollee profile
  -> food security, adequacy, trust, action, or recovery [open]
```

## Do not overread

- This is a Pennsylvania experiment among older likely-eligible non-enrollees,
  not a current national SNAP take-up estimate.
- Enrollment and benefit amount do not establish food security, adequacy,
  household debt, or later health.
- The result does not prove why the neediest people were less responsive; the
  mechanism could include information, paperwork, health, trust, or access.
- The study does not follow recipients through later security, trust, or
  political action.

Source: [NBER Working Paper 24652](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24652).

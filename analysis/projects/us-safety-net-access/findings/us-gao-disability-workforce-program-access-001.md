# Finding 001: Workforce-program eligibility does not guarantee an accessible route to completion

**Status:** bounded disability-access, worker-opportunity, and public-workforce finding · **Checked: 2026-09-22**

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed Department of Labor workforce programs and found that people who
reported disabilities had lower post-exit employment rates and median earnings
than participants who did not report disabilities. In the Adult program,
second-quarter employment was 60% for participants reporting a disability and
76% for those who did not; median second-quarter earnings were $7,048 and
$8,872. In the Wagner-Peyser program, the comparable employment rates were 57%
and 70%, with median earnings of $6,684 and $8,486.

The route itself had visible gaps. Seven percent of reviewed participants
reported a disability, but 43% of those participants did not report a
disability type. In the Adult program, 33% of participants reporting a
disability used training services, compared with 47% of those who did not.
Officials at selected job centers reported difficulty providing sign-language
interpreters and making training outside job centers accessible. DOL also did
not routinely analyze state and local monitoring reports at an aggregate level.

The practical route is:

```text
job-search need and disability-related access need
  -> disclosure, accommodation, technology, interpreter, and training route
  -> service use and program exit
  -> employment, earnings, retention, and mobility
  -> [open] correction, trust, durable security, and benefit transition
```

The broader insight is that a public workforce program can be formally open
while the practical route still depends on local capacity, disclosure choices,
outside training providers, and whether the agency can see repeated failures.
An employment gap is real evidence of unequal outcomes, but it is not by itself
evidence that inaccessible services caused the gap.

## Limits and next test

- The comparison is among selected program participants, not all disabled
  workers or all job seekers.
- Disability reporting is voluntary and sensitive; missing type is not
  automatically a data-collection failure.
- The 12 visited job centers were selected sites, not a representative sample.
- Employment and earnings differences may reflect disability severity, prior
  work history, local labor demand, selection, service mix, or other factors.
- Program exit is an administrative definition, not necessarily job exit or
  voluntary withdrawal; earnings are not hourly wages.
- The audit does not show accommodation requests, receipt, abandonment,
  placement quality, retention, employer response, trust, or later benefits.

The next decisive evidence would link voluntary disability/access information
to the requested and received accommodation, service completion, placement,
retention, wages, and local monitoring or correction.

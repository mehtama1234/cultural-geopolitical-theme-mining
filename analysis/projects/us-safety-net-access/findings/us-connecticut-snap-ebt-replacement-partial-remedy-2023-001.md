# A SNAP theft claim can produce partial replacement before the appeal ends

**Status:** primary administrative case record; partial benefit episode with
verified replacement issuance and a residual claim denied
**Checked:** 2026-10-19

## What happened

The Connecticut Department of Social Services published Notice of Decision,
OLCRAH Request No. 226201. The record anonymizes the appellant and dates but
preserves the case sequence. The appellant retained her physical EBT card while
living periodically in Connecticut and Virginia. SNAP benefits were used for
purchases in New York and Arizona. After discovering the loss, she reported the
theft, requested a new card, submitted the stolen-benefit replacement form, and
filed a police report through her daughter.

The Department issued a Notice of Action finding the appellant eligible for
$582.00 in replacement benefits, issued a new EBT card, and credited $582.00 to
the account. The appellant then requested a hearing for an additional $2,125.34.
The hearing officer found that the covered loss was $590.16, that the $582.00
credit represented two months of the appellant's $291.00 maximum allotment, and
that the remaining requested amount was not timely reported. The appeal was
denied, while the already-issued $582.00 replacement remained part of the
record.

## Why it matters

This case supplies a positive remedy endpoint that the New Jersey denial case
does not: the administrative record explicitly states that replacement benefits
were issued. It also shows why “replacement” is not a single binary outcome.
The household received a bounded credit, then pursued a larger residual claim
through a hearing. The public record closes before showing whether the credit
restored food access, prevented bill arrears, or changed later trust or behavior.

The record should not be interpreted as a full household recovery measure. The
dates are redacted, the appellant is anonymized, and the decision does not
report consumption, food security, other resources, or later benefit use. The
case also cannot estimate the frequency of partial replacement or residual
denial.

## Remaining open arrows

```text
stolen EBT transactions
  -> theft report, card replacement, form, and police report
  -> $582 replacement issued
  -> hearing for larger residual claim
  -> residual claim denied
  -> [open] food access, bill substitution, adequacy, and household recovery
  -> [open] recurrence, trust, complaint, action, or exit
```

The next decisive field would be a lawful follow-up connecting the replacement
credit to subsequent food purchasing or household material security, or a later
administrative record showing reconsideration, continued benefits, or another
theft episode.

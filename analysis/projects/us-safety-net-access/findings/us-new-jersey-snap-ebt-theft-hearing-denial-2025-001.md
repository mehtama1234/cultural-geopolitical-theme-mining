# A SNAP replacement claim can reach a formal hearing without restoring the lost benefit

**Status:** primary administrative case record; partial benefit episode with
final denial and no later household-recovery endpoint  
**Checked:** 2026-10-19

## What happened

The New Jersey Department of Human Services published a final decision in
*F.W. v. Essex County Division of Welfare*, OAL DKT. NO. HPW 02676-25 and
Agency DKT. NO. C736453007. The petitioner disputed three EBT transactions at a
particular supermarket on December 6 and 7, 2024, totaling $400.34, and filed a
request for replacement of electronically stolen SNAP benefits on December 9.
The agency denied the request on December 13 after reviewing the transactions;
the petitioner appealed, an administrative-law judge held a hearing on May 20,
2025, and the final decision dated June 17, 2025 affirmed the denial.

The agency's investigator reported that the transactions were at local stores
the petitioner regularly frequented, did not fit the agency's skimming pattern,
and involved manual entry of the card number and PIN. The petitioner denied
making the transactions and denied that a family member made them. The final
decision adopted the ALJ's conclusion that the agency had not determined that
the benefits were electronically stolen.

## Why it matters

This case closes more of the public-benefit administrative route than a
participation rate or retrospective barrier survey: it preserves a claimant,
transaction amount, claim date, agency investigation, appeal, hearing, and
final disposition. It also supplies a negative remedy case. A benefit-control
system can offer a replacement route and a hearing while the public record ends
with a denial and no evidence of how the household absorbed the disputed loss.

The case must not be read as proof that the petitioner was wrong or that the
agency process was accurate. It records the agency's adopted finding under a
specific statutory replacement regime. It also cannot estimate the frequency
of theft, false claims, denials, or successful replacement.

## Remaining open arrows

```text
disputed transactions
  -> replacement claim
  -> agency investigation and denial
  -> administrative hearing and affirmed decision
  -> [open] benefit adequacy, food/bill substitution, persistence, and recovery
  -> [open] trust, complaint, action, recurrence, or exit
```

The next decisive field would be a lawful follow-up showing whether the
household had other food or cash support, later SNAP continuity, an amended
decision, or a material consequence after the denial. The current decision
does not expose those fields.

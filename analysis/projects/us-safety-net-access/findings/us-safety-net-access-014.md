# Finding 014: Work and public-benefit reliance are parts of one labor system

**Status:** bounded work–safety-net–employer finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

GAO reports that about 13.8 million Medicaid-enrolled adults ages 19–64 and
about 10.6 million adults living in SNAP-receiving households worked at some
point in 2024. About 88% of the working adults in each reported private-sector
employment. The groups were less likely than comparison wage earners to work
full-time hours for at least 50 weeks per year.

In selected administrative data from 15 state agencies across 11 states, 46
companies appeared among the top 25 employers of Medicaid enrollees or SNAP
beneficiaries in at least two states. This is a concentration signal, not proof
that employers caused eligibility, deliberately shifted costs to government, or
offered inadequate jobs.

The important cultural and economic correction is simple: employment is not
the same thing as material independence. Public benefits can be part of the
operating environment that lets households work, manage food and health costs,
and remain attached to jobs.

## The chain

```text
work attachment and wages/hours
  <-> Medicaid/SNAP eligibility and household receipt
  -> food and health-cost buffering or administrative uncertainty
  -> employer labor system and public-resource interaction
  -> security, mobility, trust, political interpretation, or exit [open]
```

The decisive missing question is not whether benefit-connected adults work. It
is whether the same household can maintain adequate security, predictable work,
continuous coverage, and a real alternative over time.

## Limits and next field

- Medicaid is individual enrollment; SNAP is household receipt, so the two
  populations are not interchangeable.
- “Worked at some point” does not establish stable employment, adequate wages,
  predictable schedules, or continuous benefits.
- The employer analysis uses selected state data and different eligibility
  universes; top-employer overlap is not per-worker incidence.
- The next decisive evidence is a state-year worker–household–employer episode
  joining wages, hours, benefits, eligibility/churn, household need, notice,
  remedy, later security, and worker choice.

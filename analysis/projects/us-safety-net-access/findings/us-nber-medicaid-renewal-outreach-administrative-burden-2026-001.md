# Finding 001: Navigator outreach can convert administrative help into Medicaid renewal, with uneven contact reach

**Status:** bounded experimental public-system and administrative-burden finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

An NBER field experiment with Covering Wisconsin tested whether an additional
outreach route could prevent Medicaid loss during renewal. All participants
received written outreach; a randomized group also received a prerecorded call
offering free one-on-one navigator assistance.

The study covered 92,957 fee-for-service Medicaid beneficiaries in 80,016
households facing renewal deadlines from June 2023 through June 2024. For
62,499 individuals in 55,310 households, receipt of the prerecorded call was
randomized. Assignment to the call increased renewal by 1.0 percentage point
from a 67.8% baseline and reduced procedural denials by 1.1 points from a
22.3% baseline. Effects were larger in several groups: renewal rose 4.0
points for Tribal members, 2.1 points for children, 1.9 points for people with
chronic conditions, and 1.4 points for participants below the sample-median
income.

The route is:

```text
renewal deadline and paperwork requirement
  -> written outreach plus optional navigator-call route
  -> contact delivery and assistance
  -> renewal or procedural denial
  -> [open] continuous coverage, care use, health, cost, and household room
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a rare experimental closure of an administrative-friction arrow:

> Eligibility can remain unchanged while the route used to complete a renewal
> changes who retains coverage.

The result also makes reach part of the mechanism. J-PAL reports that at most
about 61% of prerecorded calls were estimated to reach the correct person, and
only about half of phone numbers or addresses could be confirmed as likely
correct. The intervention therefore tests both assistance and the contact
infrastructure needed to deliver it.

The finding should not be read as proof that all nonrenewal is wrongful or
that renewal guarantees care. The study concerns fee-for-service Wisconsin
enrollees facing renewal, not managed-care enrollees or the national Medicaid
population.

## Limits and next test

- The sample and delivery system are Wisconsin-specific and selected around
  imminent renewal deadlines.
- Assignment to receive a call differs from successfully receiving it; the
  call arm stopped early after effectiveness evidence.
- Renewal and procedural denial are administrative outcomes, not continuous
  coverage, care receipt, health, or financial recovery.
- The study does not show which families used navigator help, what paperwork
  was completed, or whether coverage persisted at the next transition.

The next decisive artifact is a protected renewal episode ledger linking
eligibility, notice, contact validity, outreach mode, navigator contact,
paperwork, decision, coverage continuity, care use, medical cost, work/care
adaptation, trust, and later renewal or exit. Compare written, text, call,
human navigator, and automated routes without treating delivery as receipt.

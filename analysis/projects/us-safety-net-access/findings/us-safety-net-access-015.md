# Finding 015: Formal workforce access can fail before employment recovery

**Status:** bounded disability workforce-access finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

GAO's review of selected DOL workforce programs shows a measurable gap between
formal access and employment outcomes. Seven percent of participants reported a
disability, and 43% of those participants did not report a disability type.
In the Adult program, 33% of participants reporting disabilities used training
services compared with 47% of participants reporting no disability.

After program exit, participants reporting disabilities had lower employment
and earnings in each core program. In the Adult program, second-quarter
employment was 60% versus 76%, and median second-quarter earnings among those
employed were $7,048 versus $8,872.

GAO also found selected local implementation problems: difficulty providing
interpreters, inaccessible training outside job centers, and monitoring reports
that DOL did not routinely analyze in aggregate.

This is not proof that inaccessible services caused the outcome gap. It is
evidence that eligibility, a job center, assistive technology, and monitoring
are different stages from usable completion, placement, and durable work.

## The chain

```text
disability / job-search need
  -> disclosure and accommodation route
  -> accessible intake, training, and counseling
  -> completion and placement
  -> employment, earnings, retention, and household security [partly open]
  -> correction, trust, voice, and exit [open]
```

The broad lesson is that institutional capacity is experienced at the
interface. A system can formally offer access while shifting the practical
cost of interpretation, technology, transport, or disclosure onto the person
seeking work.

## Limits and next field

- The participant comparison is not causal and does not identify disability
  type, prior work history, local labor demand, or service mix.
- The 12 visited job centers are not a representative sample.
- Non-disclosure may be voluntary and privacy-protective; missingness is not
  automatically evidence of institutional failure.
- The next decisive evidence is a voluntary participant-level route joining
  accommodation request/receipt, service completion, placement, retention,
  wages/hours, correction, and later household security.

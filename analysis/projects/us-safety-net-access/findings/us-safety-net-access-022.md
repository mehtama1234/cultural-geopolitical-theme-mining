# Finding 022: Expanding one benefit can lower the application cost of other benefits

**Status:** bounded quasi-experimental cross-program spillover finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The finding

A border-county and sub-state comparison of ACA Medicaid expansion found that
Medicaid expansion increased participation in SNAP and TANF, with a possible
positive but less precise effect on the EITC. The ACS evidence indicates that
the main change was participation conditional on eligibility, not a labor-supply
response that newly made people eligible.

The authors interpret the pattern as consistent with Medicaid eligibility
reducing the marginal cost of applying for SNAP, especially in places with low
pre-expansion SNAP take-up. One public-program doorway can therefore change
navigation of another program.

## Why it matters

This adds a cross-program implementation stage to the atlas. Public systems do
not operate only as separate benefit silos: eligibility information, contact,
or application routines created by one program may make another program easier
to claim. But the result does not establish that the additional SNAP or TANF
receipt produced food security, health, debt reduction, trust, or political
action.

## The chain

```text
Medicaid eligibility expansion
  -> lower marginal cost of navigating/applying
  -> conditional participation in SNAP/TANF
  -> benefit adequacy and household security [open]
  -> trust, action, or recovery [open]
```

## Do not overread

- This is a border-based quasi-experimental study of ACA expansion, not a
  randomized individual experiment or a current national estimate.
- The direction and mechanism are clearer than a single universal effect size;
  the study reports noisier or imprecise results for some programs and designs.
- Participation conditional on eligibility is not the same as benefit receipt,
  adequate benefits, food security, or completed care.
- The evidence does not follow the same household through multiple notices,
  applications, benefit amounts, adaptation, trust, or political action.

Source: [NBER Working Paper 26504](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26504).

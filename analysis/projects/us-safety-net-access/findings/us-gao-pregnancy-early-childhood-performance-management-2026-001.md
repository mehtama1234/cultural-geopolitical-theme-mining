# Finding 001: A broad early-childhood support system can coordinate programs while leaving outcome management incomplete

**Status:** bounded public-system, care, and early-childhood performance finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

GAO identified 242 federal programs and tax expenditures that provide some
benefit or service to pregnant women, children through age 5, or their
families, among other groups. Fifteen programs provide some direct service
solely to this population, spread across five federal agencies. The system is
therefore broad and fragmented by design, not a single benefit route.

Officials from all 15 focused programs reported coordinating with other
programs. But coordination is not the same as a common outcome ledger. Three
of the 15 programs had not fully established a federal performance-management
process that sets goals, collects performance information, and uses it to
assess results: HHS's Preschool Development Grants Birth Through Five program,
USDA's WIC Farmers Market Nutrition Program, and VA's Veterans Health
Administration Maternity Care Coordinator Program.

The route is:

```text
pregnancy, birth, or early-childhood need
  -> multiple federal programs and state/local delivery routes
  -> coordination and service contact
  -> [open] usable receipt, timing, burden, and continuity
  -> [uneven] federal goals, data, and outcome assessment
  -> [open] family, child, maternal, trust, and later-life outcome
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a governance concept to the public-systems theme:

> A support ecosystem can be large and coordinated at the program-official
> level while remaining unable to say, in a consistent federal frame, whether
> every component is achieving the intended result.

The distinction matters because program counts and coordination activity can
look like capacity, while the missing performance process limits correction,
resource targeting, and public accountability. It also protects against an
opposite error: a missing federal metric is not proof that state-defined goals,
local practice, or family experience are ineffective.

## Limits and next test

- The 242-program inventory includes programs serving other groups and tax
  expenditures; it is not an eligible-family denominator.
- The 15 focused programs are not a national service-use or beneficiary panel.
- Coordination was reported by officials and does not establish seamless
  access, lower burden, or improved outcomes.
- Performance-management status is a governance measure, not a causal estimate
  of child, maternal, or family wellbeing.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected family-program ledger linking
need or eligibility, outreach, application, referral, service receipt, timing,
cost and paperwork burden, cross-program handoff, outcome, correction, and
later stability. Compare programs with federal performance processes against
those relying mainly on state-defined goals while preserving the different
delivery units.

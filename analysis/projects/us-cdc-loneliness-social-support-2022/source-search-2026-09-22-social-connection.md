# Source search: loneliness, social support, and mental health in 2022

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** 26 U.S. states  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

Who has social connection and support, who is more likely to lack it, and how
does that social condition relate to reported mental-health measures?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| US-CDC-BRFSS-SOCIAL-CONNECTION-2022 | [CDC MMWR: Loneliness, Lack of Social and Emotional Support, and Mental Health Issues](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7324a1.htm) | Weighted prevalence of loneliness, lack of support, subgroup differences, and adjusted associations with stress, frequent mental distress, and depression history. | Cross-sectional, self-reported data from 26 states; it does not establish causation, later recovery, belonging, service use, or political action. |

## Observed pattern

Among 218,915 adults in the analytic sample, 32.1% reported loneliness and
24.1% reported lacking needed social and emotional support. Loneliness was
43.3% among adults ages 18–34 and 47.9% among adults with household income
below $25,000. It was 56.7% among bisexual adults and ranged from 56.4% to
63.9% among transgender groups. The adjusted prevalence ratios for stress,
frequent mental distress, and depression history were 3.6, 3.0, and 2.4 among
those reporting loneliness compared with those who did not.

## Working interpretation

```text
income, age, identity, household, and social conditions
  -> perceived connection and needed support
  -> reported stress, mental distress, or depression history
  -> [open] care use, belonging, civic participation, trust, recovery, and persistence
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

The adjusted associations do not prove that loneliness caused the mental-health
measure or that support would eliminate it. The sample covers 26 states and
uses self-reports, and the 2022 loneliness question measured feeling socially
isolated rather than the later direct loneliness wording. This lane stops at
the unequal social-connection and association boundary unless a repeated or
event-linked source adds support received, service use, later wellbeing,
belonging, or civic action.

# Finding 001: Social support is unevenly distributed and associated with mental-health burden

**Status:** bounded cultural, social-connection, and unequal-exposure finding · **Checked: 2026-09-22**

## The bounded finding

CDC's analysis of the 2022 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System found
that, among 218,915 adults in 26 states, 32.1% reported loneliness and 24.1%
reported a lack of needed social and emotional support.

The exposure was not evenly distributed. Loneliness was 43.3% among adults ages
18–34, 47.9% among adults with household income below $25,000, 56.7% among
bisexual adults, and between 56.4% and 63.9% among transgender groups. Lack of
needed support was 39.8% below $25,000 in household income, 36.5% among
bisexual adults, and between 34.4% and 44.8% among transgender groups.

After adjustment for demographic characteristics, people reporting loneliness
had adjusted prevalence ratios of 3.6 for stress, 3.0 for frequent mental
distress, and 2.4 for depression history compared with people who did not
report loneliness. Those reporting a lack of support also had elevated adjusted
ratios: 3.0, 2.6, and 1.8.

The practical route is:

```text
income, age, identity, household, and social conditions
  -> perceived connection and needed support
  -> reported stress, mental distress, or depression history
  -> [open] care use, belonging, civic participation, trust, recovery, and persistence
```

The cultural insight is that “community” is not a single resource shared
equally. A person may be surrounded by people yet lack the support they need,
and social connection itself is stratified by age, income, sexual orientation,
and gender identity. That makes belonging a material and institutional issue,
not only a private feeling.

## Limits and next test

- The sample includes 26 states and 218,915 respondents; it is not a complete
  national panel.
- Loneliness, support, stress, distress, and depression history are
  self-reported measures.
- The associations are adjusted cross-sectional prevalence ratios, not causal
  estimates and not evidence that loneliness caused a later condition.
- The 2022 question measured feeling socially isolated; CDC changed the wording
  in 2023 to ask directly about loneliness.
- The study does not show support received, care use, later recovery,
  organizational participation, trust, or political action.

The next decisive evidence would repeat the same respondents or link a dated
social-support intervention or disruption to support received, care use,
belonging, wellbeing, and later civic or political participation.

## Source

- [CDC MMWR: Loneliness, Lack of Social and Emotional Support, and Mental Health Issues — United States, 2022](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7324a1.htm)

**Evidence status:** official survey analysis establishes unequal social-
connection prevalence and adjusted associations with mental-health measures;

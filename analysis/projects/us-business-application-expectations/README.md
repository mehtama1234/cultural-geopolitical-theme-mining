# Project: Business applications, expectations, and realized entry

This project tracks the gap between deciding to start a business and creating
one that actually employs people. A Census working paper links 17 million U.S.
business applications to outcomes. Applicants overestimate employment on
average, mainly because many expect to enter but do not. Among applicants who
expect and achieve entry, expected employment is usually below realized
employment. Expectations predict entry and realized employment, while local
conditions and application characteristics shape the errors.

The safe theme is not “entrepreneurs are irrational.” It is that application,
entry, hiring, learning, financing, permitting, and local demand are separate
stages. The project must keep intention, entry, first-year employment, survival,
worker outcomes, household resources, and community benefit separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected applicant-to-firm panel joining
application expectations, financing, permits, location, industry, entry,
hiring, wages, survival, local conditions, household resources, and later
outcomes. The public abstract does not provide that complete bridge.

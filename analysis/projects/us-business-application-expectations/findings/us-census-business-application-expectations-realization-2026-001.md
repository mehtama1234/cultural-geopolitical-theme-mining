# Finding 001: Business applications overstate entry overall while successful entrants often understate first-year employment

**Status:** bounded firm-formation, expectation, and local-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper links administrative records on 17 million
U.S. business applications to outcomes. On average, applicants overestimate
employment, mainly because many expect to enter but do not.

Among applicants who expect and achieve entry, expected employment is typically
below realized employment. Expected employment predicts both entry and realized
employment, but conditional on entry realized employment rises less than
one-for-one with expectations. Expectation errors vary with application
characteristics and local economic conditions and predict near-term employment.

The practical route is:

```text
business application and expectation
  -> financing, permits, learning, and local demand
  -> entry or non-entry
  -> first-year employment
  -> [open] survival, worker conditions, household security, and community effect
```

The key split is between expecting to enter, actually entering, and hiring
people after entry.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an expectations-to-capacity version of the program's firm theme. A
large application count is not a business-formation count, and a business that
enters is not yet a durable employer. At the same time, successful entrants may
underestimate their first-year employment, so the gap is not simply optimism.

The pattern also gives local institutions a role. Financing, permitting, local
demand, information, and selection can shape whether an expectation becomes a
firm and whether that firm becomes an employer. The public abstract does not
identify one of those routes as the universal explanation.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper; the public abstract does not provide the full
  application questions, entry definition, estimates, or local controls.
- Expectation gaps may reflect uncertainty, strategic reporting, changing plans,
  financing, permitting, local demand, or selection—not simple error.
- Near-term employment prediction does not prove survival, firm quality,
  worker conditions, household welfare, or community benefit.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected applicant-to-firm panel linking
expectations, financing, permits, location, industry, entry, hiring, wages,
survival, local conditions, household resources, and later outcomes. Until then,
the safe claim is that application, entry, and employment are different stages
with structured expectation gaps.

# Source search: US housing type and energy affordability

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** matched evidence pass complete; same-household housing, bill, repair, and recovery records remain open

## Working question

How do renters, manufactured-home residents, and homeowners differ in energy cost, control over repairs, and ability to recover from a bill shock?

## Sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| DOE-LEAD-RENT-OWN | [DOE LEAD frequently asked questions](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/slsc/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool-frequently-asked-questions) | LEAD calculates energy burden from annual housing energy cost divided by annual household income and separates renter and owner households. | The measure is an average annual share, not a monthly bill, repair record, or control measure. |
| DOE-LEAD-SPLIT | [DOE LEAD tool](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool) | DOE identifies a renter split: tenants may pay energy bills while landlords control building improvements. | The source describes the mechanism but does not measure each landlord's action or each renter's savings. |
| EIA-RECS-2024 | [EIA 2024 Residential Energy Consumption Survey](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/) | The preliminary survey covers housing type, ownership, energy characteristics, and energy insecurity across all 50 states and DC. | The initial release does not join every bill, repair, health, and later recovery record for one household. |
| EIA-RECS-2024-HC11.1 | [EIA: Household energy insecurity, 2024](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/hc/pdf/HC11.1_2024.pdf) | EIA separates energy insecurity by ownership and housing characteristics and counts unsafe temperature, skipped food or medicine, shutoff notices, and failed equipment. | Reported categories can overlap and do not show who controlled the needed fix. |
| GAO-MANUFACTURED-HOME | [GAO: Home energy assistance for low-income occupants of manufactured homes](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-12-848r) | GAO found older manufactured-home occupants paid more energy per square foot than occupants of detached homes in the data it reviewed and examined how LIHEAP assistance covered those costs. | The comparison uses older data and does not establish current costs or later household outcomes. |

## First pattern to test

```text
housing type and tenure
  -> bill, equipment, and repair control
  -> energy burden and safe-temperature risk
  -> upgrade, aid, debt, move, or continued sacrifice
```

The same bill amount can create different choices because the household's control over the building and its ability to make an upgrade differ.

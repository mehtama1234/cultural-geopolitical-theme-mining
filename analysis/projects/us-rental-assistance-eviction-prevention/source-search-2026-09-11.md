# Source search: US rental assistance and eviction prevention

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States, with evidence from four cities and low-income urban rental markets  
**Status:** opening pass; immediate relief is supported, lasting prevention remains open

## Working question

When does rent aid prevent displacement, and when does it only buy time?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-RENT-AID | [The Effects of Emergency Rental Assistance During the Pandemic](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32463) | A lottery-based study of more than $200 million in four city programs finds that assistance increased rent payment and reduced concerns about eviction, with suggestive improvements in self-reported health. It found little overall effect on housing stability or financial distress. | NBER working paper, revised October 2025 | Eviction moratoria, other aid and softer rental markets may have changed the measured result. The study does not set a permanent national design. |
| US-NBER-NONPAYMENT | [Nonpayment and Eviction in the Rental Housing Market](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33155) | Landlord records show that many tenants fall behind, some later repay, and landlords often wait before filing. The model says short-term aid, legal delay and a filing tax prevent different kinds of cases, with broad policies unlikely to prevent most evictions. | NBER working paper | The records come from professionally managed low-income rental markets and the policy results are model estimates, not a trial of every policy. |
| US-NBER-LEGAL-HELP | [The Effects of Legal Representation on Tenant Outcomes](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29836) | Legal representation changes court and eviction outcomes, giving a comparison point for a non-cash intervention. | NBER working paper | It does not compare legal help directly with rent aid for the same tenant. |

## First pattern to test

```text
income, health, or rent shock
  -> missed payment and landlord response
  -> rent aid, legal help, payment plan, or no help
  -> payment, judgment, move, or eviction
  -> later debt, credit, work, health, and housing
```

The evidence supports a narrower claim than “rent aid solves eviction.” Aid can improve immediate payment and reduce fear, while the average effect on housing stability may be small. Timing and targeting may decide whether the payment prevents a move or only delays it.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

A modest measured average can still mean that some families avoided a severe loss. A moratorium or other support can also change what “housing stability” means during the study window.

## Main gaps

- the first income, health or rent shock;
- application delay and who never receives aid;
- rent owed, rent level and landlord payment plans;
- interaction between legal help and cash aid;
- housing, credit, work and health after assistance ends;
- cost per prevented eviction and effects on landlords and neighbors.

## Decision rule

Keep immediate payment, fear, housing stability and later recovery as separate measures. If a study reports only one, do not use it as proof of the whole prevention chain.

# Claims ledger: US rental assistance and eviction prevention

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; rent arrears, emergency aid, legal help, eviction, housing stability, and later recovery

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Emergency rent aid can increase payment and reduce fear without producing a large average housing-stability effect. | Lottery-based study | NBER 32463 studied more than $200 million across four city areas and found more rent payment and less concern about eviction, but little overall effect on housing stability or financial distress. | Pandemic protections and rental-market conditions shaped the study period. |
| C-002 | A missed payment does not always end in eviction. | Landlord records and model | NBER 33155 finds nonpayment is common, often tolerated, and sometimes followed by recovery; landlords weigh waiting against the cost of filing. | The data come from professionally managed low-income rental markets. |
| C-003 | Legal representation can change the tenant's position in court. | Housing-court evidence | NBER 29836 finds lawyers reduced possession judgments, monetary judgments, eviction warrants, and completed evictions in New York City. | Legal help and cash aid act at different points and were not directly compared. |
| C-004 | Immediate payment, fear, housing stability, and later credit are separate outcomes. | Outcome comparison | C-001 through C-003 show that money, procedure, landlord choice, and time affect different links in the chain. | The same tenant's full post-assistance path remains open. |
| C-005 | Rent aid may buy time; targeted aid or legal help may change the case. | Our inference | The evidence distinguishes a payment gap from a court and landlord decision. | It does not establish the best mix or cost for every tenant. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
income, health, or rent shock
  -> missed payment and landlord response
  -> rent aid, legal help, payment plan, or no help
  -> payment, judgment, move, or eviction
  -> later debt, credit, work, health, and housing
```

## Next test

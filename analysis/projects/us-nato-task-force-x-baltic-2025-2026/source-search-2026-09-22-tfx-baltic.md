# Source search: Task Force X-Baltic and allied capability conversion

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** Baltic Sea; NATO Allies and critical undersea infrastructure  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

Did a NATO technology experiment become a sustained multinational capability,
and what does that establish about cooperation versus deterrence?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| US-NATO-TFX-BALTIC-2025-2026 | [NATO Allied Command Transformation: Task Force X-Baltic](https://www.act.nato.int/activities/tfx-b/) | A 2025 live test, system availability and surveillance activity, eight-Allied Phase II letter of intent, national ownership, and NATO tasking/integration. | NATO's account does not establish adversary behavior, deterrence, uninterrupted coverage, cost distribution, or public acceptance. |

## Observed pattern

During three weeks in June 2025, NATO says Task Force X-Baltic fielded more
than 70 air, surface, and subsurface systems, with an average 75% availability
for eight hours per day, and tracked hundreds of vessels daily. In February
2026, eight Allies signed a letter of intent to move into Phase II, shifting
from experimentation fleets toward nationally owned capabilities that NATO can
task for persistent Baltic coverage. NATO also reports later contracts or
procurement by the United Kingdom, United States, Germany, and continued Danish
experimentation.

## Working interpretation

```text
critical-infrastructure threat
  -> live multinational technology test
  -> shared operating picture and lessons
  -> national procurement/ownership plus NATO tasking
  -> [open] persistent coverage, adversary response, deterrence, public consent, and durability
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

The source is NATO's institutional account and reports capability progress,
not an independent evaluation of mission performance. A letter of intent,
contracts, and procurement do not prove continuous coverage or deterrence.
This lane stops at the cooperation/institutionalization boundary unless a
named operation supplies repeated coverage, an attributed external response,
maintenance or reversal, and evidence about burden or acceptance.

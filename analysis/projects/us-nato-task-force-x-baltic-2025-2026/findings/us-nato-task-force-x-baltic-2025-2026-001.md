# Finding 001: A NATO technology experiment became a multinational capability commitment without proving deterrence

**Status:** bounded geopolitical cooperation and infrastructure-security finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NATO reports that Task Force X-Baltic tested more than 70 air, surface, and
subsurface systems during three weeks in June 2025. The systems conducted
surveillance and monitoring missions in the Baltic Sea, with an average 75%
availability for eight hours per day. NATO says the test tracked hundreds of
vessels daily and demonstrated that different commercial and national systems
could feed a common operating picture.

The capability then moved beyond a one-time experiment. In February 2026,
Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Germany, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, and Sweden
signed a letter of intent to carry the task force into Phase II, using
nationally owned systems that NATO could task and integrate for persistent
coverage. NATO also reports that the United Kingdom and United States awarded
contracts to participating companies, Germany procured maritime uncrewed
systems, and Denmark continued experimentation.

In July 2026, NATO described the next transition as moving participating
nations from demonstration toward procurement and direct deployment of
uncrewed capabilities in real-world conditions across the European area of
responsibility. This closes a reported deployment-stage observation, but not a
repeated availability series, mission-success measure, maintenance record,
public-cost distribution, or attributed external response.

The practical route is:

```text
critical-infrastructure threat
  -> live multinational test
  -> shared surveillance and operating picture
  -> national ownership, procurement, and reported real-world deployment
  -> [open] persistent coverage, mission effect, external response, deterrence, burden sharing, and public acceptance
```

The geopolitical insight is that allied capability is built through a social
and institutional conversion, not just a technology demonstration. The test
created enough shared experience for several states to commit to an integrated
follow-on route. That is evidence of cooperation and institutionalization; it
is not evidence that an adversary changed behavior or that the public accepted
the costs.

## Limits and next test

- The source is NATO's own account and does not provide independent performance
  validation or a full cost ledger.
- Availability during a three-week test is not the same as continuous coverage
  over time or during a crisis.
- A letter of intent, contracts, and procurement do not prove deployment,
  maintenance, tasking, or durable interoperability.
- The record does not identify an attributed adversary response, deterrence,
  refusal, substitution, or escalation change.
- The July 2026 deployment account is institutional reporting and does not
  disclose a common denominator, repeated availability, mission-success, or
  maintenance series.
- It does not measure local burden, environmental effect, public consent, or
  political legitimacy in the Baltic states.

The next decisive evidence would follow a named Phase II mission through
repeated operational coverage, a specific external response, maintenance or
reversal, and the distribution and acceptance of costs.

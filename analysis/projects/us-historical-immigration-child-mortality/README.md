# Project: historical immigration exposure and child-mortality spillovers

This project studies a historical public-health pathway: local immigration
exposure → crowded disease environment and public-health capacity → infant and
child mortality → effects across immigrant and native-born households. It
keeps place-level exposure, birth composition, and environmental spillover
distinct.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35743. In Massachusetts towns
from 1860–1915, increases in the foreign-born share raised infant and child
mortality, with the infant effect concentrated in the post-neonatal period.
Changes in birth composition explain only part of the result, consistent with
disease-environment spillovers affecting children of native-born parents as
well.

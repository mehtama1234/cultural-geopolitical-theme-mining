# Finding 001: Childcare effects can persist after preschool years

**Status:** bounded comparative childcare and labor-market finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35514 studies Quebec's universal subsidized childcare
program. It reports enduring positive effects on mothers' employment after
children age out of preschool, with long-run earnings effects growing to about
twice the participation effect. The study also reports occupational upgrading,
growth in tax remittances, lower social-assistance and employment-insurance
receipt, and an estimated fiscal benefit stream whose net present value may
recapture 75% to 117% of upfront costs.

```text
universal subsidized childcare
  -> early care access and maternal work intensity
  -> experience, occupation, and earnings after preschool
  -> taxes, benefit receipt, and modeled fiscal balance
  -> [open] care quality, child outcomes, wellbeing, and U.S. transferability
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a persistence test to the childcare lane. The U.S. Head Start finding
shows a targeted near-term enrollment-to-work conversion, while the Quebec
evidence asks whether an early care intervention changes later career intensity
and earnings. It also shows why fiscal return should remain a separate currency:
modeled recapture does not prove household wellbeing, equal access, or a direct
political legitimacy effect.

## Limits and next test

- Quebec is comparative context, not a U.S. treatment estimate.
- The abstract does not provide the full analytic denominator or all
  implementation details.
- Employment, earnings, taxes, and benefit receipt do not measure care quality,
  child wellbeing, maternal time satisfaction, or equal access.
- The 75%–117% fiscal figure is a net-present-value model estimate, not cash
  returned to families or a guarantee of fiscal surplus.

The next decisive test is a cross-jurisdiction mother-child-center panel joining
eligibility, slot access/use, fees, travel, child outcomes, maternal schedules,
earnings, occupational movement, benefits, taxes, health, and later wellbeing
or political meaning.

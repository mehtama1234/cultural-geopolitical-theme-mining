# Source search: US utility debt and household mobility

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** matched evidence pass complete; customer-level move and connection outcomes remain open

## Working question

When a household leaves an address with an unpaid utility balance, does the debt become a collection record, a new deposit, or a barrier to service at the next address?

## Sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |

| FTC-UTILITY-SERVICE | [FTC: Getting utility services and why credit matters](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/getting-utility-services-why-your-credit-matters) | A utility may review past utility payment history when a customer applies at a new address. Unpaid bills can lead to collections and charge-offs; payment arrangements may require current bills plus past-due payments. | Consumer guidance does not measure how often an old balance blocks a move or how much a deposit costs. |

## First pattern to test

```text
old address and unpaid utility bill
  -> collection record or specialty utility record
  -> new deposit, payment arrangement, or connection delay
  -> move cost, housing choice, or continued unsafe service
```

The record can follow the customer through both credit reporting and utility screening. The actual move outcome still needs customer-level evidence.

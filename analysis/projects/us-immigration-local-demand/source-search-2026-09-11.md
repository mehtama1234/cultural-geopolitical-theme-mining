# Source search: US immigration, local demand and place

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; local demand effect is measurable, housing and political meaning remain open

## Working question

What changes in a place when new residents add both workers and customers?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-LOCAL-DEMAND | [Are Immigrants a Shot in the Arm for the Local Economy?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21123) | Using US Census data from 1980–2000, the study estimates that each immigrant created about 1.2 local jobs for local workers, mostly in services that are used where they are provided. | NBER working paper | The period is older, and the estimate is local and average; it does not show current housing, prices or political response. |
| US-NBER-ENTREPRENEURSHIP | [Immigration and Entrepreneurship in the United States](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27778) | Immigrants can expand labor supply and competition, but may also start firms and expand labor demand. | NBER working paper | The abstract does not settle which effect dominates in a particular place or industry. |
| US-NBER-RECENT-SURGE | [The Postpandemic US Immigration Surge](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35168) | Recent research studies the supply and demand effects of the postpandemic immigration surge and finds roughly offsetting effects on inflation in its estimates. | NBER working paper | Inflation is not the same as local housing, service quality, wages or belonging. |
| US-HBS-LOCAL-INNOVATION | [When Migrants and Locals Innovate Together](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/when-migrants-and-locals-innovate-together-communities-gain) | HBS reports that mixed migrant-local teams generated more socially beneficial business ideas than local-only teams in the study. | HBS Working Knowledge | Idea quality is not the same as a lasting firm, job, service or community change. |

## First pattern to test

```text
new residents arrive
  -> more workers, customers and local needs
  -> service demand, business entry, jobs, wages and housing respond
  -> residents experience different prices, access and belonging
  -> local and national political demands change
```

The NBER evidence makes customer demand part of the immigration question rather than only a labor-supply question. The missing link is how quickly housing and services respond, and who receives the new jobs or carries the added cost.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

New demand can support local workers, businesses and services. It can also add pressure where housing or public systems cannot expand quickly. Both effects can occur in the same place.

## Main gaps

- current local demand and service use;
- housing supply, rents and crowding;
- wages and job quality for immigrants and existing residents;
- business entry, survival and ownership;
- schools, health care, transport and public capacity;
- local attitudes, trust and political response.

## Decision rule

Compare labor supply, customer demand, housing and public capacity separately. If only employment changes are measured, do not claim a full local economic or social effect.

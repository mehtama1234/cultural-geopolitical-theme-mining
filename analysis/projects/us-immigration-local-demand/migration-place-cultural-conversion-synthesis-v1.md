# Place conditions do not determine cultural response on their own v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** bounded migration/place synthesis; contextual comparisons are not causal or respondent-level joins

## The finding

Local population growth and foreign-born composition change the conditions in
which residents encounter housing, firms, providers, language needs, and public
systems. But the current place evidence does not support a single cultural or
political response. Trust varies across combined place conditions, while civic
action is comparatively stable. Separately, perceived immigration-related risk
can produce protective non-use of benefits, clinics, work, police, or community
events.

The defensible mechanism is therefore:

```text
place change and capacity
  -> who encounters what housing, service, language, and institutional route
  -> perceived fairness, control, welcome, or threat
  -> trust, continued use, protective non-use, civic action, or exit
```

The middle interpretation requires direct resident experience; it cannot be
inferred from population change alone.

## What the joint place screen shows

In the 2024 CES contextual comparison across 620 counties, county population
growth and ACS foreign-born share were crossed into 16 cells. Federal trust was
not monotonic on either axis: the lowest displayed cell was high-growth/Q2
foreign-born share at 27.846%, while high-growth/high-share was 36.055% and
low-growth/high-share was 39.542%. State trust was also non-monotonic.

Civic action was comparatively stable, ranging from 34.629% to 42.015% across
the joint cells. That is a useful counterexample to both “growth causes local
withdrawal” and “stable action proves local acceptance.” People can maintain
measured civic activity while judging institutions differently.

These are weighted contextual estimates. The county axes do not identify a
respondent’s migration history, legal status, direct service encounter,
language access, housing move, or belief about what caused local change.

## Cultural conversion and non-use

The local capacity layers show potential demand and supply conditions: housing,
crowding, language-access need, firms, health providers, and employment. The
WBNS immigration-concern layer shows a different endpoint: nearly one in five
families reported going without public benefits because of immigration concerns,
and 16% reported not applying for or stopping noncash programs over green-card
concern.

Together these records support a narrow insight: a place can have nominal
capacity while residents do not safely use every available route. The reason
may be perceived data risk, stigma, language, travel, eligibility uncertainty,
or anticipated institutional treatment. That is a lived conversion problem,
not a simple capacity shortage.

## What remains separate

- population change is not immigrant arrival or individual exposure;
- foreign-born share is not belonging, threat, or political identity;
- provider or firm presence is not reachable service or usable employment;
- trust is not fairness, belonging, or action;
- civic action is not satisfaction or safe institutional use; and
- non-use is not automatically distrust, legal ineligibility, or free choice.

The atlas keeps these distinctions because the same place can contain growth,
capacity, distrust, civic participation, and protective non-use simultaneously.

## Next place-level test

Select matched places with similar growth and housing pressure but different
language access, provider reach, or direct attitude measures. Then follow a
resident or household episode from encounter through route effort, use or
non-use, material consequence, institutional response, belonging/trust, and
later action or exit. Until that unit exists, the place screen is a contextual
counterexample layer rather than a causal cultural explanation.

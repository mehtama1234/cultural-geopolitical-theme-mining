# Finding: domestic dollar liquidity can remain convenient while global relative privilege erodes

**Status:** bounded macro-financial and geopolitical finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35742 measures the convenience yield on U.S. dollar safe
assets over the prior five years. It reports that, within the set of dollar
assets, safe-asset convenience yields were about the same in 2025 as in 2019
and are expected to persist at those levels for the next decade.

Across currencies, however, the richness of U.S. dollar safe assets—especially
Treasurys—relative to other currencies’ safe assets has fallen considerably.
The paper identifies U.S. dollar repo as the highest-convenience dollar safe
asset.

The geopolitical-financial insight is:

> Domestic liquidity and global privilege are separate layers of monetary
> power. A system can remain convenient for internal dollar-market purposes
> while becoming less exceptional relative to competing currencies.

## Limits and next test

- The abstract does not disclose the full asset sample or estimation details.
- A convenience yield is not household purchasing power or fiscal health.
- Relative safe-asset appeal is not the same as reserve-currency loss,
  sanctions leverage, or geopolitical compliance.
- The next-decade expectation is not a verified future outcome.

The next decisive artifact is an asset–investor–currency ledger joining
convenience yields, official and private holdings, repo collateral, funding
stress, fiscal and monetary events, currency substitution, sanctions use, and
external policy response.

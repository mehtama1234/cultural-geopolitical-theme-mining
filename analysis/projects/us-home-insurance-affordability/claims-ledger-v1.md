# Claims ledger: US home insurance affordability

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; disaster risk, insurance price, credit, home value, and ability to stay

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Home insurance became more expensive in higher-risk places. | National and local analysis | GAO finds average premiums rose 3% after inflation from 2019 to 2024, while some southern coastal areas rose 25% or more. | Averages do not show the bill for one household. |
| C-002 | Disaster risk affects both price and availability. | Government analysis | GAO finds higher wind and wildfire risk linked to higher premiums and reports that increased risk can reduce insurance availability. | Associations do not show each owner's response. |
| C-003 | Insurance premiums can affect home values. | Large mortgage-escrow study | NBER Working Paper 32579 uses more than 74 million inferred premiums and estimates over $40,000 less home-price growth in the most exposed ZIP codes. | This is an area estimate, not a loss for every owner. |
| C-004 | Credit score can affect the price of identical home coverage. | Large policy study and natural experiment | NBER Working Paper 34848 uses 70 million policies and reports that low-credit-score homeowners pay 24% more for identical coverage; a Washington State policy change weakened the link. | The opening result does not show why a score changed or whether the premium was paid. |
| C-005 | Insurance is part of housing affordability, not only disaster recovery. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 place risk, monthly cost, credit pricing, coverage, and home value in one housing path. | The same household's premium, mortgage, coverage, repair, and move decision are not yet linked. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
disaster exposure, home condition, or credit record
  -> insurance price, deductible, nonrenewal, or coverage gap
  -> mortgage and household budget
  -> repair, borrowing, sale, move, or staying decision
  -> home value, local supply, and public insurance policy
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Owners reduce risk or change coverage so the total cost of staying does not rise.
- Premium increases do not change mortgage trouble, repairs, sales, moves, or tenure.
- Credit-based pricing reflects real risk without reducing access for otherwise similar owners.
- Public mitigation or insurance support offsets the cost for households with less cash.

## Next test

Join policy, mortgage escrow, credit, claims, repair, nonrenewal, assistance, sale, and move records for the same properties over time. Compare similar homes in places with and without mitigation or insurance support. Keep price, coverage, home value, default, and moving separate.

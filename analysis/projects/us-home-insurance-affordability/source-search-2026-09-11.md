# Source search: US home insurance affordability

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; price links are measured, household decisions remain open

## Working question

When does keeping a home protected become a new barrier to keeping the home?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-HOME-INSURANCE-RISK | [Property Insurance and Disaster Risk](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32579) | Using millions of mortgage-escrow observations, the study finds sharply higher property-insurance premiums from 2020 to 2023, larger disaster-risk pricing, and slower home-price growth in the most exposed ZIP codes. | NBER working paper, revised November 2025 | Premium and price records do not show the same owner's coverage, payment trouble or move decision. |
| US-NBER-HOME-INSURANCE-CREDIT | [Pricing Protection: Credit Scores, Disaster Risk, and Home Insurance Affordability](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34848) | Using about 70 million policies linked to mortgages and property risk, the study reports that credit scores affect homeowners-insurance premiums alongside disaster risk. | NBER working paper, 2026 | The opening result does not show why a score changed, whether the premium was paid, or how the owner responded. |

## First pattern to test

```text
disaster exposure or lower credit score
  -> higher insurance price
  -> larger mortgage-related monthly cost or loss of coverage
  -> home value, borrowing, sale or move choice
```

The evidence supports the price links. It does not establish the final household choice for every owner.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Higher prices can reflect real expected loss and may help keep insurance available. Some owners can reduce risk, change coverage or absorb the cost; others may not have those choices.

## Main gaps

- premium, deductible and coverage changes for the same owner;
- mortgage escrow and missed-payment records;
- insurer withdrawal, nonrenewal and replacement coverage;
- credit change before and after the premium change;
- home sale, move, repair and adaptation decisions;
- effects on renters and local housing supply.

## Decision rule

Keep risk, price, coverage, home value and household action separate. If a source measures only premiums or values, do not claim that insurance alone caused a move or default.

# Finding 001: A granted workplace change is an access stage, not proof of worker control

## The bounded finding

BLS reports that 5.9 million wage and salary workers ages 16–75—4.1% of the
employed population in that age range—had requested or made a change in their
current job to help them do it better in July 2024. The largest categories were
schedule changes (2.609 million), new or modified equipment (1.477 million),
work tasks (1.412 million), and telework (802,000). Workers could request more
than one change.

Among schedule requests, 70.1% were fully granted, 21.9% partially granted,
and 7.5% not granted. Full-grant shares were 69.2% for telework, 67.8% for
equipment, 66.0% for work tasks, 61.0% for training, and 59.1% for increased
workplace accessibility.

Separately, BLS's 2025 Occupational Requirements Survey estimates that 53.5%
of workers had pause control, 18.5% had self-paced workloads, and 48.3% had
employer-driven schedule variability. These are job-level requirements, not
the same workers' reported experiences or request outcomes.

The safe route is:

```text
worker need or desired improvement
  -> request for schedule, equipment, task, telework, training, or access change
  -> employer fully, partly, or does not grant request
  -> actual usability, pay/time/health/care effect, persistence, and exit (open)
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a concrete implementation and control stage between formal workplace
access and worker outcomes. It prevents “accommodation available,” “request
made,” “request granted,” “change usable,” and “worker retained” from being
treated as one event. It also makes job structure visible: workers can have a
granted request while still operating in a variable schedule or low-discretion
environment.

## Limits and next test

- The CPS supplement does not identify the worker's employer, request reason,
  persistence, pay, health, care, or later job outcome.
- Granting a request does not establish adequacy or absence of stigma and
  workload trade-offs.
- ORS is an establishment-based job-level survey and is not a worker-level
  panel; BLS says the estimates are preliminary and not a time series.
- Neither source establishes bargaining power, discrimination, household
  security, trust, or exit.

The next decisive artifact is a protected worker/workplace ledger linking the
request, decision, actual use, schedule and pay, care/health, voice, retention,
and exit.

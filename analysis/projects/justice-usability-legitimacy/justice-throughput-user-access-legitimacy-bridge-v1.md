# Justice throughput to usable resolution and legitimacy

**Status:** fifty-fifth active depth artifact in the ongoing program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** justice capacity, user access, plural legal pathways, resolution,
and institutional legitimacy

## What we found

The World Bank's 2025 JUPITER assessment of Ethiopia shows a sharp split
between back-office performance and user experience. Federal courts had
clearance rates above 95% in the prior three years, but 67% of surveyed court
users said lengthy processes discouraged them from bringing cases to statutory
courts. The federal case-management system lacked a public-facing route for
online filing, document sharing, and case follow-up.

The alternative pathway is substantial, not anecdotal. Oromia customary courts
received more than 374,000 cases in fiscal year 2023/2024, nearly half the
caseload received by the state court system. Twenty-five percent of Addis
Ababa court-user respondents said they sought legal assistance from community
elders. The report also identifies more than 30% vacancies among judges at two
federal court tiers, only 53% of judicial-administration posts occupied at the
Federal Supreme Court, and a judiciary budget below 0.5% of the national
budget.

These figures show a route from capacity to access and substitution. They do
not show which pathway produced fair resolution, protection, remedy, or durable
trust for a particular person.

## What this connects to

| Link | Evidence | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Internal throughput -> lived access | Clearance above 95% coexists with reported deterrence from lengthy processes. | Clearance is not fairness, timeliness from the user's view, or quality of judgment. |
| Digital capacity -> practical participation | No public filing, document-sharing, or follow-up portal is reported. | A portal alone would not solve connectivity, privacy, language, disability, legal-aid, or trust barriers. |
| Cultural proximity -> legal route choice | Oromia customary courts carry a large caseload and community elders are used for assistance. | Volume or proximity is not proof of impartiality, gender safety, procedural protection, or legally comparable outcomes. |
| Staffing and legal clarity -> legitimacy risk | Vacancies, low administrative staffing, legal fragmentation, and reported quality concerns constrain the system. | Resource scarcity does not by itself explain any individual outcome or establish corruption or political influence. |

## The deeper question

**When does justice capacity become a usable and trusted resolution pathway,
and when does internal throughput push people toward customary substitution or
non-use without proving that their rights were protected?**

### Why ask it

This question changes what counts as successful public infrastructure. A court
system that clears cases but deters filing may look strong in administrative
reports while leaving affected people to informal settlement, delay, or silence.
Conversely, a customary pathway may be culturally trusted and affordable while
still needing safeguards, referral, appeal, and protection for vulnerable
groups.

Priority is **high** because the missing link connects public systems, culture,
time, cost, voice, technology, status, and trust. Effort is bounded to one
reform or dispute pathway; scope risk is controlled by not generalizing from
Ethiopia to all justice systems.

## What we know so far

- Institutional output and user access can move in opposite directions.
- Legal problems are common enough that non-use and deterrence are substantive
  parts of the population, not noise around filed cases.
- Plural justice systems can provide access and cultural legitimacy while also
  creating uneven standards and accountability.
- Digitalization is an access possibility, not an outcome.
- Staffing, legal information, budget, procedure, and independence interact;
  no single input proves legitimate justice.

## Bounded public discussion

The [public-systems discussion](../../social-discussions/records/public-systems-003.json)
shows how an administrative program can become a legitimacy dispute: competing

underlying X posts are not retained individually. It is included only as a
cross-domain reminder that public-system performance quickly becomes a dispute
about fairness, waste, motive, and future capacity.

## What could change the answer

- Customary courts may resolve disputes faster and with greater local trust
  than statutory courts, even if formal safeguards differ.
- Statutory courts may be preferred for impartiality, enforceability, or rights
  protection in disputes involving powerful actors.
- A public portal may reduce travel while increasing exclusion for people with
  weak connectivity, literacy, language access, or privacy.
- High clearance may reflect genuine institutional effort and still be a poor
  measure of user experience.
- People who never file may have resolved a dispute safely elsewhere, or may
  have abandoned it; the denominator is essential.

## Challenge checks

- Throughput was not treated as outcome: clearance, filing, resolution, and
  fairness remain separate.
- Association was not treated as cause: the assessment does not identify the
  causal effect of staffing, budget, recognition, or digitalization.
- Scope was checked: Ethiopia, federal and Oromia pathways, 2021-2025/2023-24
  administrative periods, and a bounded Addis Ababa user survey are not a
  global justice estimate.
- Related source claims were not counted as independent replications; this is
  one World Bank assessment with multiple evidence components.
- Affected parties were retained: rural users, women, minority groups,
  low-income people, people unable to travel, legal-aid users, judges, lawyers,
  elders, and people deterred before filing.
- The public discussion was treated as a question source, not as evidence of
  the Ethiopian findings.

## Current answer

**Partly answered.** Ethiopia provides strong evidence that administrative
throughput, practical access, and legitimacy can diverge, and that customary
pathways matter at scale. It does not yet show whether a particular reform or
pathway delivered fair resolution, protection, remedy, or durable trust.

## What to look for next

Follow one bounded pathway:

```text
legal problem and affected person
  -> information, cost, travel, and route choice
  -> filing, participation, delay, and representation
  -> decision, enforcement, remedy, and safety
  -> later trust, repeat use, appeal, customary/statutory switching, or non-use
```

Record the people who were deterred before filing, the legal issue, pathway,
dates, cost and time, decision quality, remedy receipt, and later status. Stop
if the source reports only clearance, caseload, or reform announcements; call
that an acquisition gap rather than a justice-outcome finding.

## Stopping rule

Do not call a justice system effective or legitimate until a dated episode or
valid comparative design observes access or non-use, route choice,
participation, resolution quality, remedy or enforcement, and later trust or
repeat use with compatible denominators and a credible counterexample.

# Finding 001: Shared federal goals can create momentum without securing durable coordination

**Status:** bounded institutional and public-systems finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed cross-agency priority goals, which are meant to coordinate work on
problems such as food safety, cybersecurity, and public-health preparedness.
Participants from three groups said these goals can define ownership, create
energy around shared priorities, and give agencies opportunities to work
together.

The same participants also described inconsistent tools for measuring progress,
uneven leadership engagement, and limited staff and funding to sustain the
work. The route is:

```text
cross-agency problem
  -> shared goal and ownership
  -> agency coordination and resources
  -> measurement and transition
  -> [open] durable implementation and improved public outcome
```

The broader insight is that coordination has its own operating requirements.
A goal can make a problem visible and create early momentum without ensuring
that the people, money, data, and authority survive leadership or administration
changes.

## Limits

- This is a qualitative review of discussions and interviews, not a causal
  evaluation or representative agency survey.
- The report does not estimate outcomes for one specific cross-agency goal.
- Recommendations are not ranked and may involve legal, resource, and
  implementation tradeoffs.
- Better coordination capacity is not the same as better service, public trust,
  or mission results.

## Next test

Follow one named cross-agency goal through ownership, staff and funding,
shared data, transition, implementation, output, outcome, correction, and
public or stakeholder response. Until then, the safe claim is about the
conditions for durable coordination, not achieved effectiveness.

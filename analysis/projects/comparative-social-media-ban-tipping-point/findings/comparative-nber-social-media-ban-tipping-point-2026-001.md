# Finding 001: A social-media restriction can erode below its coordination threshold

**Status:** bounded comparative platform-policy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35162 studies Australia’s December 2025 restriction on
under-16 social-media accounts. In a reported sample of 746 teenagers,
approximately one-quarter of 14–15-year-olds complied four months after the
restriction. Teenagers reported needing roughly two-thirds of their peers to
comply before they would do so themselves, and compliers were perceived as less
popular. The study reports that compliance fell to about one in twenty two
months later.

```text
formal platform restriction
  -> actual compliance
  -> perceived peer behavior and social status
  -> [open] persistence, wellbeing, family burden, trust, or political meaning
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a measured policy-to-behavior mechanism rather than an announcement or
attitude snapshot. It shows why a nominally available exit from a platform can
remain socially costly: the individual payoff depends on what peers do and on
whether compliance carries a status penalty. It also adds time: early policy
compliance is not the same as durable behavior change.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence is Australian and age-specific; it is a comparative mechanism
  test, not a U.S. estimate.
- The public summary does not provide a full follow-up denominator or enough
  detail to establish a panel-level causal decline.
- Compliance is not the same as reduced exposure, improved mental health,
  better school performance, family relief, or durable platform exit.
- Enforcement, circumvention, platform substitution, and pre-existing norms
  remain plausible explanations for part of the result.

The next decisive test is a linked adolescent or household panel that records
policy exposure, verification/circumvention, platform substitution, peer use,
social-status cost, family enforcement, time use, wellbeing, school outcomes,
and later persistence or return.

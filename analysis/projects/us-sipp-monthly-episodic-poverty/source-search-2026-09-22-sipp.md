# Source search: monthly versus lasting poverty

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States; SIPP 2024 reference year  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; duration and concentration are measured, causes and later outcomes remain open

## Working question

Can a stable monthly poverty rate hide different poverty durations and unequal
exposure across groups?

## First pattern

```text
stable monthly poverty average
  -> different spell lengths and repeated exposure
  -> unequal time with too little pre-tax cash income
  -> coping, assistance, debt, care, health, housing, and work effects [open]
```

The key distinction is between the rate in a month and the duration of the
spell. A stable monthly rate is not evidence that the same people were poor
for the same length of time, nor that hardship was temporary.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The report measures official poverty using pre-tax money income and monthly
thresholds that do not vary geographically. It does not measure assets,
noncash benefits, consumption, debt, housing quality, food hardship, unpaid
care, health, or whether assistance prevented a deeper loss. Poverty duration
is therefore a strong exposure signal, not a complete measure of lived
well-being or a causal explanation.

## Decision rule

Use this source for the duration and concentration of household economic
pressure. Do not turn it into a story about one household's complete journey,
or claim that the racial, age, or education differences identify a cause.

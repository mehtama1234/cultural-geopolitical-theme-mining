# Finding 001: A shared federal service can exist without shared performance

**Status:** bounded federal-institution and service-coordination finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed federal shared services in cybersecurity, financial management,
grants management, and human resources. Four lead agencies act as Quality
Service Management Offices, with GSA coordinating the marketplaces.

Adoption varied across agencies and functional areas. Agencies reported possible
or experienced efficiency and cost benefits, but also struggled to find
services that met legal, technical, and operational needs, integrate legacy
systems, or fit agency requirements.

GAO found that key leadership roles were unfilled and that GSA and agencies did
not have comprehensive data showing how well the shared services met agency
needs. The report therefore documents a coordination and measurement gap, not a
verified government-wide savings result.

The route is:

```text
centralized shared-service marketplace
  -> agency adoption and integration
  -> operating fit, leadership, and performance measurement
  -> [open] realized savings, service quality, and mission improvement
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a public-institution version of a recurring pattern:

> Centralized capacity can be visible while usable performance remains hard to
> measure and uneven across the organizations expected to use it.

It also shows why local variation is not automatically inefficiency. Different
legal, technical, or operational requirements can make a common service harder
to adopt even when centralization has a plausible cost case.

## Limits and next test

- The agency questionnaire was not a complete census of federal user
  experience.
- Reported benefits and challenges are not standardized performance outcomes.
- The 2019 OMB savings estimate is a possible-savings estimate, not a result
  measured by this review.
- The report does not establish that shared services outperform local systems.

The next decisive test is a protected agency-service ledger joining service
choice, legal and technical fit, migration, cost, processing time, failure,
correction, user satisfaction, and mission performance. Until then, the safe
claim is that centralization creates a marketplace and a new governance problem
at the same time.

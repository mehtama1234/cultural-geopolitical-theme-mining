# Finding 001: The explanation for surveillance can matter more than surveillance alone

**Status:** bounded randomized workplace-implementation finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 33348 uses a randomized control trial in an online labor
market. It divides workers into lower- and higher-productivity groups and
randomizes both whether digital surveillance is used and whether its use is
explained according to the worker's baseline productivity.

The study reports no statistically significant average performance effect from
surveillance alone. But not explaining the presence or removal of surveillance
based on worker performance significantly reduces output. The result makes
managerial clarity and the perceived rationale part of the technology's
implementation, rather than treating monitoring as a standalone treatment.

The safe interpretation is:

> A monitoring system can alter the work relationship through uncertainty and
> information asymmetry even when its average measured performance effect is
> near zero.

## Evidence chain

```text
surveillance assignment
  -> explanation and performance-based rationale
  -> worker interpretation of management intent
  -> output response
  -> [open] pay, autonomy, voice, appeal, health, retention, exit,
     household security, trust, and collective action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

The GAO review establishes a broad set of possible worker effects and oversight
routes. This NBER experiment adds a causal implementation mechanism: the same
monitoring practice can have different performance consequences depending on
whether its rationale is explained. It therefore sharpens the distinction
between tool adoption, workplace implementation, worker interpretation, and
realized control.

## What the source does not show

- It is not a nationally representative estimate of worker surveillance.
- It does not show pay, promotion, discipline, health, autonomy, bargaining,
  appeal, or job exit.
- Online labor-market output may not generalize to ordinary employment,
  unionized workplaces, or high-stakes personnel decisions.
- The experiment does not establish long-run effects or household security.
- A null average effect does not mean all workers or workplaces respond alike.

## Next decisive test

Replicate the treatment in a named workplace with administrative monitoring
records, worker notice, explanation, task-level output, schedule and pay,
appeal or representation, health and stress measures, retention/exit, and
household-security follow-up. Compare transparent, performance-aligned,
opaque, and contested implementations.

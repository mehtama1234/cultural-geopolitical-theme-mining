# Primary-source extract: digital surveillance and managerial clarity

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked paper.

## Official source

- Namrata Kala and Elizabeth Lyons, “The Effects of Digital Surveillance and
  Managerial Clarity on Performance,” NBER Working Paper 33348, January 2025,
  [NBER page](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33348), [author-hosted paper PDF](https://namratakala.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/digitalmonitoring_kalalyons-2.pdf).

## Extracted claims

- The randomized control trial studies digital worker surveillance in an online
  labor market and separately randomizes surveillance use and whether its use
  is justified based on workers' baseline productivity.
- Workers are divided into lower- and higher-productivity performers.
- Digital surveillance has no statistically significant average effect on
  worker performance.
- Not explaining the presence or elimination of digital surveillance based on
  worker performance significantly reduces worker output.
- The authors conclude that the relationship between monitoring and performance
  depends on how monitoring is rationalized to workers.

## Boundary preserved

The experiment does not provide a national exposure rate or establish pay,
health, autonomy, bargaining power, remedy, job exit, household security, or
long-run effects. Online labor-market performance is not the same outcome as
ordinary employment quality or worker wellbeing.

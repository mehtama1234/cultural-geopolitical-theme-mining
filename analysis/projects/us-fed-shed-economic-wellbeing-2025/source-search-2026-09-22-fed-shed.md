# Source search: personal financial well-being and economic meaning in 2025

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

Do people judge their own financial situation, local economy, and national
economy in the same way, and who experiences the largest gaps?

## Observed pattern

Near the end of 2025, 73% of adults said they were doing okay financially or
living comfortably; 28% said they were worse off than a year earlier, and 53%
said price increases were a major concern. Only 25% rated the national economy
good or excellent, while 45% rated their local economy good or excellent.

The personal measure varied sharply: 86% of adults with at least a bachelor's
degree were doing okay or comfortable, compared with 41% of those with less
than a high-school degree. Black adults were at 60%, Hispanic adults at 62%,
White adults at 79%, and Asian adults at 82%. Adults age 18–29 were at 63%,
compared with 83% among adults age 60 or older.

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

People can judge their own situation, local conditions, and national economy
using different reference points and information. A gap is not proof that one
judgment is inaccurate or that national policy caused it. This lane stops at the
meaning/interpretation boundary unless a repeated or event-linked source adds
attribution, action, behavior, or later persistence.

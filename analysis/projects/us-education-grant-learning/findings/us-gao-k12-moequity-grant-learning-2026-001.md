# Finding 001: Emergency funding can protect a rule without preserving the learning system

**Status:** bounded education-governance and institutional-learning finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed the Department of Education's oversight of Maintenance of Equity
(MOEquity) requirements attached to COVID-19 relief funding. The requirements
generally prohibited states and districts from disproportionately cutting funds
from schools and districts serving high percentages of low-income students.

Education developed guidance and technical assistance in real time, but did not
create written internal procedures for that assistance. GAO says this made it
harder to ensure that states received consistent information, especially with
staff turnover.

GAO's analysis of six states found that districts generally identified their
poorest schools, but Education lacked reliable data on how states identified
their poorest districts. Missing or duplicative data meant GAO could not
determine whether the requirements fully achieved their intended result or the
amounts paid in supplemental funding.

States and districts reported capacity and data-access problems and wanted
lessons learned. Education officials said they had not documented and shared
those lessons because there was no procedure ensuring it happened and it was
not a priority at the time.

The route is:

```text
emergency funding and equity rule
  -> guidance and technical assistance
  -> state and district implementation
  -> reliable oversight data
  -> documented lesson and future reuse [not yet secured]
  -> durable protection or student outcome [not observed]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an institutional-memory version of the atlas's recurring pattern:

> A government can act at scale during a crisis without reliably preserving
> the data and lessons needed for the next crisis.

The problem is not simply whether money was available. It is whether the rules
were applied consistently, whether the system could identify who was protected,
and whether implementation knowledge became durable organizational capacity.

## Limits and counterevidence

- GAO reviewed one emergency grant requirement, selected states, and selected
  districts; this is not a finding about every Education program.
- In three selected states, GAO interviewed districts receiving the most
  supplemental funding; the evidence is not a national district survey.
- Data-quality and documentation gaps do not prove that schools received no
  protection or that funds were misallocated.
- Education reported that some other programs maintain question-and-answer
  banks, a counterexample to a claim that the agency has no learning practices
  anywhere. GAO's finding concerns MOEquity implementation and the lack of a
  reliable procedure for this effort.

The next decisive artifact is a later audit showing whether Education adopted
written technical-assistance procedures, verified data reliability, documented
lessons, and used them in a new grant or emergency while linking the process to
school funding and student outcomes.

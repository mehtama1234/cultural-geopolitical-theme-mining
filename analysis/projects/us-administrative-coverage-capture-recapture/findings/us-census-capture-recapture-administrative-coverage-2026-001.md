# Finding 001: Being visible in records is not the same as being protected

**Status:** bounded administrative-legibility and population-measurement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES report evaluates whether administrative and third-party records
could help estimate how many people or addresses the 2020 Census missed. The
2020 Census already used such records in address canvassing and nonresponse
followup. The experiment applies an Expectation Maximization Log Linear Model to
test a capture-recapture approach.

The important result for this atlas is methodological rather than a single new
population number:

```text
survey or enumeration
  + administrative and third-party records
  -> linkage and capture-recapture estimate
  -> better visibility of possible undercount [experimental]
  -> enumeration, representation, services, privacy, and trust [separate open stages]
```

More records may make missingness easier to see. But a person can be statistically
estimated and still not have a usable service route, political voice, privacy
protection, or a way to correct a wrong record.

## What this adds to the atlas

This sharpens the administrative-legibility subtheme. It shows that “data-rich”
government is not one condition: record existence, match quality, coverage
estimation, actual enumeration, and downstream protection run on different
clocks. It also preserves a critical counterpoint: the records used to improve
visibility can themselves be incomplete, duplicated, stale, or unevenly linked.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper and methodological evaluation, not a final
  Census count or Census Bureau endorsement.
- The abstract does not give one national undercount estimate from the experiment.
- Administrative and third-party records are not complete or equally accurate
  for every group or place.
- Better estimation does not prove that every person was enumerated, represented,
  served, or able to correct a record.
- Additional data can improve measurement while increasing privacy, governance,
  and mislinkage risks.

The next decisive artifact is a protected linkage audit joining record source,
match quality, missingness, correction, enumeration outcome, service use,
privacy incident, and later trust without exposing individuals.

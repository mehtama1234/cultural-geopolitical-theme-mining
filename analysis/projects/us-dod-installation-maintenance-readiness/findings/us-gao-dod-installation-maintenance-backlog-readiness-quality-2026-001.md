# Finding: maintenance capacity is an unmeasured middle layer between defense spending and usable state capability

**Status:** bounded defense-infrastructure oversight finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-107255 reports that the Department of Defense estimated $285 billion in
deferred installation-maintenance requirements for fiscal year 2025. The
military services funded about 80% of identified maintenance needs, below a
90% department goal.

GAO also found that DOD had not fully determined the effects of this gap on
mission and personnel quality of life. Installation work-order systems were
not reliable enough to assess maintenance effectiveness across the department.
GAO notes that neglected mission-critical infrastructure can harm readiness,
while deficiencies in HVAC, plumbing, and other facility systems can affect
health, safety, recruitment, and retention.

The state-capacity insight is:

> A budgeted capability is not yet a usable capability. Maintenance funding,
> workforce capacity, and measurement systems determine whether defense
> infrastructure becomes readiness and a tolerable daily work environment.

## Limits and next test

- The $285 billion backlog and 80% funding rate are department-level estimates.
- The eight-installation site sample is explicitly non-generalizable.
- A reported risk is not a measured readiness decline or service-member health
  effect.
- The report does not identify which maintenance categories produce which
  mission, recruitment, retention, or quality-of-life outcomes.

The next decisive artifact is an installation–facility–work-order ledger that
joins funding, workforce, repair timing, facility condition, mission use,
service-member exposure, health or safety incidents, recruitment and retention,
and realized readiness or substitution outcomes.

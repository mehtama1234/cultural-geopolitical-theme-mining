# Finding 001: US arms dominance can deepen allied dependence without proving political control

**Status:** bounded geopolitical arms-transfer finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

SIPRI reports that the volume of major arms transferred between states rose
9.2% in 2021–25 compared with 2016–20. The United States supplied 42% of global
major-arms exports, up from 36%, and supplied 99 states. Europe became the
largest importing region: its imports rose 210%, and the US supplied 48% of
European arms imports. European NATO members' imports rose 143%, with the US
providing 58% of those imports.

The route is:

```text
security concern and supplier choice
  -> cross-border arms transfer
  -> maintenance, training, interoperability, and operational use
  -> dependence, autonomy, alliance behavior, or leverage [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a geopolitical-dependence subtheme:

> A state can gain influence through the systems its partners import, but the
> transfer itself does not prove that the partner will obey, remain dependent,
> or produce the strategic result the supplier wants.

It gives the atlas a measured middle stage between defense spending and alliance
behavior. It also shows why supplier concentration matters culturally and
politically: security relationships can become material and technical before
they become publicly accepted or strategically decisive.

## Limits and next test

- SIPRI's transfer-volume measure is not financial value or a count of weapons
  in usable service.
- Orders, deliveries, training, maintenance, and operational integration are
  not interchangeable.
- European import growth does not establish loss of autonomy or US political
  control.
- The record does not measure public consent, parliamentary conflict,
  battlefield effect, or external diplomatic response.

The next decisive artifact is an identifier-bearing transfer route linking a
system to delivery, training, sustainment, operational use, replacement or
switching, and an attributed recipient-government response. Until then, the
safe claim is about supplier concentration and transfer dependence, not
geopolitical control.

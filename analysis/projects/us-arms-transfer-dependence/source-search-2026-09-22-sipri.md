# Source search: US arms transfers and geopolitical dependence

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** international major-arms transfers, with US and European focus  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; transfer dependence is not the same as operational leverage

## Working question

What changed in international major-arms transfers in 2021–25, and what does
that show about US geopolitical position?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-SIPRI-ARMS-TRANSFERS-2025 | [SIPRI Trends in International Arms Transfers 2025](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2026/sipri-fact-sheets/trends-international-arms-transfers-2025) | Measures the volume and direction of major arms transfers between states using the SIPRI Arms Transfers Database. | Established international research source | Transfer volume is not financial value, order value, delivery timing, operational readiness, political consent, or strategic effect. |

## First pattern

```text
security demand and supplier choice
  -> arms transfer and recipient dependence
  -> training, maintenance, interoperability, operation
  -> alliance behavior, autonomy, leverage, and external response [open]
```

The volume of major arms transferred between states rose 9.2% in 2021–25
compared with 2016–20. The United States supplied 42% of global major-arms
exports, up from 36%, and exported to 99 states. Europe became the largest
importing region, with imports up 210%; 48% of European arms imports came from
the US. European NATO members' imports rose 143%, and the US supplied 58% of
those imports.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Higher imports can reflect threat perception, replacement, aid, timing, or a
small number of large systems. SIPRI's transfer-volume measure does not say
whether systems were delivered, integrated, maintained, used, or politically
accepted. Dependence can coexist with efforts to build European production and
alternative suppliers.

## Decision rule

Use SIPRI for cross-state transfer flows and supplier concentration. Do not
convert those flows into operational control, alliance obedience, deterrence,
or public consent without a linked delivery, use, maintenance, or political
response record.

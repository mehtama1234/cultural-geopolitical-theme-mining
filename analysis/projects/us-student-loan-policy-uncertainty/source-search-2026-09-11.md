# Source search: US student-loan policy uncertainty

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; payment and spending responses are measured, wider life effects remain open

## Working question

What does a policy promise make households do before the promise becomes real?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-STUDENT-POLICY-UNCERTAINTY | [Beliefs and Actions under Government Policy Uncertainty](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35319) | A US information experiment linked student-loan beliefs to credit, employment and spending records. More optimistic borrowers reduced student-loan payments by about $40 per month and increased nondurable spending by about $80 per month. Borrowers optimistic about payment pauses were 7.5 percentage points more likely to be delinquent after payments resumed. | NBER working paper | It does not show how beliefs formed outside the experiment, which need received extra spending, or the full later housing and family effect. |
| US-NBER-STUDENT-DEBT | [Student Loan Forgiveness](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33462) | Provides a separate source on actual US loan cancellation and borrower outcomes to compare with belief-driven behavior. | NBER working paper | The policy episode and outcome measures do not answer every question about uncertainty before implementation. |

## First pattern to test

```text
policy promise or legal uncertainty
  -> belief about future payment
  -> loan payment and spending choice
  -> policy settles, pauses, or fails
  -> delinquency, credit, housing and family choice
```

The source supports a link from beliefs to payment and spending. It does not say that the expected benefit was received.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Using money elsewhere may be a sensible response when a payment pause seems likely. Delinquency after payments resume may reflect the end of the pause, not only a mistaken belief.

## Main gaps

- where borrowers got the policy information;
- income, liquidity and household need;
- nondurable and durable purchase purpose;
- payment history, delinquency and credit access;
- housing, work, family and political responses;
- differences by balance, age, race, income and loan type.

## Decision rule

Keep promise, belief, payment, spending, delinquency and received relief separate. A policy announcement can change behavior without changing the debt balance.

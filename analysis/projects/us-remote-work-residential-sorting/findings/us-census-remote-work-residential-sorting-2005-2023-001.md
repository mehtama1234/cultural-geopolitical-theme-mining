# Finding 001: Remote work can free households from the job's location, but place choice becomes a tax-and-service choice

**Status:** bounded Census CES place-and-mobility finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Census CES Working Paper 26-34 uses office-lease expirations as a source of
quasi-random variation in remote work. It estimates that remote work raised
one-year migration by 11 percentage points, reduced the chance of living in an
income-taxing state or city, and shifted households toward more space,
homeownership, and public-school enrollment. Housing spending rose rather than
falling. At the neighborhood level, both outward and inward migration rose,
creating more turnover and some sorting by age and school demand.

```text
office location matters less
  -> some households can move
  -> space, ownership, schools, and tax treatment shape the move
  -> places gain or lose residents, revenue, and public-service demand
  -> [open] security, belonging, displacement, and political response
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a broader cultural and political point about place: remote work is not
only a worker benefit or a commute reduction. It can change what households are
buying when they choose a home. A move can be a choice about room, schools,
ownership, and the visible connection between local taxes and services. That
can give some households more room to choose while putting pressure on the tax
base and service mix of the places they leave.

The result is not “remote work improved life.” It says that loosening one
constraint—being near the workplace—makes other constraints more important:
housing supply, money, school access, tax rules, and the ability to move.

## Limits and next test

- The paper is a working paper, not a Census Bureau publication endorsement.
- It uses a repeated ACS cross-section, not a record following the same household through a later outcome.
- CompStak covers about 20% of US office stock and is stronger in large urban markets.
- Working at home as the usual commute mode does not capture every hybrid arrangement.
- A move, higher housing spending, or public-school enrollment does not establish wellbeing, school quality, displacement, stronger community ties, or political action.
- Income-tax states and cities also differ in politics and other economic conditions, even though the lease-expiration design supports a causal remote-work estimate.

The next decisive test is a protected household-place panel joining work arrangement,
housing search and cost, space and ownership, school quality, travel and care
time, neighborhood entry and exit, local revenue, and later housing security or
political response.

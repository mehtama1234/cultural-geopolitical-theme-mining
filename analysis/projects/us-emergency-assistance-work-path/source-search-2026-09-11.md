# Source search: US emergency assistance and the work path

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States, with evidence from a Chicago homelessness-prevention hotline  
**Status:** opening pass; later work gains are supported, the housing mechanism remains open

## Working question

Does preventing a housing break protect work, or does work recover for another reason?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-ASSISTANCE-WORK | [The Effect of Emergency Financial Assistance on Employment and Earnings](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32856) | The study links callers to a homelessness-prevention hotline with federal tax records and uses changing funding availability to compare similar families. Up to four years later, it finds evidence of employment and earnings gains, especially for the lowest earners, and no evidence that short-term help lowers work or earnings. | NBER working paper, later published in the Journal of Public Economics | It does not show which housing event was prevented, whether the same job was kept, or whether the result carries across places and aid designs. |

## First pattern to test

```text
housing crisis
  -> emergency payment or no payment
  -> housing continuity or displacement
  -> ability to reach and keep work
  -> later employment and earnings
```

The source supports a later work result for a selected crisis population. It does not prove that keeping the home caused every later gain, or that higher earnings mean stable work or a safer home.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Temporary help may give a household time to solve another problem, and even a modest earnings gain can matter. But the study does not tell us whether the household's rent became affordable.

## Main gaps

- the exact rent, utility or housing event;
- job loss, missed shifts, commute and care constraints;
- job quality, hours, benefits and stability;
- housing outcome after help ends;
- credit, debt, health and family effects;
- results outside one hotline and city.

## Decision rule

Keep housing continuity, employment, earnings and job quality separate. If only tax-record earnings are available, record a work outcome without claiming full household security.

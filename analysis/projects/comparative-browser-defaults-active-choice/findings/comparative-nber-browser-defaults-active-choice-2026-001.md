# Finding 001: Choice screens can weaken defaults, but platform rollout matters

**Status:** bounded comparative digital-market finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35112 studies browser usage around the European Union Digital
Markets Act choice-screen mandate. From month 15 onward, Firefox usage was
reported as 91% higher on iOS and 13% higher on Android than a no-mandate
counterfactual. Safari declined on iOS and Chrome declined on Android, with
gains concentrated differently across alternative browsers. The authors link
part of the platform gap to rollout: iOS reached existing users, while Android
primarily reached new devices.

```text
platform default
  -> choice-screen exposure and rollout timing
  -> browser selection/use
  -> [open] durable switching, privacy understanding, data control, and welfare
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is an observed platform-to-user-response mechanism, not just a policy
announcement or a stated preference. It shows that a formal active-choice
intervention can weaken an incumbent default, while the implementation route
and platform population determine the size of the response. It also sharpens
the atlas distinction between a nominal alternative and a practically used
alternative.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence concerns EU browser markets, not U.S. consumers or every
  platform-design intervention.
- Browser usage and market share do not prove informed choice, privacy gain,
  security improvement, satisfaction, or durable exit.
- The iOS and Android effects are not interchangeable; rollout and user mix
  differ.
- A switch away from a default does not establish a broader reduction in
  platform dependence or downstream data collection.

The next decisive test is a user-level or carefully linked market panel joining
choice-screen exposure, device replacement, interface comprehension,
accessibility, browser adoption and retention, privacy settings, data-sharing
outcomes, support or complaint route, and later switching or non-use.

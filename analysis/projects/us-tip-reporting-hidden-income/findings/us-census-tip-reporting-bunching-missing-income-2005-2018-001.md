# Finding 001: The visible reporting gap is only part of the hidden income

**Status:** bounded workplace-reporting, informal-income, and measurement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES paper studies tipped workers at full-service, single-unit
restaurants using worker and employer tax reports plus restaurant revenue data.
It finds that reported tips bunch at the minimum reporting threshold when wage
rules create an incentive to report some but not necessarily all tips.

The threshold-related missing tips were estimated at nearly $63 million a year
in 2018 dollars. But using restaurant revenue and an assumed 12% average tip
rate, the paper estimates total unreported tips above $4 billion per year.
Threshold bunching therefore explains only about 1.5% of estimated missing tips.

The practical chain is:

```text
wage and tax-reporting rule
  -> worker/employer reporting incentive
  -> recorded tip income
  -> tax, wage, benefit, and measurement consequences [not all observed]
  -> household security and workplace power [open]
```

The broader social point is that an official reporting threshold can make one
part of hidden activity visible while leaving most of the underlying gap
unexplained.

## What this adds to the atlas

This links informal work, firm reporting, public revenue, and household room.
It also sharpens the atlas’s measurement rule: a detectable discontinuity is not
the whole hidden population or the whole causal story. The same record can show
an incentive response without telling us who kept the unreported money or what
it meant for the worker’s actual pay and security.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper, not a Census Bureau endorsement.
- The study covers full-service single-unit restaurants and 2005-2018.
- The total missing-tip estimate depends on an assumed 12% average tip rate.
- Missing reported income does not identify worker intent, employer conduct, or
  who retained the money.
- Taxable income, actual cash received, wage compliance, benefits, and household
  security are different outcomes.

The next decisive artifact is a worker-employer-payment panel linking tips,
payment method, wage floor, schedule, employer records, tax reporting,
correction or enforcement, benefits, and later household security.

# Finding 001: Toxic-exposure records can support claims and care before affected people have full control of the record

**Status:** bounded health-access and administrative-record finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-24

## The bounded finding

The Department of Defense and Department of Veterans Affairs' Individual
Longitudinal Exposure Record (ILER) links military exposure information and
related data for clinicians, researchers, and disability-claims staff. GAO
reports that use increased after the PACT Act expanded eligibility for certain
toxic exposures, with Veterans Benefits Administration staff becoming the
largest user group.

GAO also reports an access asymmetry. Service members gained access and the
ability to add self-reported updates in March 2026, while officials planned
correction requests for service members by September and veteran access and
correction by October 2026. Stakeholder organizations identified possible gains
in transparency and clinician conversations, but also warned that missing or
incomplete records could create frustration.

The practical route is:

```text
service exposure and screening
  -> linked administrative record
  -> claims/clinical/research use
  -> service-member or veteran access and correction
  -> claim decision, care, health, and household outcome [open]
```

This adds a concrete data-governance mechanism to the health and public-system
atlas: administrative visibility can expand before the person affected has
full practical control over the evidence used in a claim or care conversation.
It is not evidence that ILER records are inaccurate or that access changes
improve benefits; those are the next tests.

## Limits and next test

- The cited 48% figure is a VA estimate among 6.8 million veterans screened
  since 2022, not all-veteran exposure prevalence.
- Login counts do not show claim approval, treatment, health improvement, or
  correction success.
- The stakeholder sample is selected and nongeneralizable.
- The next decisive evidence would follow a named record through access,
  correction request, verification, claim or care decision, treatment, health,
  and later trust or appeal outcome.

# Finding 001: Tribal input is more durable when it carries authority and capacity

**Status:** bounded cultural, institutional, and place-governance finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed federal agreements with Tribes over public lands and waters that
are also ancestral territories. In five agreements examined in depth, GAO
identified 11 features that strengthen shared decision-making, including a
commitment to consensus and a clear dispute-resolution process.

But the route is uneven. The Forest Service and NOAA’s marine-sanctuary office
do not have the same self-governance-type agreement authority available in some
Interior programs. GAO also found that agencies had not planned their workforce
capacity for developing these agreements amid major workforce reductions.

```text
Tribal knowledge and treaty relationship
  -> legal authority and agreement
  -> staff, decision rights, consensus, dispute resolution
  -> management action and correction
  -> [open] cultural legitimacy, resource outcome, and durable self-governance
```

The broader insight is that consultation and shared control are different
institutional states. Cultural legitimacy requires a workable route for input
to change decisions, not only a formal invitation to participate.

## Limits

- The five agreements are selected cases, not a national sample.
- The review does not measure individual community wellbeing, resource
  outcomes, public acceptance, or equal power.
- Open recommendations show unfinished institutional work, not proof that
  specific agreements failed.
- Other forms of collaboration may exist outside the agreement structures GAO
  reviewed.

## Next test

Follow one named agreement through Tribal input, decision authority, dispute or
consensus process, staffing, management action, implementation, community
response, and later revision or continuation. Until then, the safe claim is
about the conditions for durable voice, not achieved co-governance.

# Official-source extract: digital surveillance and workers

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked GAO report.

## Official source

- U.S. Government Accountability Office, “Digital Surveillance: Potential
  Effects on Workers and Roles of Federal Agencies,” GAO-25-107126, published
  September 2, 2025, publicly released November 24, 2025, reissued with
  revisions December 10, 2025, [official landing page](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107126),
  [report PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-107126.pdf).

## Extracted claims

- GAO reviewed 122 studies published from 2020 through 2024 that met its
  methodological-quality standards and interviewed stakeholders from 11
  organizations.
- The tools reviewed include cameras, microphones, computer-monitoring
  software, geolocation trackers, phone applications, and wearable devices.
- The reviewed evidence identifies both benefits and harms: monitoring can
  identify health problems or increase a sense of safety, while productivity
  targets can push workers to move faster and increase injury risk.
- Mental-health effects can also run in both directions and may depend on
  employer practices, including transparency about information collection.
- Flawed productivity benchmarks, incomplete accounting of worker tasks, or
  unintended use can contribute to low evaluations, lower pay, discipline, or
  termination.
- EEOC, NLRB, and DOL OSHA investigate claims that could involve digital
  surveillance; GAO also reports that several agencies had rescinded or were
  reassessing prior guidance in 2025.

## Boundary preserved

This is a review and stakeholder synthesis, not a prevalence estimate or a
worker-level causal study. It does not identify a named worker's exposure,
consequential decision, appeal, remedy, pay change, health outcome, job exit,
household effect, or collective action.

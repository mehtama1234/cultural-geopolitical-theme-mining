# Finding 001: Workplace surveillance is a control technology with mixed safety and employment effects

**Status:** bounded U.S. workplace-surveillance and oversight finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO-25-107126 reviews 122 method-screened studies published from 2020 through
2024 and interviews stakeholders from 11 organizations. It describes employer
digital-surveillance tools—cameras, microphones, computer monitoring,
geolocation, phone applications, and wearables—as technologies that can help
with safety or operations while also creating worker risks.

The reviewed evidence identifies a two-sided pathway. Tools can detect health
problems or increase a sense of safety, but productivity targets can also push
workers to move faster and increase injury risk. Transparency about data
collection may shape mental-health effects. Flawed benchmarks, incomplete
accounting of tasks, or unintended use can feed into low evaluations, lower
pay, discipline, or termination. GAO separately identifies EEOC, NLRB, and
DOL OSHA as agencies that investigate claims that could involve surveillance,
while reporting that several agencies had rescinded or were reassessing prior
guidance in 2025.

The safe interpretation is:

> Workplace surveillance is not merely a productivity tool; its consequences
> depend on implementation, transparency, targets, contestability, and the
> institutional route available when the measurement is wrong.

## Evidence chain

```text
monitoring tool or sensor
  -> worker exposure and information asymmetry
  -> target, pace, evaluation, safety, or privacy effect
  -> stress, injury risk, pay, discipline, or job-security risk
  -> [open] worker voice, appeal, remedy, switching, exit, household security,
     later trust, and collective action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds an implementation and oversight stage to the existing AI/work-control
lane. Adoption, productivity, and employer expectations do not by themselves
show how a named worker is evaluated or whether time savings become worker
discretion. GAO supplies a source-screened bridge from the tool's design and
use to possible health and employment consequences, while preserving the
missing link to actual worker remedy and exit.

## What the source does not show

- The review does not estimate how many U.S. workers are exposed to each tool.
- It does not prove that a particular employer's surveillance caused injury,
  anxiety, lower pay, discipline, or termination.
- It does not observe worker notice, consent, appeal, remedy, union response,
  job switching, or household security.
- The agency roles and guidance status do not establish effective enforcement
  or worker access to remedy.
- Positive safety uses and legitimate operational purposes remain plausible
  counterexamples to a uniformly harmful surveillance narrative.

## Next decisive test

Link a named workplace implementation to worker notice and exposure, the
metric or target applied, schedule and pace, evaluation or pay change, injury
or stress measure, appeal or representation, remedy, job exit or retention, and
household consequences. Compare transparent versus opaque implementations and
test whether oversight changes the practical ability to contest a decision.

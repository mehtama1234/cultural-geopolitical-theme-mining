# Service-member record access exposes privacy and delay risks while claimant outcomes remain open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** record request → processing and privacy-failure evidence; correction and claimant recovery remain open

GAO's October 16, 2026 review adds a concrete access-and-privacy route beneath
public-system accountability, health-record infrastructure, and service-member
trust. Military services reported almost 50 improper releases affecting more
than 1,000 service members during fiscal years 2021–2025. In a GAO covert test
of 26 requests, 18 were processed and one was improperly released without
redacting personally identifiable information.

The time and system mechanism is also visible. During the review period, Navy
officials reported that one employee was processing FOIA requests and average
processing time rose from 1.6 to 55.5 days. Defense Health Agency processors
must identify health records across multiple data systems and review them for
personally identifiable information; system issues and complex workflows
challenged request processing. GAO made five open recommendations, including
workforce planning and streamlining health-record request systems.

The evidence boundary is material. The covert test is not a population breach
rate, and the administrative release count is not a linked claimant-outcome
cohort. The record does not show requester receipt, correction, privacy harm,
litigation cost, health-care continuity, benefits decisions, trust, or completed
corrective action.

# Project: US broadband access, adoption, and practical choice

## Question

When broadband is technically available, can households actually subscribe,
afford it, and switch providers?

## Decision rule

Keep physical availability, advertised speed, subscription, affordability,
device access, reliability, provider choice, actual use, and downstream benefit
separate. A map of service is not a household outcome.

## Current bounded finding

The [broadband access/adoption finding](findings/us-digital-access-001.md)
shows that FCC adoption is calculated only among households where qualifying
service is available, and that 2023 adoption at the 100/20 Mbps benchmark was
about 53% of those households. The same FCC materials show uneven provider
choice. Price, device, reliability, and meaningful use remain open.

The [digital infrastructure, interoperability, capability, and value-capture
bridge](digital-infrastructure-interoperability-capability-value-capture-bridge-v1.md)
extends this access work into international payment rails, digital trade, and
regional public goods. It separates throughput, active capability, and local
value capture, and defines the named corridor or payment episode needed for a
stronger test.

The [public AI readiness, service outcomes, oversight, and dependence bridge](public-ai-readiness-service-oversight-dependence-bridge-v1.md)
adds government AI readiness, public-service evaluation, cloud procurement,
vendor lock-in, human oversight, SSA disability-service implementation, and

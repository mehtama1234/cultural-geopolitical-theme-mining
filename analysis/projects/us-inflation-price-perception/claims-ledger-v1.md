# Claims ledger: US inflation and the price people feel

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; prices, wages, shopping, fairness, trust, and political response

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | People can experience inflation as a loss of buying power even when the national inflation rate slows. | US survey evidence and price logic | NBER finds respondents mainly point to wages not keeping up with prices; a slower rate still leaves the price level higher. | The survey does not measure each person's full basket and wage path. |
| C-002 | Cheaper goods can lose their role as a low-cost option when their prices rise faster than expensive goods. | Product-level retail analysis | HBS reports the cheapest food products rose faster than the most expensive products in its data. | It does not show what each household bought or why each price changed. |
| C-003 | Price increases can change a customer's sense of fairness and demand, not only the amount paid. | Customer and pricing research | HBS reports that fairness and value judgments shape demand after price increases. | This does not prove a broad change in trust or voting. |
| C-004 | The same inflation number can produce different household choices. | Our inference from C-001 through C-003 | Income, product mix, shopping place, housing, debt, and ability to trade down alter the path from prices to lived pressure. | Same-household panel evidence remains missing. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
price and wage change
  -> repeated checkout and buying-power judgment
  -> buy less, trade down, save less, or stockpile
  -> view of firms, government, and the future
  -> political demand or pressure on business
```

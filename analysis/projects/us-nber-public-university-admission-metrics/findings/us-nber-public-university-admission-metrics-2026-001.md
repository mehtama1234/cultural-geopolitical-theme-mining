# Finding 001: Admissions metrics do not travel unchanged across institutions

**Status:** bounded education-selection and measurement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34975 analyzes administrative data for first-year cohorts
entering a large urban public university system from 2010 through 2019. In that
setting, high-school GPA is the dominant predictor of first-year academic
performance and graduation rates, rather than SAT scores.

The result contrasts with recent evidence from Ivy-Plus institutions, where
standardized tests have been reported to predict college success better than
high-school GPA. The implication is not that one metric is universally fair or
correct. It is that the value and meaning of an admissions measure depend on the
institutional setting, student population, school context, and the decision the
measure is being used to make.

The atlas-relevant insight is:

> A measurement rule can appear objective while changing what institutions see
> as merit, readiness, and legitimate access. Predictive validity in one
> institution does not automatically justify a gatekeeping rule elsewhere.

The route is:

```text
admissions metric
  -> institutional prediction and classification
  -> selection or exclusion route
  -> college performance and completion [not causally established here]
  -> mobility, legitimacy, and public trust [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an institutional-context and measurement-legitimacy layer distinct
from school AI diffusion and long-run human-capital interventions. It shows how
the same policy debate can imply different access effects when the predictive
relationship changes across institutions.

The next decisive test is a multi-institution panel joining score and GPA
submission, admissions decisions, student preparation, financial and geographic
context, enrollment, completion, debt, earnings, and later perceptions of
fairness or institutional legitimacy.

## Limits and counterevidence

- The study concerns one large urban public university system and does not
  disclose the full sample count in the public abstract.
- Predictive association does not establish that requiring or removing SAT
  scores changes admissions, preparation, completion, or earnings.
- GPA may capture school context, sustained effort, support, or grading practices
  rather than a pure readiness construct.
- Standardized tests can carry their own coaching, access, and measurement
  biases; GPA is not automatically a fairer gate.
- The Ivy-Plus comparison is a separate institutional context, not a pooled
  national estimate.

# Official-source extract: FEMA workforce and mission readiness

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked GAO report.

## Official source

- U.S. Government Accountability Office, “FEMA Workforce: Staff Reductions and
  Lack of Planning May Impact Mission Readiness,” GAO-26-108427, August 4,
  2026, [official GAO report](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108427/index.html).

## Extracted claims

- FEMA employed about 25,134 people on average in fiscal year 2025; more than
  4,300 employees separated during that fiscal year, a 55% increase from the
  prior fiscal year.
- GAO reports that FEMA rescinded its strategic plan and did not base 2025 and
  2026 workforce reductions and proposed actions on a workforce analysis.
- FEMA officials told GAO that separations caused loss of institutional
  knowledge and worsened longstanding workforce challenges. As of April 18,
  2026, FEMA reported approximately 20,968 personnel.
- During Hurricanes Helene and Milton, FEMA deployed 13,500 employees; GAO
  reports that only 4% of the incident-management workforce was available to
  deploy in November 2024 after the two disasters.
- FEMA officials anticipated about 240 DHS Surge Capacity Force employees for
  the 2026 hurricane season, down from 600 in 2025. GAO also reports long wait
  times and unanswered calls for Texas flood survivors after reductions in
  surge-support and call-center staffing.
- GAO recommends workforce planning, a strategic plan, and congressional
  reporting before hurricane seasons; DHS agreed with the recommendations.

## Boundary preserved

The report documents workforce and planning conditions plus selected operational
evidence. It does not supply a representative survivor-level rate of unanswered
calls, a causal staffing counterfactual, assistance denial, housing or income
recovery, health, trust, or political action. Deployment and contact problems
may also reflect demand, systems, policy, and application complexity.

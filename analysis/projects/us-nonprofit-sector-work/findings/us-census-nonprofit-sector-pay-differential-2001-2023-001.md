# Finding 001: Social infrastructure can depend on work that pays less on average

**Status:** bounded Census CES nonprofit labor-market finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Census CES Working Paper 26-33 reports that nonprofits employ roughly 10% of
the American workforce. Across near-universe administrative tax data, nonprofit
earnings were 12% lower than for-profit earnings on average. The authors' worker-
firm job-ladder model estimates that most of the gap is causal rather than
explained by worker selection, with large differences by industry. Health and
education can be closer to or above for-profit pay, while publishing and the
arts show larger penalties.

```text
nonprofit mission and organizational form
  -> worker-firm pay, selection, and mobility
  -> staffing capacity and retention
  -> [open] service quality, worker wellbeing, community support, and trust
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This connects two parts of the program that are often analyzed separately:
community institutions and labor-market conditions. A nonprofit can supply
care, education, arts, or civic services while its workers face a different pay
structure from comparable for-profit workers. That does not make the mission
inauthentic or the service ineffective; it identifies a material condition that
may shape who can remain in the work and what capacity the institution can
maintain.

## Limits and next test

- The paper is a working paper, not an official Census Bureau publication.
- The causal estimate depends on the AKM model and worker-mobility assumptions.
- Pay is not total compensation, household security, job quality, or mission
  value.
- The study does not observe service quality, staffing gaps, client outcomes,
  community trust, or political action.

The next decisive test is a nonprofit organization and worker panel joining pay,
hours, benefits, turnover, vacancies, funding, service volume, service quality,
client access, and later community outcomes.

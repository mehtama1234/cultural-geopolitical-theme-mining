# Source search: US payment fees and who pays

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; payment-user redistribution is measured, household price effects remain open

## Working question

Who pays when the same store accepts payment methods with different costs?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-PAYMENT-FEES | [Who Pays for Payments?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35067) | Using data across US merchants and payment methods, the study estimates that interchange fees transfer about $30 billion a year from cash and debit users to credit-card users; consumer sorting and different merchant fees reduce but do not remove the transfer. | NBER working paper | The estimate depends on how fee costs reach prices and does not show every shopper's actual price or reward. |
| US-NBER-CARD-FEES | [Do Credit Card Companies Screen for Behavioral Biases?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22360) | NBER finds that card issuers use more back-loaded and hidden fees with some customer groups and market conditions. | NBER working paper | Cardholder-fee patterns are different from merchant interchange fees. |
| US-HBS-PAYMENT-REWARDS | [How Credit Card Rewards Became a $9.2 Billion Wealth Transfer](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-credit-card-rewards-became-multibillion-dollar-wealth-transfer) | HBS reports how merchant fees can enter common prices while rewards return more value to premium-card users, creating different effective costs for shoppers. | HBS Working Knowledge | It summarizes the NBER study and does not replace the study's limits about incidence. |
| US-HBS-PRICE-COHERENCE | [Price Coherence and Consumers](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/its-called-price-coherence-and-its-surprisingly-bad-for-consumers) | HBS shows another way an intermediary's costs can appear in retail prices even when the shopper sees no separate fee. | HBS Working Knowledge | The setting is intermediary pricing, not card interchange alone. |

## First pattern to test

```text
shopper chooses cash, debit, or credit
  -> merchant pays a different fee
  -> price, reward, surcharge, or margin responds
  -> households carry different effective costs
  -> payment policy and merchant competition become distribution questions
```

The NBER study makes payment choice part of who gains and who pays in ordinary commerce. The missing proof is the same household's full price, rewards, income and shopping locations over time.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Cards can provide security, records, credit and rewards, while merchants may gain sales or lower handling costs. The test is the full value of the payment method, not only the interchange fee.

## Main gaps

- prices by payment method and merchant;
- rewards, fees, interest and credit risk;
- cash and debit use by income and place;
- small-merchant margins and surcharges;
- changes after payment regulation;
- whether households can choose another merchant or payment method.

## Decision rule

Keep the transfer estimate separate from the household's actual net cost. If only fees or rewards are measured, record the redistribution mechanism and move on without claiming a final welfare result.

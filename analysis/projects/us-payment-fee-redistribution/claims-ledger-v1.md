# Claims ledger: US payment fees and who pays

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; payment choice, merchant fees, rewards, and household cost

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Card payments are now a large part of ordinary retail payment. | Observed survey result | Federal Reserve payment research reports credit cards as the most common retail method by number in its 2023 survey, followed by debit and cash. | Survey choice is not the same as a household's full cost or ability to switch. |
| C-002 | Cash is used more often by older and lower-income households than by some other groups. | Observed subgroup pattern | Federal Reserve notes payment behavior differs by income, age, race and ethnicity, and says cash is more common among older and low-income households. | The note does not by itself explain why the choice was made. |
| C-003 | Merchant interchange fees can move money from lower-reward payment users to higher-reward credit-card users through common prices. | Estimated mechanism | NBER Working Paper 35067 models merchant fees, payment choice, sorting and fee differences. | It is an estimate of system-wide redistribution, not each shopper's bill. |
| C-004 | The estimated transfer is about $30 billion per year, reduced by sorting and merchant fee differences but not erased. | Estimated result | NBER Working Paper 35067 reports this estimate and a 25% reduction from those two forces. | The paper is a working paper; price incidence and welfare still need separate checks. |
| C-005 | Rewards do not have the same value for every cardholder. | Administrative and complaint evidence | CFPB reports complaints about hidden conditions, devaluation and blocked redemption. | Complaints show problems and patterns, not the rate for all users. |
| C-006 | A rewards card can cost more than it returns for a person who carries a balance. | Regulatory analysis | CFPB says revolving borrowers can pay much more in interest and fees than they receive in rewards. | The result depends on balance, APR, fees, reward use and timing. |
| C-007 | The strongest current finding is unequal exposure, not a universal claim that cards are harmful. | Our inference | C-001 through C-006 fit a path in which payment access, reward value, merchant fees and borrowing cost differ by user. | We still lack linked household, merchant, price and payment records over time. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
payment need or preference
  -> cash, debit, credit, or digital choice
  -> merchant fee and reward funding
  -> common price, surcharge, margin, or benefit
  -> household net cost and later balance
  -> shopping, saving, borrowing, or switching choice
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Merchants do not pass fee costs into common prices, or pass them back in a way that offsets the transfer.
- Lower-income and cash users receive equal or greater value from other payment benefits.
- Rewards, security, records, and credit access exceed fees and interest for the groups described as carrying the cost.
- Households can easily switch merchants and payment methods, so the effective price difference is small.

## Next test

Use household scanner or transaction-panel data joined to merchant payment acceptance, prices, rewards, balances and income. Compare the same household across merchants and months, then test a fee, surcharge, or rewards-rule change. Keep four outcomes separate: price paid, payment benefit, borrowing cost, and ability to switch.

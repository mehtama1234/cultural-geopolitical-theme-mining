# Finding 001: A formal accessibility complaint route can exist while the people who need it do not know about it

**Status:** bounded federal-accessibility and contestability finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed accessibility complaints involving General Services Administration
office buildings under the Architectural Barriers Act. From October 2022
through June 2026, the Access Board received 41 complaints, closed 25, and had
16 still in process. GAO also reports that industry stakeholders, federal
employee unions, and disability advocates generally believed people were not
aware that they could file these complaints, while GSA had not publicized the
route in its buildings.

This creates a practical sequence:

```text
accessibility standard
  -> complaint route exists
  -> person knows about it and can file
  -> complaint is processed
  -> barrier is corrected
  -> usable access to work or public services
```

The source observes the route, its use, and a visibility problem. It does not
show how many barriers existed, whether each closed complaint produced a usable
correction, or whether people later accessed the building successfully.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a concrete version of a recurring program pattern:

> A right or remedy can be formally available without being practically
> discoverable, reachable, or effective for the person who needs it.

It also adds a disability-and-infrastructure layer to the broader distinction
between capacity and control. A building can be designed or reviewed against a
standard, and an enforcement body can process complaints, while public users
still lack the information needed to contest a barrier.

The finding is not evidence that GSA buildings are broadly inaccessible. It is
evidence that complaint visibility is part of accessibility accountability, and
that complaint counts cannot be read as the number of barriers.

## Limits and next test

- The 41 complaints are reports received, not a prevalence estimate.
- Six site visits and stakeholder interviews are not a national user survey.
- Complaint closure does not establish correction quality, timing, or continued
  access.
- The source does not follow a person from a barrier through filing, response,
  correction, and later use.
- Lack of public awareness may coexist with low barriers, other reporting
  routes, or other reasons for low complaint volume.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected building-level complaint
ledger joining building, barrier type, user awareness, filing channel, response
time, correction, accessibility after correction, and repeat or unresolved
access. A public sign or complaint count is an accountability input, not a
completed accessibility outcome.

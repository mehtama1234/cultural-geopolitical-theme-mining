# Project: Military readiness, sustainment, and usable capability

## Question

Can a military modernize and operate at high tempo while maintenance, people,
data, and quality-of-life limits reduce what is actually ready at the point of
need?

## Decision rule

Keep budget, modernization, activity, inventory, mission-capable status,
personnel readiness, sustainment, partner contribution, and deterrence separate.
Force size or operational activity is not the same as usable capability.

## Current bounded finding

The [GAO readiness finding](findings/us-gao-military-readiness-sustainment-tradeoffs-2026-001.md)
summarizes persistent cross-domain gaps and gives a concrete Army watercraft
example: the fleet's fully mission-capable rate fell from 75% in 2020 to under
40% in 2024 against a 90% policy goal.

The route is:

```text
strategic demand and modernization
  -> personnel, maintenance, data, repair, and quality-of-life capacity
  -> mission-capable equipment and trained force
  -> operation or partner use
  -> [open] deterrence, safety, burden sharing, and geopolitical effect
```

The finding makes sustainment part of geopolitical capability rather than an
afterthought.

The [defense spending, procurement, readiness, and geopolitical leverage bridge](defense-spending-procurement-readiness-leverage-bridge-v1.md)
extends the route through SIPRI spending and arms-transfer concentration, DoD
contract and testing milestones, USAspending supplier structure, GAO readiness
and international-arrangement friction, IMF fiscal trade-offs, and bounded
public discussion.

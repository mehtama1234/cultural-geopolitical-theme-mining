# Finding 001: Military activity and modernization can coexist with readiness gaps

**Status:** bounded GAO readiness-and-sustainment finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO's March 2026 testimony synthesizes readiness work across the air, sea,
ground, and space domains. It says DOD has generally not met availability goals
for parts of the current force structure, including aircraft, ships, vehicles,
and the personnel needed to operate and maintain them. GAO also identifies
maintenance, logistics, training, data, housing, and fatigue problems as part of
the readiness picture.

Across the reports summarized, GAO had made nearly 200 recommendations and said
DOD still needed additional action on more than 150 of them.

The clearest concrete example is the Army watercraft fleet. GAO reports that
the fleet had 70 watercraft, with its fully mission-capable rate falling from
75% in 2020 to less than 40% in 2024. Army policy identified a 90% goal for
ground equipment, including watercraft.

The route is:

```text
strategic demand and modernization
  -> maintenance, repair, staffing, training, data, and living conditions
  -> mission-capable force at the needed place and time
  -> operation, partner use, or humanitarian mission
  -> [open] deterrence, safety, burden sharing, or external response
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a geopolitical version of the program's main conversion problem:

> A capability can be funded, modernized, and visibly active while the people,
> maintenance, information, and time needed to make it dependable remain thin.

It also makes quality of life part of state capacity. Housing conditions and
fatigue are not separate from readiness when they reduce the force's ability to
operate safely and consistently.

The watercraft example shows why inventory or mission counts are not enough.
The relevant question is whether the asset is maintained, crewed, available at
the point of need, and able to sustain the mission—not merely whether it exists
or has been used before.

## Limits and next test

- This is a GAO synthesis of earlier reports, not one new common readiness
  measure across all services.
- The watercraft series is one fleet example and does not describe the whole
  military.
- Mission-capable status does not show combat effect, crew quality, location,
  substitution by other assets, or partner contribution.
- The public testimony excludes classified evidence and does not establish an
  attributed deterrence or geopolitical outcome.
- Recommendation counts show unfinished oversight work, not a direct measure
  of military weakness.

The next useful test is a named mission or theater record joining asset status,
maintenance, crew, repair time, location, partner support, task completion, and
later operational or political response. Until then, the safe claim is that
sustainment and human capacity remain separate clocks from modernization and
strategic demand.

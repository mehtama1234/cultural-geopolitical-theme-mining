# Finding 001: A college credential can remain while the first job gets weaker

**Status:** bounded AI-exposure and early-career finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's Center for Economic Studies working paper reports that,
after ChatGPT became available, the most AI-exposed decile of college majors
experienced a five-percentage-point decline in initial employment and a 13%
decline in full-quarter initial earnings in regression-adjusted estimates.
About half of the earnings decline came from lower earnings within the sectors
that hired these graduates; the rest came from movement toward lower-paying
sectors such as restaurants and retail. The paper says the effects attenuate
as graduates move farther from entry, but remain substantial for the most
exposed majors.

The broader social meaning is a conversion problem:

```text
education and graduation
  -> major-level exposure to changing employer demand
  -> initial job search and hiring
  -> pay and sector placement
  -> later recovery, switching, debt, household room, and career path [open]
```

Education can still supply status and a credential while the first conversion
into paid work becomes less reliable. That is a testable social and economic
subtheme, not a claim that education has stopped working or that AI caused all
of the observed change.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds an entry-stage version of the atlas's capacity-versus-usability
pattern. A person may possess training and a degree while employers, sectors,
and pay determine whether that capacity becomes a usable livelihood. It also
connects technology exposure to unequal timing: the same educational milestone
can lead to different first-job conditions depending on major and the sector
available at entry.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a working paper, not a final reviewed Census publication.
- The reported estimates compare major-level exposure groups; they do not
  follow every graduate's full household or career experience in this record.
- AI exposure is not the same as actual automation, job loss, or employer use.
- The result can reflect other post-2022 demand, sector, macroeconomic, or
  selection changes, depending on the full study design.
- Initial employment and earnings do not establish lifetime earnings, job
  quality, bargaining power, wellbeing, or political reaction.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected graduate-to-work panel joining major,
exposure, applications, offers, employer and sector, pay, switching, location,
debt, household support, later employment, and career progression. That would
show whether the entry shock is temporary sorting, durable scarring, or a
sector transition with different long-run outcomes.

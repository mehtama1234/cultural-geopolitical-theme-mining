# Source search: US credit records and financial visibility

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; record differences and gatekeeping are visible, causes and later housing effects remain open

## Working question

How does a financial record become a map of who can borrow, rent or keep a bank account?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-CREDIT-ACCESS | [Credit Access in the United States](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34053) | Linked Census and credit-bureau data show large differences in credit scores and serious delinquencies by race, class and hometown that emerge in young adulthood and persist. | NBER working paper | The gaps are correlated with income and wealth, but the study does not by itself identify every cause or prove a lender's intent. |
| US-HBS-OVERDRAFT | [Are Banks the Bad Guys? Overdraft Fees](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/are-banks-the-bad-guys-overdraft-fees-are-crushing-low-income-customers) | HBS describes how payment ordering can multiply overdraft fees and how unpaid fees can lead to account closure and a separate consumer record used by banks. | HBS Working Knowledge | The example concerns overdraft and account records, not every credit-bureau or housing decision. |
| US-NBER-EVICTION | [Eviction and Poverty in American Cities](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30382) | NBER finds that an eviction order reduces earnings, durable-goods consumption and access to credit in the first two years while increasing homelessness and hospital visits. | NBER working paper | The result concerns eviction orders and cannot be applied to every late payment or credit record. |
| US-HBS-FINTECH | [The Dark Side of Fintech Borrowing](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/the-dark-side-of-fintech-borrowing) | HBS reports that alternative-data lending did not necessarily identify safer borrowers and that later defaults can rise when borrowers add new loans to existing debt. | HBS Working Knowledge | Fintech borrowers and lenders are a selected group; the result does not describe every alternative-data product. |

## First pattern to test

```text
late payment, overdraft, eviction, or thin record
  -> bank, lender or landlord sees a signal or a blank
  -> account, credit, housing or loan access changes
  -> household pays more, moves, borrows elsewhere, or loses a buffer
  -> record differences become a lasting place and class divide
```

The sources show that financial visibility is uneven and can matter beyond the original payment event. They do not show that every record difference reflects unfair treatment or that one score alone caused a later housing outcome.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Records can help lenders price risk, protect depositors and extend credit to people who would otherwise be unknown. More data can improve access for some people and expose others to harsher sorting.

## Main gaps

- thin-file and no-file households;
- which bills are reported and when;
- lender, landlord and bank use of the record;
- fees, interest and alternative products after denial;
- housing, work and account outcomes over time;
- differences by race, class, age, disability and place.

## Decision rule

Keep the record, the underlying hardship and the later gatekeeping decision separate. If only group gaps are visible, describe the pattern and move on without assigning a single cause.

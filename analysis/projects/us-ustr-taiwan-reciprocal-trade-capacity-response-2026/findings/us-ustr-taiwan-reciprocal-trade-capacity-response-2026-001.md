# Finding 001: Taiwan's trade response creates a capacity promise without proving replacement

## The bounded finding

The U.S. Trade Representative reports that the United States and Taiwan
reached a reciprocal-trade agreement in February 2026. The announced terms
include Taiwan reducing or eliminating 99% of tariff barriers, addressing
named non-tariff barriers, and the United States applying a stated 15% tariff
framework subject to the agreement's conditions.

The fact sheet also describes a January memorandum under which Taiwan would
work with the United States on industrial parks and clusters in semiconductors,
electronics manufacturing and AI, energy, and related sectors. It lists
planned Taiwanese purchases from 2025–2029 of $44.4 billion in LNG and crude
oil, $15.2 billion in civil aircraft and engines, and $25.2 billion in power,
grid, materials, storage, and related equipment.

The safe interpretation is an external partner-response and planned-capacity
stage: trade pressure and strategic alignment are being converted into
investment, market-access, and procurement commitments. The record does not
show that the commitments were implemented, that goods were delivered, that
Taiwanese dependence changed, or that the United States gained durable
geopolitical leverage.

## What this adds to the atlas

This closes a narrow arrow after the existing Taiwan semiconductor record's
partial domestic realization:

```text
partial domestic semiconductor realization
  -> bilateral pressure and alignment
  -> partner tariff, investment, and purchase commitments
  -> implementation, delivery, replaceability, and leverage (open)
```

It is useful precisely because it separates a partner's announced response
from the later operational tests. A bilateral agreement can expand the option
set for U.S. production while leaving ownership, imported inputs, host-community
burden, and actual switching capacity unresolved.

## Limits and next test

- The public fact sheet does not provide legislative approval, contract IDs,
  payment records, product-level delivery, or realized trade flows.
- The $84.8 billion combined figure is an arithmetic sum of three planned
  categories, not observed purchases.
- No firm, worker, household, or host community is followed through the
  agreement.
- The agreement does not establish semiconductor output, substitute suppliers,
  crisis performance, or a changed Taiwanese or U.S. strategic choice.

The next decisive artifact is a dated implementation ledger linking agreement
terms to domestic formalities, contracts, payments, deliveries, facility
output, supplier switching, and later policy response.

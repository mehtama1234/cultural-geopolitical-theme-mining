# Finding 014: A denial can become a care delay before an appeal restores authorization

**Status:** bounded Medicare Advantage prior-authorization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The HHS Office of Inspector General reviewed skilled-nursing-facility (SNF)
admission requests handled by 19 Medicare Advantage organizations in June
2024. Plans denied 12% of requests. Enrollees or providers appealed 18% of
those denials, and plans overturned 95% of the appealed denials in favor of
the enrollee. For denials issued by the contractor naviHealth, the overturn
rate was 97%.

The high overturn rate is evidence of a serious mismatch between the initial
access gate and the later review—not proof that every unappealed denial was
wrong, and not proof that an overturned request produced timely, completed,
adequate care. The appeal itself is a resource-demanding alternative: it
requires that a patient, family, or provider recognize the denial, gather
documentation, continue the contest, and remain able to wait for a decision
after a hospital stay.

## The chain

```text
illness, injury, or surgery
  -> post-acute SNF need and prior-authorization request
  -> denial / delay / documentation burden
  -> appeal by enrollee or provider [only 18% of denials in the review]
  -> authorization reversal [95% of appealed denials]
  -> admission, rehabilitation, health, work, and household burden [open]
  -> trust, future plan choice, complaint, or exit [open]
```

This is a health version of the atlas's broader realization rule: **an appeal
win is not the same as care received, and care received is not the same as
recovery**.

## What is and is not observed

| Stage | Evidence status |
|---|---|
| Need and gate | SNF requests after illness, injury, or surgery were reviewed under Medicare Advantage prior authorization. |
| Initial institutional decision | 12% of requests were denied across the 19 organizations. |
| Contestability | 18% of denials were appealed by enrollees or providers. |
| Formal reversal | 95% of appealed denials were overturned; naviHealth-issued denials were overturned 97% of the time. |
| Lived care and recovery | Not observed: days waiting, admission date, interim care, out-of-pocket cost, functional recovery, work, caregiver burden, or later health. |
| Meaning and exit | Not observed: trust, complaint, plan switching, non-use, or future care-seeking. |

## Limits and next field

- The review is a selected administrative sample, not a population estimate of
  all Medicare Advantage denials or a causal estimate of health harm.
- Appeal rates are not the same as need rates; people may not appeal because
  of health, knowledge, time, money, exhaustion, or provider capacity.
- The report does not establish that every overturned denial caused a harmful
  delay or that every unappealed denial was medically necessary.
- The next decisive evidence is a request-level episode linking denial,
  appeal effort, decision date, admission or substitute care, patient cost,
  functional outcome, and later plan behavior.

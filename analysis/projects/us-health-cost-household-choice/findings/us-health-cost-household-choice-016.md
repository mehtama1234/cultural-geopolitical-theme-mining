# Finding 016: Public coverage can spill across household members without becoming durable household security

**Status:** bounded randomized household-spillover finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The finding

The Oregon Medicaid lottery also linked adult lottery selection to state
administrative enrollment data for children. For every nine adults who enrolled
in Medicaid because of the lottery, about one additional child in the household
enrolled at the same time. The direct adult enrollment effect and the child
spillover were statistically significant but modest and transitory; the
spillover faded over the following year.

## Why it matters

This adds a household-diffusion stage to the atlas. A public benefit aimed at
one eligible adult can change the coverage route of another household member,
so the relevant unit is not always the applicant alone. But diffusion is not
the same as durable family security: the evidence does not establish the
child's care use, continuity, health, household budget, trust, or later
coverage status.

## The chain

```text
adult Medicaid lottery access
  -> adult enrollment
  -> child enrollment spillover
  -> fade in the following year
  -> care, security, trust, action, or recovery [open]
```

## Do not overread

- This is a historical Oregon lottery among low-income uninsured adults and
  their households, not a current national estimate.
- Administrative enrollment shows coverage, not completed care or adequacy.
- The result does not identify whether the spillover came from information,
  application assistance, household eligibility, or another route.
- A temporary child-enrollment response is not proof of lasting household
  security or intergenerational political change.

Source: [NBER Oregon Health Insurance Experiment results](https://www.nber.org/programs-projects/projects-and-centers/oregon-health-insurance-experiment/oregon-health-insurance-experiment-results).

# Source search: US health costs and the choices people give up

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

Does the cost of medical care move pressure from the health system into household choices before it appears as a large economic or political event?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-FED-SHED-HEALTH-2025 | [Federal Reserve: economic hardships](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-economic-hardships.htm) | 26% of adults skipped some medical care because of cost in 2025; the rate was 38% below $25,000 income and 13% at $100,000 or more; 18% had medical debt | Official household survey | Self-reported and descriptive; does not identify which price or coverage rule caused the choice |

| US-NBER-HEALTH-DEBT | [NBER household finance search starting point](https://www.nber.org/papers) | Search base for medical debt, health shocks, employment, bankruptcy, and household finance papers | Research index | Each paper must be read for its data, period, and identification |

## First pattern to test

```text
medical price or coverage gap
  -> skipped care or medical debt
  -> change in health, work, saving, housing, or credit
  -> family strain and loss of trust
  -> public or private response
```

The first link has current survey evidence. The later links need a specific study or record.

## Main gaps

- which types of care are most often skipped and what happens later;
- how medical debt changes credit, housing, work, or insurance after a bill;
- differences by state, provider, insurer, employer, and public program;
- whether debt relief changes care or only changes the credit record;
- how families trade medical bills against rent, food, child care, and work;
- evidence that challenges the idea that medical costs are driving the later outcome.

## Decision rule

Do one next pass using one NBER paper, one state or provider record, and one household dataset. If they cannot connect cost to a later choice or outcome, record the topic as a clear pressure point and move on.

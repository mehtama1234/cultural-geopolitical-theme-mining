# Claims ledger: US health costs and household choice

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US medical care, household money, work, debt, and coverage

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Cost can cause people to skip medical care even when the need remains. | Federal Reserve household survey | In 2025, 26% of adults reported skipping some care because of cost; the rate was higher at lower incomes. | Self-reported and descriptive; it does not identify the exact bill or later health result. |
| C-002 | A serious health event can affect work and household income, not only medical spending. | NBER hospital and labor studies | NBER finds hospital admissions affect unpaid bills, earnings, credit, and borrowing; other work finds family labor responses after severe shocks. | Effects differ by age, insurance, shock, and family resources. |
| C-003 | Coverage can protect household finances. | NBER Medicaid study and Oregon experiment | Medicaid expansion reduced medical collections and improved credit measures; Oregon coverage reduced out-of-pocket costs and medical debt while increasing care use. | Policy and study populations differ; results are not a universal estimate for every family. |
| C-004 | Removing medical debt from a credit record is not the same as removing the health or bill problem. | NBER randomized debt-relief studies | Debt relief improved some credit access or reduced payments, but did not improve average health, care use, or financial wellness in one experiment; deleting small debts showed little credit effect. | Results depend on debt size, reporting status, and the relief design. |
| C-005 | Employer-linked coverage can shape work choices after a health shock. | NBER employer-insurance study | Workers with employer-dependent coverage were more likely to remain employed after some shocks and more likely to lose coverage. | The study predates later coverage changes and does not measure job quality. |
| C-006 | The full household path is still not visible in one record. | Synthesis of C-001 through C-005 | The evidence covers care, health shocks, work, debt, credit, and coverage in separate designs. | We still need linked cost, care, work, debt, and family-choice data. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
price, bill, or coverage rule
  -> care delayed or changed
  -> medical debt, missed work, or lost income
  -> saving, housing, family, and credit choice
  -> trust and political pressure
```

## Next test

Join a care decision to the bill, insurance rule, household balance sheet, work change, and later health result. Keep debt relief, credit repair, and health improvement as separate outcomes.

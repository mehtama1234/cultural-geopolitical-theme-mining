# Private health-insurance enrollment concentration can narrow issuer choice while effects remain open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** issuer enrollment concentration → market-power signal; premium, plan, care, and household outcomes remain open

GAO's October 16, 2026 review adds a market-power layer to the health-cost
route. GAO defines a state market as concentrated when three or fewer issuers
hold at least 80 percent of enrollment. In 2024, that condition held for the
individual and employer-group markets in at least 36 states. Across 2011–2024,
individual-market concentration generally increased, peaked in 2019, and then
became slightly less concentrated.

Individual exchanges were concentrated in 47 states in 2015, reached all 51
states and the District of Columbia in 2020, and stood at 49 states in 2024.
The series is based on issuer enrollment reported to CMS, state data collected
by GAO for 2015–2017, and prior GAO analyses. It identifies a structural choice
and competition signal, not a direct consumer outcome.

The evidence boundary is material. Enrollment concentration does not establish
premium effects, issuer choice loss for each consumer, plan quality, provider
access, claims, medical care, employer wages, household burden, or health
outcomes.

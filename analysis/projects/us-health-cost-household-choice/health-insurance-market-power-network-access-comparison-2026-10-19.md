# Health-insurance market power to practical access

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded market-to-interface comparison; no enrollee-level care or welfare claim

The current health-insurance evidence covers three linked but distinct stages.

| Stage | Evidence | Observed | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Market structure | NBER W34928 across commercial, Medicare Advantage, and Medicaid | Concentration is high and rising; adverse selection interacts with market power; consolidation raises premiums | Enrollee-level incidence, plan generosity, provider access, and realized welfare |
| Selection and financial assistance | CMS 2026 Exchange report | 23.1 million plan selections; average monthly premium $619 before and $178 after APTC; 87% selected with APTC and 37% with CSR | Individual affordability after all costs, payment, care use, switching success, and health outcomes |
| Network interface | CMS 2026 Network PUF | 548 rows across 30 states and 359 issuer identifiers; 534 rows contain a network URL | Directory accuracy, provider availability, travel, appointment access, claims, and care receipt |

## Bounded synthesis

The evidence supports a route from market structure to the point where a
consumer encounters a plan and a network link. It does not support the shorter
claim that concentration, enrollment, subsidy, or a populated URL equals
usable choice or received care. A lower after-credit premium can coexist with
deductibles, narrow networks, administrative friction, or unmet care; a
network URL can coexist with inaccurate directories or no timely appointment.

The meaningful next interface is therefore premium and plan choice → verified
provider availability → appointment or care receipt → out-of-pocket burden →
switching, complaint, appeal, or health outcome. The CMS public files expose
the first metadata bridge but not a same-enrollee longitudinal outcome.

## Next bounded test

Use one plan or issuer and a reproducible network check to measure provider
availability, travel or appointment friction, and total cost exposure. Keep
the result separate from the NBER market-structure estimate and do not infer
care receipt from plan selection or a network URL.

# Vertically integrated Part D sponsors can steer prescriptions while effects remain mixed

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** plan/PBM/pharmacy ownership → utilization, payment, and cost-sharing differences; choice and health outcomes remain open

GAO's reissued October 6, 2026 Medicare Part D review adds a vertical-
integration layer to the health-market-power route. Four large vertically
integrated sponsors represented more than 40 percent of Part D enrollment,
utilization, and pharmacy payments in 2023. Their owned pharmacies accounted for
about 28 percent of Part D drug utilization and 24 percent of pharmacy payments,
and were primarily mail-order or specialty pharmacies.

For the 100 highest-utilization drugs, payments and cost sharing were lower at
owned pharmacies for at least 94 percent of drugs. But the direction was not
uniform: for about half of the 20 highest-payment-per-30-day-supply drugs,
non-employer-plan cost sharing at owned pharmacies was up to about $340 higher.
GAO also reviewed 36 contracts; owned-versus-non-owned payment rates were not
consistently higher or lower. The evidence shows a steering incentive and mixed
payment surface, not a settled consumer-welfare result.

The evidence boundary is material. The four-sponsor, 2023 sample is not
generalizable, gross payments omit some rebates and adjustments, and the report
does not establish steering, constrained choice, adherence, access,
independent-pharmacy revenue effects, net drug costs, health outcomes, or
household burden.

# Caremark settlement specifies PBM price and access controls while patient savings remain projected

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** alleged PBM pricing/access mechanism → settlement controls; patient conversion remains open

The FTC's July 14, 2026 Caremark settlement adds a named prescription-drug
route beneath consumer power, health-cost, and firm-market-power themes. The
FTC says the settlement locks in up to $8.5 billion in consumer savings over
the next 10 years and may unlock up to $4.5 billion in additional savings from
point-of-sale rebates. Those are projected settlement effects, not observed
patient savings.

The specified controls include delinking PBM fees from drug list prices,
offering plan-sponsor designs that pass rebates through at the point of sale,
and preventing discrimination against lower-list-price versions of covered
drugs. The settlement also gives community pharmacies an opportunity to shift
to cost-plus reimbursement and addresses alleged interference with patient and
pharmacy access to hub pharmacy services.

The evidence boundary is essential. The public record does not show
implementation, the affected patient or prescription denominator, individual
out-of-pocket prices, fill completion, adherence, medication access, pharmacy
participation, household recovery, or trust. A formal pass-through or access
control can change the institutional route without producing equal savings or
access for every patient.

# Project: Export controls and domestic cost

This project examines the inside-the-United-States side of geopolitical
economic policy. A Census CES working paper reports that the share of U.S.
exports subject to controls doubled from 2010 to 2024. Using the 2014
restriction on exports to Russia, it finds lower exports of targeted goods,
spillovers to other products and destinations, and lower average payroll for
affected firms.

The working theme is not that export controls are ineffective. A restriction
can impose real domestic costs and still pursue a security objective. The
atlas must keep exporter access, firm adjustment, payroll, workers, consumers,
foreign adaptation, and strategic outcome on separate tracks.

The decisive next artifact is a protected policy-firm-product ledger joining
the rule, compliance exposure, export and destination changes, substitution,
employment, payroll, investment, foreign response, and later security or
diplomatic outcome. The current paper does not identify that full tradeoff.

# Finding 001: A national-security restriction can spread costs through U.S. firms

**Status:** bounded geopolitical-policy and domestic-firm-cost finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's Center for Economic Studies working paper reports that the
share of U.S. exports subject to controls doubled between 2010 and 2024. Using
the 2014 U.S. restriction on exports to Russia as a natural experiment, it
finds that affected firms became less likely to export targeted goods to
Russia, also reduced exports of other products and to other destinations, and
had lower average payroll.

The route is:

```text
national-security rule
  -> compliance and market-access constraint
  -> targeted export change
  -> spillover across products and destinations
  -> payroll and firm adjustment
  -> foreign adaptation, worker effects, and strategic outcome [open]
```

The broader cultural-geopolitical point is that public policy can ask firms to
carry part of a geopolitical burden. The domestic cost is real evidence, but
it is not by itself a verdict on whether the policy was strategically wise.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a direct state-policy-to-firm-cost pathway to the atlas. It connects
geopolitical action to the distribution of adjustment: firms with different
products, destinations, sizes, and alternatives may experience very different
costs. It also reinforces the program's rule that capability or intent does
not establish final external effect.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a working paper, not a final reviewed Census publication.
- The public abstract does not give the full sample, subgroup estimates,
  effect sizes, or timing details.
- Export and payroll changes can reflect other geopolitical or market forces,
  selection, substitution, and firm-specific adjustment.
- Lower payroll is not the same as a measured job count, worker income loss,
  or household hardship.
- Domestic cost does not prove that the security objective failed, and this
  source does not measure whether the objective succeeded.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected policy-firm-product ledger joining the
restriction, compliance burden, export and destination changes, substitution,
employment, payroll, investment, foreign response, and later security or
diplomatic outcome. That would make the policy tradeoff observable rather
than treating cost or intent as the whole result.

# Source search: OFAC sanctions reach and firm correction

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** Adani Enterprises Limited, India; Iran-related transactions and U.S. financial institutions  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; enforcement and firm correction are not the same as strategic deterrence

## Working question

What does the Adani OFAC settlement show about the path from cross-border firm
transactions through U.S. financial infrastructure to institutional correction?

## First pattern

```text
foreign firm transaction and sanctions red flags
  -> U.S.-dollar payment through U.S. financial institutions
  -> OFAC investigation and settlement
  -> penalty, remedial measures, and compliance program
  -> future screening, recurrence, firm behavior, and strategic deterrence [open]
```

OFAC says Adani purchased Iranian-origin liquefied petroleum gas from a Dubai
trader between November 2023 and June 2025, causing 32 U.S.-dollar payments
totaling approximately $192,104,044 to be processed by U.S. financial
institutions. OFAC announced a $275 million settlement on May 18, 2026. The
agreement requires a sanctions-compliance program for at least five years and
states that the apparently violating conduct was terminated.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The case shows U.S. financial and regulatory reach into a foreign firm’s
transactions, but a settlement can reflect negotiated correction rather than a
measured change in Iranian strategy or global sanctions evasion. Remediation
and cooperation may reduce recurrence, while the public record still does not
show later compliance performance or how many similar transactions were
prevented.

## Decision rule

Use this case for a named-firm route through cross-border transaction,
financial-system exposure, enforcement, and compliance correction. Do not turn
the penalty into a sanctions-effectiveness rate, a claim of intentional conduct
beyond the reported apparent violations, or proof of geopolitical deterrence.

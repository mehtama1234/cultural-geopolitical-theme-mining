# Finding 001: Domestic capability does not automatically remove material dependence

**Status:** bounded infrastructure and geopolitical-dependence finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in plain language

The United States can invest in domestic mineral recovery, semiconductor production, or battery technology and still depend on foreign mining, refining, processing, or specialized inputs.

GAO and USGS report that the United States considers 60 minerals critical. Selected materials—including arsenic, gallium, graphite, indium, and manganese—had 100% net import reliance in the cited 2025 measures. China was a major source for 14 of the 33 critical minerals on which the United States most depends.

But “dependence” is not the same for every use. Battery recycling may reduce reliance on some minerals in roughly two to three years if collection and processing systems work. Semiconductor substitution is harder in the near term because replacement materials must meet demanding performance requirements. Domestic recovery projects can therefore increase capability without yet creating a qualified, scaled, second-source supply.

```text
foreign mining or processing
  -> imported material with price, quality, and shipment exposure
  -> battery, chip, defense, grid, or consumer product
  -> substitution, recycling, redesign, inventory, or delay
  -> domestic capability and cost
  -> [open] supplier switching, alliance response, or geopolitical leverage
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a concrete version of the atlas's recurring capacity-versus-control pattern:

> Building a capability is not the same as controlling the full system that makes it usable.

It also links geopolitics to ordinary American systems. A supply problem may appear as a delayed product, higher price, redesign, lower performance, reduced investment, or a changed alliance choice—not necessarily as a visible national crisis.

The correct unit is not “the mineral” alone. It is the mineral, application, purity, process, supplier, inventory, qualification requirement, and time available to switch. A material may be replaceable in one battery chemistry and difficult to replace in a semiconductor.

## What the source does not show

- Net import reliance is not proof of a shortage, coercion, or realized U.S. harm.
- A federal selection, grant, prototype, or research partnership is not operating output, customer qualification, or import displacement.
- A projected recycling timeline is not a measured recycling loop.
- The evidence does not identify which firm, worker, community, consumer, or alliance would bear the cost of a disruption.
- It does not prove geopolitical leverage, strategic independence, or a foreign concession.

# UK digital support, proxy access, and practical independence

## Question

When essential services move online, does informal help create a bridge to
independence, or does it transfer time, credential risk, and responsibility to
family, friends, neighbours, and colleagues?

## End-to-end goal

Follow a named service task from digital barrier through teaching, proxy access
or an alternative channel, completion, time and security cost, repeat support,
formal escalation, and later independent use or exit.

```text
service or task need
  -> interface, device, skill, disability, or confidence barrier
  -> teaching, doing-with, proxy access, or non-digital alternative
  -> task completion, delay, cost, credential risk, or abandonment
  -> repeat dependence or growing independence
  -> remedy, trust, continued use, or exit
```

This is a depth lane in the ongoing 14-theme program. It joins digital
infrastructure to household time, disability access, consumer power, public
systems, and social relationships without treating connectivity as completed
participation.

## Current audit

The [digital support and dependency stage audit](digital-support-dependency-stage-audit-v1.md)
compares UK informal support, Canadian disability-access barriers, Malaysian
device and place access, Ofcom consumer AI navigation, and bounded public
discussion. It finds that access can be socially completed while remaining
personally dependent, insecure, or costly.

## Next test

Find one lawful, identifier-bearing service episode with the task, barrier,
support mode, completion, time or money cost, credential or privacy exposure,
repeat use, provider response, and later independence or exit. Do not infer
service failure or household harm from support prevalence alone.

# Property shock, repair, staying, and moving audit

**Status:** active depth artifact; staged housing-security comparison, not a universal displacement estimate  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** property or household housing shock -> assistance, insurance, or court response -> repair, safe occupancy, staying, moving, and durable security

## What we found

Housing security is not closed by a payment, a claim, a repair offer, or a
court outcome. The current sources show several different pathways:

```text
rent, property, hazard, or insurance shock
  -> usable alternative or institutional response
  -> payment, repair, protection, or court outcome
  -> safe occupancy, return, stay, move, or homelessness
  -> income, health, work, school, belonging, and durable place security
```

The strongest evidence is a **repair-and-mobility gap**. Housing interventions
can affect immediate payment, eviction concern, or court status while the same
household's final repair, safe return, affordability, health, work, and move or
stay decision remain unobserved.

| [GAO FEMA housing-reach review](../../records/us-gao-fema-assistance-housing-reach-2026.json), more than $3 billion to 1.2 million people/households across selected disasters; 56 institutional interview/written-response sources | Disaster survivors needed temporary lodging, rental housing, and repairs; helpline and information barriers were reported in selected Helene/Milton and Texas-flood cases | FEMA assistance can cover eligible needs; survivors faced long waits, misunderstood information requests, and post-disaster housing constraints; delivery shifted toward state/local centers | Program scale and eligible uses can coexist with access friction and intergovernmental dependence | No survivor-level eligibility, award, receipt, repair, safe occupancy, move/stay, or durable security |

## What this connects to

Three counterexamples prevent a simple housing story.

First, **assistance is not stability**. Rental assistance improved rent payment
and eviction anxiety, but average housing stability did not move much in the
evaluated pandemic programs. That is not evidence that assistance failed; it
shows that payment relief and durable housing status are separate stages.

Second, **a formal housing event is not a complete explanation**. The eviction
study finds large downstream effects, but tenants already had worsening
earnings and health before court. The court order adds harm without replacing
the earlier income, rent, health, and market causes.

Third, **staying is not always security**. Mortgage lock-in can reduce mobility,
while insurance pricing and nonrenewal can make staying costly or unsafe. A
household that remains at an address may be protected, trapped, underinsured,
or unable to finance a move; the address alone cannot distinguish these states.

## Bounded public discussion

Public discussion supplies lived framing and hypotheses, not prevalence:

| Record | Discussion signal | Use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Insurance as a housing force](../../social-discussions/records/housing-place-linkedin-002.json) | Rising insurance costs are framed as entering rents, fees, deferred maintenance, and what gets built | Makes insurance a place-stability mechanism; a professional post supplies no property-level rent or premium panel |
| [Eviction and children's welfare](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-batch-0046-eviction-children-linkedin-001.json) | Eviction is discussed as a route into residential, school, and developmental disruption | Connects formal housing events to child outcomes; the post is a research announcement, not the full estimate or a universal result |
| [Layered homelessness and exclusion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-oecd-homelessness-housing-exclusion-linkedin-001.json) | Housing exclusion is framed as a pathway involving poverty, markets, family conflict, health, institutions, and hidden temporary stays | Preserves hidden forms of insecurity; discussion does not identify which intervention prevents exclusion |

The social layer reinforces that housing is interpreted as safety, continuity,
family life, school, dignity, and future mobility—not only as a physical asset.
It does not establish a household's reason for staying or moving.

## Missing links and challenge checks

- Do not treat FEMA dollars, insurance stock, or a court order as repair or
  safe-occupancy evidence.
- Do not combine renter, homeowner, address, policy, and court units into one
  housing-security rate.
- Do not treat non-return as voluntary exit without household choice, income,
  insurance, health, family, and place information.
- Do not treat an unchanged housing-stability average as proof that every
  recipient lacked benefit; examine heterogeneous routes and counterfactuals.
- Keep direct destruction, rent arrears, eviction orders, insurance prices,
  nonrenewal, and mortgage lock-in as distinct shocks.
- Preserve the counterexample that a household can remain housed while losing
  affordability, safety, school continuity, health, or meaningful choice.

## Stronger next test

The next high-value record is a property- or household-year ledger with:

1. dated hazard, rent, payment, insurance, court, or damage event;
2. ownership/tenure, coverage, mortgage or lease, income, and alternatives;
3. claim, assistance, court, repair, contractor, and payment milestones;
4. safe occupancy, health, school, work, debt, and residual cost;
5. stay, move, temporary placement, return, or non-return reason; and
6. later affordability, coverage renewal, place attachment, trust, and durable
   housing security.

If a compatible property/household panel is unavailable, retain the stage
comparison and identify which source owner could supply the missing fields.
Do not infer durable recovery from a payment or an occupied address alone.

## Deeper question

**When does housing assistance or insurance protection produce a safe, chosen,
and durable stay—or a genuinely usable move—rather than merely postponing the
next housing loss?**

## Stopping rule

This pass is sufficient when shock, alternatives, response, payment, repair,
safe occupancy, stay/move, and durable security have been separated by unit,
date, method, and limits; public discussion is bounded; and the property-level
missing middle is explicit. The broader 14-theme program remains active.

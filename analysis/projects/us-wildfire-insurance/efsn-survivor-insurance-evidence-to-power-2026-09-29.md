# Survivor evidence can become institutional power before household recovery is complete

The EFSN route adds a missing political and cultural layer to the California
wildfire-insurance episode. EFSN says 1,600 survivor accounts about State Farm
delays, denials, and underpayments helped Los Angeles County build a lawsuit
seeking restitution. Its current public homepage also claims that seven in ten
Los Angeles fire survivors are still not home, roughly half have taken on new
debt while waiting for recovery funds, and more than $100 million in previously
stalled insurance payments has been moved to families.

These claims show a conversion route that official claims and permit data do
not capture: distributed survivor experience becomes evidence aggregation,
then litigation, legislation, regulatory pressure, and payment movement. The
household recovery endpoint remains partly open.

The limits are material. EFSN does not publish the 1,600-account file, a
recruitment frame, response rate, weighting, insurer denominator, or independent
verification of the homepage percentages. “Not home,” “new debt,” and “payment
moved” are not standardized measures of claim adequacy, safe occupancy, or
durable household recovery. The record therefore treats EFSN as survivor-led
evidence and collective-action infrastructure, not as a population survey.

The next decisive test is the promised public-use insurer dataset or a lawful
evaluation that links documented advocacy exposure to payment, repair, return,
debt, and later insurance status.

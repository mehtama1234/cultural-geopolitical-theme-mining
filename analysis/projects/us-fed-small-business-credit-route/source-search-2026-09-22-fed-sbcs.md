# Source search: small-business credit as a differentiated institutional route

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

When small firms seek financing, how do provider choice and firm credit risk
shape the route from application to approval?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| US-FED-SBCS-2023 | [Federal Reserve Consumer & Community Context: Small Business Credit](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-march-consumer-community-context.htm) | Application sources, requested amounts, approval rates, and lender/risk differences for small employer-firm applicants in the 2023 Small Business Credit Survey. | Survey measures applicant reports and approval stages; it does not follow firms through funding use, repayment, jobs, survival, or local effects. |

## Observed pattern

Thirty-seven percent of small employer firms applied for a loan, line of
credit, or merchant cash advance in the prior 12 months in 2023. Among
applicants, 44% applied to a large bank, 28% to a small bank, 23% to an online
lender, 14% to a finance company, 7% to a credit union, and 6% to a CDFI.

Approval depended on reported credit risk. Low-risk firms were approved at 76%
by large banks and 83% by small banks, compared with 46% and 48% for
medium/high-risk firms. Across provider types, at-least-partial approval ranged
from 66% at large banks to 76% at other nonbank finance companies and credit
unions; full approval was lower for online lenders (31%) than for small banks
(52%). These are different applicant and lender cells, not a single ranking of
provider quality.

## Working interpretation

```text
firm cash need
  -> application amount, risk, and lender choice
  -> partial/full approval and terms
  -> [open] actual funding, investment, repayment, survival, jobs,
     owner-household effects, and local economic consequence
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

Different approval rates may reflect different applicant pools, product types,
amounts, underwriting standards, or speed preferences. Online lenders may serve
firms seeking faster funding, while banks may screen differently. This lane
stops at the credit-route boundary unless a source adds firm-linked funding use,
terms, repayment, survival, employment, or owner-household outcomes.

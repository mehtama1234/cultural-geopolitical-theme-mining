# Finding 001: Small-business credit is a differentiated route shaped by lender choice and firm risk

**Status:** bounded firm-finance and institutional-route finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Federal Reserve's 2023 Small Business Credit Survey found that 37% of
small employer firms applied for a loan, line of credit, or merchant cash
advance in the prior 12 months. Among applicant firms, large banks were the
most common reported source (44%), followed by small banks (28%) and online
lenders (23%).

Approval was sharply patterned by reported credit risk. Low-risk applicants
were approved at 76% by large banks and 83% by small banks, while medium/high-
risk applicants were approved at 46% and 48%. Full and partial approval also
varied by provider type: at least partial approval ranged from 66% at large
banks to 76% at other nonbank finance companies and credit unions, while full
approval ranged from 31% at online lenders to 52% at small banks in the reported
comparison.

The practical route is:

```text
firm need for cash
  -> amount, risk category, lender choice, and application effort
  -> partial/full approval and loan terms
  -> [open] funding receipt, use, repayment, survival, jobs, and local effects
```

The broader institutional insight is that “access to credit” is not one switch.
It is a route through classification, provider choice, underwriting, amount,
speed, and terms. A firm may technically have lenders available while its risk
category or need for a larger amount narrows the usable options.

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a firm-side version of unequal practical agency. Existing business
records show firm entry, exit, revenue, and capacity; this record adds the
intermediate financing gate before those outcomes. It also preserves a useful
counterexample: a lower approval rate at one lender does not prove that lender
is worse, because applicant pools and products differ.

## Limits and next test

- The survey concerns small employer-firm applicants, not all small businesses
  or all firms that needed credit.
- Risk categories are survey-defined and not a randomized comparison of
  lenders.
- Approval is not receipt, amount funded, affordability, or usable working
  capital.
- The source does not show loan terms, repayment, investment, revenue, jobs,
  survival, owner-household income, or local spillovers for the same firm.
- Lender categories can serve different products, speeds, and applicant pools;
  approval percentages should not be read as a universal provider ranking.

The next decisive evidence would link an applicant or firm to requested amount,
terms, funding receipt, use, repayment, later employment or survival, and any
owner-household or community effect.

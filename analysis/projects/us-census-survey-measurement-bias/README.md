# Project: Survey measurement, nonresponse, and the social atlas

## Question

When fewer people answer a national survey, how much can the missing answers
change the picture of income, poverty, and material life?

## Decision rule

Keep the published survey estimate, the nonresponse-adjusted benchmark, the
observed trend direction, and the unknown parts of the population separate.
Administrative adjustment improves the comparison; it does not reveal every
nonrespondent's experience or create a perfect population measure.

## Current bounded finding

The [CPS ASEC nonresponse finding](findings/us-census-cps-asec-nonresponse-bias-2026-001.md)
reports a 61.3% weighted response rate in 2026. Since 2020, Census finds that
survey-only income estimates have generally been 2% to 4% higher than its
alternative nonresponse-adjusted estimates; the 2026 median difference was
2.8%, and the survey-only poverty rate was 0.6 percentage points lower.

The direction of many year-to-year changes remains usable, but the level of
material pressure is not neutral to who answers.

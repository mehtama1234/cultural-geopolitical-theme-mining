# Claims ledger: US childcare, work, and the price of family time

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US children, paid and unpaid care, work time, income, and family tradeoffs

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Paid childcare is a large recurring household cost for families who use it. | Federal Reserve and Census surveys | The Fed reports a median paid-care cost of about $240 per week; Census reports an average annual cost of $10,520 among paying households in 2024. | Median and average use different samples and are not the cost for every family. |
| C-002 | Many families use unpaid care alongside or instead of paid care. | Federal Reserve household survey | 46% of parents reported using some unpaid care; mothers more often reported being the main caregiver. | It does not measure the value or hours of all unpaid care. |
| C-003 | Care problems can take work time. | Census working paper | Households reporting childcare problems lost an average of 65.8 work days in 2024. | The care problem and work loss are reported together; the estimate is not a randomized effect. |
| C-004 | Care access reflects ability to pay, not only need. | Federal Reserve survey | Higher-income parents were more likely to use paid care intensively. | The survey does not separate local supply, schedule, quality, and price. |
| C-005 | Care can make work possible as well as constrain it. | Household and employer evidence | Paid care can release work time; unavailable or unaffordable care can reduce work or create missed days. | The same household is not followed through both choices. |
| C-006 | The cost is both money and family time. | Synthesis | A family may pay a provider, ask relatives, change shifts, or lose work days. | Need linked cost, care, work, income, and later stability. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
child needs care
  -> paid-care price, supply problem, or unpaid family arrangement
  -> money cost, lost work time, or parent changing work
  -> food, housing, debt, health, or public-help choice
  -> employer response and household security
```

## Next test

Pair care cost and disruption with the same household's work hours, earnings, benefits, commute, and later stability. Keep paid care, unpaid care, and no-care coping separate.

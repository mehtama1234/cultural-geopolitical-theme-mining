# Finding 001: Public-pension pressure can travel into private-equity and firm outcomes

**Status:** bounded public-finance, private-equity, and productivity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's Center for Economic Studies working paper traces U.S.
public-pension private-equity investments to target firms using private-
investment data linked with confidential Census data. It reports that more
severely underfunded public pensions receive lower average private-equity
returns and match with smaller general partners. Firms financed by the most
underfunded pensions and smallest funds show labor-productivity decreases.

The route is:

```text
public-pension funding pressure
  -> investment mandate and private-equity matching
  -> fund and target-firm selection
  -> productivity and firm adjustment
  -> jobs, pay, worker control, pension returns, and beneficiary security [open]
```

The broader institutional point is that public financial pressure may travel
through private capital markets before it appears in the lives of workers or
pension beneficiaries. The observed productivity pattern is an important
intermediate signal, not the final social outcome.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a public-finance-to-firm pathway to the atlas. It links institutional
capacity, capital allocation, and work outcomes without collapsing a pension's
financial problem into a firm-level explanation. It also reinforces the need
to follow both sides of the chain: who bears the investment risk and who bears
the workplace adjustment.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a working paper, not a final reviewed Census publication.
- The public abstract does not give all sample, return, fund-selection, or
  productivity details.
- Underfunding and fund size may proxy for governance, sector, risk, selection,
  or macroeconomic conditions.
- Labor productivity is not employment, wages, hours, worker control, or
  household security.
- The result does not establish pension solvency, beneficiary outcomes, or
  whether another investment policy would improve returns.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected pension-fund-firm-worker ledger joining
funding status, mandate, investment, fund, target firm, productivity,
employment, pay, hours, survival, pension returns, and beneficiary outcomes.
That would show whether financial pressure creates a durable cross-domain
burden or only a firm-level association.

# Finding 001: Platform exit can be formally available but practically reversible

**Status:** bounded comparative qualitative platform-governance finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Kim and Kim interviewed 20 young Korean adults in their 20s about Instagram
use and intentional non-use. At interview, 11 were active users, 2 had
deactivated accounts, 5 had deleted accounts, 1 had an inactive account, and 1
had partially deactivated accounts. These are sample-status counts, not a
platform deletion rate.

Participants described several levels of non-use: limiting time or features,
using device or app controls, deactivating or deleting accounts, and changing
personal routines. The study reports platform-specific resource and UX
challenges, psychological discomfort, and social pressure. Non-use attempts
were described as nonlinear and reversible. Some participants reported relief,
stronger offline relationships, and greater control; others reported exclusion,
continued social connection to Instagram despite non-use, or compensating use
of other platforms.

The safe interpretation is:

> A nominal account-exit option does not necessarily create durable practical
> exit. Platform utility, social connectivity norms, reported user experience,
> and alternative platforms can make non-use reversible and redistribute the
> burden of disengagement to the user.

## Evidence chain

```text
platform utility and connectivity norms
  -> motivation to limit or stop use
  -> time, app, account, or routine-level non-use strategy
  -> UX, psychological, and social friction
  -> reversible non-use, exclusion, or substitution to another platform
  -> [open] durable exit, data erasure, wellbeing, remedy, or collective action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds the lived practical-exit middle between formal platform control and
the outcome labels the atlas already tracks. It also supplies a counterexample
to treating non-use as either a clean consumer choice or a simple platform
failure: participants could experience real relief and agency while remaining
socially connected to, dependent on, or redirected across platforms.

## What the source does not show

- The 20-person snowball sample is not representative of Korean users or U.S.
  users.
- Account status at interview does not establish successful deletion, data
  erasure, reactivation, or long-term non-use.
- Reported UX challenges do not by themselves prove intentional dark patterns
  or unlawful obstruction by Instagram.
- The study does not estimate population wellbeing, consumer harm, trust,
  political behavior, or collective platform change.

## Next decisive test

Combine platform-specific task audits with a longitudinal user panel that records
attempted deactivation/deletion, prompts and alternatives, completion, data
erasure, reactivation, substitution, social and work consequences, and later
trust or collective action. Keep formal exit availability, experienced effort,
completed exit, and durable non-use as distinct measures.

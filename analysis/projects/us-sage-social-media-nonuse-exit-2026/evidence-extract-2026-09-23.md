# Official-source extract: social-media non-use and practical exit

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable comparative record. This is not a substitute for the linked
article.

## Official source

- Danbi Kim and Yong-Chan Kim, “Disconnecting in a Connected World: The
  Dynamics of Social Media Non-Use in a Platformized Society,” *Social Media +
  Society*, first published March 27, 2026, [SAGE article page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20563051261419391),
  [official PDF](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/20563051261419391).

## Extracted claims

- The study used semi-structured, in-depth interviews with 20 young Korean
  adults in their 20s. Interviews were conducted in Korean in February and
  March 2024 and lasted about one hour each.
- The sample included 11 active users, 2 participants with deactivated
  accounts, 5 with deleted accounts, 1 with an inactive account, and 1 with
  partially deactivated accounts. Recruitment primarily used snowball sampling;
  the authors sought variation in gender, age, occupation, and account status.
- The authors identify non-use strategies at spatial-temporal, app, account,
  and personal-use levels. Participants encountered platform-specific resource
  and UX challenges, psychological discomfort, and social pressure.
- The authors describe non-use attempts as nonlinear and reversible, with a
  gap between intended outcomes and actual experiences. Reported consequences
  included relief and a sense of control for some participants, but also
  exclusion, incomplete disconnection, and increased reliance on other
  platforms.
- The study reports that participants generally did not translate personal
  non-use into collective or political action, even when they criticized
  platform monetization and attention capture.

## Boundary preserved

This is qualitative evidence about reported experience and meaning. It does not
measure population prevalence, platform-wide account-deletion friction,
verified data erasure, durable non-use, wellbeing, consumer remedy, or U.S.
effects. Account status at interview is sample composition, not a deletion
success rate.

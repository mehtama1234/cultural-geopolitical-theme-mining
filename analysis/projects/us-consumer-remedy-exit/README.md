# Project: US consumer remedy and practical exit

## Question

When a firm or public agency announces a refund, correction, or account
control, does the person actually receive usable relief—and can they keep using
the service, switch, or leave?

## Short end-to-end goal

Follow a consumer or worker problem from exposure through complaint, decision,
payment or correction, actual receipt, adequacy, continued use, switching, and
exit.

```text
exposure or loss
  -> contact, complaint, or escalation
  -> decision, redress, or control
  -> receipt and practical adequacy
  -> continued use, switching, non-use, or exit
  -> trust, household room, worker security, or market response
```

This is a separate depth lane in the ongoing 14-theme program. It complements
the broader customer-service work by keeping formal remedy, usable remedy, and
practical exit as different stages.

## Current bridge

The [redress-to-practical-exit bridge](redress-receipt-practical-exit-bridge-v1.md)
compares FTC refund administration, CFPB compensation, platform-worker cases,
and bounded public discussion. It finds that issued money and prospective
controls are more visible than receipt, adequacy, continued use, switching, or
exit.

The new [formal-remedy stage audit](formal-remedy-stage-audit-v1.md) compares
the administrative funnel across FTC, CFPB, and platform-worker records. It
separates sent, cashed, mailed, distributed, access-restored, and durable-exit
observations rather than treating them as one remedy rate.

## Next test

The next qualifying record is an identifier-bearing account, recipient, worker,
or complaint episode that can show payment or correction, receipt, residual
loss, repeat contact, and later use or exit. Aggregate program totals remain
context, not remedy rates.

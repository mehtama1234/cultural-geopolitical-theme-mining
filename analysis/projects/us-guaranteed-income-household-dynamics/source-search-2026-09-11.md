# Source search: US guaranteed income and household dynamics

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; household spillovers are measured, health meaning remains open

## Working question

What does regular cash change inside a household when only one person receives it?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-GI-HOUSEHOLD | [Income Shocks and Intrahousehold Dynamics](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35589) | In a randomized guaranteed-income experiment, other household members were more likely to take part in education or training. Recipients were also more likely to report that someone in the household had forgone health care, which the authors say may reflect greater engagement with care and the finding of unmet needs. | NBER working paper | The result does not show whether health worsened, whether care was newly identified, or how effects change after payments end. |
| US-NBER-GI-BALANCE | [The Impact of Unconditional Cash Transfers on Consumption and Household Balance Sheets](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32784) | A US experiment with $1,000 or $50 monthly payments measured spending, assets and debt, finding higher short-term consumption and near-zero average net-worth change because debt also rose. | NBER working paper | This study does not identify every within-household decision or health effect. |
| US-HBS-CASH-CONTROL | [More Proof That Money Can Buy Happiness](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/more-proof-that-money-can-buy-happiness) | HBS reports that money can give people more control over daily problems and reduce stress. | HBS Working Knowledge | The research does not test the new guaranteed-income experiment or its household spillovers. |

## First pattern to test

```text
one household member receives regular cash
  -> money, time and decision power move inside the home
  -> another member trains, works, receives care, or changes spending
  -> the family gains room or finds an unmet need
  -> later work, health, debt and family roles change
```

The NBER experiment shows that cash can affect people other than the recipient. The health-care result is not simple: more reported forgone care may mean more unmet need, more care-seeking that exposes a need, or both. It needs follow-up rather than a quick label.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Cash can give a household flexibility to solve a problem in its own way. A new report of forgone care can reflect better awareness or access to a system, not a direct harm from the payment.

## Main gaps

- who controls the payment and how it is shared;
- work, training and care by each household member;
- health need, care use and out-of-pocket cost;
- spending, saving, debt and housing changes;
- conflict, dependence and family support;
- results after the payment stops.

## Decision rule

Keep recipient effects, other-member effects and health reports separate. If a household measure changes without a clear mechanism, record it as a spillover and move on.

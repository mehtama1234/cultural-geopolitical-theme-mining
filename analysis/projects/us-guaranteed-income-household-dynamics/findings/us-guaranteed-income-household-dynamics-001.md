# Finding 001: Cash paid to one person can change the work, care, and relationships of the whole household

**Status:** bounded household spillover and care finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

In a randomized U.S. experiment, 1,000 participants received $1,000 per month
for three years and 2,000 control participants received $50 per month. The
study found no effect on aggregate household-stability measures, but the money
did not stay confined to the named recipient.

Other household members were more likely to participate in education or
training. Household income excluding the recipient fell by about $1,700 per
year, and partners were less likely to be promoted or move to a better job.
Transfers to friends and family increased. Relationship quality and trust in
partners improved suggestively among recipients in relationships.

Recipients also more often reported that someone in the household had forgone
healthcare. The authors say this may reflect greater engagement with healthcare
and the discovery of needs that were already unmet. Decision-making power and
division of labor changed little. Some intimate-partner-violence indicators
declined, especially for women, but those results were limited by low incidence
and multiple-hypothesis adjustment.

The route is:

```text
one person receives regular cash
  -> money and time are renegotiated inside the household
  -> work, training, transfers, relationships, and care choices change
  -> [mixed] household room, security, health, and family power
  -> [open] what lasts after payments stop
```

The deeper point is that a cash policy aimed at one person can reorganize a
small social system. A lower partner income is not automatically a failure: it
could reflect care, training, or a chosen change. But it is also not proof that
the whole family became more secure.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a stronger same-household intervention finding than an aggregate
benefit count, but it still does not produce one simple story. Cash can increase
trust and education while reducing some employment advancement and revealing
healthcare needs. Household stability, family meaning, work, care, and health
therefore run on different clocks.

It also sharpens the program's cultural question: who controls new room inside
a household, whose work changes, which needs become visible, and whether a
family interprets the change as relief, dependence, opportunity, or strain.

## Limits and next test

- This is one randomized program and its household structures may not represent
  all U.S. families.
- The public abstract does not provide every outcome's denominator or full
  treatment-effect table.
- Forgone healthcare is ambiguous and cannot be labeled simply as harm.
- Lower partner earnings do not identify whether the change was chosen,
  necessary, beneficial, or costly.
- The next decisive test follows households after payments end through work,
  care, health use, debt, housing, relationship quality, and whether the new
  arrangement persists.

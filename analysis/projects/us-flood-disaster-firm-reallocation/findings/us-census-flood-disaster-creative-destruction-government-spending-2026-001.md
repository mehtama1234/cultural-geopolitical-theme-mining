# Finding 001: Disaster recovery can rebuild productivity while disrupting local continuity

**Status:** bounded disaster, firm-reallocation, and public-spending finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES paper follows plants after large federally declared floods. It
finds that the plants most likely to exit were the least productive. Used
machines from those plants were then acquired by more productive entrants, and
surviving plants upgraded machinery as they rebuilt and saw productivity gains.

The paper also finds that federal disaster spending expanded financing access
for nimble young and small firms, helping productive reallocation. Its framework
reports relative income gains that generated tax revenues exceeding the policy's
upfront cost.

The practical chain is:

```text
flood
  -> plant damage, finance constraint, exit, or rebuilding
  -> machinery transfer and entrant/survivor investment
  -> productivity, employment, wages, and local service change [not all observed]
  -> household recovery, place attachment, and durable security [open]
```

The simple social insight is that “recovery” can mean a more productive local
economy and still involve closures, job loss, ownership change, and disruption
for people who lived through the disaster.

## What this adds to the atlas

This complements the household flood-risk record with a firm and public-finance
layer. It shows that disaster assistance can be a selection mechanism: it may
help some young or small firms enter and expand, while the productive process
also reallocates assets away from exiting firms. The atlas must therefore keep
efficiency, survival, employment, and community continuity separate.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper, not a Census Bureau endorsement.
- Plant productivity is not worker wellbeing, household recovery, or local
  service access.
- Closure can be efficient in the model and still be devastating for workers,
  owners, suppliers, or a place.
- Entrant productivity does not establish local hiring, fair wages, or shared
  gains.
- The tax-revenue conclusion is study-specific and does not prove every disaster
  program pays for itself.

The next decisive artifact is a protected plant-worker-place panel linking flood
damage, aid, financing, exit, new ownership, machinery, jobs, wages, migration,
local services, household recovery, and later firm and community security.

# Project: US workplace communication and the price of fitting in

## Question

When a worker sounds like the people who already hold power at work, does that change access to people-facing jobs, advancement and the ability to be heard?

## Short first pass

Use the HBS account of research based on Census, labor-market data and audio records as the starting point. Keep speech pattern, social skill, job sorting, hiring, promotion and pay as separate outcomes.

## Possible connection

As more routine work is handled by software, communication-heavy work may become more valuable. If managers mistake familiar speech for stronger social skill, the shift can reward cultural matching instead of actual ability.

## Decision rule

Start with one measured job-sorting result and one serious counterpoint about occupation, region or skill. Move on unless the data can show who got access, who advanced and what the employer judged.

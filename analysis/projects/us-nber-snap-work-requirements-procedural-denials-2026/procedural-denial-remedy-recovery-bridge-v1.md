# SNAP procedural denial: from work requirement to remedy and recovery

**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Status:** bounded administrative-denial realization pass  
**Scope:** SNAP work requirements, exemption rules, documentation, procedural
denial, appeal, benefit restoration, food security, work, and household recovery

## What we found

NBER Working Paper 34698 uses SNAP administrative data and difference-in-
differences designs around age-based exemptions for Able-Bodied Adults Without
Dependents and periods when work-requirement waivers applied. The paper reports
that work requirements significantly increased procedural denials as well as
overall denials. It interprets the pattern as evidence that administrative
burden can deter participation, rather than treating every disenrollment as a
change in need or eligibility.

This adds a policy-linked denial mechanism to the existing SNAP access chain.
It still stops before the lived result. The public record does not show a
specific claimant's notice, paperwork, exemption proof, appeal, restored
benefit, gap days, food adequacy, work effect, or later trust.

## The deeper question

When does a procedural denial become a reversible administrative interruption,
and when does it become an unobserved loss of food support that changes work,
health, household time, trust, or later participation?

## Why this matters

A work requirement can be described as an eligibility rule, a labor-market
policy, or an administrative process. Claimants experience the whole chain. If
the rule increases denials but there is fast notice, accessible documentation,
successful appeal, and continuous food support, the harm path is different from
a denial that persists through missed notice, unaffordable time, and no remedy.

Priority is **high** for decision value, missing-link value, connection value,
and disconfirmation value. Effort is medium and scope risk is high: the next
pass must keep ABAWD age, waiver status, jurisdiction, denial type, claimant,
case, benefit, and household units separate.

## What we know so far

- The NBER design links work requirements to significant increases in
  procedural and overall denials within study-selected administrative settings.
- Age exemptions and waiver periods create a policy comparison, but the public
  abstract does not disclose the full denominator, jurisdictions, or effect
  magnitudes.
- A procedural denial can reflect incomplete paperwork, missing exemption proof,
  notice failure, or another process route; it is not automatically a valid
  final eligibility decision.
- Existing survey and SIPP evidence shows time, notice, paperwork, work, care,
  and resource constraints around SNAP, but not the same claimant's full
  remedy episode.

## What could change the answer

The interruption would look reversible if a compatible case record links the
work-requirement notice to documentation, denial reason, appeal or correction,
restored benefits, gap duration, and continued food access. It would look more
harmful if denial persisted, the claimant substituted unpaid time or debt,
missed work or care, lost food support, or did not know how to appeal.

The policy interpretation would weaken if administrative records show that
most procedural denials were quickly corrected, did not interrupt payment, or
were concentrated among cases that became ineligible for independent reasons.

## Challenge checks

- Procedural-denial activity was not treated as food insecurity or household
  harm.
- The NBER identification strategy was not expanded into a claim about every
  claimant's motive or experience.
- Denial, disenrollment, non-filing, appeal, restoration, and payment receipt
  remain separate stages.
- Study-selected jurisdictions and ABAWD age/waiver contrasts were not treated
  as a national denial rate.
- Survey barriers and SIPP resource movements were retained as adjacent
  evidence, not same-unit replication.
- Exempt people, waived areas, non-filers, successful appeals, people with
  disabilities or care duties, and people without stable notice or digital
  access remain visible.

## Current answer

**Partly answered.** The evidence supports a policy-to-procedural-denial route
and a plausible participation-deterrence mechanism. It does not yet establish
whether a named claimant lost benefits, received a remedy, experienced a food
or work consequence, or later trusted, reused, or exited the program.

## What to look for next

```text
work requirement and exemption status
  -> notice, documentation, interview, and timing
  -> procedural or substantive denial
  -> appeal, correction, continued payment, or restored benefit
  -> gap days, food access, debt, work/care substitution, and health
  -> later renewal, trust, action, or route exit
```

Use a privacy-approved case or claimant ledger when available. Preserve
successful appeals and no-gap cases as counterexamples, and report the missing
middle if the source only supplies aggregate denials or participation.

## Stopping rule

Stop this pass when one compatible claimant or case episode links requirement
exposure, notice, denial reason, appeal or correction, benefit receipt, gap
duration, food/work outcome, and later renewal or exit, with a credible no-gap
or successful-appeal counterexample. If those fields remain unavailable,
retain the result as a procedural-to-security gap rather than calling the rule
harmful or the remedy effective.

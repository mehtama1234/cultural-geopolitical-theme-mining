# Source search: US cash aid and household structure

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; cash and housing results are linked, longer-term effects remain open

## Working question

What does regular cash allow a low-income family to change about its home?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-CTC-HOUSING | [The 2021 Child Tax Credit, Living Arrangements and Housing Affordability](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31339) | Using national data on about 20,500 low-income parents connected to SNAP, the study finds that the monthly Child Tax Credit was associated with changes in living arrangements, fewer people in the home, fewer expected moves due to rent or mortgage difficulty, and less back-owed housing payment. | NBER working paper | The study uses reported arrangements and a modeled comparison; it does not show that every change was lasting or better for every family. |
| US-NBER-CASH-TRANSFER | [The Impact of Unconditional Cash Transfers on Consumption and Household Balance Sheets](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32784) | An experiment in two US states found that large temporary transfers increased short-term consumption and improved some financial measures, while higher debt offset gains in net worth on average. | NBER working paper | This is not a housing-only study and its results do not identify the best payment size or schedule. |
| US-HBS-CASH-AGENCY | [More Proof That Money Can Buy Happiness](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/more-proof-that-money-can-buy-happiness) | HBS reports that having more money can give people more control over daily problems, including transport, food and care tasks. | HBS Working Knowledge | The article does not test the Child Tax Credit or housing arrangements. |

## First pattern to test

```text
regular cash arrives
  -> rent or mortgage arrears fall
  -> a household can move, separate, stay, or reduce crowding
  -> housing stability, care time, work access, or child outcomes change
  -> public support is judged by the room it creates, not only the payment amount
```

The NBER housing study supports a link between monthly cash and immediate housing conditions for the studied families. It does not tell us whether every household became safer, whether a move lasted, or how the result changed after the credit ended.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

A smaller household may reflect needed independence or a loss of support. Staying together may be protective in one family and forced by money in another. The data should describe the choice and the housing condition separately.

## Main gaps

- who moved in or out and why;
- rent, mortgage, utility and moving costs;
- crowding, safety and child well-being;
- work, school and care access after a move;
- effects after the monthly payment ends;
- differences by race, income, state and family structure.

## Decision rule

Follow cash, housing payment, people in the home and later stability together. If only living arrangements change, record the change without calling it a housing improvement.

# Finding 001: A rapid acquisition pathway can still produce slow delivery

**Status:** bounded defense-acquisition and capability-realization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO assessed 104 of the Department of Defense's costliest weapon programs and
selected rapid-acquisition efforts. The average time to deliver a capability
across the reviewed major programs rose above 12 years.

DOD planned to invest at least $49 billion across 23 of its most expensive
Middle Tier of Acquisition programs. But 18 of 40 reviewed Middle Tier programs
entered the pathway with immature technologies. GAO also found that some such
programs ended without a fieldable capability; in seven of eight reviewed
programs, technologies remained immature and needed more development.

The route is:

```text
rapid-acquisition label and investment
  -> technology development and integration
  -> testing, milestones, and fielding
  -> [open] reliable operational capability, sustainment, and strategic effect
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a defense-delivery version of a recurring pattern:

> A system can be funded, prioritized, and placed on a fast pathway while the
> underlying technology is not ready to produce a usable result.

It is the defense counterpart to other atlas findings where formal capacity or
an award arrives before the practical service, control, or outcome.

## Limits and next test

- The 104 programs were selected by cost and acquisition status, not randomly.
- A schedule average is not the outcome of every program.
- Immature technology is one cause among testing, software, integration,
  requirements, safety, and management factors.
- The report does not measure battlefield performance, deterrence, reliability,
  sustainment, or adversary response.

The next decisive test is a named-program ledger joining technology maturity,
milestones, test results, production, fielding, reliability, sustainment,
operator use, cost, and external response. Until then, the safe claim is that
rapid acquisition can accelerate attention without guaranteeing rapid delivery.

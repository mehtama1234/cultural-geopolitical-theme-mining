# Recurring payment can turn inattention into a consumer-exit problem

**Status:** provisional case finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

The FTC's May 2026 Shutterstock case makes the subscription question concrete.
The Commission alleged that the company failed to clearly disclose renewal and
cancellation terms, charged consumers without express informed consent, and
made cancellation difficult. The proposed settlement provides $35 million for
consumer relief and requires clearer material-term disclosure, express consent,
and simple cancellation mechanisms.

```text
subscription or content pack
  -> renewal term, charge, or cancellation fee is not understood
  -> attention or support contact is required to stop payment
  -> continued payment, cancellation effort, relief, or switching
  -> household room, trust, and seller/consumer power
```

The case closes the institutional-response arrow, but only partly closes the
consumer arrow. It gives us a named mechanism and proposed controls; it does
not give us a representative denominator, an observed unwanted-renewal rate,
the time or money burden per customer, or proof that each continuing customer
wanted to stop.

## Transportation and delivery membership comparison

The FTC's amended Uber One complaint supplies a second US case with a different
service dependency. The FTC and 21 states plus the District of Columbia allege
that Uber marketed monthly savings of $25 without accounting for a subscription
cost of up to $9.99, charged some consumers without consent, failed to deliver
promised savings, and made cancellation difficult. The release says some users
could face as many as 23 screens and 32 actions to cancel.

This comparison matters because the subscription is attached to rides and food
delivery: an exit decision may involve transport, convenience, delivery access,
and household scheduling, not only media or digital content. It remains a case
allegation. The public record does not show how many customers encountered the
flow, how many canceled, what savings were realized, whether refunds occurred,
or whether users switched to other transport or delivery options.

## What the case contributes

| Layer | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Product design | FTC allegations concern annual paid-monthly plans and on-demand packs, including renewal and early-cancellation terms | The public announcement does not provide account-level exposure or current-flow telemetry |
| Exit | Before 2024, the FTC says early cancellation could require phone, chat, or email support; the complaint also alleges difficult-to-find terms | A burdensome route is not the same as an impossible exit, and the case does not report completion rates |
| Remedy | Proposed $35M consumer relief and requirements for disclosure, consent, and simple cancellation | Proposed controls do not establish implementation, payment receipt, or recurrence prevention |
| Household effect | The mechanism is plausibly connected to recurring cost and attention/time | The case does not measure budget strain, overdrafts, missed essentials, or trust change |
| Cross-service comparison | Uber One extends the same alleged billing/exit mechanism to rides and delivery; up to 23 screens and 32 actions are reported in the amended complaint | Service-specific exposure, actual savings, cancellation completion, switching, refund, and household mobility outcomes |

## The key distinction

Three states must remain separate:

1. **Continued payment:** the account renewed.
2. **Unwanted payment:** the customer says the renewal was not desired or understood.
3. **Failed practical exit:** the customer attempted to stop, but could not do so without material time, money, access, or support cost.

The FTC case is evidence about the design and governance of the route between
these states. It is not a national estimate of any of them. Automatic renewal
can also be useful when it preserves software, news, food, care, or other
service continuity for a customer who knowingly wants it.

## Broad-program connection

This is a small but important bridge between the culture/attention and
consumer-power themes. Attention is not only time spent on a platform. It can
also be the scarce attention required to monitor recurring charges, remember
terms, find a cancellation route, contact support, and verify that billing
stopped. A seller can therefore capture value from both the service and the
customer's failure to notice or act, while the public remedy attempts to
restore informed choice.

The September 17, 2026 FTC docket recheck still lists the case as **pending**.
It displays the complaint and a joint motion to enter a stipulated order, but
the public docket check does not verify a signed final order, consumer-relief
distribution, or implemented cancellation controls. The next high-value
evidence is therefore implementation depth: a signed final order,
refund or relief receipt, current enrollment and cancellation screens, support
contact burden, repeat complaints, and account-level outcomes. Those should be
paired with the NBER payment-card mechanism, but not merged into a single loss
rate. The strongest comparison would preserve plan type, renewal notice,
customer intent, attempted exit, realized charge, relief, continued use, and
switching.

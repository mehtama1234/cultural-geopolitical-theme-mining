# Source search: US subscriptions and the cost of not noticing

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States consumer market  
**Status:** promoted case finding; prevalence and implementation follow-up remain open

## Working question

When people stop valuing a subscription, what keeps the payment going?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NBER-W31547 | [NBER: Selling Subscriptions](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31547) | Using payment-card data for ten digital and non-digital products, the authors use card replacement as a moment when customers must actively renew. Cancellations rise sharply then, which is consistent with some people having stopped valuing the service earlier but not canceling. Estimated inattention raises seller revenue by 14% to more than 200% compared with immediate cancellation after value falls below price. | Working paper with payment data | The sample covers one card network and 2017–2021; the revenue range depends on the model and does not equal a direct loss for every customer. |
| NBER-DIGEST-2023 | [NBER Digest: Consumer Inattention and Subscription Renewals](https://www.nber.org/digest/202310/consumer-inattention-and-subscription-renewals) | The digest reports that the card network covered about 30% of subscribers and that an active choice every six months could cut the revenue effect of inattention by about half in the model. | Research summary | It summarizes the working paper and does not measure the effect of current cancellation laws or every subscription type. |
| HBS-OFek-2023 | [HBS Working Knowledge: With Subscription Fatigue Setting In, Companies Need to Think Hard About Fees](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/with-subscription-fatigue-setting-in-companies-need-to-think-hard-about-fees) | HBS describes the business appeal of recurring revenue and the risk that poorly chosen subscription models alienate customers. It also reports that subscriptions had spread across many everyday products. | Business research summary | The article uses industry estimates and advice; it does not measure household loss from unwanted renewals. |
| US-FTC-SHUTTERSTOCK-2026 | [FTC Shutterstock settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices) | The FTC says Shutterstock agreed to pay $35 million to settle allegations that it charged consumers without informed consent and made cancellation difficult; the proposed order requires clearer terms, consent, and simple cancellation. | Official settlement record | It concerns one company and allegations; it does not estimate the wider subscription burden or prove that automatic renewal is harmful when clearly understood. |

## First pattern to test

```text
subscription starts
  -> payment renews without a fresh choice
  -> use falls or value becomes unclear
  -> attention, time or card replacement finally forces a choice
  -> cancellation, continued payment, fee or overdraft
  -> different room in the household budget and different trust in the seller
```

The NBER study supports the link between forced action and cancellation. It does not prove why each customer waited, whether every continued payment was unwanted, or that the result changed political trust.

The September 17, 2026 [FTC case-docket recheck](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/shutterstock-inc)
lists Shutterstock as pending and displays the complaint plus a joint motion to
enter a stipulated order. The proposed remedy is therefore not yet treated as
a verified signed-order or implementation outcome in this project.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Automatic renewal can be useful. It prevents a lapse in needed software, news, food, insurance or care, and some customers knowingly keep a service while using it only occasionally. A canceled card is also not a perfect test of lost value.

## Main gaps

- current US subscription counts and total monthly burden by income;
- cancellation effort, notice design and state or federal rules;
- overdrafts, late fees and other effects after an unwanted charge;
- differences between entertainment, health, food, software and essential services;
- whether customers understand renewal terms at sign-up;
- whether easier cancellation lowers useful service continuity.

## Decision rule

Pair the NBER result with current US complaint, payment, or household-survey data before calling recurring charges a household tax. Keep “not canceled” separate from “not valued.”

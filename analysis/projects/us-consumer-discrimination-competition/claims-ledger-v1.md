# Claims ledger: US consumer discrimination and competition

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US customers, competition, price, treatment, information, and ability to switch

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Competition does not automatically make treatment equal. | Historical US market evidence | NBER finds increases in nondiscrimination were concentrated in the least competitive markets before 1964. | Historical setting; it does not measure today's firms or online markets. |
| C-002 | A customer group can pay more even when treatment improves. | Historical price data | NBER finds nondiscriminatory firms charged higher prices than discriminatory firms in the same local market in its sample. | Price may reflect service, costs, or market structure; the paper's setting is specific. |
| C-003 | Uniform pricing can hide different burdens across places and customers. | US retail-chain data | NBER finds near-uniform prices across stores despite local differences and suggests poorer households may pay more relative to income. | The study does not identify discrimination as the cause of each price. |
| C-004 | Better information and easier online comparison can narrow some customer price gaps. | US car and mortgage evidence | NBER studies find internet search and some FinTech lending reduce certain observed racial price gaps, though gaps remain in lending. | Online users may differ from people who do not shop online. |
| C-005 | Customer power depends on ability to leave, not only the number of sellers. | Synthesis | Information, money, time, transport, identity, and switching cost shape whether a customer can discipline a firm. | These factors need direct measurement in current markets. |
| C-006 | A higher or lower price alone does not prove unequal treatment. | Counterpoint | Service quality, cost to serve, risk, and product differences can explain some price variation. | Need comparable product, customer, and treatment records. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
customer group and ability to leave
  -> firm competition and incentive
  -> treatment, price, service, or access
  -> customer cost and choice
  -> regulation, trust, and market entry
```

## Next test

Compare similar customers across firms using the same product, price, service, search path, and ability to switch. Add complaints and enforcement, and separate unequal treatment from ordinary price variation.

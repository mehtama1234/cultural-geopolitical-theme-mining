# Finding: scalable AI measurement shifts the bottleneck to validation

**Status:** bounded methodological and institutional finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35744 reviews how AI models convert unstructured text and
images into structured variables at low cost. The authors argue that this
changes the measurement bottleneck: the challenge is less often finding any
scalable proxy and more often choosing among plausible constructs that can
support different empirical conclusions.

The paper separates three stages at which AI can enter a measurement pipeline:
discovery, construct definition, and observation. It argues that credible
inference requires explicit criteria and appropriately designed validation,
including careful treatment of biased predictions and settings where a random
validation sample is unavailable.

The atlas-relevant insight is:

> More measurement can create more visibility without creating more agreement
> about what is being measured. Scale therefore increases the value of
> validation and the institutional stakes of construct choice.

## Limits and next test

- This is a methodological review, not a population estimate or causal test of
  a named AI deployment.
- The abstract does not disclose a systematic-review corpus count.
- The framework does not establish accuracy, subgroup error, discrimination,
  trust, appeal, or resource-allocation effects in a particular institution.
- The next test is a named-system ledger joining the construct, training or
  validation sample, subgroup errors, human review, appeal route, decision
  consequence, and whether affected people can contest the measure.

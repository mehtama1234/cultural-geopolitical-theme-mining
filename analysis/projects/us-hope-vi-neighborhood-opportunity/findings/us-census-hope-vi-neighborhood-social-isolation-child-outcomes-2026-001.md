# Finding 001: Neighborhood redevelopment can change children's opportunity without raising public-housing adults' earnings

**Status:** bounded housing, place, childhood, and social-connection finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper studies HOPE VI, a $17 billion effort to
revitalize 262 distressed public-housing developments. Using matched
difference-in-differences with anonymized tax records, plus movers and sibling
comparisons, it reports lower neighborhood poverty after revitalization but no
causal earnings effect for adults living in public-housing units.

Children raised in revitalized units earned more as adults, were more likely to
attend college, and were less likely to be incarcerated. The study reports that
increased interaction with peers in surrounding neighborhoods helped explain
the child improvements, especially for children exposed to higher-income peers.

The practical route is:

```text
public-housing redevelopment
  -> neighborhood composition and social interaction
  -> children's exposure and opportunity
  -> adult earnings, college, and incarceration outcomes
  -> [open] adult wellbeing, resident control, housing stability, and belonging
```

The adult null and child gains should be held together; neither cancels the
other.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a place-based version of the program's recurring split between
capacity and lived conversion. A neighborhood can change its social opportunity
structure for children without immediately raising the earnings of adults who
live in the public-housing units.

It also makes social connection measurable as infrastructure. The reported
mechanism is not only buildings or poverty rates; it is interaction with people
and institutions in surrounding neighborhoods. But a lower poverty rate can
also reflect attracting higher-income families, so resident retention,
displacement, housing quality, services, safety, and control remain essential.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper, and the public abstract does not provide all site-
  selection details, estimates, displacement patterns, or housing outcomes.
- Lower neighborhood poverty can reflect attracting higher-income families,
  not improvement for every original resident.
- The adult earnings null is specific to adults in public-housing units and does
  not mean no adult outcome changed.
- Child earnings, college attendance, and incarceration do not establish
  complete wellbeing, belonging, safety, or resident control.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected family-place panel linking site
selection, redevelopment, unit retention, moves, rent, housing quality,
services, schools, peers, safety, adult work and wellbeing, child outcomes, and
resident voice. Until then, the safe claim is a neighborhood-connection effect
on children's long-run opportunity alongside an adult-earnings null.

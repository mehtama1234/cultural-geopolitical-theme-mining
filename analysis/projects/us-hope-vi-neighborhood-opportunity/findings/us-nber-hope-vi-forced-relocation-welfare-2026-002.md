# Finding 002: Urban redevelopment can create large displacement losses while vouchers and counseling only partly offset them

**Status:** bounded housing, mobility, and redevelopment-welfare finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

An NBER study of Chicago's HOPE VI public-housing demolitions combines
administrative records tracking displaced residents with Census data on
neighborhood sorting and prices. The paper estimates that demolitions produced
large welfare losses driven by displacement costs, partly offset by housing
vouchers.

Redevelopment also raised housing prices and reshaped neighborhood composition,
with gains concentrated among higher-income homeowners. Counterfactuals suggest
that more generous vouchers and counseling mitigate but do not eliminate
displaced residents' losses, while expanded redevelopment creates larger
citywide gains through general-equilibrium effects.

The route is:

```text
public-housing demolition and redevelopment
  -> forced relocation and neighborhood choice
  -> displacement cost, voucher/counseling route, and price change
  -> resident welfare loss or partial offset
  -> [open] housing stability, school/work continuity, health, belonging, and trust
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a strong housing/place example of why aggregate improvement cannot be
treated as universal resident recovery. Redevelopment can generate citywide
gains and higher-income homeowner gains while the original residents carry
large transition losses. The counterfactuals also show that a formal voucher
route may mitigate but not erase the cost of forced movement.

It complements the existing HOPE VI child-outcome finding: neighborhood and
long-run child outcomes are one stage; displacement, resident welfare, and
practical recovery are another.

## Limits and next test

- The abstract does not disclose the full resident count, welfare metric, site
  selection, or all model assumptions.
- Modeled welfare and general-equilibrium gains are not observed household
  recovery or resident control.
- The study does not establish individual housing stability, school continuity,
  work travel, health, belonging, trust, or later political action.
- Citywide gains and displaced-resident losses can coexist; they must not be
  pooled into one welfare claim.

The next decisive artifact is a protected resident episode ledger linking
demolition notice, move, voucher and counseling receipt, destination, rent,
travel, school/work continuity, housing quality, service access, recovery, and
later staying, returning, or exit.

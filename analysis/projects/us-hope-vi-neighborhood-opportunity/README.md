# Project: HOPE VI, neighborhood connection, and children's opportunity

This project tracks whether changing a neighborhood changes the life paths of
children and adults differently. A Census working paper studies HOPE VI, which
invested $17 billion to revitalize 262 distressed public-housing developments.
It reports lower neighborhood poverty and no causal earnings effect for adults
living in public-housing units, but higher later earnings and college attendance
and lower incarceration among children raised in revitalized units.

The study points to increased interaction with peers in surrounding
neighborhoods as an important mechanism. The safe theme is not “redevelopment
helps everyone” or “lower neighborhood poverty proves resident prosperity.” It
is that neighborhood social connection can operate on children's long-run
opportunity while adult income, housing stability, displacement, safety,
services, and resident control remain separate questions.

The decisive next artifact is a protected family-place panel joining site
selection, redevelopment, unit retention, moves, rent, housing quality,
services, schools, peers, safety, adult work and wellbeing, child outcomes, and
resident voice. The public abstract does not provide that complete bridge.

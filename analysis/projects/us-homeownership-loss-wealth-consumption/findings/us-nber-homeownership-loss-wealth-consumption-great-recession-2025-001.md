# Finding 001: Housing wealth is not automatically usable household room

**Status:** bounded housing-finance, wealth-conversion, and household-security finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

Keeping a home can raise measured wealth without giving a household more money to
spend right away.

NBER Working Paper 33692 compares seriously delinquent Great Recession
homeowners who were nearly identical in pre-trends but differed in whether they
received a mortgage modification. Modification increased home retention by 36
percentage points, and more than half of that difference remained nearly a decade
later. The resulting housing-wealth difference was about $70,000, but consumption
did not rise because tighter credit constraints limited borrowing against equity.

The practical chain is:

```text
mortgage distress
  -> modification or loss
  -> home retention and housing wealth
  -> credit access, consumption, repairs, move/stay, and security [separate]
```

The broader social insight is:

> An asset can be valuable on paper while remaining difficult to use when a
> household needs cash, flexibility, or recovery.

## What this adds to the atlas

This sharpens the housing and household-room lanes. It separates asset retention,
wealth, liquidity, current consumption, residence, and long-term security, and
it provides a counterexample to treating homeownership as automatic financial
resilience.

## Limits and next test

- This is a working paper revised in 2026 and concerns a specific Great
  Recession mortgage-distress setting.
- Near-identical pre-trends do not remove every selection or servicing concern.
- Consumption is not the whole of housing quality, health, neighborhood,
  security, bequest value, or identity.
- Lack of current consumption growth does not mean housing wealth has no future
  or non-cash value.

The next decisive artifact is a protected homeowner-year ledger joining
delinquency, modification, payment, equity, credit access, consumption, repairs,
health, neighborhood, move or stay, bequest, and later security.

# US outdoor recreation economy

This project examines outdoor recreation as both a cultural resource and a
regional economic system. It asks what national and state economic accounts
can show about the scale of recreation, employment, and place dependence—and
what they cannot show about actual access or belonging.

The source is a BEA satellite account, not a survey of participation. It
measures production, value added, compensation, and employment connected to
outdoor recreation. It therefore adds a firm/place/culture layer without
turning economic activity into proof of equal access, wellbeing, or community
benefit.

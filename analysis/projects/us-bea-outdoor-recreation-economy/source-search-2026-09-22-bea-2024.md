# Source search: outdoor recreation as cultural and regional economy

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States and all 50 states plus D.C.

## Working question

When outdoor recreation becomes visible as a large economy, what is known about
regional dependence and employment—and what remains unknown about access,
participation, and public benefit?

## Observed pattern

BEA estimates that outdoor recreation generated $696.7 billion in value added,
or 2.4% of U.S. GDP, in 2024. Employment increased in 36 states and D.C.; the
state change ranged from 4.3% in North Dakota to -4.0% in Hawaii. Outdoor
recreation's share of state GDP ranged from 6.1% in Hawaii to 1.0% in D.C.

The account also separates activity from supporting systems. Conventional
activities were 29.5% of national value added, other activities 19.0%, and
supporting activities—travel and tourism, local trips, construction, and
government expenditures—51.5%.

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

High recreation value added can reflect visitors, expensive travel, or
supporting industries rather than broad local participation. A state can have a
large recreation economy while residents face cost, transport, disability,
seasonality, land-access, or displacement barriers. This lane stops at the
economic-footprint boundary unless a later source adds participant-level access,
place-level distribution, or a measured community outcome.

# Institutional friction is route-specific, not one social mechanism

**Status:** broad-program rotation 11 · cross-domain synthesis  
**Checked:** 2026-09-16  
**Scope:** health care, public benefits, consumer recourse, household adaptation, and political meaning

## The question

When a person encounters a denial, delay, notice problem, complaint route, or
administrative burden, can it be treated as one general form of institutional
friction with one predictable social consequence?

No. The current evidence shows that friction is a family of routes. The
institution, decision, timing, remedy opportunity, and available alternative
change what the experience can mean and what outcome can follow.

```text
need, bill, eligibility, product problem, or disputed decision
  -> route-specific notice, delay, denial, application, complaint, or appeal
  -> effort, waiting, payment, substitution, or abandonment
  -> institutional response or remedy
  -> protected/sacrificed household outcome
  -> trust, attribution, action, switching, or exit
```

The atlas currently observes pieces of this chain in different populations. It
does not merge them into one friction rate or one causal estimate.

## What the route-specific evidence shows

| Route | Observed endpoint | What the result supports | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prescription affordability/access delay | In MEPS 2024, delayed-access reporters had 24.62% perceived-health improvement and 19.12% worsening, versus 20.42% and 22.11% among non-reporters | This route does not show the same health-direction pattern as authorization friction | Fill/adherence, treatment continuity, payment, household substitution, and remedy |
| Denial/prior authorization | Reporters had 19.04% improvement and 25.11% worsening, versus 20.29% and 21.74% among non-reporters | A different reported route has a different descriptive surface; improvement and worsening coexist | Claim date, appeal, insurer/provider response, treatment completion, and later recovery |
| SNAP administration | State application timeliness ranges from 61.24% to 97.22%; recertification from 25.23% to 99.87% | Administrative handling varies sharply and can affect participation without proving household security | Notice, effort, benefit amount, correction, food outcome, and respondent-level trust/action |
| SNAP lived route | In the published WBNS route evidence, interruption reporters include people reporting insufficient notice time or no notice | Eligibility or receipt does not guarantee a usable route under time and paperwork constraints | Case record, appeal, correction, amount, and later food/rent/utility outcome |
| Consumer complaint route | CFPB receipt, routing, timeliness, and response labels are observable; visibility and response mix vary by product and period | A complaint is an institutional record, not automatically a correction or recovery | Verified remedy, repeat effort, residual loss, trust, switching, and exit |
| Downstream debt remedy | Randomized medical-debt relief improves selected credit outcomes but not average health, care use, or financial wellness | A remedy can repair one currency while leaving other outcomes unchanged | Original care trigger, household substitution, provider response, and political meaning |

The main result is a boundary condition: **route labels are not mechanisms
until the route's object, clock, alternatives, and remedy are specified.**

## The counterexample that matters

The MEPS health screens are especially useful because they block two opposite
mistakes. It would be wrong to say that all institutional friction harms
health: the prescription-delay comparison has improvement in both groups and a
lower worsening share among reporters in that selected screen. It would also
be wrong to say that friction is harmless: the denial/authorization screen has
more perceived worsening among reporters, while improvement remains common.

These are not contradictory causal findings. They are different route
definitions, rounds, valid universes, and selected populations. Underlying
illness, medication need, coverage, age, employment, severity, selection, and
regression toward the mean can affect both the route report and the later
health direction.

The same discipline applies to public benefits and complaints. A benefit exit
is not recovery. A complaint closure is not verified correction. A timely
administrative rate is not a person's notice experience. A debt remedy that
improves credit access is not proof that health, care, time, or trust were
restored.

## What this adds to the broad goal

This rotation advances the broad program's institutional-mediation question by
replacing the vague phrase **institutional friction** with a route-specific
map:

```text
institutional condition
  -> object of decision (medication, authorization, benefit, account, claim)
  -> notice, timing, effort, and alternatives
  -> response and verified remedy
  -> protected/sacrificed outcome
  -> attribution, trust, action, switching, or exit
```

It also adds a currency rule: a system can improve access, credit, benefit
receipt, or complaint visibility while leaving health, food, time, dignity,
residual loss, or future room unchanged. Every claimed improvement must name
the currency and denominator it actually measures.

## Smallest decisive test

The next stronger artifact is one de-identified same-case episode ledger, not
another aggregate friction comparison. It should contain:

1. the exact need, obligation, eligibility decision, product problem, or care
   episode and its date;
2. the route object and decision-maker;
3. notice, waiting, attempted contacts, payment, alternatives, and effort;
4. response, appeal, correction, denial, partial remedy, or no remedy;
5. a protected and sacrificed outcome in money, time, health, food, housing,
   work, or care; and
6. later persistence, recovery, trust, action, switching, non-use, or exit.

The minimum comparison should retain a counterexample with a similar route but
different liquid room, coverage, schedule control, transportation, language
access, or family support. That is necessary to distinguish institutional
performance from a household's ability to absorb the problem.

## Evidence boundary

The current comparison is non-pooled and descriptive except where a cited
source supplies its own experimental or design-based result. MEPS route screens
do not share a claim key; SNAP administrative and lived-route sources do not
share a case file; CFPB records are a selected complaint population; and the
medical-debt experiments begin downstream of the original care decision. No
claim is made that one route caused one health, food, trust, or political
outcome.

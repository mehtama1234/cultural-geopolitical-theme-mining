# Source search: US student debt and delayed life choices

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

What does student debt change after school: the monthly budget, housing, work, family timing or political views?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-FED-STUDENT-2024 | [Federal Reserve higher education and student loans report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-higher-education-and-student-loans.htm) | 20% of borrowers reported being behind on a student loan payment or in collections in 2024; the figure was 30% for borrowers with some college, a technical degree or an associate degree | Official household survey | Self-reported and descriptive; being behind does not show the later effect on work or housing |
| US-NYFED-STUDENT-2026 | [New York Fed student debt data](https://www.newyorkfed.org/microeconomics/topics/student-debt) | Student loan balances were about $1.66 trillion and 10.3% of balances were 90+ days delinquent in 2026 Q1; reporting changes affect the series | Official credit-record panel | Delinquency rates are balance-based and can be distorted by re-reporting after the payment pause |
| US-NBER-LAWYERS-23453 | [NBER student debt, career and marriage choices](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23453) | A model and data for female lawyers estimate effects of student debt on career, marriage and education-quality choices | Research study | Narrow occupation and older data; results do not apply automatically to all borrowers |
| US-FED-SHED-DATA | [Federal Reserve SHED data](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed_data.htm) | Public survey files and codebooks allow subgroup and time comparisons for payment difficulty and household choices | Official data portal | Preserve question wording, weights and survey vintage |
| US-CENSUS-SIPP-2024 | [Census SIPP program](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/sipp.html) | A longitudinal survey can connect education, debt, income, wealth, health, family and work over time | Official longitudinal data | Requires careful panel construction and does not measure every loan term |

## First pattern to test

```text
education financed with debt
  -> payment, delinquency, or repayment rule
  -> housing, work, marriage, family or further-school choice
  -> wealth, status, trust, or political response
```

The Fed and New York Fed show current payment trouble and balances. NBER offers a life-choice mechanism. The missing link is a current household panel that follows payment status and the choice that changed.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Debt can finance a credential with a high return, and income-based repayment can protect some borrowers when earnings are low. A large balance is not proof of a bad outcome; compare debt with education, earnings and the repayment rule.

## Main gaps

- current payment trouble by debt size, income, education and place;
- housing, marriage, work and family timing for the same borrower;
- public-service work and repayment-plan choices;
- effects of re-reporting and administrative changes on delinquency measures;
- whether debt changes political views beyond policy preference.

## Decision rule

Pair a balance or payment measure with one observed life-choice outcome for the same borrower or panel period. Do not use delinquency alone as a measure of the debt's social effect.

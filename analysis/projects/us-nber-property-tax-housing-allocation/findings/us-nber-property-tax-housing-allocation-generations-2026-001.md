# Finding: housing tax design can trade older-owner lock-in against younger access

**Status:** bounded modeled housing-allocation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35587 studies property-tax design in an overlapping-
generations model with financially constrained households. The model says low
property taxes can amplify lock-in among older homeowners and limit access for
younger families. Raising taxes can lower capitalized prices and required
downpayments for constrained young buyers, while raising holding costs for
older owners.

In the paper's California-to-Texas counterfactual, raising the modeled property
tax rate from 0.8% to 2% lowers house prices by 12.6%, reduces homeownership
among people age 75 and older by 7.6 percentage points, and raises modeled
homeownership among people age 25–34. Removing step-up basis also lowers older
homeownership, suggesting that bequest-tax treatment contributes to tenure
lock-in in the model.

The atlas-relevant insight is:

> Housing affordability is not only a price level. Tax design can redistribute
> downpayment requirements, holding costs, and the ability to remain or enter
> across generations.

## Limits and next test

- These are model counterfactuals, not observed effects of a California reform.
- Results depend on assumptions about supply, capitalization, financial
  constraints, bequests, mobility, exemptions, and public services.
- The evidence does not establish displacement, rental burden, neighborhood
  change, household wealth, service quality, or political response.
- The next test is a state-and-local ledger joining tax rules, exemptions,
  assessment, prices, downpayments, tenure transitions, rents, moves, family
  transfers, and local service changes by age and wealth.

# Finding 001: Better survey contact can raise participation without ending response decline

**Status:** bounded Census institutional-participation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's 2026 review of ACS contact-strategy research finds that
some specific design changes improved self-response: a sequential questionnaire
was followed by a 1.2 percentage-point increase in 2008, adding internet
response was followed by a 1.3-point increase in 2013, and a 2015 mailing change
was followed by a 1.1-point increase. A 2022 pressure-seal mailing test had a
neutral overall effect. The report also says self-response continued to decline
over the longer period despite repeated changes.

```text
public survey request
  -> message, mode, layout, trust, burden, and access
  -> response or nonresponse
  -> data quality and cost of follow-up
  -> [open] equal representation, public use, policy feedback, and trust
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a public-systems version of the program's central interface pattern.
The government can learn how to make a formal participation route more usable,
and particular changes can work, while the broader social conditions behind
nonresponse remain unsettled. The result is neither “the survey works” nor
“people are simply disengaged.” Participation is shaped by design, time,
language, digital access, privacy, legal obligation, and trust.

## Limits and next test

- The report summarizes many studies and aggregate response comparisons rather
  than one household-level causal chain.
- Response is not the same as accurate completion, equal representation, or
  trust in government.
- Some years were affected by shutdowns or the pandemic.
- The report excludes Puerto Rico from its contact-strategy discussion.

The next decisive test is a protected address or household panel joining
contact received, mode access, burden, language, privacy concern, response,
item completion, follow-up cost, weighting adjustment, and later use of the
result in a named public decision.

# Finding 001: Federal IT modernization has a realized-savings stage before public value is known

**Status:** bounded U.S. public-capacity and technology-realization finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed 68 Technology Modernization Fund projects with about $1.03
billion invested as of June 2025. Eleven projects had reported $13.5 million
in savings, while 24 expected $1.06 billion in combined savings. Many projects
anticipated future savings; some had not saved money and did not expect to.

The evidence therefore separates three stages:

```text
modernization investment
  -> project implementation
  -> reported realized savings
  -> [open] agency capacity, service quality, user time, access, and public value
```

The projection-to-realization gap is itself important. A funded project or
large modeled saving is not the same as a realized budget reduction, a usable
public service, or a benefit that reaches households.

## What it adds to the atlas

This is a named federal technology implementation route in the infrastructure
and public-systems lanes. It complements records that stop at appropriations,
deployment, or expected capacity by adding a limited reported-realization
stage. It also creates a sharper audit question: who receives the saved money,
time, reliability, or access, and who bears migration, training, procurement,
or service interruption costs while the system changes?

## What the source does not show

- It does not estimate causal savings or agency productivity.
- It does not show whether savings became available for other public uses.
- It does not identify user-level service quality, access, wait time, or equity.
- It does not measure worker workload, training burden, cybersecurity, or
  public trust.
- Projected savings are not realized savings, and realized savings are not
  automatically household benefit.

## Next decisive test

Follow a named project through implementation milestones, contract changes,
training, migration, realized cost and time effects, service reliability,
user access, incident/correction, and later agency or household outcomes.
Preserve project-level accounting, implementation dates, and the difference
between avoided cost, cash saving, and reallocated capacity.

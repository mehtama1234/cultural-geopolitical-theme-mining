# Claims ledger: US public aid and the shape of a dollar

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; cash and food aid, timing, spending, debt, housing, and household security

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Aid form and timing change short-run spending even when value is similar. | High-frequency household data | NBER 35698 finds larger food-store spending responses for in-kind and recurring aid than for cash and one-time aid. | The study concerns pandemic-era transfers and short-run food-store spending. |
| C-002 | Recurring cash can raise spending on basic needs and mobility. | US randomized experiment | NBER 32784 finds monthly cash increased spending, largest for housing, food, and car expenses, and increased housing and neighborhood mobility. | The study tested a large, time-limited transfer for young low-income adults in two states. |
| C-003 | More spending does not necessarily mean a stronger balance sheet. | Credit and asset records | The cash experiment found higher debt offset modest asset gains, leaving near-zero average net-worth change. | Net worth is not the only measure of security or choice. |
| C-004 | Labeled aid can protect a named need while cash can cover needs a program designer cannot see. | Design comparison | The spending differences are consistent with labeling and mental accounting; cash gives households broader choice. | The evidence does not rank one form as best for every family. |
| C-005 | Transfer design decides what relief arrives now and what risk remains later. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 connect form, timing, spending, mobility, debt, and financial health. | Food security, care, trust, and long-run security remain open. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
public need
  -> aid amount, form, and timing
  -> what the household can buy or postpone now
  -> food, housing, transport, debt, care, or savings choice
  -> short-run relief or lasting security
  -> trust in the program and demand for the next policy
```

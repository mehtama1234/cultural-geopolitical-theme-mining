# Finding 001: Better loan information can improve bargaining while increasing borrowing

**Status:** bounded comparative consumer-credit and information finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER randomized trial with 112,063 loan seekers, conducted with Chile's
financial regulator, tested what happens when people are asked about interest
rates or shown a price-comparison tool built from administrative loan data.

Most participants initially underestimated both interest-rate levels and how
widely rates varied. Eliciting beliefs led people to search more and obtain
lower rates. The comparison tool updated beliefs, increased negotiation,
produced lower rates without more searching, and made people more likely to
take out a loan.

The route is:

```text
limited knowledge of loan prices
  -> belief update or comparison information
  -> search and negotiation
  -> lower rate and greater loan take-up
  -> [open] total cost, repayment, and household security
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an information-and-bargaining version of a recurring pattern:

> Information can give consumers more negotiating power, but the same tool can
> also increase participation in the market being negotiated.

So a lower interest rate is not automatically a lower financial burden. The
loan may be larger, longer, fee-bearing, or harder to repay. Consumer power and
consumer exposure can rise together.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence comes from Chile, not the United States.
- The public abstract does not give treatment effect sizes, product details,
  follow-up length, or repayment outcomes.
- Lower rates do not establish lower total cost if maturity, fees, or loan size
  change.
- More borrowing may help smooth a crisis or may increase later distress.

The next decisive test is a protected borrower ledger joining initial beliefs,
comparison exposure, offers, negotiation, rate, fees, maturity, loan size,
repayment, delinquency, refinancing, and later household room. Until then, the
safe claim is that usable price information can change bargaining and borrowing
at the same time.

## Source

- [NBER Working Paper 35605: Search and Negotiation with Biased Beliefs in Consumer Credit Markets](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35605)

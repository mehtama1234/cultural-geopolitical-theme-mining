# Finding 001: State budgets can keep growing while quietly using adjustment tools

**Status:** bounded state-capacity and public-systems finding · **Checked: 2026-09-22**

## The bounded finding

The National Association of State Budget Officers' Spring 2026 Fiscal Survey
shows a mixed state-budget picture. Governors' proposed fiscal 2027 general
fund budgets increased a median **0.6%** from estimated fiscal 2026 levels,
while fiscal 2026 spending was estimated at **3.9% above** original enacted
budgets. The survey says 22 states reported targeted spending cuts, 14
reported eliminating vacant positions, and 14 reported using revenue
increases.

Revenue was not collapsing everywhere: 29 states said fiscal 2026 revenue was
above its original forecast, nine were on target, and 11 were below forecast.
But the proposed budgets also projected fiscal 2027 revenue only **2.5% above**
current fiscal 2026 estimates. Most states expected to keep or increase rainy
day balances in dollar terms, while total balances were projected to decline
as states spent down prior surplus funds, mainly for one-time purposes.

The practical route is:

```text
revenue and prior surplus
  -> reserves, one-time spending, cuts, vacancies, and tax changes
  -> agency staffing, program timing, eligibility, and service route
  -> [open] resident/worker/firm access, burden, trust, and political response
```

The broader insight is that a state can look fiscally stable in aggregate
while adjusting underneath the headline budget. A small top-line increase can
coexist with targeted cuts or fewer filled positions. A reserve balance can
preserve room for a response without proving that a particular service was
protected or usable.

## Limits and counterexamples

- NASBO reports state-budget-office information and proposed fiscal 2027
  budgets; proposals are not the same as enacted policy or implementation.
- General-fund totals do not show local-government capacity, federal funds,
  debt, earmarks, or the legal availability of each balance.
- A targeted cut or vacant-position reduction does not identify which service,
  place, worker, or household bears the effect.
- Spending down a surplus can fund a planned one-time investment rather than
  signal immediate service deterioration.
- Revenue above forecast does not establish affordability, program quality, or
  public approval.

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a different layer from household financial room and from a program's
formal eligibility. It measures the institutional room in which states can
choose to absorb, delay, shift, or transmit a pressure. It strengthens the
recurring theme that capacity and lived protection run on different clocks:
the budget can remain positive while staffing, access, or local delivery
changes.

It also supplies a useful counterexample to simple fiscal-crisis stories:
many states still reported revenue above original forecasts and retained
substantial reserves. The evidence supports uneven adjustment under continued
fiscal room, not uniform collapse.

## Next test

Join state fiscal years to a named service or shock: enacted budget and
implementation date, agency staffing and processing time, eligibility or
notice, resident/worker/firm exposure, completed service, and later trust or
political action. The comparison should include high-reserve states with poor
access and lower-reserve states that protect a service through other funding.

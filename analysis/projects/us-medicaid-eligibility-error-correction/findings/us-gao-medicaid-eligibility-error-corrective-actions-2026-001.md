# Finding: benefit-system correction is not the same as a closed feedback loop

**Status:** bounded Medicaid administration and oversight finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-108017 reviews Medicaid eligibility errors across national reporting and
examines corrective-action plans from seven selected states for 2019–2024.
The selected states used responses such as caseworker training, guidance, and
updates to eligibility systems.

GAO found that CMS feedback did not ensure that state corrective-action plans
consistently included all required elements. GAO also found that CMS did not
systematically analyze errors and corrective actions across states and years to
determine which interventions worked or whether they could transfer.

The public-system insight is:

> An administrative system can acknowledge error and launch correction without
> producing a durable learning loop. The missing middle is evidence that a
> correction changed the determination, continuity, burden, or security of an
> actual beneficiary.

## Limits and next test

- Seven selected states are not a national estimate of corrective-action
  quality.
- Error rates and corrective-action plans do not show individual coverage loss
  or restoration.
- Training, guidance, and system updates are response categories, not verified
  effects.
- The report does not measure beneficiary time, health, cost, trust, or exit.

The next decisive artifact is a state–case–correction ledger joining error type,
notice, correction date, coverage continuity, application or renewal burden,
health-care use, and beneficiary appeal or trust outcomes.

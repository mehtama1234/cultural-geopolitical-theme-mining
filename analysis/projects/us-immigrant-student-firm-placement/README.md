# Project: Immigrant students and firm placement

This project tracks how immigrant students move from U.S. college into the
labor market. A Census working paper follows college students from 2000 through
2020 and reports disproportionate placement at both ends of the firm-size
distribution: large firms, especially multinationals, and small firms or
self-employment. Degree fields predict large-firm and multinational placement;
ethnic specialization helps explain small-firm sorting. Immigrant students who
remain in the United States earn more than native peers in the study.

The safe theme is not “immigrants are blocked” or “immigrants are more
productive.” It is that education can feed into sharply different firm routes,
shaped by geography, degrees, firm capabilities and visas, and ethnic
self-employment specialization. The project must keep sorting, selection,
legal status, opportunity, earnings, firm size, worker power, and household
security separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected student-to-job panel joining origin,
degree, geography, visa or work authorization, networks, applications, offers,
firm size, multinational status, self-employment, earnings, job changes,
household resources, and later stay or departure. The public abstract does not
provide that complete bridge.

# Finding 001: Immigrant college students sort toward both very large and very small firms

**Status:** bounded immigrant mobility, firm placement, and unequal-opportunity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper links Census and longitudinal employment data
to trace jobs taken by college students in 2000 through 2020. It reports that
immigrant students were disproportionately employed in either large firms—
especially multinationals—or small firms and self-employment.

The study evaluates four routes into that split: geographic clustering, degree
specialization, firm capabilities and visas, and ethnic self-employment
specialization. Degree fields predict large-firm and multinational placement;
ethnic specialization explains small-firm sorting. Immigrant students who
remain in the United States earn more than native peers in the study.

The practical route is:

```text
U.S. college and immigrant background
  -> degree, geography, visa/firm capability, and network route
  -> large multinational or small/self-employed firm
  -> earnings and career path
  -> [open] worker power, household security, belonging, and stay/exit choice
```

This is a sorting pattern, not a complete judgment about whether the sorting
represents opportunity, constraint, or both.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

The finding adds an identity-and-firm version of the atlas's unequal-optionality
theme. The same educational system can route people into very different
organizational scales. Large multinational placement may offer capabilities or
earnings; small-firm and self-employment placement may reflect specialization,
opportunity, constraint, or a combination.

It also keeps migration and selection visible. The higher earnings result is
for immigrant students who remain in the United States, not all immigrant
students, and therefore cannot by itself distinguish opportunity from the
selection of who stays.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper, and the public abstract does not provide the full
  sample, immigrant definition, visa categories, firm-size thresholds, or
  earnings estimates.
- Firm sorting does not establish blocked opportunity, equal treatment, or
  causal effects of visas, degrees, geography, or ethnicity.
- Higher earnings among those who stay may reflect selection, occupation,
  location, networks, or unobserved resources.
- Small-firm or self-employment sorting can be opportunity, constraint, or both.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected student-to-job panel linking origin,
degree, geography, visa or work authorization, networks, applications, offers,
firm size, multinational status, self-employment, earnings, job changes,
household resources, and later stay or departure. Until then, the safe claim is
that immigrant college students sort into distinct firm routes with different
possible meanings.

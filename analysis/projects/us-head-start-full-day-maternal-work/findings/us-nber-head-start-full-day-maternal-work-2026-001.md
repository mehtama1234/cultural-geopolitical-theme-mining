# Finding 001: More full-day Head Start was followed by higher maternal work outcomes

**Status:** bounded U.S. childcare and labor-market finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34831 studies a 2016 federal funding initiative that targeted
Head Start grantees with low full-day enrollment. Linking administrative program
data, center locations, and household employment data, the difference-in-
differences estimates report a 19% increase in full-day enrollment and increases
among single mothers of 2.5% in employment, 3.2% in full-time employment, 2.9%
in hours, and 7% in earnings.

```text
targeted full-day funding
  -> formal childcare hours and enrollment
  -> maternal work availability and hours
  -> earnings and household room
  -> [open] care quality, child outcome, wellbeing, durability, and political meaning
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This provides a policy-capacity counterpoint to the wildfire-smoke caregiving
finding: formal childcare can be present yet intermittently fail when schools
close, while a targeted increase in full-day capacity can improve measured work
outcomes. The result makes the missing middle more specific—hours and enrollment
are not merely program counts, but still do not prove that every household got a
usable slot or that work gains translated into wellbeing.

## Limits and next test

- The policy targeted low-full-day-enrollment grantees, so this is not a random
  national childcare expansion.
- The abstract does not provide the full analytic denominator or all subgroup
  estimates.
- Employment, hours, and earnings are not care quality, child wellbeing,
  schedule control, or maternal time satisfaction.
- The result does not establish equal access, transport feasibility, fees,
  waitlists, persistence, or household-level recovery.

The next decisive test is a linked mother-child-center panel joining eligibility,
slot offer and use, hours, fees, travel, child attendance and wellbeing,
maternal work schedule, earnings, care substitution, health, and later stability
or exit.

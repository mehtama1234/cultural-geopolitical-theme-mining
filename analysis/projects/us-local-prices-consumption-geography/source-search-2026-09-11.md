# Source search: US local prices and the geography of consumption

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; local prices and consumption are measured by income and skill group, the later move and household-choice path remains open

## Working question

How much does a family's real standard of living depend on where it lives, even when its income looks the same on paper?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-LOCAL-PRICES | [Where is Standard of Living the Highest? Local Prices and the Geography of Consumption](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29533) | Using household consumption data covering about 5% of the US population and local prices for more than 140 products, the authors find that price differences between commuting zones are especially large for low-income households. The spatial spread of their price indexes is almost twice the spread for high-income households. | NBER working paper | The opening result does not show why households stay, move, change stores, change products, or experience the local price as a better or worse life. |

## First pattern to test

```text
place
  -> local price and service mix
  -> real buying power
  -> product, housing or moving choice
  -> household security and political judgment
```

The source supports unequal local price exposure. It does not establish the full household or political path.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

High prices may come with better jobs, services or amenities, and a low price may reflect fewer choices. A price index alone is not a complete measure of a place's value.

## Main gaps

- the same family's income, prices and purchases;
- housing, transport, health and food prices together;
- service quality and product availability;
- moving costs and job access;
- effects by race, age, disability and family type;
- local political response.

## Decision rule

Keep local price, real consumption, place quality, moving choice and household well-being as separate claims until a source joins them.

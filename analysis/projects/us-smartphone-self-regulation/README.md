# Project: Smartphone self-regulation and reversible exit

## Question

When people feel that smartphone use is excessive, does a cutback become real
and durable behavior change, or does utility, social pressure, work, and
platform substitution pull them back?

## Working chain

`utility and habitual use -> perceived excess -> cutback attempt -> app/device
restriction or selective non-use -> observed use and substitution -> sleep,
time, connection, privacy, and wellbeing -> durable change or return`

## Relationship to existing research

The digital-habits project already records Pew's smartphone self-regulation
layer. This companion lane isolates the missing behavioral endpoint: the survey
reports perceived success, while the qualitative and public-discussion layers
test reversibility, substitution, social cost, and what users count as success.

## Next test

Use a repeated-use panel or privacy-preserving device log linking app-level
exposure, notifications, work/school and care obligations, cutback tactics,
observed behavior, sleep/time displacement, privacy choices, substitution, and
later persistence. Keep perceived success separate from logged behavior.

# Source search: US rising income risk at the top

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; income risk and saving responses are measured, the household and political spillovers remain open

## Working question

What happens to the wider economy when high earners face more income risk and respond by saving more?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-INCOME-RISK-TOP | [Rising Income Risk at the Top](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35664) | Using newly digitized US tax returns from 1969 to 2019, the authors find that permanent income risk rose across the distribution and nearly 70% among the top 5%. Large negative income shocks predict financial distress even among top earners. In a life-cycle model, higher risk at the top raises saving, wealth inequality and the modeled risk-free rate effect is a 0.7 percentage-point reduction. | NBER working paper | The model does not show how these changes reach consumers, workers, borrowers or voters, and the tax records do not explain each saving decision. |

## First pattern to test

```text
income risk at the top
  -> precautionary saving
  -> wealth and interest-rate change
  -> credit, asset and demand conditions
  -> different room for households lower down
```

The source supports rising risk, distress and modeled saving and rate effects. It does not establish the full distributional or political path.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Higher saving can provide investment funds and a cushion against shocks. The model's wealth and rate effects depend on its assumptions and do not say that every high earner or lower-income household experiences the same result.

## Main gaps

- which assets receive the extra saving;
- who bears changes in borrowing costs;
- consumer demand and business investment;
- labor-market and wage effects;
- differences within the top group;
- political response to wider wealth gaps.

## Decision rule

Keep top-income risk, saving, wealth, interest rates and lower-income household outcomes as separate claims until a source links them.

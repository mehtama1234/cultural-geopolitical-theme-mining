# Finding 001: Services exports can help multinational firms keep goods sales moving during a global crisis

**Status:** bounded globalization, firm-capacity, and geopolitical-economic finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper compares U.S. multinational goods exporters
during the 2008–2009 global financial crisis. Firms that also exported services
to the same destination had higher goods-export growth than firms that exported
goods only.

The reported difference was driven by sales of intellectual-property rights
related to industrial processes, such as patents and trademarks. The same
bi-exporters also had higher growth in foreign-affiliate services sales and
domestic employment in services sectors.

The practical route is:

```text
global crisis
  -> goods and services exposure to the same destination
  -> intellectual property and firm capability channel
  -> foreign goods sales and affiliate services
  -> domestic services employment
  -> [open] wages, worker power, local benefit, and national resilience
```

This makes cross-border services a possible firm-capacity channel, not proof of
general crisis immunity.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

The finding adds a geopolitical-economic version of the atlas's capacity-versus-
dependence tension. A firm may be more capable because it can combine goods,
services, intellectual property, and foreign affiliates. That same structure can
also create dependence on foreign customers, legal regimes, intellectual-
property systems, and cross-border demand.

It also connects firm resilience to domestic work without collapsing the two.
Higher domestic services employment at bi-exporters is a real reported route,
but it does not establish who got those jobs, at what pay, or whether local
communities became more secure.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper focused on multinational exporters during one crisis.
- Bi-exporters may differ from goods-only exporters in capability, technology,
  management, market power, or pre-crisis strength.
- Intellectual-property sales may be a marker of capability rather than the
  only mechanism.
- Higher domestic services employment does not prove higher wages, worker power,
  net employment, or broad local benefit.
- The result does not establish national resilience or strategic autonomy.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected firm-destination crisis panel joining
goods and services sales, intellectual property, affiliates, customers, prices,
employment, wages, investment, supply chains, and later recovery. Until then,
the safe claim is that combined cross-border goods-and-services activity was
associated with stronger export adjustment for these multinationals in this
crisis.

# Project: Services trade and multinational resilience

This project tracks how firms with multiple kinds of cross-border activity
absorb a global crisis. A Census working paper compares U.S. multinationals that
exported only goods with those that also exported services to the same
destination during the 2008–2009 financial crisis. The bi-exporters had higher
goods-export growth, driven by industrial-process intellectual-property sales;
they also had higher foreign-affiliate services sales and domestic services
employment growth.

The safe theme is not “multinationals are resilient” or “services trade saves
jobs.” It is that goods and services can reinforce each other across borders,
and that intellectual property and firm capabilities may become crisis-response
infrastructure. The project must keep export growth, foreign affiliate sales,
domestic employment, worker outcomes, consumer welfare, and national resilience
separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected firm-destination crisis panel joining
goods and services sales, intellectual property, affiliates, customers, prices,
employment, wages, investment, supply chains, and later recovery. The public
abstract does not provide that complete bridge.

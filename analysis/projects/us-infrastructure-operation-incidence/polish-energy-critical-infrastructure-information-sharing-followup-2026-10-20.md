# Polish energy-sector critical-infrastructure information-sharing follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** sectoral coordination intent and planned analytic mechanism; implementation and incident outcomes remain open

## Observed route

The Polish Ministry of State Assets reports that ORLEN, PGE, Enea, Tauron, and
KGHM signed an intent agreement on October 7, 2026 to cooperate on critical-
infrastructure security and create a sectoral mechanism for exchanging
information about threats, vulnerabilities, and incidents.

The ministry says the initiative responds to cyber, physical, insider, and
hybrid threats. It plans a sectoral information-sharing platform,
“CyberEnergyInfo24,” and an analytical center for threat analysis and risk
modeling. The stated rationale is faster detection, shared standards,
situational awareness, and coordinated response across strategic companies.

## Stage boundary

| Stage | What is public | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | Five named strategic companies and a signed intent agreement | Binding obligations, funding, governance, membership, and accountability |
| Information mechanism | Planned CyberEnergyInfo24 platform and analytical center | Launch, data-sharing rules, interoperability, staffing, and actual use |
| Security response | Stated goals of faster threat exchange, common standards, and coordinated response | Incident detection, response time, avoided disruption, exercises, and audit results |
| Public conversion | Ministry links infrastructure security to economic and everyday citizen security | Reliability, outage exposure, price effects, firm continuity, and distributional outcomes |

This is a planned firm-sector coordination mechanism, not evidence that the
platform exists, improved detection, or prevented an infrastructure incident.

# Harmony Link cross-border energy-security follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** technical interconnector agreement and joint-response framework; construction, operation, and household effects remain open

## Observed route

The Polish government reports that Poland's PSE and Lithuania's Litgrid signed
an agreement governing the technical aspects of the Harmony Link interconnector
at the Polish-Lithuanian border. The governments describe Harmony Link as a
planned second electricity connection intended to increase system security and
energy exchange between the Baltic states and Continental Europe.

The same meeting produced a joint statement on energy-infrastructure security
and mutual assistance. The public account says the framework moves beyond
information exchange toward rapid deployment of personnel, equipment, spare
parts, and technical support during serious failures or crises, with joint
exercises, threat information, physical protection, and cybersecurity.

## Stage boundary

| Stage | What is public | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Governance | PSE–Litgrid technical agreement and shared implementation responsibility | Detailed technical milestones, permits, financing, procurement, and construction schedule |
| Physical interconnection | Planned second Poland–Lithuania connection | Completion, commissioning, transfer capacity, reliability, and outage performance |
| Crisis response | Mutual-assistance framework covering people, equipment, parts, technical support, exercises, physical protection, and cyber | Exercise results, mobilization time, real incident response, and resource sufficiency |
| Household/firm conversion | Official claim of more reliable supply and better conditions for competitive prices | Actual prices, interruptions, renewable integration, firm costs, and distributional effects |

This is a governance and implementation stage, not proof that Harmony Link is
operational or that households already received more reliable or cheaper power.

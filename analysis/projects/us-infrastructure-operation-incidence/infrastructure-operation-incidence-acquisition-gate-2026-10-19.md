# Infrastructure operation-to-incidence acquisition gate

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded acquisition audit; no named route yet qualifies from operation through incidence and durable legitimacy

## Question

Can one identifiable infrastructure project, facility, customer class, or
operating incident be followed from realized operation through the burden or
benefit experienced by an affected unit, a usable mitigation or alternative,
and later public or external response?

The required chain is:

```text
named project, facility, route, or incident
  -> dated operation, dispatch, outage, or disruption
  -> affected customer/place/service and money, time, health, land, water,
     labor, or reliability incidence
  -> mitigation, compensation, replacement, alternative, or contestation
  -> later acceptance, reversal, conflict, or external dependence response
```

## Result

The current records establish real operation and governance response, but they
do not join the same project or incident to a compatible affected unit and later
response. Capacity, system flows, modeled bills, announced credits, toll
revenue, and opinion measures remain distinct stages. The safe finding is a
stage boundary, not that any candidate is beneficial or harmful.

The PJM follow-up is the strongest operating-stage improvement in this pass:
it supplies a mechanism, timing, magnitude decomposition, system recovery
measure, historical comparison, and a more precise physical boundary. The
primary presentation remains preliminary and still stops before individual
facility identity, backup cost/emissions, household service, or local
legitimacy, so it does not qualify the full route.

## Virginia regulatory follow-up — 2026-10-19

The Virginia regulatory route has advanced from a modeled allocation to more
specific payment and collateral machinery. The [SCC facts
page](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/about-the-scc/scc-facts/) says large-load
customers, including hyperscale data centers, will be obligated to pay at
least 85% of the transmission and distribution costs incurred to serve them
each month; qualifying new large-load customers may also have to guarantee
funds covering up to 60% of minimum charges over the contract term. The [SCC
tax instructions](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/regulated-industries/public-taxation/taxes-and-fees/)
specify registration and monthly remittance for the data-center electricity
consumption tax, while the [enacted budget
item](https://budget.lis.virginia.gov/item/2026/2/HB30/Enrolled/3/3-5.24/)
sets the first collection in September 2026 and includes taxes owed from July
1 onward.

This closes more of the formal obligation and administrative-route arrows. It
does not establish a named facility's billed amount, collateral posting, tax
remittance, customer-class recovery, residential credit posting or receipt,
net household burden, reliability effect, or local acceptance. The route
therefore remains a governance/payment mechanism, not realized incidence.

The same bounded search found the [SCC monthly-report
form](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/regulated-industries/public-taxation/forms/)
and the applicable due-date instructions, but no public filed return, monthly
remittance amount, facility-level payment record, or posted residential credit.
That is a public-record acquisition gap as of this check, not evidence that no
tax was collected or no customer was credited. The next qualifying request
must therefore target a filed report, SCC docket exhibit, utility bill tariff
sheet, or customer-facing credit record with a date and denominator.

## Named large-load docket follow-up — 2026-10-19

The SCC docket search adds identifiable institutional actors without closing the
incidence route. In Dominion's [PUR-2026-00011 large-load connection-queue
proceeding](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch/DOCS/8%40%23101%21.PDF),
Dominion filed an application for approval of its queue-process standards on
February 2, 2026. [Google's notice of
participation](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch/DOCS/8%40%24z01%21.PDF)
states that Google owns and operates data centers and related infrastructure in
Virginia; [Microsoft's notice](https://www.scc.virginia.gov/docketsearch/DOCS/8%40jk01%21.PDF)
states that it operates substantial Virginia data-center infrastructure and
plans expansion. The SCC's current facts page identifies the supplemental
line-extension docket as PUR-2026-00131 and says Dominion's amendments are to
be filed there.

This narrows the next acquisition target from an unnamed “large load” to named
operators and docketed connection/line-extension decisions. It still does not
provide a facility identifier, energization date, metered consumption,
85%-minimum-charge payment, collateral posting, tax remittance, household bill
posting, reliability effect, or local acceptance. Participation in a docket is
not proof of a project receiving service or paying a particular amount.

## CHPE operating follow-up — 2026-10-19

An independent market-data analysis adds a more detailed operating record than
the public milestone and EIA summary. [Modo Energy's first-summer
analysis](https://modoenergy.com/research/es/chpe-market-impact-first-summer-nyiso)
reports a cable-splice outage that removed CHPE for 20 full operational days
after July 4, zero output in 83% of five-minute intervals during June–August,
an average running level of 940 MW against the 1,250 MW rating, and a 13%
first-summer capacity factor versus a 95% contract expectation. It describes
an afternoon/evening operating block and reports a lower Zone J day-ahead
four-hour spread premium on 85–90°F days when CHPE ran.

This advances the operation arrow toward availability, interruption, and a
conditional wholesale-market mechanism. It does not by itself establish
causal retail-price savings, household bill changes, reliability protection,
route-community burden or acceptance, or compensation after the outage. The
analysis is a secondary market account, and the underlying five-minute series
and counterfactual construction should be retained for reproduction before
promoting the price comparison as a causal estimate.

The NYISO public Long Term ATC/TTC preschedule adds a later bounded operating
checkpoint: the HQ-CHPE interface is listed at 1,250 MW through October 11,
zero from October 12 through October 16, and 1,250 MW again from October 17
through October 30. This is a planning/transfer-capability sequence, not a
metered-delivery or customer-outage record. It advances the chronology from
operator-reported restoration toward a dated availability window while leaving
the cause of the zero values, actual dispatch, retail bill effect, customer
reliability, and route-community response open. The public page was available
through the indexed HTML view but returned 404 on direct retrieval at recheck;
that access boundary is retained rather than treated as proof of nonoperation.

## NYC microhub operating-conversion checkpoint — 2026-10-19

The new [NYC DOT Microhubs Pilot record](../../records/us-nyc-microhubs-last-mile-delivery-operation-2026.json)
adds a distinct infrastructure-to-alternative stage. NYC DOT reports that the
first-year pilot replaced more than 3,000 truck trips, delivered about 860
packages per day by handcart and 110 by cargo bike, replaced 1.5 and 3 daily
truck vehicle miles respectively, and estimated nine grams of toxic-air-
pollution reduction. Two additional Manhattan microhub locations were added,
and the agency sought new business partners for the next phase.

This is evidence of realized operational substitution, not a complete benefit
claim. The release does not identify recipient-level service quality, worker
time or pay, neighborhood noise or safety exposure, operating cost, or durable
community acceptance. The route therefore advances from infrastructure
operation to a measured alternative mode while leaving incidence and later
legitimacy open.

Source: [NYC DOT Microhubs Pilot results](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/microhubs-reduced-truck-trips.shtml).

The strongest next route is a lawful CHPE or PJM operating bundle if it exposes
project-level hourly availability or dispatch together with customer-class
allocation, service/reliability, or host-place evidence. If those records are
not public, the non-observation should be retained rather than replaced by a
second forecast, modeled charge, or national opinion endpoint.

## CHPE delivery-to-payment control — 2026-10-19

NYSERDA's official [Tier 4 program page](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/Large-Scale-Renewables/Tier-Four)
adds a more precise administrative middle stage. It says that eligible
generation, scheduling, and delivery into NYISO Zone J must be independently
verified on an hourly basis before a Tier 4 renewable-energy certificate is
minted; NYSERDA then issues payment to Hydro-Québec after receiving the
certificates. This is stronger than an announcement-only payment promise: it
specifies the unit of verification and the event sequence that gates contract
payment.

The control still stops at the project and contract layer. It does not disclose
the verified hourly series, payment amount, customer-class allocation, retail
bill effect, household receipt, route-community burden, or later acceptance.
The new stage is therefore coded as **reported administrative delivery/payment
control**, not customer benefit or realized affordability.

NYSERDA's current [Tier 4 voluntary-sales
page](https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/Clean-Energy-Standard/Voluntary-REC-Sales/Tier-4-REC-Voluntary-Sales)
adds process timing around the same verified REC unit: a 14-day sale window,
pro-rata allocation when demand exceeds supply, invoices within seven business
days, and payment due within 30 calendar days. This is a more specific
administrative payment route, but it still does not show a completed sale,
buyer-level payment, retail bill effect, household receipt, or route-community
outcome.

## Smallest qualifying acquisition

Prioritize one source bundle containing:

1. a stable project, facility, tariff-area, route, or incident identifier;
2. dated metered output, dispatch, outage, traffic, or service observation;
3. an affected customer class, host place, worker group, or ecological unit;
4. observed bill, tax, toll, reliability, environmental, land, water, labor, or
   service consequence;
5. a feasible mitigation, compensation, replacement, alternative, or
   contestation route; and
6. a later measure of receipt, adequacy, acceptance, conflict, reversal,
   replacement, or external dependence.

The unit and clock must remain compatible. A system-wide import total cannot be
used as a household bill; a proposed credit cannot be used as receipt; and a
poll cannot be used as local consent without an exposure and participation
link.

## Rūdninkai host-nation infrastructure checkpoint — 2026-10-20

The [Lithuanian Ministry of National Defence reports](https://kam.lt/en/lithuania-completes-construction-of-residential-blocks-for-german-brigade-at-rudninkai-military-campus/)
that construction of residential blocks for German Brigade personnel at the
Rūdninkai Training Area was completed on October 5, 2026. The ministry places
the completed housing within Phase 1 of the larger Rūdninkai Military Campus,
whose later stages include service, training, equipment-maintenance, medical,
logistics, and other facilities intended to support the brigade's full
operation in Lithuania by the end of 2027.

This is a new named host-infrastructure realization stage: physical delivery
of accommodation is now distinguished from the earlier stationing plan and
from the future full-operational-capability claim. It does not establish
occupancy, German troop arrival or use of the completed blocks, Lithuanian
construction cost incidence, local traffic/noise/land burden, service quality,
local consent, or whether the brigade reaches the stated capability milestone.
The facility is therefore a **completed host-support asset with use and
legitimacy still unobserved**, not evidence of realized deterrence or local
acceptance.

The next lawful follow-up is a dated occupancy or handover record paired with
campus operating cost, local-service/traffic evidence, and a host-community or
municipal response. The ministry's statement that the project is progressing
does not substitute for those affected-place measures.

The ministry's [Rūdninkai campus FAQ](https://kam.lt/en/faq/rudninkai-military-campus/)
adds the financing and scale boundary: the campus is planned across 190
hectares for about 5,000 German-Brigade soldiers and civilians; state
commitments total €1.8 billion including 12.5 years of maintenance, and the
European Investment Bank provided a €540 million loan. The ministry's broader
[German-Brigade location page](https://kam.lt/en/german-brigade-in-lithuania/)
states that initial elements are currently serving in Vilnius and that troops
arriving in 2025–2026 use Lithuanian facilities near Vilnius, including
Nemenčinė and Rokantiškės, while Rūdninkai remains the future main location.
It also reports 81 German-Brigade families and 155 children currently residing
in Lithuania, without assigning them to Rūdninkai.

This closes a sharper cost-and-use distinction: national financing and a
broader host-country presence are observable, but Rūdninkai-specific occupancy,
maintenance spending, local-service demand, transport burden, and host-place
acceptance remain unmeasured. The current personnel and family figures must
not be used as Rūdninkai residents.

## Rūdninkai access and operating-obligation checkpoint — 2026-10-20

The Ministry of National Defence reports that, in coordination with the
transport ministry, Lithuania planned rehabilitation of 34 km of road sections
near the Rūdninkai training area, including the Mikašiūnai–Rūdninkai and
Ąžuolijai–Juodšiliai–Jašiūnai routes. This is a named host-region access
intervention rather than a generic claim that the campus will have roads. It
establishes planned physical connectivity work, but the cited announcement does
not provide traffic counts, construction costs allocated to the municipality,
road-user delay, safety outcomes, or a local consultation/acceptance measure.
Source: [Ministry road-upgrade announcement](https://kam.lt/atnaujintos-dvi-kelio-atkarpos-prie-rudninku-karinio-poligono/).

A later ministry account of the Phase-II-A contract states that work covers
more than 52 hectares and 27 buildings totaling almost 94,000 square metres,
and that the contract price includes design, construction, maintenance, service
provision for 12.5 years, financing costs, interest, and other bank obligations.
This creates an observable long-duration host-state operating obligation, while
the public record still does not identify annual service payments, realized
maintenance performance, local supplier/workforce incidence, or a remedy or
contest route for affected residents. Source: [Phase-II-A infrastructure
contract announcement](https://kam.lt/isigaliojo-rudninku-karinio-miestelio-ii-etapo-a-dalies-infrastrukturos-pletros-projekto-sutartis-isibegeja-darbai/).

The evidence therefore advances the episode to **access works and contracted
service obligation observed; local burden, adequacy, and acceptance still
unmeasured**. Do not convert the 34-km road plan into realized traffic relief,
or the 12.5-year contract term into proof that services have been delivered.

## Amtrak A-Interlocking operating checkpoint — 2026-10-20

Amtrak's named **A-Interlocking / First Street Tunnel** work provides a new
operating-stage test. Amtrak's [service-status page](https://www.amtrak.com/about-amtrak/washington-dc-train-status-pause-for-progress)
dates the disruption to October 16–26, 2026 and identifies reduced service,
train cancellations, route modifications, and travel disruption south of
Washington Union Station. It also identifies the affected route segments and
three round-trip Alexandria–Washington bus-bridge runs, plus express-bus
alternatives from Virginia cities. Amtrak's [project announcement](https://media.amtrak.com/2026/08/amtrak-announces-pause-for-progress-infrastructure-upgrade-in-washington-d-c-for-nine-days-this-october/)
identifies the facility, the 10-mph pre-work speed restriction, the switch,
tunnel-track-base, and electrical-system work, and the stated reliability
objective.

This closes the named-facility, dated-disruption, affected-service, and
operator-provided-alternative fields more tightly than the prior aggregate
routes. It does **not** yet close customer-level incidence or durable response:
the episode is still in progress at this checkpoint, and the public pages do
not provide completed passenger counts on the bus bridge, rider-level delay or
cost exposure, compensation or rebooking uptake, alternative adequacy, or
post-restoration acceptance/reversal. The route is therefore coded as a
**live operating disruption with planned mitigation**, not as a completed
operation-to-legitimacy chain.

The lawful next observation is post-October-26 service evidence: completed
restoration, route-level on-time/cancellation or passenger-use data, and any
published customer remedy or public response. Until that exists, this route
must not be converted into a claim that the outage was successfully absorbed
or that the reliability benefit was realized.

## Stopping rule

Stop this infrastructure pass when one lawful source bundle supplies the
minimum fields above or when the source owner confirms that project-level
incidence, mitigation, or later response is unavailable. Record the exact
missing field and rotate to another thin-middle lane. Do not repeat collection
of capacity forecasts, announcement milestones, modeled charges, or broad
attitude polls as if they close the operation-to-incidence middle.

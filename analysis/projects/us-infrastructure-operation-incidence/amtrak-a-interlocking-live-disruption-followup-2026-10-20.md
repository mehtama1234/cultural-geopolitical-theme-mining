# Amtrak A-Interlocking live disruption follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** official live operating-disruption milestone; passenger incidence and post-restoration response remain open

## Observed route

Amtrak identifies a temporary infrastructure outage at A-Interlocking in the
First Street Tunnel just south of Washington Union Station from October 16
through October 26, 2026. The operator describes A-Interlocking as a critical
track and control point routing Amtrak and Virginia Railway Express movements
between Washington and Virginia. The work replaces aging switches and
crossovers, rehabilitates the tunnel track base, and installs modern electric
switch systems.

Amtrak reports reduced service, train cancellations, route modifications, and
travel disruption south of Washington. The posted mitigation includes two
Amtrak Virginia train round trips terminating at Alexandria, three timed
Alexandria–Washington bus-bridge round trips, additional express bus service
from Virginia cities, a Washington–Alexandria bus connection for the
Carolinian, and rebooking or alternative-travel support.

## Stage boundary

| Stage | What is public | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Named operation | A-Interlocking / First Street Tunnel work with a dated October 16–26 outage | Final work completion and post-work operating condition |
| Affected service | Reduced service, cancellations, route modifications, and disruption south of Washington | Passenger counts, delay minutes, fares, missed connections, accessibility, and worker effects |
| Mitigation | Bus bridges, express buses, route segmentation, Metro connection guidance, and rebooking/alternative-travel support | Actual use, capacity, adequacy, extra cost, and who could not use the alternatives |
| Intended benefit | Safer and more reliable service with fewer delays over time | Post-restoration reliability, on-time performance, recurrence, compensation, and passenger acceptance |

This is stronger than an infrastructure announcement because a named facility,
dated service disruption, affected routes, and operator-provided alternatives
are all public. It remains a live disruption record rather than evidence that
the mitigation absorbed the burden or that the future reliability benefit was
realized.

## Official status recheck — 2026-10-20

Amtrak's current customer-facing status page still describes the October 16–26
window as active. It retains the same operating boundary: reduced service and
train cancellations south of Washington, route modifications, three timed
Alexandria–Washington bus-bridge round trips, express buses, Metro guidance,
and rebooking or alternate-travel support. VRE's October 19–23 schedule is also
shown as modified, with service terminating at or originating from L'Enfant.

The recheck adds current accessibility of the mitigation plan, not realized
incidence. The page still does not publish passenger counts, actual bus or
Metro uptake, delay minutes, missed connections, extra fares or time, mobility
access outcomes, compensation, or post-October-26 on-time performance. The
route therefore remains **live operation plus offered alternatives**, not
completed service recovery or passenger-level adequacy.

Source: [Amtrak's current Washington, DC train-status page](https://www.amtrak.com/about-amtrak/washington-dc-train-status-pause-for-progress).

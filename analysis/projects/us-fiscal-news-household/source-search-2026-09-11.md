# Source search: US fiscal news and household choice

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; fiscal-news belief effects are measured, later action remains open

## Working question

When does a future public bill change today's household choice?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-FISCAL-NEWS | [Fiscal Policy and Households' Inflation Expectations](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28485) | A randomized US household experiment finds that current debt or deficit information has little effect on inflation expectations, while information about future debt raises expected inflation and also changes expected government spending and borrowing costs. | NBER working paper | It does not show the same household's income, spending, saving, political response or the later realized policy. |
| US-NBER-FISCAL-SPENDING | [The Effects of Fiscal News on Household Expectations and Spending](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35009) | Provides a comparison from a Korean household experiment in which expected future public debt raised expected inflation and consumer spending. | NBER working paper | This is not US evidence and is used only as a comparison, not as the US finding. |

## First pattern to test

```text
news about future public debt
  -> inflation, tax and spending expectation
  -> spending, saving or borrowing choice
  -> policy realization or reversal
  -> household cost and political judgment
```

The US source supports the expectation link. It does not establish the final household or political step.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Future debt news may provide useful information rather than create fear. A household may update a belief without changing its budget because it has no room to change spending.

## Main gaps

- income, debt and liquidity position;
- current and expected taxes and benefits;
- spending, saving and borrowing records;
- media source and trust;
- policy realization, reversal and political response;
- differences by income, age, race, party and homeownership.

## Decision rule

Keep current debt, future debt, expectation, budget action, received policy and political judgment separate. A future public bill is not a current household cost until the path is shown.

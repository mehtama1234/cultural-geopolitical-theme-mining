# Project: Ukraine assistance, wartime oversight, and strategic learning

## Question

Can a wartime assistance program produce information quickly enough to guide
oversight and budgeting while still showing whether the assistance achieved its
larger strategic purpose?

## Current bounded finding

The [GAO finding](findings/us-gao-ukraine-assistance-monitoring-evaluation-2023-2026-001.md)
shows that State used some monitoring information for oversight and changed the
reporting format, but data access, wartime conditions, and delayed outcomes
limited cross-program evaluation.

The $6.1 billion figure is the scope of assistance overseen through the
contract, not proof of benefit or strategic success.

## Next test

Follow a named assistance program through funds, delivery, Ukrainian use,
operational result, local response, maintenance, and an attributed change in
institutional or strategic behavior.

The [assistance, delivery capacity, legitimacy, and strategic-result conversion
bridge](assistance-delivery-capacity-legitimacy-conversion-bridge-v1.md)
extends this route across Ukraine oversight, household resilience, wartime
energy legitimacy, humanitarian funding, African development capacity, and
West Bank/Gaza assistance controls. It keeps authorization, delivery, use,
protection, and legitimacy separate.

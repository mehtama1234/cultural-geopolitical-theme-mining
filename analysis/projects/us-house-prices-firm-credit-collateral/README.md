# Project: House prices, firm credit, and young-business survival

This project tracks how a housing shock reaches firms through financial
channels. A Census working paper combines business, housing, and banking data
from the Great Recession. It reports large and persistent employment effects at
small businesses, especially young firms, with entry and exit contributing to
the pattern. Reliance on personal assets or home equity increased sensitivity
to house prices, and local credit shocks affected both young and older firms.

The safe theme is not “house prices cause every business failure.” It is that a
local housing shock can pass through bank credit supply and owners' collateral,
with young firms more exposed. The project must keep firm employment, entry,
exit, credit, collateral, worker outcomes, household wealth, and recovery
separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected firm-owner-bank-place panel joining
house prices, collateral, personal assets, lending, bank balance sheets, firm
entry and exit, employment, pay, investment, prices, household resources, and
later recovery. The public abstract does not provide that complete bridge.

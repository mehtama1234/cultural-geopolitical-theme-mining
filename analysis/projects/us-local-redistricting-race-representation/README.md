# Local redistricting and racial representation

This project studies how local electoral maps translate residential geography
into political voice. It keeps separate the map, the simulated baseline, the
racial composition of officeholders, legal oversight, turnout, policy, and
trust.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35623, which reconstructs
3,712 city council and county commission maps across 18,521 districts. It
reports that enacted maps overrepresent White voters and underrepresent Black
and Hispanic voters, while race-blind simulations reproduce much of the gap.
Maps subject to Voting Rights Act oversight or districting litigation improve
more on that baseline. The result is a representation finding, not a complete
theory of local political power.

## Next test

The decisive extension is a protected local-government panel joining map
changes, population and voter geography, candidate entry, officeholder race,
turnout, contestedness, policy votes, constituent service, litigation, and
later trust or participation.

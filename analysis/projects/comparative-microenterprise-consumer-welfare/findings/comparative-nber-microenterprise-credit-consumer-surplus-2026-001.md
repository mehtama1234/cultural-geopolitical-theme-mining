# Finding 001: Small-business credit can benefit customers as well as firms

**Status:** bounded comparative firm, market, and consumer-welfare finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER randomized trial in Chile gave large loans to microenterprises and
measured both firm and customer outcomes.

Treated firms' profits increased by about $292 per month, or 13.4%. Customer
survey data indicate an estimated consumer-surplus gain of about $494 per month.
Using more than 125,000 non-treated firms in the same markets, the study found
little evidence that treated firms' gains came simply from stealing sales from
other firms.

The route is:

```text
large business loan
  -> firm capacity and profit
  -> products, service, competition, or availability
  -> estimated customer surplus
  -> [open] prices, debt, repeat use, and durable household benefit
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a firm-and-consumer counterpoint to the idea that credit merely
redistributes market share:

> In some markets, helping a small firm grow can create benefits for customers
> as well as for the borrower.

But the result also reinforces the atlas rule that a modeled or estimated
consumer benefit is not the same as a household receiving more cash or lasting
security.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence comes from Chile, not the United States.
- The public abstract does not provide exact treated-firm or customer sample
  sizes, loan terms, or follow-up length.
- Consumer surplus is an estimated welfare measure, not an observed paycheck or
  bill reduction.
- The result may depend on local product markets, competition, and the scale of
  the loans.

The next decisive test is a protected firm-customer ledger joining credit,
investment, prices, quality, availability, customer choice, repeat use, firm
debt, survival, and household outcomes. Until then, the safe claim is that
credit expansion can reach customers through improved firm capacity, but the
transfer to durable household welfare remains open.

## Source

- [NBER Working Paper 35729: When Microenterprises Grow, Are Consumers Better Off?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35729)

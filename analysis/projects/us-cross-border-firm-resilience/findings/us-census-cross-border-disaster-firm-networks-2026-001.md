# Finding 001: Firm ownership can change how a foreign disaster reaches U.S. buyers

**Status:** bounded cross-border firm-network finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Census Bureau's Center for Economic Studies working paper links foreign
natural disasters to U.S. importers through supplier and ownership records. It
reports that disasters abroad cause sharp, persistent export declines at
affected suppliers and that U.S. importers' total purchase growth falls by up
to 25 percentage points. The contraction is not uniform: non-MNC buyers
contract roughly twice as much as buyers belonging to global corporations,
and the arm's-length versus intra-firm comparison shows a similar gap.

The route is:

```text
foreign disaster
  -> supplier export loss
  -> ownership and network structure
  -> U.S. importer purchase change
  -> substitution, inventory, production, prices, workers, and households [open]
```

The broader theme is simple: a firm's network can act as a shock absorber for
one buyer while also redirecting the shock somewhere else. A smaller purchase
decline is evidence about firm-level transmission, not proof that the wider
economy or households were protected.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a cross-border version of the program's capacity-versus-dependence
question. Global organization may create options that an arm's-length buyer
does not have, but those options are private and unevenly distributed. The
result makes firm boundaries part of geopolitical and infrastructure analysis
without turning a firm-network comparison into a national-security claim.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a working paper, not a final reviewed Census publication.
- The public abstract does not disclose the complete sample, product mix,
  timing windows, or all exposure definitions.
- Ownership and intra-firm links may proxy for firm size, financing,
  diversification, inventory, product type, or supplier choice.
- A purchase response does not show whether the firm substituted suppliers,
  delayed production, raised prices, reduced employment, or passed costs on.
- The result does not establish consumer welfare, household security, public
  resilience, or geopolitical leverage.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a protected firm-product-place ledger joining the
disaster, supplier, ownership link, alternative suppliers, shipment,
inventory, production, prices, workers, and recovery. That would show whether
network buffering is real resilience, delayed exposure, or a transfer of cost
to another firm, worker, or consumer.

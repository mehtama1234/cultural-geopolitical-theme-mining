# Project: Cross-border firm networks and shock absorption

This project follows how a disruption outside the United States reaches U.S.
firms through supplier and ownership networks. A Census CES working paper uses
U.S. Bill of Lading data, geocoded natural disasters, and cross-border
ownership data. Its abstract reports that foreign disasters reduce affected
supplier exports and can reduce U.S. importers' purchase growth by up to 25
percentage points, while non-MNC buyers contract roughly twice as much as
buyers inside global corporations.

The working theme is that organizational boundaries can redistribute shock
exposure. A multinational or intra-firm relationship may provide alternatives
or coordination, but that is not the same as public resilience, lower prices,
stable jobs, or household protection. The project keeps firm response,
supplier substitution, inventories, workers, consumers, infrastructure, and
state strategy separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected firm-product-place ledger joining the
foreign event, supplier, ownership relationship, alternative suppliers,
shipment, inventory, production, prices, workers, and later recovery. The
current paper does not follow that full chain.

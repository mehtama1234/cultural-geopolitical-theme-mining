# Source search: US multiple jobs and the cost of flexibility

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

Does equal income buy the same life when it comes from one job versus several?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HBS-TSai-Buell-2025 | [HBS: The True Costs of Gig Work](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/true-costs-of-gig-work) | Across surveys, General Social Survey data and transactions from more than 90,000 customers of a national bank, the study reports that similarly paid people with multiple jobs spent 17 percentage points less of take-home pay, 16 percentage points less on necessities, and almost 2 percentage points less on extras. They also had higher transport spending and saved less. | Research summary; HBS Working Paper 25-036 | Multiple jobs may be chosen by students, people with different needs or people facing unstable work; the result does not by itself show that gig work caused the spending pattern. |
| HBS-FLEX-2021 | [HBS: How Much Is Freedom Worth?](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-much-is-freedom-worth-for-gig-workers-a-lot) | A DoorDash driver study in California estimates that losing schedule flexibility has a meaningful money value for drivers, with larger value for some groups. | Research summary using platform data | California drivers and a delivery setting do not represent all multiple-job workers; flexibility value does not settle the cost of benefits or stability. |

## First pattern to test

```text
one job is not available, suitable or flexible enough
  -> several jobs or gig work
  -> split schedule, travel and uncertain income
  -> cautious spending, less saving or less care
  -> short-run flexibility versus long-run security
  -> firm staffing choices and public debate about work
```

The HBS evidence supports a spending difference among people with similar pay. It does not establish whether the cause is job instability, worker choice, family need, education, health or the number of jobs itself.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Multiple jobs can provide needed flexibility, a path around a bad manager, a way to study, or a bridge during a job change. Lower spending may also reflect deliberate saving or different goals.

## Main gaps

- worker-level income stability, benefits, hours and schedule control;
- unpaid care, health, education and household size;
- vehicle and transport costs after net earnings;
- whether multiple jobs lead to better or worse later work;
- differences by race, age, place, disability and immigration status;
- whether firms shift risk to workers while calling the arrangement flexible.

## Decision rule

Compare net pay and time—not just gross income. Keep worker choice and employer risk design visible in the same record.

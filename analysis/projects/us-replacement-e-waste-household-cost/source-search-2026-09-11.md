# Source search: US replacement spending and electronics waste

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** matched evidence pass complete; household replacement and disposal records remain open

## Sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| NBER-DURABILITY-29073 | [NBER: Do Market Failures Create a Durability Gap?](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29073/revisions/w29073.rev0.pdf) | The model studies product durability and repair-cost choices and asks whether market conditions can lead to products that are replaced sooner than is socially best. | Model, not a current US household estimate. |
| EPA-ELECTRONICS-2026 | [EPA: How to recycle common recyclables](https://www.epa.gov/recycle/how-do-i-recycle-common-recyclables) | EPA estimates 2.7 million tons of consumer electronics were generated in 2018 and says electronics generally need special collection rather than curbside recycling. | The generation estimate is older; it does not identify which products were replaced because repair was unavailable. |
| EPA-BATTERY-2026 | [EPA: Lithium-ion battery recycling](https://www.epa.gov/hw/lithium-ion-battery-recycling) | Batteries may be collected, repaired, reused, repurposed, or recycled, but need separate handling and can create fire risk when damaged. | End-of-life guidance does not measure household cost or participation. |
| GAO-EWASTE-2005 | [GAO: Electronic waste](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a112095.html) | Cost and inconvenience can discourage consumer recycling and reuse. | Older testimony; current collection access and prices may differ. |

## First pattern to test

```text
repair fails or is unavailable
  -> replacement purchase
  -> household cash, debt, and work or care access change
  -> old product is stored, reused, recycled, or discarded
  -> safety, material recovery, and local burden change
```

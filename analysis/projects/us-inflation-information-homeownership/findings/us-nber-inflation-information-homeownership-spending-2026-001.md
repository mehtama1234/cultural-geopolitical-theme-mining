# Finding 001: The same inflation information can produce different household actions

**Status:** bounded U.S. information experiment and household-adaptation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 33595 uses randomized information treatments to study U.S.
households' inflation expectations and durable purchases. Renters revise their
inflation expectations more strongly but do not adjust durable purchases in the
reported result. Homeowners revise expectations more modestly but significantly
increase durable spending. The paper interprets this negative covariance through
different attention and adjustment margins, including greater liquidity
constraints among renters.

```text
inflation information
  -> attention and expectation updating
  -> tenure-specific adjustment margin
  -> durable or nondurable spending response
  -> [open] liquidity, wellbeing, trust, attribution, and political action
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a controlled counterexample to a simple “information changes behavior”
story. Belief updating and action can move in opposite directions when
households have different assets, liquidity, and ways to adjust. It advances the
material-to-meaning bridge by showing that the conversion from interpretation to
action depends on practical room, not only on what a household believes.

## Limits and next test

- The abstract does not provide the full experiment denominator or all treatment
  details.
- Homeownership is not a complete measure of wealth, liquidity, debt, or
  adjustment capacity.
- Durable spending is not total welfare, household security, or political
  action; renters may adjust through unmeasured margins.
- The experiment does not establish a dated household hardship episode or later
  trust, attribution, vote, organizing, or recovery.

The next decisive test is a repeated household panel joining information
exposure, expectations, tenure, wealth/liquidity, durable and nondurable
spending, debt, work/care adaptation, attribution, trust, and later civic or
political action.

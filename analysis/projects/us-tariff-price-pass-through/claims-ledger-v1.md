# Claims ledger: US tariffs and the price that arrives later

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; trade policy, import costs, retail prices, household buying, and public response

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | The tariff rate announced by government is not the same as the duty finally paid. | Census-based research | NBER reports that the 2025 realized rate was lower because shipment timing, exemptions, trade-agreement use, and other behavior changed the result. | The gap can change as rules and trade behavior change. |
| C-002 | Much of the tariff cost reached US importers in the episodes studied. | Import data and price analysis | NBER estimates high pass-through to tariff-inclusive import prices. | Importer prices are not the checkout price. |
| C-003 | Retail prices rose gradually and unevenly after the 2025 tariff changes. | Daily retail and household transaction data | NBER and the Federal Reserve find slower retail movement than a one-day price jump, with different effects across goods and origins. | The estimates are averages and do not give every household's bill. |
| C-004 | Households can respond by buying less or choosing cheaper goods, with a heavier burden for lower-income households. | Household transaction data | Federal Reserve staff research finds regressive price effects, quantity cuts, and trading down among worried households. | The study is preliminary and does not prove a later political choice. |
| C-005 | A tariff can connect public policy to a later household judgment, but the political link remains open. | Inference from C-001 through C-004 | The timing chain is visible: policy, cost, price, quantity, and outlook. | We still need repeated household views tied to purchases and policy knowledge. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
announced policy
  -> duty actually paid
  -> importer cost and supplier choice
  -> firm margin and retail price over months
  -> household quantity, quality, and substitute
  -> view of firms, government, jobs, and the future
```

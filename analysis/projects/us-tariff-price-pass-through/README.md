# Project: US tariffs and the price that arrives later

The [NBER W35561 trend record](../../records/us-nber-tariff-pass-through-consumer-prices-2026.json) adds the latest dynamic mechanism: about 26% of the 2025–26 tariff increase passed through to consumer prices, with 64% of that increase direct and 36% through imported inputs and domestic markups over a 9–12 month lag. The record does not treat aggregate pass-through as a household bill; income, product basket, substitution, retailer margin, and remedy remain open.

## Question

When a trade policy raises a firm's cost, how much reaches the US shopper, how fast, and which household sees it first?

## Short first pass

Start with the NBER study of 2025 tariffs, the Federal Reserve retail and household studies, and the HBS price tracker. Keep the announced rate, duty paid, importer cost, retailer margin, shelf price, household buying power, and the policy's stated goal as separate parts of the account.

## Possible connection

A tariff can be politically visible when announced but economically quiet at first. If firms absorb part of it and pass the rest through slowly, the cause and the household effect may be separated in time.

## Decision rule

Look for US evidence by product, income, retailer and time. Move on unless the sources show whether the price reached shoppers through the tariff itself, a wider cost change, or a later margin decision.

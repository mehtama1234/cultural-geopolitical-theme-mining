# Finding: realized federal property sales do not yet establish a complete asset-release benefit

**Status:** bounded public-capacity and infrastructure realization finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed the temporary Federal Assets Sale and Transfer Act process for
disposing of federal civilian properties. As of August 2026, 14 of 23
properties recommended and approved through the 2019 and 2025 rounds had been
disposed of, producing about $576 million in sale proceeds.

That is a realized implementation endpoint: approved assets reached completed
sales and generated proceeds. The wider route remains conditional. Proceeds can
support future disposal costs, but Congress has not consistently made the
related fund available. GAO also found unclear completion timeframes for many
2025-round properties.

The end-to-end insight is:

> A public asset-disposal program can demonstrate completed transactions and
> cash proceeds while still lacking the funding continuity and schedule
> certainty needed to show reduced maintenance burden, faster disposal, reuse,
> or public benefit at portfolio scale.

## Limits and next test

- The 14-property count and $576 million are specific to the temporary FASTA
  process, not all federal property sales.
- Sale proceeds are not the same as net savings; disposal costs, avoided
  maintenance, and alternative public uses are not joined here.
- The report does not establish neighborhood effects, service access, or the
  consequences of leaving an approved property in the pipeline.
- Funding availability, market conditions, property condition, approvals, and
  estimates may all affect timing; no single cause is assigned to every delay.

The next decisive artifact is a property-level ledger joining approval,
condition, maintenance cost, funding release, marketing, sale, disposal cost,
reuse or vacancy, and local/public-service effects.

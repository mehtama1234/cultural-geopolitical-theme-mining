# Finding 033: Redress reachability is part of the remedy

**Status:** bounded CFPB Ombudsman redress-access finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The bounded finding

The CFPB Ombudsman's FY2024 report says individuals contacted the office about
reissuing redress checks because the original check was not received, was not
cashed, or had to be reissued to a next of kin after the recipient died. The
report also describes questions about eligibility, payment legitimacy, and
connecting with third-party administrators.

This is not a payment-completion rate. It is direct evidence that the route
between a formal redress decision and a usable payment can require additional
navigation, verification, and administrative contact.

## The chain

```text
formal redress eligibility
  -> check or payment route
  -> nonreceipt, noncashing, death, eligibility, or legitimacy question
  -> Ombudsman/administrator contact and possible reissue
  -> [open] cashing, account correction, adequate recovery, trust, or exit
```

The cultural and consumer insight is simple: administrative reachability is
not a back-office detail. A remedy can be legally available while the affected
person still has to locate the right office, establish eligibility, verify the
payment, or obtain a reissue.

## Limits and next field

- The report gives examples of inquiry types, not their full count or rate.
- Ombudsman contact does not prove a successful reissue or cash receipt.
- No account correction, remaining loss, household recovery, trust, or exit is
  observed.

The next decisive record is a lawful redress-contact and payment-status ledger
linking failed delivery or reissue effort to final receipt, correction, and
later financial outcome.

Source: [CFPB Ombudsman's FY2024 Annual Report](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb__ombudsmans-annual-report_fy-2024.pdf), especially pp. 25–27.

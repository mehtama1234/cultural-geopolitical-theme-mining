# Practical exit observability audit v1

**Status:** cross-domain evidence audit; no pooled exit rate  
**Checked:** 2026-09-16  
**Purpose:** determine which forms of staying, switching, non-use, moving, and
exit are actually visible in the current US-centered atlas

## The question

The broad program treats exit as a practical power outcome, not a formal menu
item. A person may remain with a provider, benefit, platform, insurer, home, or
public route because it works—or because leaving would sacrifice money, time,
care, housing, work, identity, safety, or eligibility.

```text
problem or exposure
  -> attempted route and effort
  -> remedy, restoration, denial, or continued burden
  -> stay, switch, non-use, move, or exit
  -> later security, trust, legitimacy, or bargaining power
```

This audit asks whether the current evidence observes the final branch and
whether it observes the alternatives that make the branch meaningful.

## Current observability matrix

| Domain | Current unit and concrete evidence | What is genuinely observed | What “exit” cannot yet mean | Smallest missing field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SNAP/public assistance | SIPP adjacent-month person transitions; 437 entries and 387 exits, with 188 classified exits | A program-state transition and following material context; resource ratio declined after 69.46% of selected three-month exit sequences | Exit is not recovery, voluntary choice, work transition, or improved security | Notice, reason, amount, effort/appeal, alternatives, and later food/housing/security outcome in the same episode |
| Platform work | 27-case public proceeding inventory | 9 restoration/access-resumption cases, 3 lost-remuneration orders, and 0 cases linking the episode to worker switching or exit | Reactivation is not autonomy; a lost-pay order is not payment; continued trips are not proof of free choice | Post-remedy account status, payment receipt, alternative platform/work, recurrence, and documented return/switch/exit reason |
| Consumer fraud | SHED adult respondent and incident-level conditional outcomes | 21% reported financial fraud; among non-credit-card incidents, 32% reported some money unrecovered and 30% spent at least 10 hours dealing with it | Non-reporting is not satisfaction; recovery effort is not switching; continued product use is unobserved | Provider/account identity, attempted contacts, verified recovery, replacement route, continued use, and switching/non-use |
| Consumer finance recourse | CFPB published complaint records, 5,452,107 in 2025 | Complaint submission, routing, response labels, explanation, and relief labels | A published complaint is not the full exposed customer population, verified remedy, account closure, or exit | Account-level exposure, repeat contact, correction, remedy receipt, closure/switch, and non-complainant denominator |
| Housing and insurance | State residual-market and insurer/place records | California FAIR Plan policies rose from 242,440 in September 2021 to 696,562 in June 2026; nonrenewal and backstop channels are visible | A policy-market shift is not a household move, voluntary insurer switch, affordability improvement, or safe staying | Property/household notice, premium/payment, claim/repair, alternative coverage, move/stay decision, and recovery |
| Local service capacity | Firm, provider, establishment, shortage, mobility, and place records | Nominal presence, entry, exit, travel context, public route, and neighboring capacity | Establishment count is not reachable service, completed use, replacement, or resident exit | Dated resident attempt, wait/travel/price, successful substitute, non-use, restored access, and stay/switch/move |

The rows are deliberately not additive. Their units include program spells,
proceedings, adult respondents, complaints, policies, properties, firms, and
places. No denominator in one row can be divided by another to estimate
consumer or resident exit.

## What the matrix establishes

### 1. Administrative exit is the most visible form

The SIPP SNAP route records a real change in program participation. That is
valuable, but its meaning is underdetermined: income may have risen, a rule
may have applied, paperwork may have failed, a household may have moved, or a
person may have stopped using a benefit while need continued. The observed
following resource decline is a direct counterexample to treating exit as a
security endpoint.

### 2. Formal remedy can restore access without revealing exit

The platform case inventory has unusually concrete adjudicated outcomes, yet
none of its 27 cases links restoration or refusal to a later switch or exit.
The public record can show a returned account, subsequent trips, or an ordered
payment while leaving alternatives, payment receipt, and the reason for
continued use unknown. This is contestability without an observed exit rate.

### 3. Recovery burden can be visible while choice remains invisible

SHED shows that fraud can consume money and substantial time. CFPB records
show how a complaint is routed and labeled. Together they establish exposure,
effort, and institutional visibility as distinct currencies. Neither source
shows whether the person closed an account, changed provider, stopped using a
service, or stayed because no safe alternative existed.

### 4. Market movement is not household mobility

Insurance nonrenewal, residual-market growth, firm exit, and establishment
entry are important changes in the option environment. They do not reveal
whether a particular household could obtain replacement coverage, repair a
home, move, or remain safely. “Market exit” and “household exit” must remain
separate labels.

## Exit status vocabulary for the atlas

Every future record should use the narrowest available label:

| Label | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| **Institutional exit** | A program, account, policy, platform, firm, or route changes state |
| **Access restoration** | A named case or account is documented as restored or reactivated |
| **Continued use** | Post-event use is observed in a defined follow-up window |
| **Switching** | The same unit is observed moving to an identified alternative |
| **Non-use/abandonment** | The same unit stops using or does not complete an attempted route, with the denominator defined |
| **Household move/stay** | A property or household panel observes relocation or continued residence |
| **Practical exit** | Switching, non-use, move, or refusal is observed together with alternatives, cost, and protected/sacrificed outcome |

The last label should be rare until the record contains the alternatives and
the consequence of leaving. A lower caseload, complaint silence, account
closure, policy termination, worker reactivation, or establishment exit is not
enough by itself.

## Next decisive test

The best storage-conscious next step is one small same-case ledger rather than
another national aggregate. Preferred routes are:

1. a public-benefit notice/interruption/appeal episode with amount and later
   food or housing security;
2. a platform remedy case with post-order account status, payment receipt, and
   alternative work;
3. a credit or deposit account event with correction, closure, replacement,
   and later use; or
4. a property/insurance notice with replacement coverage, repair, and
   move/stay outcome.

For each route, retain at least one comparable case with a usable alternative
or a timely remedy. Stop at the observability boundary if the follow-up or
denominator is unavailable; do not convert institutional capacity into lived
exit.

## Relationship to the broad goal

This audit strengthens the broad proposition that unequal practical
optionality is a recurring structure behind pressure and power. The current
atlas observes many conditions and institutional responses, but the decisive
consumer, public, and household outcome—whether the actor could leave without
an unacceptable sacrifice—remains sparsely observed.

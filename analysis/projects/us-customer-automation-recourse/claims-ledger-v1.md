# Claims ledger: US customer service, automation, and the right to reach a person

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; customer service, human workers, automated help, complaints, and remedy

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | AI suggestions can make a human service worker answer faster in some settings. | Randomized field experiment reported by HBS | HBS reports 138 agents and 256,934 meal-delivery chats; response time fell 22% and measured sentiment rose 0.45 points on a five-point scale. | One company, one tool, and one service setting. |
| C-002 | The gain can be larger for less-experienced workers. | Subgroup result | HBS reports a 70% response-time decline and a 1.63-point sentiment increase for less-experienced agents. | This is not evidence that the worker gained authority or pay. |
| C-003 | Repeat complaints are a boundary for the measured benefit. | Observed subgroup result | HBS reports less improvement for repeat complaints and some confusion after very fast transfers from a chatbot to a person. | The account does not show whether the final problem was fixed. |
| C-004 | A complaint database records a request for help and a company response, not a representative measure of all service quality. | Data description | CFPB says its published complaints are not a statistical sample and encourages pairing them with other data. | Complaint data still helps locate problems and questions. |
| C-005 | The current evidence supports separating speed from remedy. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 show a measured first-contact gain beside an open repeat-contact and remedy question. | No current record joins tool use, customer path, worker authority, and later outcome. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
firm cost or service goal
  -> automated prompt, routing, or answer
  -> faster first contact
  -> repeat problem, exception, denial, or need for records
  -> human access and authority to change the answer
  -> remedy, repeat contact, complaint, exit, or trust
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Automated service improves final remedies as much as first response time.
- Repeat complaints are resolved at the same rate and speed as first contacts.
- Customers can reach a person who has clear authority to correct the result.
- Differences by language, disability, age, income, or digital access are small.

## Next test

Use one public complaint system and match first contact, repeat contact, transfer, decision, response, remedy, and appeal. Mark whether automation was used and whether the worker could change the result. Start with CFPB complaint data as a problem index, not as proof of poor service.

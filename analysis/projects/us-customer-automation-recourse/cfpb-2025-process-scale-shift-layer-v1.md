# CFPB 2025 complaint-system scale and process-shift layer

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Source:** [CFPB 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/2025-consumer-response-annual-report/)  
**Window:** complaints received January 1–December 31, 2025  
**Archived PDF checksum (SHA-256):** `5364f19086de5a0ede17c11933c1b69d528dd2a3271700e34217634bb32230e0`
**Status:** institutional process and data-production trend; not a consumer-harm rate.

## Why this is a different kind of trend

The complaint database is not only a record of consumer problems. It is also a
system that receives, filters, routes, publishes, and classifies submissions.
When the system changes, the visible trend can change even if underlying
consumer harm does not move in the same direction.

```text
consumer problem or organized submission
  -> complaint channel and screening
  -> routing, company review, response category, and publication
  -> visible institutional record
  -> regulator, firm, consumer, and public interpretation
```

This layer therefore studies institutional visibility and response capacity,
not just complaint volume.

## 2025 scale and routing

The CFPB reports receiving approximately 6,635,400 complaints in 2025. It sent
approximately 5,984,100 (90%) to companies for review and response, referred
3% to other regulators, and found 7% not actionable. It sent complaints to
more than 4,000 companies; companies provided a timely response to more than
99% of complaints sent for review.

Credit or consumer reporting accounted for approximately 5,806,800 complaints,
or 88% of those received. This concentration makes the national total a poor
proxy for the experience of a typical financial-product customer.

## The reported volume sequence

The annual report gives this agency-reported sequence:

| Year | Complaints received, approximately |
|---|---:|
| 2019 | 352,400 |
| 2021 | 994,000 |
| 2023 | 1,657,600 |
| 2024 | 3,187,900 |
| 2025 | 6,635,400 |

The report says the increases were driven almost entirely by growth in credit
or consumer reporting complaints and attributes part of the 2025 screening
problem to duplicative or spurious submissions enabled by emerging automated
tools. That is the CFPB's explanation, not an independently established causal
finding in this report. The defensible result is that the complaint system's
observed volume and filtering process changed sharply; the causes and the
share representing authentic consumer grievances require separate validation.

## Response categories are not interchangeable

For complaints sent to companies, the CFPB's 2025 table reports this overall
distribution:

| Company response category | Share |
|---|---:|
| Closed with explanation | 53% |
| Closed with non-monetary relief | 37% |
| Administrative response | 9% |
| Closed with monetary relief | <1% |
| Company reviewing | <1% |
| No timely response | <1% |

The categories have different meanings. Monetary relief is objective and
measurable financial relief reported by the company. Non-monetary relief can
include correcting account information, restoring access, or correcting credit
data. An explanation means the company reported no relief but supplied a
tailored written explanation. A timely response is a process measure, not proof
that the consumer's problem was fixed.

## Screening and the visible record

The report says companies returned approximately 469,800 complaints as
suspected fraud, 63,200 as unauthorized third-party submissions, and 302,200 as
duplicates. One nationwide credit-reporting company accounted for more than
90% of the suspected-fraud returns and more than 60% of both unauthorized and
duplicate returns. These submissions are not simply equivalent to ordinary
consumer complaints, but they are part of the institutional story: organized
submission, identity protection, data quality, and access to a regulator can
all affect what becomes visible.

The report also says that companies sometimes selected “closed with
explanation” when the consumer received monetary or non-monetary relief. The
CFPB describes completeness, accuracy, and timeliness as separate response
qualities. This reinforces the project's rule that a response label is not the
same as a verified consumer outcome.

## Comparison with the 2024 snapshot

The earlier 2024 API snapshot counted 2,739,722 published records received in
2024, while the 2025 annual report discusses approximately 6,635,400 complaints
received and separately explains screening and routing. These figures must not
be treated as a clean year-over-year harm series: they use different stages of
the process, different rounding, and a changing publication/screening system.

The defensible trend is narrower: the complaint institution became a much
larger and more actively filtered channel, while credit reporting dominated
the visible workload. Whether that represents more consumer harm, more
organized submissions, changes in company reporting, or a combination is open.

## What this adds to the broad program

- **Consumer culture:** the complaint channel is a way people and organized
  actors make financial problems publicly legible.
- **Institutional power:** routing, screening, category choice, and publication
  determine which experiences become actionable records.
- **Firm power:** the largest firms can shape the data stream through response,
  duplicate, fraud, and unauthorized-submission classifications.
- **Trust and meaning:** a fast response, explanation, correction, or exclusion
  may be interpreted differently, but the annual report does not measure trust
  restoration or exit.
- **Data politics:** a rising record count is partly a trend in the recording
  institution, not automatically a trend in underlying consumer suffering.

## Next test

Build a stage-aligned series from received → actionable → sent to company →
company response → published/returned, stratified by product and firm. Add
prior-contact status, narrative presence, response category, and lawful market
denominators. Pair it with a consumer panel or repeat-case source to measure
correction, repeat effort, financial recovery, switching, trust, and exit.

Do not infer harm rates from complaint totals, and do not merge CFPB records
with SHED, SIPP, ANES, or CPS respondents as if they share identifiers.

## Source boundary

The report is an official administrative description of CFPB complaint
processing. It includes company-reported response categories and explicitly
excludes or filters some submissions from the published complaint totals.
Those records are valuable evidence about institutional behavior and visibility,
but they are not a probability sample of consumers or a verified remedy panel.

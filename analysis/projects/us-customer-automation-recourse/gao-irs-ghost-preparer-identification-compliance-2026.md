# Tax-preparer anonymity can shift filing risk to households while redress remains open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** paid-preparer exposure → IRS detection, data, and strategy gap; taxpayer correction and recovery remain open

GAO's October 15, 2026 review adds a consumer-recourse and administrative-
visibility route. Paid preparers completed more than half of federal income-tax
returns in fiscal year 2025. Ghost preparers omit their identifying information
and can leave taxpayers responsible for inaccurate or fraudulent returns,
stolen refunds, or identity theft.

The institutional route is fragmented. IRS systems tracking ghost-preparer cases
and outcomes are not connected, so IRS does not know how many preparers it has
identified, investigated, or addressed. Activities are split across six offices,
and IRS has no measurable objectives or performance goals for the initiatives,
including taxpayer outreach. Courts have ordered individual preparers to pay
millions in restitution, but officials said the total effect is likely greater
because detection is difficult. Three recommendations remain open on data,
coordination, and measurable objectives.

The evidence boundary is material. The report does not estimate ghost-preparer
prevalence or total taxpayer loss, and it does not show a taxpayer receiving a
corrected return, recovered refund, identity-theft resolution, effective
outreach, reduced repeat exposure, or restored trust.

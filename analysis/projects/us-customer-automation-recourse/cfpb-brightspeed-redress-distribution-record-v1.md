# CFPB BrightSpeed redress distribution record v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-16 (official page rechecked)
**Status:** official aggregate compensation and distribution record; individual recovery remains unobserved

## Why this is a useful comparison

The Cash App case establishes a formal remedy whose public distribution status
is not currently visible in the CFPB payment-by-case listing. BrightSpeed is a
contrasting US consumer-financial case where the CFPB publicly identifies the
eligible population, compensable harm, payment administrator, and an ongoing
distribution period.

## Documented case surface

The CFPB describes BrightSpeed Solutions and Kevin Howard as having processed
payments for technical-support fraudsters. The public payment page identifies
**122,507 eligible consumers** and **$53,885,244 in compensable harm**. It names
Epiq Systems as the payment administrator and records distribution as ongoing
from **July 23, 2024**.

These facts establish an administrative compensation route, not a recovery
rate. The page does not provide a respondent-level denominator of payments
issued, successful delivery, returned payments, net recovery, remaining loss,
repeat effort, or later trust and service use.

## Dated status recheck

The official CFPB page still lists BrightSpeed under ongoing cases in the
payments-by-case index. Its case page still records **July 23, 2024 –
Ongoing: Distribution**, and the case page reports that it was last modified
July 23, 2024. The index was last modified August 24, 2026. This is a current
administrative status observation, not evidence that every eligible consumer
was paid or that distribution is progressing uniformly.

The recheck therefore records “ongoing distribution still reported as of
2026-09-16,” while leaving payment issuance, successful receipt, amount,
residual loss, and later behavior unobserved. No administrator file or
respondent data was downloaded.

## End-to-end stage map

```text
fraudulent technical-support payment
  -> payment-processing and enforcement record
  -> eligible-victim class and compensable-harm amount
  -> administrator and ongoing distribution
  -> individual receipt, remaining loss, trust, switching, or exit
```

The first four stages are directly documented by the official case page. The
last stage remains open. A listed administrator and an ongoing distribution
date are stronger than a formal order alone, but neither proves that a given
consumer received compensation or was made whole.

## Contribution to the broad program

This advances the **consumer power and recourse**, **platforms/data and
attention**, **firm/market power**, and **public systems/policy feedback**
themes. It provides a counterexample to treating all formal remedies as one
status: the atlas can now distinguish an ordered remedy with no visible
distribution listing (Cash App) from a named ongoing administrative
distribution with an eligible class and aggregate harm amount (BrightSpeed).

The comparison still cannot answer whether compensation restores practical
choice, repairs trust, prevents repeat loss, or enables exit. Those outcomes
require individual or privacy-approved linked follow-up.

## Next verification target

Recheck the official distribution page and administrator notice for payment
counts, issuance dates, returned or unclaimed payments, and per-class rules.
Do not divide the aggregate harm amount by eligible consumers and call that an
average recovery: the page does not establish equal eligibility, payment
amounts, successful receipt, or full compensation.

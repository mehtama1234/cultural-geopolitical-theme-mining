# CFPB 2025 product-specific response asymmetry layer

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Source:** [CFPB 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/2025-consumer-response-annual-report/)  
**Status:** company-reported response categories by product; not a remedy probability or harm rate.

## Why product context changes the story

The national complaint response mix hides different institutional experiences.
A consumer asking a credit-card company to reverse a charge, a borrower asking
a mortgage servicer to correct an account, and a student-loan borrower facing a
servicing transition enter different response systems. Product, regulator,
company, issue, and customer exposure shape the visible path.

```text
product or service problem
  -> company contact and CFPB routing
  -> product-specific explanation, monetary relief, non-monetary relief,
     administrative response, or no timely response
  -> different practical room to recover, contest, or exit
```

## 2025 response comparison

The CFPB annual report's Table 1 reports the following shares among complaints
sent to companies for review and response. Categories are company-reported;
they are not independently verified consumer outcomes.

| Product | Monetary relief | Non-monetary relief | Explanation | Administrative response | No timely response |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All products | <1% | 37% | 53% | 9% | <1% |
| Credit or consumer reporting | <1% | 40% | 51% | 10% | <1% |
| Debt collection | <1% | 23% | 66% | 7% | 3% |
| Credit card | 12% | 19% | 63% | 5% | <1% |
| Checking or savings | 10% | 5% | 83% | 1% | <1% |
| Mortgage | 2% | 3% | 91% | 3% | 1% |
| Student loan | <1% | 1% | 66% | <1% | 31% |
| Prepaid card | 14% | 7% | 71% | 3% | 5% |

The contrast is not “credit cards are better” or “mortgages are worse.” It is
that the same response label has different prevalence by product and therefore
cannot be used as a universal remedy rate. A high explanation share may mean a
company documented its position without relief; a non-monetary response may
correct data or restore access; monetary relief may address money without
repairing time, trust, credit, or future access.

## Prior contact and institutional effort

The report also records whether consumers said they first tried to resolve the
issue with the company. That share was 93% for credit-card complaints, 92% for
checking or savings, 92% for mortgages, 88% for vehicle loans, 84% for debt
collection, and 77% for student loans. This is not a measure of all prior
effort: it is a reported field among complaints in the CFPB process. Still, it
helps separate “the customer never tried” from a path where company contact
preceded regulatory escalation.

Student-loan complaints are an especially important institutional case: the
report says companies responded to 69% of complaints sent to them, with 31%
receiving no timely response or no response. It links this partly to litigation
and changes in servicing, but that explanation is agency-reported context, not
a causal evaluation. The result is a visible service-capacity gap that needs
separate measurement of borrower harm, delay, payment status, and remedy.

## What this adds to the broad program

- **Consumer power:** remedy opportunity depends on product and route, not
  only whether a complaint was submitted.
- **Financial culture:** people use the complaint system after first trying
  firms in many product categories, making escalation part of ordinary
  financial life rather than an exceptional legal act.
- **Firm and sector power:** product-specific response mixes can reflect
  contract structure, servicing, company practice, regulator jurisdiction,
  issue mix, and customer leverage.
- **Trust and exit:** the report does not measure whether an explanation,
  correction, or payment restored trust or changed future use.
- **Public administration:** routing and no-response categories show where the
  complaint institution itself may have limited reach or authority.

## Next test

Build a product-specific case sequence: prior firm contact → CFPB submission →
routing → company response → correction or payment → repeat contact → account
closure, switching, credit outcome, or continued use. Use account or servicing
denominators where available and include non-complainants. For student loans,
add servicing transfer dates, payment status, borrower notices, and delays.

Do not compare the response percentages as causal product rankings, and do not
join CFPB cases to SHED, SIPP, ANES, or CPS respondents without identifiers and
a valid design.

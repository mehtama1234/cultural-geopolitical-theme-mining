# Firm and market power distribution layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** cross-source firm, consumer, worker, and place synthesis; not a single-market concentration estimate

## The broad question

When a firm or sector gains the ability to set terms, where does that power
show up—in prices, waiting, data, work, access, local capacity, or political
voice—and who can resist it?

Market power is not only a high market-share number. It can be the ability to
control a necessary input, rank what people see, set payment terms, make a
record a future gate, shift risk into a household, or leave a community with
few practical alternatives.

```text
firm strategy, ownership, concentration, or rule
  -> price, fee, data, ranking, wait, schedule, access, or risk changes
  -> customer, worker, owner, lender, resident, or public-system response
  -> revenue, margin, debt, time, service, local capacity, or exit changes
  -> trust, identity, political demand, regulation, and state capability
```

## What the current evidence layers establish

| Power channel | Unit already available | What can safely be said | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price and pass-through | Product, payment route, consumer, firm, market | A firm or policy can change the posted or effective cost and the way customers adapt | Who absorbs the cost after income, competition, and alternatives are measured |
| Data and visibility | Platform, seller, customer record, access rule | Control over data can affect entry, ranking, investment, privacy, and customer search | A named US market joined from access terms to data quality and final welfare |
| Financial intermediation | Account, lender, record, customer, institution | Fees, credit, records, and switching rules distribute future access as well as current cash | Whether a specific record becomes a later housing, work, or service gate for the same person |
| Customer recourse | Complaint, company response, product, channel | A response, explanation, narrative, and remedy are different forms of institutional power | The case-level path from response to recovery, trust, switching, or abandonment |
| Work and benefits | Worker, job, firm, task, workplace | Tools, monitoring, benefits, and employer dependence can alter control and job mobility | Who receives the productivity gain and whether worker voice changes the result |
| Local productive capacity | Application, projected formation, employer firm, county, owner | Interest in starting a business is not the same as durable employers, jobs, services, or local ownership | Whether firm entry changes prices, essential access, belonging, or political voice |
| Infrastructure and ownership | Datacenter, grid, public system, firm, country | Capacity can rise alongside private dependence and weak local control | Realized local value, replaceability, public oversight, and geopolitical leverage |

These are adjacent evidence layers, not one dataset. They establish a research
program for tracking distribution, not a universal claim that all firms are
extractive or that concentration always harms consumers.

## The distributional test

For every firm decision, record five separate destinations:

1. **Customer:** price, quality, search, waiting, privacy, safety, remedy, and
   practical ability to switch.
2. **Worker:** pay, hours, schedule, monitoring, health, discretion, benefits,
   and bargaining or exit.
3. **Owner and investor:** revenue, margin, valuation, control, risk, and
   ability to move the asset or capital.
4. **Place and public system:** jobs, tax base, service access, infrastructure
   burden, local ownership, and administrative or environmental cost.
5. **Future user or state:** data, debt, maintenance, strategic dependence,
   resilience, and the ability to replace the provider.

The same decision can transfer value to one destination and risk to another.
For example, automation may improve response speed while reducing human
recourse; a platform may expand seller entry while weakening data investment;
business applications may rise while durable local services do not; and new
infrastructure may create capacity while increasing provider dependence.

## Cultural and political meaning

People do not encounter market power as an abstract elasticity. They encounter
the firm as a price, a queue, a denied appeal, a monitored job, a platform that
cannot be left, a closing local service, or a public system that cannot inspect
its supplier. Those experiences can become judgments about fairness,
independence, deservingness, belonging, corporate legitimacy, or government
competence.

But the meaning is not automatic. The same high price can be interpreted as
scarcity, corporate abuse, government failure, or personal bad luck. The same
continued use can mean satisfaction, dependence, social necessity, or no viable
alternative. Political research must measure attribution, identity,
information, trust, and action rather than infer them from a firm outcome.

## Next bounded firm-event test

Select one firm decision and follow it across matched markets or cases. Capture:

- the decision, ownership, concentration, contract, rule, or public support;
- the terms before and after for customers, workers, owners, and public systems;
- available substitutes, switching cost, appeal route, and practical exit;
- price, quality, waiting, data, pay, schedule, service, and risk outcomes;
- who benefited, who paid, who waited, and who carried the uncertainty;
- complaints, correction, remedy, closure, entry, investment, or public response;
- later trust, local identity, political demand, regulation, or state capacity.

Use a counterexample in which a similar firm decision did not shift the burden,
or where customers or workers had a real alternative. Separate applications
from durable firms, revenue from welfare, response from remedy, capacity from
local control, and continued use from free choice.

## What would change the working picture

The interpretation would weaken if firm decisions did not change effective
terms after alternatives were measured; shifted costs were not concentrated
among particular groups; customers and workers could correct or leave without
meaningful loss; local entry reliably produced durable services and jobs; and
greater infrastructure capacity reliably came with replaceability and public
control.

## Relationship to the broad program

This layer connects household room, time, consumer recourse, platforms,
work, unequal exposure, housing and place, public systems, trust, political
action, infrastructure, and geopolitics. Its central question is: when firms
organize the conditions of everyday life, who receives the gain, who absorbs
the cost, who can object, and how does that distribution become a social or
political trend?

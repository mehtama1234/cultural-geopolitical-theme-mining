# A price-discrimination order defines a small-business compensation trigger while recovery remains open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** alleged supplier price discrimination → proposed compensation and monitoring control; retailer and local-market conversion remains open

The FTC's October 2, 2026 Southern Glazer's settlement adds a firm-sector
power route beneath small-business capacity, consumer choice, and local-market
themes. The complaint alleged that large chain retailers received discounts and
rebates unavailable to small independent competitors, including higher prices
for identical bottles sold during the same period to nearby independent and
chain retailers.

The proposed stipulated order makes the remedy operational. It covers nearly
all Southern wine and spirits sales to the five largest chain retailers in 26
states. A qualifying paired transaction can trigger a violation when the price
discrimination exceeds a state-specific operating-cost threshold or recurs at
more than $5,000 over 12 months. Southern can resolve a qualifying violation by
paying 1.5 times the aggregate price differential; if it does not redress and
the FTC prevails, the order provides for double the differential. An
independent monitor would oversee the order for six years.

The evidence boundary remains material. The order is proposed and has force of
law only after court approval. The public record does not identify the affected
retailer denominator, actual price differentials, claims, payments, price
correction, business survival, customer prices, or restored local competition.
A compensation formula can deter or price a violation without restoring lost
sales, margins, customer relationships, or household benefit.

# Finding 001: Small-business employment, revenue, profitability, and debt move on different tracks

**Status:** bounded firm-capacity and local-economic finding · **Checked: 2026-09-22**

## The bounded finding

The Federal Reserve's 2025 Small Business Credit Survey shows that small firms
do not have one economic condition. In the survey, more respondents reported
lower than higher revenue over the prior year, and the smallest firms drove
that result. The share expecting higher revenue in 2026 fell 6 percentage
points from the prior year, and smaller firms reported less pricing power than
larger firms.

At the same time, employment moved differently: small firms reported positive
employment growth while larger firms' employment growth declined but remained
positive. The survey also reports approximately $70,000 of debt per employee
on average. In the earlier survey year, the profitability diffusion index was
13 percentage points, far below its 46-point December 2019 comparator.

The practical route is:

```text
local demand, costs, financing, and firm size
  -> revenue, pricing power, profitability, debt, and employment
  -> owner and worker decisions
  -> [open] wages, hours, survival, local services, household security, and exit
```

The broader insight is that employment is not a complete measure of firm or
local economic health. A small business can keep or add workers while facing
weak revenue expectations, limited pricing power, debt exposure, or thin
profitability. Conversely, a firm-level improvement does not prove better pay,
customer prices, owner security, or durable local prosperity.

## Limits and next test

- The Small Business Credit Survey is a non-random survey of employer firms;
  question-specific samples and denominators differ.
- Diffusion indexes are not profit margins, revenue levels, or survival rates.
- Reported employment growth does not show wages, hours, job quality, or
  retention.
- Debt per employee does not show interest rates, affordability, repayment, or
  firm-level distress.
- The survey does not link firms to owners' households, local customers,
  public benefits, or later exit.

The next decisive evidence would link a firm to realized revenue, prices,
financing terms, debt repayment, worker pay and hours, employment retention,
customer access, and survival or exit.

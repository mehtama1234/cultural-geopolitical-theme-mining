# A national shock can be sharper for a region's households and firms

**Status:** provisional regional cross-source finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-13

## The bounded finding

The New York Fed's April 2026 regional EHI report shows a counterexample to
reading national averages as local experience. In the Second District,
inflation was 5.0% versus 3.7% nationally, and transportation inflation was
9.0%. Hispanic and Black households had estimated inflation 1.0 and 0.8
percentage points above the regional average; lower-income households were
0.53 points above, while high-income households were 0.62 points below.

The regional firm layer is also more adverse than the national summary: firms
reported large declines in revenue and employment growth, the net share
expecting employment generation in 2026 fell 16 percentage points, and large
firms had negative employment-growth expectations for the first time in the
survey. Regional profitability was 8 points in December 2024 versus 13 points
nationally.

This supports a place-conditioned material-capacity hypothesis. It does not
establish that regional household hardship caused firm weakness, or that either
changed trust or political action.

## What is measured

| Layer | Second District result | Limit |
|---|---:|---|
| Regional inflation | 5.0% in April 2026 versus 3.7% national | Derived regional indicator; not an official demographic CPI |
| Transportation inflation | 9.0% | Category average, not an individual fuel bill |
| Income exposure | Lower-income +0.53 points; high-income −0.62 points from regional average | Basket composition and assigned prices, not household causal exposure |
| Real fuel response | Higher-income groups cut real gas consumption less; lower-income groups cut more | Receipt-panel direction; vehicle, commute, and necessity are not observed here |
| Firm capacity | Employment expectations fell 16 points; regional profitability was 8 points versus 13 nationally | Weighted, non-random SBCS employer-firm sample; local establishments and services are not directly measured |

## Route under test

```text
regional price and transport shock
  -> household basket and room to respond
  -> fuel/travel adaptation and local demand
  -> firm revenue, employment, pricing, and financing
  -> services, jobs, place attachment, and political interpretation
```

The first three stages have regional indicators. The service, worker-household,
meaning, and political stages remain open.

## Method boundary

The regional inflation geography is the New York Fed Second District; the
regional small-business analysis covers New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut.
The EHI combines CEX budget shares with CPI data and uses group-specific
weights. The SBCS is administered to firms with fewer than 500 employees,
focuses here on employer firms, and is weighted but non-random. Its questions
have different reference periods and valid universes.

## Next test

Join county or commuting-zone fuel exposure, transit access, household income
and assets, actual purchases, local establishments, firm revenue/employment,
service availability, and later trust or political judgment. Compare the
Second District with a region where the same shock produced a smaller
transportation burden, while preserving the possibility that households and
firms absorb the shock through different channels.

**Evidence status:** official regional indicator and firm-survey evidence with
explicit geography and method limits; no linked household-firm causal result.

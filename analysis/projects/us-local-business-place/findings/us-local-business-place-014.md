# Finding 014: A named clinic and listed hours still do not prove usable care

**Status:** bounded place-based care-access finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-21

## The finding

HRSA's primary-care shortage file identifies Health Partners of Western Ohio in
Allen County, Ohio, and its companion service-site file links the same
organization identifier to North Lima Community Health Center. The site is
listed as active, permanent, and open 72 hours per week. The provider's own
pages add medical, oral-health, behavioral-health, pharmacy, social-service,
and substance-use routes, plus a phone and patient-portal path.

That is a real advance beyond a county shortage label. But the public route
still does not show a site-specific appointment slot, wait time, insurance
adjudication, or completed visit. The provider lists overlapping hour lines,
different phone numbers appear in the HRSA and current provider records, and
the portal is login-gated. The provider also reports limited same-day
appointments and sliding-fee rules, but these are organization-level policies,
not proof that a particular person received care or a discount.

```text
shortage designation
  -> named organization and active site
  -> listed hours, services, phone, portal, and price policy
  -> appointment request and paperwork
  -> completed care, continuity, and household relief [open]
```

## Why it matters

This is the missing middle between institutional capacity and lived access. A
place can have a real provider, substantial listed hours, and a public route
while a patient still has to call, reconcile information, use a login-gated
portal, prove income, wait for a slot, travel, or find that the site cannot
substitute for another unavailable provider.

The broader lesson is simple: “there is a clinic” and “I could get care when I
needed it” are different social facts. The friction is not an administrative
footnote; it is part of whether capacity becomes security.

## Do not overread

- HPSA and provider records are administrative and organizational records, not
  patient-utilization data.
- Listed hours are not appointment availability or completed visits.
- Published prices, sliding-fee rules, and same-day policies do not prove
  eligibility, take-up, affordability, or continuity.
- The phone discrepancy and unclear hour display show route uncertainty, not a
  measured access failure rate.

## Next decisive test

Follow a lawful, de-identified appointment episode from need and referral
through call or portal attempt, available slot, travel and wait, insurance or
discount handling, completed care, continuity, and later health, work, or
household effect. Preserve failed contact, successful booking, no-show,
completed care, and switching to another provider as separate outcomes.

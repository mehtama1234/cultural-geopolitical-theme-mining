# Finding 016: A racialized neighborhood startup gap narrowed during COVID without proving durable local security

**Status:** bounded racialized-place and entrepreneurship finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER study using business-registration data from 38 states finds a
long-standing startup deficit in Black neighborhoods. That gap reversed sharply
in 2020, especially in neighborhoods with larger pre-existing deficits. The
relative increase weakened over time but remained elevated through 2024, and
neighborhood-oriented entrepreneurship also remained elevated.

The route is:

```text
long-standing place and opportunity gap
  -> COVID-era shock and changed expectations/resources
  -> relative increase in new business registrations
  -> [open] firm survival, financing, jobs, wages, and local ownership
  -> [open] resident security, neighborhood services, inequality, and displacement
```

This adds a broader racialized-place pattern beside the Starbucks result. It
shows that local entrepreneurship can change sharply even where a long-running
gap existed. It does not tell us whether the new registrations became durable
firms or whether residents experienced better economic security.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

The cultural and political meaning is that neighborhood opportunity is not
fixed, but a surge in visible business formation is not automatically equal
access or durable repair. The places with the largest historical deficit saw
the strongest reversal, which makes the result relevant to unequal exposure and
local institutional trust.

It also warns against a simple story that either says “the gap disappeared” or
“nothing changed.” The measured pattern is both: relative activity improved,
and the older structural difference and its causes remain unresolved.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The public abstract does not expose every effect size, sample count,
  neighborhood definition, or comparison estimate.
- Business registration is not firm survival, profitability, jobs, wages,
  financing, or owner-household security.
- COVID-era changes may reflect emergency or necessity entrepreneurship,
  temporary demand, public aid, migration, registration behavior, or changes in
  comparison neighborhoods.
- The study does not measure customer prices, service quality, local ownership,
  displacement, housing costs, or resident political response.
- A relative increase through 2024 does not prove the historical gap was fully
  closed or that the change will persist.

## Next decisive test

The next useful evidence would link tract registrations to firm survival,
employment, wages, financing, ownership, public support, housing change, and
resident outcomes. Until then, the safe claim is that Black-neighborhood
startup activity rose relative to comparison places during COVID and remained
elevated through 2024, not that local economic security was repaired.

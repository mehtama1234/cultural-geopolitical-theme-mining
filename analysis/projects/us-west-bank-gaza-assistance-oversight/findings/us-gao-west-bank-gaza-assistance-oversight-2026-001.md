# Finding 001: Strategic foreign assistance can continue after program transfer while oversight ownership and anti-terrorism controls remain unfinished

**Status:** bounded foreign-assistance oversight and state-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

GAO reports that the United States has provided more than $7.8 billion in
assistance to the West Bank and Gaza since 1993. For FY2022–2024, USAID
allocated $624 million through 37 prime awards across areas including water
infrastructure and education. State took over administration in July 2025;
all but support for the East Jerusalem Hospital Network had ended.

The transition left an important control question open: State had not determined
which offices would be responsible for future assistance and had not finalized
policies and procedures to prevent diversion to terrorism. GAO recommends
carrying forward mandatory anti-terrorism provisions in award agreements and
timelines for completing compliance audits before awards expire.

The route is:

```text
strategic assistance allocation
  -> award and agency-administration route
  -> compliance, audit, and anti-terrorism control
  -> delivery and partner use
  -> [open] service continuity, beneficiary outcome, legitimacy, and geopolitical effect
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a state-capacity and geopolitical-legitimacy boundary to the
assistance records already in the atlas. A funding stream can be large, awards
can end or transfer between agencies, and some health support can continue while
the responsible office, future procedures, audit timing, and downstream use
remain unsettled.

The finding is not evidence that assistance was diverted or that the remaining
program failed. GAO reports that USAID largely complied with its anti-terrorism
policies and procedures. The point is that compliance of past awards and
readiness for future awards are separate stages.

## Limits and next test

- Aggregate assistance and award counts do not establish award-level delivery,
  service quality, beneficiary outcomes, partner legitimacy, or regional effect.
- An unfinished policy or open recommendation is not proof of diversion,
  misuse, or terrorist financing.
- The report does not trace a named award through delivery, use, incident,
  correction, partner response, and later strategic consequence.
- The remaining East Jerusalem Hospital Network support is a bounded continuing
  route, not evidence of all-program continuity.

The next decisive artifact is a named-award ledger linking office ownership,
award terms, subaward, delivery, audit timing, compliance incident, correction,
recipient use, service continuity, partner response, and later legitimacy or
strategic effect.

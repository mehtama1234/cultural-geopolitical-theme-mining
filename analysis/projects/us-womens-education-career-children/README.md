# Project: Women's education, careers, and children

This project tests whether more education changes several parts of life at
once. A Census working paper uses a school-entry birthdate regression
discontinuity and linked records. It reports that women induced into some
college experienced greater employment and earnings, more professional and
higher-status jobs, more socially meaningful work, and better working
conditions. Their children also had better early-life health and prenatal
inputs.

The safe theme is not “college solves inequality.” It is that a specific
education margin can affect wages, job quality, time and meaning at work, and
children's early-life conditions together. The project must keep local causal
effects, broader access, job quality, maternal time, child health, autonomy,
and household security separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected woman-job-child panel joining school
cutoff exposure, education, occupation, wages, schedules, benefits, work
conditions, pregnancy and prenatal inputs, child health, care time, partner
resources, and later outcomes. The public abstract does not provide that full
mechanism and population bridge.

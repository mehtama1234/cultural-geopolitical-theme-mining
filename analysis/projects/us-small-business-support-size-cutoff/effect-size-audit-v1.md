# Effect-size audit v1: small-business size standards

**Checked:** 2026-09-17  
**Purpose:** add quantitative scale to the size-rule finding without retaining
large administrative files.

## Version boundary

The current NBER Working Paper 35703 (September 2026) reports the broad
directional result and adds wage and patenting outcomes. The numerical values
below come from the earlier June 2024 IZA/Census discussion-paper version,
which studies the same research question but is not assumed identical to the
current revision. They are version-labeled anchors pending a table-level check
against the NBER revision.

## Transcribed design and estimates

- The study exploits quasi-random variation in the timing of industry size
  standard increases and uses confidential US Census Bureau microdata.
- The smallest firms become about **0.5 percentage points more likely to exit**
  after a size-standard increase.
- **Industry-wide firm exits rise 12.7%**, while new firm creation is reported
  as unchanged in the searchable earlier-version result.
- The authors link the shift in small-firm outcomes to a reallocation of
  government procurement contracts from smaller to larger firms.
- The earlier abstract reports that industries become more concentrated and
  growth declines; the current NBER abstract additionally reports lower wages
  and patenting.

## Interpretation boundary

The 0.5-point estimate is a firm-level exit probability change, while 12.7% is
an industry-wide relative change; they are not interchangeable and should not
be combined into one effect. Neither identifies customer prices, product
quality, worker hours or benefits, owner-household income, or public trust.

The quasi-random timing design strengthens the allocation claim relative to a
simple cross-section, but the estimates remain tied to the studied US size
standards and implementation episodes. The direction of the current NBER
revision should be treated as authoritative for the latest paper; the earlier
values are historical version-specific evidence.

## What this changes in the atlas

The policy boundary now has a measurable scale: broadening eligibility can
increase access for larger firms while imposing a small-firm exit margin and a
larger industry-level dynamism cost. The result still does not say whether the
larger firms' products, wages, prices, or resilience improve enough to offset
the loss of smaller-firm entry and survival.

## Reproduction and storage decision

This is a transcription audit, not a replication. No PDF, code, Census
microdata, procurement records, or firm identifiers were downloaded or
retained. The next check is the current NBER version's tables and standard
errors, using the smallest official supplementary artifact available.

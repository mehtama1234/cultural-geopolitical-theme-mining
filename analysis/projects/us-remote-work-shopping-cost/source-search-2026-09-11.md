# Source search: US remote work and shopping cost

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; shopping effects are measured, household welfare remains open

## Working question

When work moves home, who gains time and who takes on the shopping system?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-SHOPPING-FROM-HOME | [Shopping From Home](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34883) | A 2026 study using a new survey and Nielsen consumer-panel data finds that working from home increased online shopping and weekday store trips, raised total spending and product quantity and range, and shifted spending toward food and general merchandise. It also finds higher prices paid, less deal use and lower price sensitivity, especially as shopping responsibility shifted in married households. | NBER working paper | It does not show whether higher spending reflects need, choice or welfare, nor the full unpaid-work and time tradeoff for the same household. |

## First pattern to test

```text
remote work location
  -> shopping time, mode and household responsibility
  -> product range, deal use and price paid
  -> total spending and unpaid work
  -> household time, money and customer welfare
```

The source supports changes in shopping behavior and prices paid. It does not settle whether the household gained or lost after counting convenience, product range, time and money together.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Higher spending can buy more goods, better access or useful convenience. Fewer deal searches can be a choice when time is more valuable than the price difference.

## Main gaps

- commute time saved and shopping time added;
- who makes the purchases and who does related unpaid work;
- product need, quality and substitution;
- income, price sensitivity and deal use;
- total household welfare and stress;
- differences by children, gender, marriage, race, place and remote-work access.

## Decision rule

Keep time, price, quantity, convenience and unpaid work separate. If spending rises, do not call it harm until the household's need and value are visible.

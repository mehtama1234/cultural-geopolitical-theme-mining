# SHED food pressure and adaptation layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Status:** official repeated annual snapshots; descriptive, not a panel

The Federal Reserve's SHED reports provide a household-resource and adaptation
context around the BLS food-price and USDA food-security layers. In 2024, 7%
of adults reported that household members sometimes or often did not have
enough to eat in the prior month; the corresponding 2025 figure was 8%. The
published income gradient was wide in both years: 19% versus 2% in 2024 and
21% versus 1% in 2025 for the below-$25,000 and $100,000-or-more groups.

The 2024 SHED also reports multiple adaptations to higher prices: 63% switched
to cheaper products, 61% used less or stopped using a product, 46% delayed a
major purchase, 43% reduced savings, 16% increased borrowing, and 18% worked
more or got another job; 79% took at least one listed action. These measures
are multiple-response population snapshots, not an ordered household sequence
and not food-specific adaptations.

The [machine-readable SHED trend record](../../records/us-shed-food-pressure-adaptation-2024-2025.json)
preserves the separate year, income, food, price-impact, bill, and action
universes. The source reports are [SHED 2024 income and expenses](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-us-households-in-2024-income-and-expenses.htm)
and [SHED 2025 economic hardships](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-us-households-in-2025-economic-hardships.htm).

## Interpretation boundary

The 2024 and 2025 figures are annual cross-sectional snapshots, not a linked
respondent panel in this record. The SHED food-insufficiency question refers to
the prior month, whereas USDA's principal measure refers to the prior year;
the BLS index is a national price series. Together these sources establish a
useful pressure–security–adaptation comparison, not a household-specific price
shock, causal food-insecurity estimate, benefit-adequacy result, or downstream
health, care, trust, political-action, or recovery path.

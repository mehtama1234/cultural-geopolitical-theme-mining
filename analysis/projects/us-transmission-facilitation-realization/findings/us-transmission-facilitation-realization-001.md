# Finding 001: Federal transmission support can be real while the line is still unbuilt, withdrawn, or not yet delivering service

**Status:** bounded infrastructure and public-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

DOE's Transmission Facilitation Program uses federal capacity contracts to help
transmission projects overcome financing barriers. DOE's program page says the
first round involved four projects and the second round selected four more
capacity-contract projects, with roughly $1.5 billion associated with the
second-round selections. The current DOE selections page records later
termination or withdrawal notices for SWIP-N, Cross-Tie, Twin States, and
Cimarron Link. It describes the remaining projects with planned capacity and
award amounts, but does not show that they are energized and providing service.

DOE's own program rules keep the stages separate: conditional selection is not
a formal funding commitment, and DOE says it does not pay for transmission
service until construction is complete and the line is energized and ready to
provide service.

The route is:

```text
federal selection or capacity support
  -> negotiation, permitting, financing, and construction
  -> energization and transmission service
  -> cost recovery, rate effects, reliability, and community benefit [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a clear infrastructure version of a recurring subtheme:

> Public capacity can reduce the financing barrier without guaranteeing that
> the physical service arrives.

It also shows why infrastructure claims need a stage ledger. A project can be
politically announced, financially selected, and described in megawatts while
the decisive public question—whether a line is energized and who receives or
pays for its service—remains unanswered.

## Limits and next test

- The DOE page is a program and project-status record, not a complete national
  transmission construction database.
- A termination or withdrawal can lead to replacement financing, a redesigned
  project, or a different route; it does not by itself prove public loss.
- The page does not provide a project-level completion denominator, construction
  dates, energization dates, delivered flow, rate effects, reliability results,
  or household outcomes for every selection.
- Planned capacity and award amounts are not actual electricity delivered.
- The source does not measure local consent, land impacts, environmental effects,
  or who ultimately bears costs.

The next decisive artifact is a project-linked ledger joining selection,
contract, permitting, construction, energization, transmission flow, cost
recovery, rate allocation, and local reliability. Until then, the safe claim is
that federal support and planned capacity do not establish realized service.

# Source search: US safe temperature and energy insecurity

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** matched evidence pass complete; same-household health and recovery records remain open

## Working question

When energy costs or broken equipment make a home unsafe, what does the household give up first—and does help restore a safe temperature?

## Sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| EIA-RECS-2024-HC11.1 | [EIA: Household energy insecurity, 2024](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/hc/pdf/HC11.1_2024.pdf) | Preliminary 2024 RECS estimates cover 132.54 million homes. They count 43.56 million with at least one measured energy-insecurity problem, 32.89 million reducing or forgoing food or medicine, 17.55 million leaving home at an unhealthy temperature, 16.19 million receiving a disconnect or delivery-stop notice, 6.83 million unable to use heating equipment, and 8.16 million unable to use air-conditioning equipment. | These are reported household conditions and the categories can overlap. They do not show which problem came first or prove a health effect. |
| EIA-RECS-2024 | [EIA: 2024 Residential Energy Consumption Survey](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/) | The survey covers all 50 states and DC and includes household energy insecurity among its first preliminary releases. | The release is preliminary and does not join every bill, temperature, health, and work record for one family. |
| DOE-WAP-APPLY | [DOE: How to apply for weatherization](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/wap/how-apply-weatherization-assistance) | Weatherization audits energy use and home safety, accepts renters and owners, and prioritizes high-burden and vulnerable households. | Program access and eligibility do not prove that work was completed or that the home became safer. |
| DOE-LEAD-HEALTH | [DOE: Low-Income Energy Affordability Data tool](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool) | DOE places energy affordability beside indoor air quality, safety, comfort, and health in describing the problem and possible response. | This is a planning and program source, not a causal estimate of health improvement. |

## First pattern to test

```text
energy bill or equipment failure
  -> unsafe temperature, skipped food or medicine, or shutoff risk
  -> illness, lost work, care burden, or housing strain
  -> help, repair, debt, relocation, or continued sacrifice
```

The EIA table shows the household tradeoffs. DOE shows where public help is meant to enter. The link to actual health, work, and later housing outcomes is still open.

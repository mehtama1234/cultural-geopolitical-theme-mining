# Source search: US vehicle repair as a household shock

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; mechanism is plausible, same-household evidence remains open

## Working question

When a car repair arrives before the next paycheck, what does the household give up to keep moving?

## First pattern to test

```text
unexpected repair
  -> cash is not enough before payday
  -> borrow, delay another payment, ask family, or stop a trip
  -> work, care, food and service access change
  -> later debt, missed income, or a move to another place
```

The opening sources support the first timing problem and the value of access. They do not yet show the full chain for the same US household. That missing link is the useful next test.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

A repair can preserve a job, a medical visit or a child's school trip. Paying for it is not automatically a loss. The question is what the household could still pay or reach afterward.

## Main gaps

- repair size and timing relative to pay;
- whether the car was the only usable route;
- work hours, care visits and food trips after the repair;
- cash, credit, family help and delayed-payment choices;
- differences by income, race, age, disability and place;
- whether public transit or repair aid prevented a larger loss.

## Decision rule

Pair an emergency-expense measure with observed transport use and a later work, care or payment outcome. Until then, call this a timing and access question, not a proven job-loss effect.

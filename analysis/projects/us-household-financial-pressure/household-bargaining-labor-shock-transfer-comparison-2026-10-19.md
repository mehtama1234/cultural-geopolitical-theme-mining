# Household bargaining: labor shocks, cash transfers, and visible consumption

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded comparison of household allocation and spillover evidence; no direct bargaining or welfare index

Two different shocks show why household room and household power cannot be
read from one spending or employment measure.

| Surface | Evidence | Observed | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gendered labor-market shocks | NBER W35118: robot and fracking exposure linked to product-level consumption | Robot exposure shifted spending toward goods more commonly bought by single women and toward daughter-associated products, without changing total child-product spending; fracking evidence for male-favored goods was weaker | Product composition is not direct decision authority, welfare, child wellbeing, or parental time |
| Individual guaranteed income | NBER W35589: 1,000 treatment and 2,000 control participants | $1,000/month for three years produced mixed household spillovers: lower nonrecipient household income and partner advancement, more education/training and family transfers, suggestive relationship/trust gains, limited aggregate stability change, and qualified healthcare/safety results | A lower partner employment outcome may reflect care, education, health, or choice; effects are outcome- and household-specific |

## Bounded synthesis

The labor-shock study observes where household spending moves; the transfer
experiment observes how money assigned to one person can diffuse through other
household members' work, education, transfers, relationships, and healthcare
decisions. Neither directly observes bargaining power. Composition changes can
be a household adaptation without proving better wellbeing, while reduced
partner employment can be a cost, a chosen trade-off, or a care investment.

The common route is shock or resource → relative economic position → spending,
work, care, or relationship adjustment → household security and meaning. The
records support the middle adjustment stage but do not identify a universal
direction for security, decision rights, or trust.

## Next bounded test

The next useful input is a household panel that observes income or labor shock,
who controls money, unpaid care and time, product or service use, health and
child outcomes, and persistence after the shock or transfer ends. Do not pool
the robot, fracking, and guaranteed-income estimands.

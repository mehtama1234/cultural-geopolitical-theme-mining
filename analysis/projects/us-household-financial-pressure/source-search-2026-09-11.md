# Source search: US household financial pressure and the price of access

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short opening packet; no settled finding; move on after the next verification pass
**Search rule:** begin with HBS/NBER discovery, then move to US primary records and a small comparison layer.

## Working question

Do payment and credit products lower the visible barrier to purchase while moving cost and risk toward households with less liquidity, weaker credit access, or fewer ways to avoid the system?

## First mechanism map

```text
payment or credit rule
  -> visible price, fee, reward, or monthly payment
  -> purchase or borrowing choice
  -> household balance, stress, or buffer changes
  -> later spending, work, health, housing, or family decision
  -> firm revenue and risk allocation
  -> public complaint, regulation, court action, or political demand
```

Only the first few links are represented in the opening sources. The later links remain open until we connect household data to firm records and institutional action.

## Known gaps

- the underlying HBS paper on payment rewards and its identification;
- current US transaction-level evidence by payment method and income;
- state and local differences in fees, enforcement, and household buffers;
- firm pricing and pass-through records rather than only household outcomes;
- whether financial stress changes political behavior, beyond reported concern;
- comparisons across race, age, disability, family type, geography, and immigration status;
- evidence that challenges the risk-transfer interpretation;
- a time series that separates inflation, interest rates, income loss, and product design.

## Next test

Pair the 2025 SHED subgroup measures with CFPB market and complaint records, BLS price and expenditure measures, and one product-level study. The first finding should not be written until we can state whether the observed burden comes from the fee itself, the payment method, the household's liquidity, or a different factor.

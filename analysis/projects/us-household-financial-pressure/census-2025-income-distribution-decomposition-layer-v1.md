# Census 2025 income-distribution decomposition layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** bounded national distribution finding; not a same-household panel

## What the release separates

The Census Bureau reports real median household income of **$87,460 in 2025**,
up 2.6% from 2024 and the highest in the series since 1967. But the movement
was not uniform across the distribution. Income at the 90th percentile rose
for a third consecutive year, while pre- and post-tax income at the 10th
percentile did not differ significantly from 2024.

Over the longer 1967–2025 period, pretax household income rose about 56% at
both the 10th and 50th percentiles, versus about 121% at the 90th percentile.
The 90th-to-10th percentile ratio widened from 9.23 in 1967 to 13.06 in 2025.

## Interpretation boundary

The safe finding is not that every high-income household benefited or that
every low-income household lost ground. It is that a record median can conceal
different movement above and below the midpoint. Median improvement, bottom-
distribution movement, inequality, disposable resources, and household
security are separate objects.

## Next decisive field

Join percentile movement to household composition, prices, taxes and credits,
benefits, debt, liquid assets, housing and health costs, work intensity, and

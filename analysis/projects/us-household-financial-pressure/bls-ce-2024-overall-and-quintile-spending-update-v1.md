# BLS 2024 consumer spending composition and income-quintile update v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** official published consumer-unit comparison; not a household welfare or causal estimate

## What this adds

The BLS annual report provides two complementary views of the 2024 consumer
adjustment: the average consumer unit's budget composition and the different
income-and-expenditure movements observed across income quintiles. The first
shows where the budget is concentrated; the second shows why one national
spending change cannot represent every household's room to adapt.

## Published 2024 composition

Average annual expenditures were **$78,535 per consumer unit**, up 1.8 percent
from 2023. The largest reported shares were housing (**33.4%**), transportation
(**17.0%**), food (**12.9%**), personal insurance and pensions (**12.5%**),
and healthcare (**7.9%**). These are nominal consumer-unit averages, not
individual household cash-flow observations.

## Income-quintile divergence

| Consumer-unit income group | Income before taxes, change from 2023 | Average expenditures, change from 2023 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lowest quintile | +6.8% | +3.8% |
| Second quintile | +5.3% | +2.3% |
| Third quintile | +4.8% | +2.3% |
| Fourth quintile | +4.1% | +2.4% |
| Highest quintile | 0.0% | +0.4% |

BLS reports that the lowest-quintile expenditure increase was concentrated in
housing, while housing was also the largest dollar contributor to the increase
for the fourth quintile. The comparison is therefore consistent with unequal
budget exposure, but it does not identify whether spending increases reflect
higher prices, more quantity, unavoidable bills, substitution, or deferred
need.

## Contribution to the broad societal trend atlas

This strengthens the **household room and consumption**, **unequal exposure and
status**, and **housing/place/mobility** themes. It establishes a current
population-average distributional anchor:

```text
income position and budget composition
  -> different observed spending movements
  -> possible unequal room to adapt
```

The next arrows remain open: a dated price or payment event, the alternative
available to the same unit, the protected or sacrificed outcome, debt or time
substitution, recovery, trust, political action, and exit. Income quintiles are
consumer-unit groupings and should not be merged with person-level SIPP, SHED,
ANES, or HTOPS observations.

## Source and limits

The source is the [BLS Consumer Expenditures in 2024 report](https://www.bls.gov/opub/reports/consumer-expenditures/2024/home.htm),
published August 2026, supported by the [BLS Consumer Expenditure annual
release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.htm). BLS states that the
report draws on the Diary and Interview Surveys and presents nominal dollars
reported by surveyed consumer units. The report is a published aggregate
summary; no raw file or spreadsheet was downloaded for this update.

Do not read the result as a welfare index, a liquidity measure, a within-unit
panel transition, a causal price response, or evidence of political judgment.
The existing [income-quintile distribution layer](ce-2024-income-quintile-distribution-layer-v1.md)
contains the earlier bounded comparison and its detailed next-test boundary.

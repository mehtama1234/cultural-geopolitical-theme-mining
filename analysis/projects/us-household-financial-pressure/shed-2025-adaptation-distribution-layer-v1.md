# SHED 2025 price adaptation distribution layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Unit:** US adult respondent; 2025 Federal Reserve SHED  
**Method:** weighted descriptive percentages using the public-use `weight`; each metric uses its own nonmissing denominator; no design-based standard errors or causal estimate

## The question

Do people face the same adaptation menu when prices worsen their finances, or
does room to absorb pressure differ across income, age, ethnicity, and work
status? This is a population distribution question inside the broader
societal program. It is not a one-household explanation.

## What this adds to the broad societal program

1. **Adaptation is stratified.** The same price environment reaches groups
   through different combinations of substitution, reduced use, saving loss,
   borrowing, delayed purchases, and added labor.
2. **Buffer and exposure are different axes.** Emergency capacity rises with
   income and age in these descriptive cuts, while reported pressure and
   adaptation do not follow a single monotonic pattern across every group.
3. **Demographic categories are not mechanisms.** They locate unequal exposure;
   they do not explain whether the relevant cause is income, wealth, debt,
   housing, care, discrimination, place, job control, or social support.
4. **Consumer change can become social reproduction.** A purchase cut may
   protect cash while reducing food quality, care, transport, health, or
   participation; extra work may preserve a bill while displacing family or
   civic time.
5. **Political meaning remains separate.** These adaptations can be compared
   with trust, attribution, identity, and action measures, but they do not
   establish distrust, polarization, or voting behavior.

## Boundaries and next test

The SHED categories are self-reported, cross-sectional, and weighted. The
income field is a broad reported category, not wealth or liquid cash. The
tables do not identify a specific price, product, firm, policy, household
bill, health outcome, food-security transition, or later recovery. Subgroup
differences may reflect composition and measurement error, and no uncertainty
intervals are computed here.

The next depth test is an intersectional panel comparison using valid repeated
fields: financial-condition path × income or employment × race/ethnicity,
with panel weights and uncertainty estimates. Then follow a dated essential
expense into food, care, work, debt, assistance, remedy, and later trust or
action. Include counterexamples where similar exposure is absorbed without the
predicted sacrifice or loss of buffer.

## Reproduction

```text
python3 scripts/analyze_shed_price_pressure.py \
  --input /path/to/SHED_2025.csv.zip \
  --output /tmp/shed-2025.json
```

The calculation uses the official [Federal Reserve SHED data release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed_data.htm) and the existing project script. Raw files and generated JSON are not committed.

# Census 2025 income, poverty, and insurance vintage layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-17
**Source:** Census Bureau, 2025 Income, Poverty and Health Insurance Coverage release
**Unit:** CPS ASEC households and people; national estimates
**Status:** current national descriptive release; no causal household pathway

## Why this update matters

The September 15, 2026 Census release is the latest national checkpoint for
income, poverty, and health-insurance coverage. It is useful precisely because
the measures do not all move together. The release reports a record real
median household income, a lower official poverty rate, an SPM rate not
statistically different from 2024, and a full-year uninsured rate also not
statistically different from 2024. These are different clocks and universes,
not a single household-wellbeing score.

## Recorded release surfaces

| Surface | 2025 result | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Real median household income | $87,460 | A national median-income checkpoint; Census describes it as the highest in the series since 1967 | Equal gains, liquidity, spending room, or household-level improvement |
| Official poverty | 10.2%; 34.5 million people | Pretax-money-income poverty status under the official thresholds | Post-tax resources, noncash benefits, medical costs, geography, or a causal explanation |
| Child official poverty | 13.4% | A child-population distributional endpoint; reported historic low | Family shielding, food security, care access, or child-level event histories |
| Supplemental Poverty Measure | 13.1%; not statistically different from 2024 | A broader poverty concept including taxes, transfers, work/medical expenses, and housing-cost geography | A bill-to-benefit or exact household recovery episode |
| Uninsured entire year | 7.9%; 26.7 million people; not statistically different from 2024 | A national full-year coverage checkpoint | Underinsurance, network access, care delay, medical debt, or treatment continuity |

The central finding is not simply “income improved.” The release creates a
useful split between a median-income high, an official-poverty decline, and
SPM/insurance measures that did not show statistically significant movement in
the press summary. A median can rise while households below it face different
prices, debts, benefit rules, medical costs, housing markets, or regional
constraints.

## Position in the broad atlas

This layer strengthens the following themes:

- **Household room and consumption:** national income is a context for room,
  not a measure of liquid capacity or spending choice.
- **Unequal exposure and status:** poverty and child-poverty rates describe
  distributional endpoints that must remain separate from the median.
- **Care, health, and social reproduction:** full-year insurance coverage is a
  gateway condition, not proof of affordable or continuous care.
- **Public systems and policy feedback:** the SPM incorporates public programs
  and work/medical expenses conceptually, but does not reveal an individual's
  route through those systems.
- **Political judgment and collective action:** these national measures do
  not observe attribution, trust, action, turnout, or vote.

## Boundaries and next test

The CPS ASEC release is a strong national context layer, but it is not a
longitudinal household event record. It does not identify which price, job,
benefit, bill, insurer, employer, or policy changed a particular household's
condition. It also does not replace the USDA food-security measure, SHED
reported hardship, SIPP monthly transitions, or MEPS event records; those
sources use different units, reference periods, and outcome definitions.

The next valid comparison is a subgroup and same-period design that preserves
the official and SPM universes, income distribution, insurance duration,
medical spending, food security, housing/utility pressure, and work/care
outcomes. A later political interpretation still requires attribution and a
repeated respondent or event-compatible action measure. No report PDF or raw
microdata was downloaded for this vintage checkpoint.

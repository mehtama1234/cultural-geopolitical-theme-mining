# Finding 001: Schools can become safer on average while some students still avoid participation

**Status:** bounded youth safety and institutional-participation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

The NCES/BJS school-safety report shows an important split. At-school nonfatal
victimization for students ages 12–18 fell from 52 per 1,000 in 2012 to 22 per
1,000 in 2022. But in 2021–22, 3.8% of students said they were afraid of attack
or harm at school, 3.6% said they avoided school activities or classes because
of that fear, and 2.2% said they stayed home for that reason.

The route is:

```text
school conditions and perceived safety
  -> fear
  -> avoidance of activities, classes, or school
  -> [open] attendance, learning, support, belonging, and later trust
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a school version of a recurring pattern: aggregate improvement and
individual usability can move on different clocks. A lower national rate is
important, but it does not tell us whether a particular student feels safe
enough to attend a class, join an activity, or remain connected to school.

The finding also keeps fear separate from crime. A student can be afraid without
an observed attack, and a student can experience harm without reporting fear in
the survey. Avoidance may protect the student in the short run, but the source
does not show what happened to attendance, learning, mental health, or trust.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The report combines different surveys, populations, and years; the measures
  are not one same-student sequence.
- Fear is self-reported and does not identify the cause or a school response.
- Avoiding an activity is not automatically a lost educational or social
  outcome; it may be a deliberate protective choice.
- Lower victimization rates do not prove equal safety across schools or groups.

## Next decisive test

Use repeated student or school-linked records that preserve the safety event,
fear, bullying or threat, avoidance, school contact, accommodation or support,
attendance, learning, belonging, and later continuation or exit.

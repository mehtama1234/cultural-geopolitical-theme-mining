# Source search: school safety, fear, and participation

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; aggregate safety improvement does not erase student-level avoidance

## Working question

When official school-crime measures improve, do students still change their
behavior because school feels unsafe?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NCES-SCHOOL-SAFETY-2023 | [Report on Indicators of School Crime and Safety: 2023](https://nces.ed.gov/pubs2024/2024145.pdf) | The joint NCES/BJS report combines student, teacher, principal, and other sources and reports school victimization, bullying, fear, and avoidance. | Official NCES/BJS report | It does not follow a student from fear through attendance, support, learning, or later trust, and its indicators come from different samples and years. |

## First pattern

```text
school conditions and perceived safety
  -> fear of attack or harm
  -> avoid activities, classes, or school
  -> [open] attendance, learning, support, belonging, and later trust
```

The report gives two facts that need to be held together. The at-school
nonfatal victimization rate for students ages 12–18 fell from 52 per 1,000 in
2012 to 22 per 1,000 in 2022. But in 2021–22, 3.8% reported being afraid of
attack or harm at school and 3.6% reported avoiding school activities or classes
because of fear; 2.2% said they stayed home from school for that reason.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The lower victimization rate is an aggregate trend and does not prove that
every student experienced improvement. Fear is not a crime count, and avoidance
does not identify the source of fear or the later academic or social result.
The report combines indicators from different surveys, populations, and years.

## Decision rule

Keep aggregate victimization, bullying, fear, avoidance, attendance, school
response, support, belonging, and learning separate. Reopen this lane only with
repeated student records or a linked school episode that adds response and later
participation outcomes.

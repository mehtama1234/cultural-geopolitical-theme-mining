# Finding 001: A child benefit works through the household, not just the child’s check

**Status:** bounded disability-benefit, family-work, and life-course finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The same disability benefit can have different effects depending on what happens
to the household's total resources afterward.

NBER Working Paper 35725 uses Social Security administrative data and two
quasi-experiments. It reports positive effects when parents do not reduce their
labor supply and the benefit raises household income. When parents reduce
earnings enough to offset the transfer, effects are zero or negative. The study
also reports that the income channel is substantial and that parental work can
improve children's outcomes when it increases total household resources, even
though it may reduce parent time.

The practical chain is:

```text
child disability and SSI eligibility
  -> benefit receipt
  -> household income + parental work + parent time + care
  -> child human capital and later outcomes
  -> adult earnings, disability, security, and family meaning [partly open]
```

The broader social insight is:

> A benefit does not act alone. Its effect depends on whether it adds resources
> to the household or merely replaces income that was already being earned.

## What this adds to the atlas

This gives the public-benefit lane a family-work mechanism. It connects disability,
care, labor supply, household room, and long-term opportunity while preserving
the difference between a parental work response and a simple “work disincentive.”

## Limits and next test

- This is a working paper; the public abstract does not provide every cutoff,
  sample restriction, outcome definition, or estimate.
- The channel decomposition depends on the authors' model assumptions.
- Parental earnings reduction may reflect caregiving, health, or schedule needs,
  not only an incentive response.
- Human capital and adult earnings are not the whole of family wellbeing,
  autonomy, stress, relationships, or durable security.

The next decisive artifact is a protected family-year ledger joining eligibility,
benefit amount, parental work and time, care needs, health, school, household
income, expenses, stress, services, adult earnings, disability, and later
security.

# Finding 001: Geopolitical funding can grow before agencies can show what changed

**Status:** bounded geopolitical, state-capacity, and measurement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed U.S. State Department and USAID funding intended to counter
Chinese influence. The agencies reported an estimated 470 projects worth about
$1.2 billion from fiscal years 2020–2023, with at least $1.625 billion directed
by Congress across fiscal years 2020–2024.

But the portfolio was hard to see as a whole. Officials lacked readily usable
data on project type and status; time frames were missing for 129 projects,
lines of effort for 38, and specific project information for nearly a third of
approved proposals. The agencies had also not assessed results across the
portfolio, and a cross-project monitoring framework was paused while still in
early development.

The route is:

```text
strategic concern
  -> funding and project proposals
  -> partner implementation
  -> local use, trust, and response
  -> [open] changed choices, reduced influence, or durable legitimacy
```

The key insight is not that the funding had no value. It is that strategic
intent and spending can become visible before project-level and portfolio-level
results are comparable enough to guide the next allocation.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This is a geopolitical version of the program's administrative-legibility
theme. A state can possess money, a proposal process, and a strategic goal but
still lack a reliable view of what was funded, where it operated, and what
changed afterward. That makes political accountability and learning harder,
even when individual projects may be useful.

It also adds a counterweight to simple great-power competition stories: a
portfolio can be strategically important while its own evidence system is
unfinished.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The project counts and dollar totals are agency-data estimates with known
  missing fields and errors.
- Missing portfolio evaluation does not prove that projects failed or had no
  local effect.
- The report does not follow named partners through implementation, consent,
  behavior, or later dependence.
- Strategic influence is multidimensional and may not be captured by one
  outcome, although the absence of a shared framework still limits comparison.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a named-project ledger joining proposal and expert
input, award, funds, implementer, partner use, local response, continuation or
termination, and an attributed change in policy, behavior, or dependence.
Until then, the safe claim is that U.S. counter-influence spending had a
portfolio-level visibility and evaluation gap.

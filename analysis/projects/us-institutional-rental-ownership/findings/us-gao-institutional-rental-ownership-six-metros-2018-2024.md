# Finding: Institutional rental ownership grew in selected metros without becoming most single-family housing

**Status:** bounded housing-market and place finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO's 2026 review found that institutional investors increased their holdings
of single-family rental homes from 2018 through 2024 in all six selected metro
areas: Cincinnati, Dallas, Jacksonville, Nashville, Phoenix, and Seattle.

The scale depends on the denominator. By 2024, institutional investors owned
less than 1% to 3% of all single-family homes in those metros, but 4% to 22% of
single-family rental homes. Their presence can therefore be important within
the rental channel without meaning that they own most single-family housing.

GAO also found that acquisitions came from owner-occupants, other
non-owner-occupants, and new construction. In Nashville, for example, 35% of
institutional investor-owned homes were acquired from owner-occupants and 15%
were newly constructed by 2024. Sales out of these portfolios were limited:
they never exceeded 8% of institutional holdings in any year in any of the six
metros.

The safe broader insight is:

> A firm can become more visible and consequential in a specific housing
> channel without controlling the whole housing market.

That distinction matters for cultural and political interpretation. A tenant,
small landlord, prospective buyer, and local official may encounter the same
ownership growth as a question about price, maintenance, stability,
homeownership, or local belonging, but this report does not measure those
experiences.

## What remains open

- rent levels, fees, repairs, code compliance, and eviction;
- tenant stability, housing quality, and ability to contest a landlord;
- whether institutional ownership changes first-time homeownership or
  neighborhood displacement;
- differences between large institutional owners and smaller investors; and
- whether ownership changes produced durable changes in local trust,
  belonging, or political action.

The decisive next artifact is a property- and tenant-protected panel joining
ownership changes to rent, notice, repair, eviction, vacancy, sale, tenure,
home purchase, and neighborhood outcomes. The current GAO report does not
provide that same-household route.

# Finding: mixed native–immigrant founding teams can convert social heterogeneity into startup scale

**Status:** bounded firm-level entrepreneurship finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 33804 links U.S. startups to founders' and employees'
employment and education histories. It reports that startups founded by mixed
native–immigrant teams outperform native-only and immigrant-only teams: they
are larger three years after founding, more likely to secure funding and larger
funding rounds, and more likely to achieve successful exits.

The paper identifies access to diverse labor pools, successful venture
capitalists, and expanded product markets as important channels. An
instrumental-variables strategy using native shares in university-degree
programs reports the same direction for team size and funding access.

The cultural-economic insight is:

> Immigration can operate through team composition and institutional access,
> not only through the individual labor supply. The relevant conversion point
> may be whether heterogeneous founders can jointly reach workers, capital,
> and markets.

## Limits and next test

- The paper does not disclose the exact abstract sample denominator here.
- Startup size, funding, and exits are not worker wages, bargaining power,
  consumer welfare, or community benefit.
- Mixed teams may differ in unobserved education, networks, sectors, legal
  status, geography, or founder selection.
- The proposed labor-pool, venture-capital, and product-market mechanisms are
  not each separately identified in the abstract.

The next decisive artifact is a founder–firm–worker ledger that follows hiring,
wages, retention, referrals, financing, product-market reach, and local
spillovers while preserving founder-team composition as the unit of analysis.

# Source search: US inflation, real wages, and elections

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; local purchasing power and election results are compared, the individual blame and vote path remains open

## Working question

Do voters punish rising prices, falling buying power, or the feeling that pay no longer covers the same life?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-INFLATION-ELECTIONS | [Do Voters Punish Inflation or Pay Cuts? Inflation and Real Wages in U.S. Elections](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35301) | Using US county data on family-budget costs, nominal income and election results from 2021 to 2024, the authors find that falling real wages, rather than higher inflation alone, predict gains for Republican candidates. Inflation still has an association with presidential vote share beyond the real-wage measure. | NBER working paper | County results do not show the same voter's price basket, pay change, blame, media path or vote. The result is predictive, not proof of one person's reason. |

## First pattern to test

```text
local prices and pay
  -> buying power and household room
  -> blame or economic story
  -> vote share, turnout or policy demand
```

The source supports a local real-wage and election relationship. It does not establish the full individual or family path.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Inflation may still matter through prices people notice, expectations, or national judgment even when local real wages are included. Elections also reflect candidates, parties, identity and events outside the household budget.

## Main gaps

- each voter's actual price basket and pay;
- debt, housing and benefit changes;
- media and party messages;
- turnout and vote choice for the same person;
- differences by race, age, income and place;
- whether a later policy changed the household result.

## Decision rule

Keep local prices, real wages, household experience, blame and vote as separate steps until individual-level evidence connects them.

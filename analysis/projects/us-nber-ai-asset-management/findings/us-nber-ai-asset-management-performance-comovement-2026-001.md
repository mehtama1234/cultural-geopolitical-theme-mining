# Finding: AI investing grows while early performance advantages decay

**Status:** bounded AI-finance and market-structure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35273 combines investment-adviser regulatory disclosures,
labor-market data, fund strategy descriptions, and performance data to track
AI-driven investing. The paper reports steady growth since the early 2010s,
concentrated among hedge funds.

AI hedge funds outperformed non-AI hedge funds in the early years, but the
advantage declined over time, including among early adopters. Contrary to a
simple homogeneity story, AI hedge funds also exhibited lower return comovement
than non-AI peers.

The atlas-relevant insight is:

> Financial AI can diffuse as an organizational capability without producing a
> permanent performance advantage. Adoption, alpha, strategy similarity, and
> systemic risk are separate stages.

## Limits and next test

- The abstract does not disclose full fund-level denominators, classification
  error, strategy thresholds, or every effect size.
- Disclosure language can distinguish direct investment use from research,
  operations, or risk discussion imperfectly.
- Performance comparisons do not prove causal AI alpha, household gains, or
  lower systemic risk.
- The next test is a fund-level ledger joining AI use, model type, data,
  leverage, factor exposure, fees, investor flows, returns, drawdowns,
  comovement, and regulatory response.

# Finding 001: A competing platform can give gig workers more leverage

**Status:** bounded gig-work, competition, and worker-optionality finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER study combines randomized pay experiments among Uber drivers with a
natural experiment in access to a competing platform. It reports that access
to one competitor nearly doubled drivers' firm-specific labor-supply
elasticity—their willingness to change labor supplied to Uber when conditions
changed.

The study estimates that the implied monopsony markdown fell from about 68% to
about 50%. It also reports that women were about twice as responsive to the
market as men, while being no less responsive to their employer.

The route is:

```text
access to another platform
  -> ability to shift labor away from Uber
  -> greater firm-specific responsiveness and lower implied markdown
  -> [open] net earnings, time cost, security, and durable worker control
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This is a concrete test of a recurring program idea:

> A formal alternative can create leverage even before it produces a better
> life—but only if workers can actually use it.

It adds a firm-and-worker version of practical exit. Competition is not just a
market count; it can change how strongly workers respond to one firm. But the
study does not show whether drivers earned more after fuel, vehicle, insurance,
tax, waiting, and unpaid-time costs, or whether they became safer or more
secure.

## Limits and next test

- The public abstract does not disclose the exact driver count or complete
  study-area frame.
- Elasticity and implied markdown are not the same as take-home pay or welfare.
- Competitor access may be technically available but hard to use because of
  local demand, account rules, scheduling, vehicle costs, or care constraints.
- The result concerns Uber drivers and does not automatically generalize to all
  contractors, employees, or platforms.

The next decisive U.S. test is a protected driver-period ledger joining both
platform accounts, offers, hours, trips, gross and net pay, expenses, waiting,
deactivation, safety, switching, and household consequences. Until then, the
safe claim is that competitor access can improve a worker's leverage with a
platform without proving durable economic security.

## Source

- [NBER Working Paper 35626: Outside Options and Labor Supply](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35626)

**Evidence status:** experimental and natural-experiment evidence supports a
competition-to-worker-response pathway; net household benefit and durable exit
remain open.

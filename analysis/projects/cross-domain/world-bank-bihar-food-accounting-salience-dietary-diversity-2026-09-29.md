# Salience can change food habits, but it cannot manufacture food access

## Finding

The World Bank's cluster-randomized trial across 2,682 households in Bihar gave mothers of children aged 6–24 months a wall-mounted food-tracking journal. The intervention increased attention to and automaticity of feeding behaviors and raised the likelihood that children achieved minimum dietary diversity. It did not change the quantity of food fed or children's mid-upper arm circumference, and dietary-diversity gains were small five months after treatment.

The deeper finding is a **salience-within-constraint** mechanism. A low-cost tool can make a behavior easier to notice, remember, and repeat. But it cannot by itself create household income, food availability, market access, time, or care capacity. The intervention's value is therefore complementary: it can help a larger nutrition system work, but cannot replace that system.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This extends the Tunisia agency-measurement record from measurement visibility into household action. What becomes visible can change behavior, but the conversion from attention to material outcome is conditional on resources and relationships.

`food choice becomes visible -> attention and habit -> dietary diversity`

`dietary diversity -> adequate quantity and nutritional status`

The second arrow did not follow automatically in this trial. Frontline-worker effects show that delivery relationships are part of the intervention, while household food availability and income limit what tracking can accomplish. Better-off households showed larger gains in knowledge, perceived norms, and habit formation, but dietary-diversity treatment effects did not differ by household economic status in the reported study.

## Cultural and social significance

- Household consumption is partly an accounting and attention practice, not only a preference or knowledge problem.
- Behavior-change tools can shift unpaid caregiving work toward more deliberate monitoring; that work should not disappear from the analysis simply because the tool is cheap.
- Frontline workers are social infrastructure: implementation quality and trust mediate whether a reminder becomes a routine.
- Consumer and nutrition policy must distinguish “knowing what to do,” “remembering to do it,” “being able to buy it,” and “achieving health outcomes.”

## Boundary and next test

The RCT establishes short-run behavior and dietary-diversity effects, not long-run growth, food security, or health. The next test is whether salience tools paired with food access, income, market, and care supports produce durable nutritional gains without shifting more monitoring labor onto mothers.

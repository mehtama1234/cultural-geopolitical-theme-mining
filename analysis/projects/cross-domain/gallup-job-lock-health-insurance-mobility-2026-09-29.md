# Employer health insurance is constraining job mobility

## Finding

West Health and Gallup report that 24% of U.S. workers who rely primarily on employer-sponsored health insurance stay in a job they want to leave because they fear losing coverage—about 23 million adults. The reported share is eight percentage points higher than in 2021.

The finding makes healthcare an employment and mobility institution. Coverage is not only a benefit attached to a job; it can become a condition that limits exit, bargaining, training, entrepreneurship, relocation and family decisions.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`healthcare need or cost risk -> employer coverage dependence -> job lock -> reduced mobility and bargaining room -> household and life-course constraint`

The exposure is sharply differentiated. Job lock is reported by 44% of workers with medical debt versus 21% without medical debt, 29% of workers with chronic conditions versus 17% without, and 30% of women versus 20% of men. These are not causal estimates, but they identify where healthcare, labor-market power and household security may intersect.

## Boundaries

The underlying study surveyed 5,660 U.S. adults from October 27–December 22, 2025; the job-lock estimate focuses on 2,322 employed respondents whose primary coverage was employer-sponsored. The measures are self-reported and cross-sectional. They do not observe actual job transitions, alternative coverage, wages, employer behavior, entrepreneurship, or later health and wellbeing outcomes.

Source: [West Health-Gallup, One in Four U.S. Employees Locked in Jobs for Health Insurance](https://news.gallup.com/poll/712166/one-four-employees-locked-jobs-health-insurance.aspx).

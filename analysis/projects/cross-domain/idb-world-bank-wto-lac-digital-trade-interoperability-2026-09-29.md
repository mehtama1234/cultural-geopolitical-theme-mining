# Digital trade becomes regional infrastructure, not merely online commerce

## Finding

The joint IDB–World Bank–WTO report finds that digitally delivered services exports from Latin America and the Caribbean grew nearly fivefold, from US$18.5 billion in 2005 to US$87.7 billion in 2024. Yet the region represented only about 2% of global digitally delivered-services exports in 2024. A scenario combining technological progress with improved policy conditions reaches up to 7.9% annual export growth through 2040, with digitalization contributing up to 4.5 percentage points.

The central constraint is not simply whether firms or consumers are online. It is whether the region can make infrastructure, regulation, cross-border payments, customs, logistics, skills, finance, privacy, cybersecurity, and consumer protection work together.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`connectivity and online demand -> payment, regulatory, skills, logistics, and trust infrastructure -> cross-border participation -> firm growth, consumer choice, and regional integration`

Intraregional digitally deliverable-services exports were only US$7.8 billion in 2023, or 8.4% of the region’s category exports. About 60% of foreign visits to LAC e-commerce platforms came from within LAC, while only 13% of LAC consumers’ visits to foreign platforms went to other LAC platforms. Regional demand exists, but the systems that convert it into regional supply and value capture remain incomplete.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Digital trade is a consumer-infrastructure issue: payment speed, privacy, platform liability, and delivery rules shape who can buy and sell across borders.
- Regional integration increasingly depends on interoperability standards and trust, not only tariff reduction.
- Small firms and women-led firms may use neighboring markets as an export springboard, but aggregate digital growth can still remain concentrated.
- The political question is who controls the rules, data, payment rails, and enforcement institutions through which regional digital commerce becomes ordinary life.

## Boundary and next test

The report identifies constraints and scenarios, not causal effects of individual reforms. The next test is a country- or corridor-level intervention linking interoperable payments, data rules, platform safeguards, skills, and export support to realized firm entry, consumer use, women-led participation, prices, trust, and retained regional value.

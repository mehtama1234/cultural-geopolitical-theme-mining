# Reproductive autonomy has broad public legitimacy, but access remains conditional

## Finding

Afrobarometer’s 2026 seven-country East Africa analysis shows a layered reproductive-politics pattern. Eighty percent of respondents support women and girls deciding for themselves whether and when to marry, 73% support women deciding how many children to have and when, and 79% support sexuality education in schools. These positions have majority support in every surveyed country.

But the access boundary is narrower. Fifty-eight percent support contraceptives being available regardless of marital status, while only 42% support availability regardless of age. Abortion is most legitimate when a woman’s health or life is at risk (67% saying always or sometimes justified), less so after rape or incest (47%), economic hardship (30%), or an unwanted pregnancy without another stated circumstance (26%).

The key theme is not a simple traditional-versus-progressive divide. Public legitimacy is strongest for autonomy as a principle and education as preparation, then becomes more conditional as policy touches age, sexuality, marriage, economic responsibility, and abortion. The distance between endorsement and usable agency is where family norms, schools, providers, law, affordability, and stigma operate.

## Theme and subthemes

- **Principle-to-access gradient:** autonomy receives broader support than unrestricted service access.
- **Education as bridge infrastructure:** sexuality education is more publicly legitimate than every form of contraceptive or abortion access.
- **Circumstance-shaped legitimacy:** health risk receives greater support than economic hardship or unwanted pregnancy alone.
- **Marriage and age as gatekeepers:** people distinguish marital status and age when judging who should access contraception.
- **Voice versus delivery:** stated support does not prove safe, confidential, affordable, or equitable reproductive care.

## What the source supports

The source supports weighted public-attitude estimates from 12,000 interviews across seven countries, with country-comparable margins of error and explicit warnings about sensitive-question and social-desirability bias. It supports a cultural-legitimacy gradient across autonomy, education, contraception, and abortion scenarios.

It does not establish actual reproductive behavior, service availability, legal access, clinical outcomes, or whether public opinion causes policy change.

## Next test

Follow named sexuality-education, contraceptive, maternal-health, or reproductive-rights interventions from public legitimacy through school delivery, provider practice, affordability, confidentiality, uptake, safety, and household bargaining. Test where stated autonomy becomes usable agency and where institutional or social constraints interrupt it.

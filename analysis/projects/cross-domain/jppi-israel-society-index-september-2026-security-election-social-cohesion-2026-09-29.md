# JPPI Israeli Society Index: security, identity and social legitimacy converge at the election

## Finding

The September 2026 JPPI Israeli Society Index shows a public interpreting the
coming election through several connected but non-identical legitimacy tests.
Forty percent of respondents call the election the most important in Israel’s
history and another 41% call it especially important. Security is the leading
decisive issue at 31%, followed by relations between government and the
judiciary and unity, with sharp ideological and Jewish-Arab differences.

The social assessment is more negative than the economic or security assessment:
52% describe the past year as poor socially, compared with 30% economically and
37% in security terms. Meanwhile, security and territorial preferences remain
highly identity-sorted: 72% of Jewish Israelis reject withdrawal from a Gaza
security perimeter under any circumstances, while Arab Israeli views are much
more supportive of withdrawal. The report also finds a split over whether
Israeli policy contributes to rising antisemitism and a deteriorating assessment
of the education system, described as not good by 35%.

The cross-source implication is that geopolitical legitimacy is not only a
security judgment. It is a bundle of security, judicial authority, unity,
identity, social repair, education and attribution. A shared election can be
highly salient while the public inhabits sharply different causal stories and
acceptable futures.

## Boundary

JPPI reports a weighted September 1–3 sample of 663 Jewish-sector respondents
and 206 Arab-sector respondents using Madad.com and Afkar Research. The public
page does not provide a full probability-sample design or item-level confidence
intervals. These are stated perceptions and preferences, not objective security
conditions, turnout, policy delivery, conflict outcomes, or causal evidence.

Source: [JPPI Israeli Society Index, September 2026](https://jppi.org.il/en/34481-2/).

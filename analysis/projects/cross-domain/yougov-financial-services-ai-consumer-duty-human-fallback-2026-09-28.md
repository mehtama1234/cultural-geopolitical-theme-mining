# Financial-services AI is accepted conditionally on customer control

**Source:** YouGov Consumer Duty Index, “Will AI help financial services companies act in consumers' best interests? The public aren't convinced” (14 September 2026)  
**Geography:** Great Britain  
**Method:** Weighted online survey of 8,220 adults, conducted August 2026  
**Source:** [YouGov publication](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55555-will-ai-help-financial-services-companies-act-in-consumers-best-interests-the-public-arent-convinced)

## Finding

The public does not evaluate financial-services AI as a neutral efficiency upgrade. Most respondents think it is more likely to serve operating-cost reduction and profit than customer outcomes. The route to confidence is conditional: a person must remain reachable, the company must remain accountable for AI decisions, and personal information must be protected.

```text
AI adoption
  -> perceived firm motive: cost/profit rather than customer benefit
  -> risks: less human interaction, less personalisation, weaker customer understanding
  -> conditional trust: human fallback + accountable decisions + data protection
  -> open question: actual fair outcomes, remedy, and durable financial trust
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Household room and consumption | Financial-service access and reversibility | Automation is judged by whether customers can still obtain help and recover from a bad decision |
| Platforms, data, and attention | Automated customer interface | AI changes the service interface, not just back-office efficiency |
| Trust, identity, and meaning | Human/accountable legitimacy | Human access and named accountability operate as trust conditions |
| Public systems and feedback | Consumer Duty and remedy | Good-outcome rules need visible implementation, explanation, and escalation |
| Unequal exposure and status | Vulnerable and older customers | Risk perception and human-support demand are higher among exposed groups |
| Political judgment and action | Corporate-purpose attribution | Consumers attribute adoption to firm incentives and can judge whether benefits reach them |

## Boundary

This is weighted public-opinion evidence, not a deployment audit or causal test. It does not show whether financial firms actually substitute AI for people, whether customer outcomes worsen, or whether the proposed safeguards restore trust. “Vulnerable customer” follows YouGov's source-defined category.

## Next test

Join a named financial-service AI deployment to human-escalation availability, decision explanation, data use, complaint and remedy pathways, subgroup access, realized customer outcomes, and repeat trust.

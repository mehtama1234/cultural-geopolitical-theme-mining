# Media trust has rebounded slightly without recovering institutional legitimacy

## Finding

Gallup’s September 2026 Governance poll finds that 33% of U.S. adults have a great deal or fair amount of confidence in newspapers, television and radio to report news fully, accurately and fairly, up from 28% in 2025. But 66% still express limited or no confidence, leaving the rebound near the bottom of Gallup’s more-than-50-year trend.

The important distinction is between movement and restoration. A five-point improvement can signal a real change in political evaluation while still failing to rebuild a shared information institution.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`political conflict and media exposure -> partisan trust filter -> source choice and credibility judgment -> correction, shared facts or civic fragmentation`

The partisan gap is central: confidence is 22% among Republicans, 50% among Democrats and 31% among independents. This makes media trust an institutional and cultural infrastructure problem, not only a communications problem. The open middle is whether reporting quality, transparency, correction, local or specialized intermediaries and plural verification can create durable legitimacy across identity groups.

## Boundaries

This is descriptive polling and historical trend evidence. Gallup does not provide the public article’s sample size, outlet-level exposure, content accuracy, source-use behavior, causal drivers or post-September-17 effects. The survey predates major White House-media-access events beginning September 18, so their effects are outside the measurement window.

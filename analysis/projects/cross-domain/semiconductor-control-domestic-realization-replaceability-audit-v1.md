# Semiconductor control, domestic realization, and replaceability audit

**Status:** active depth artifact; capability-stage and external-response audit, not a leverage estimate  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** strategic dependence -> domestic capacity or control -> production and compliance -> substitute/replaceability -> external response

## What we found

Semiconductor policy has reached several concrete stages, but they are not one
outcome. TSMC Arizona has a reported first-fab production milestone. Export
controls have produced named penalties, audits, license restrictions, and a
foreign government response. Yet the evidence does not show that a specific
external supply route was replaced, that controlled capability fell, or that a
partner or adversary changed behavior because of the new capacity or controls.

```text
strategic dependence or security objective
  -> investment, rule, license, or enforcement
  -> facility, production, and compliance realization
  -> qualified substitute, inventory, portability, or output replacement
  -> partner alignment, countermeasure, concession, deterrence, or leverage
```

## What this connects to

The comparison produces three useful distinctions.

First, **domestic production is not replacement capacity**. TSMC Arizona's N4
production milestone is stronger than an announcement, but it does not show
that a defined customer, process node, or supply route can be substituted at
the required volume, yield, cost, and timing.

Second, **control is not capability denial**. The VEU rule preserves intended
operation of existing fabs while restricting expansion and upgrades. The
Applied Materials and Cadence settlements show compliance consequences, not a
measured reduction in downstream production or capability.

Third, **a stated external response is not a countermeasure or leverage**.
China's objection to the VEU change is an observed diplomatic response. It is
not evidence of retaliation, concession, substitution, partner alignment, or
changed production. The missing arrow remains external behavior that is
temporally tied to a realized capability or dependence and survives plausible
alternative explanations.

| [Energy and AI infrastructure discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-iea-key-questions-energy-ai-linkedin-001.json) | Infrastructure scale is linked to grid queues, affordability, emissions, and energy security | Extends the capability question to public incidence; it supplies no household price or distributional result |

The discussion layer shows why semiconductor autonomy is culturally and
politically legible as more than a factory count: it combines sovereignty,
alliance trust, jobs, compliance, energy, and local burden. It does not prove
which audience accepts or rejects a particular project or control.

## Missing links and challenge checks

- Do not treat planned investment, a CHIPS award, construction, or employment
  as full domestic supply replacement.
- Do not treat first-fab production as proof of portfolio capacity, yield,
  uptime, customer qualification, or strategic independence.
- Do not treat penalties, audits, licenses, or export holds as measured loss of
  foreign capability.
- Keep existing-fab operation, capacity expansion, upgrade, and substitution
  as separate stages.
- Do not treat China's stated objection as an enacted countermeasure or
  concession.
- Do not treat modeled mineral exposure as a realized semiconductor shortage.
- Keep company, facility, state, partner, worker, household, and adversary units
  separate.

## Stronger next test

Build one application-specific capability ledger with:

1. named process, product, facility, customer, and input;
2. control or disruption date and affected shipment/license;
3. actual output, yield, uptime, inventory, and customer qualification;
4. substitute route, switching time, cost, and performance;
5. worker, community, energy, and household incidence; and
6. partner adoption, foreign countermeasure, concession, or changed behavior.

For the TSMC route, the decisive next evidence is measured output and customer
qualification linked to a defined supply dependency. For the control route, it
is a post-rule shipment, production, license, substitution, or enacted foreign
response. Do not merge the two until their units and dates support a valid
comparison.

The GF–TSMC interposer route creates a second packaging-specific test: follow
the agreement through site readiness, first production, yield, customer
qualification, and CoWoS deployment. A U.S. packaging input can reduce one
bottleneck while leaving wafer fabrication, substrates, equipment, advanced
packaging assembly, or customer allocation dependent on other locations.

## Deeper question

**When does a domestic semiconductor capability become replaceable supply and
therefore strategic leverage, rather than a production milestone or control
signal that still depends on external firms, inputs, licenses, and customers?**

## Stopping rule

This pass is sufficient when investment, operation, control, compliance,
substitution, external response, and leverage have been separated by unit,
date, method, and limits; the social discussion is bounded; and the missing
replaceability arrow is explicit. The broader 14-theme program remains active.

# The contact channel is part of who gets counted

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau, *Evaluating Text Messaging as a Contact Strategy and Predictors of Text Message Consent: Evidence from the Census Household Panel* (September 2026)  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/us-census-household-panel-text-consent-access-stratification-2026.json)

## Core finding

The working paper treats text messaging as an efficient way to accelerate
survey response, but its more consequential social finding is that the contact
channel is itself a participation gate. Among approximately 10,000 Census
Household Panel Topical 7 respondents, text invitations were associated with
faster initial response, while access and consent differed across demographic
groups. A measurement system can therefore become faster without becoming
equally reachable.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The new theme is **the social life of the invitation**. Survey participation is
often treated as a neutral moment between sampling and answer, but the paper
shows that device access, mobile-number provision, consent, channel familiarity,
and response timing intervene first. This makes “voice” partly infrastructural:
some people are not silent about a condition; they are reached differently,
later, or not through the channel being optimized.

The finding also keeps agency visible. Refusing text can be a privacy choice,
not a deficit. The design question is therefore not to maximize text consent at
any cost, but to preserve multi-mode reach, understand who is missing at each
stage, and avoid turning a cheaper contact method into an invisible social
selection mechanism.

## Open middle

The next valid test should join:

```text
contact-mode design
  -> invitation reach / consent / response timing
  -> subgroup-specific participation and missingness
  -> estimate visibility and institutional feedback
  -> multi-mode correction or service redesign
  -> durable, equitable public voice
```

The working paper does not establish downstream policy bias, nor that text
messaging is less representative than other modes after weighting and follow-up.

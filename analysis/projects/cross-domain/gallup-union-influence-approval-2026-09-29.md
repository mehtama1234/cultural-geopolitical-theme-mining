# Public demand for collective worker voice is rising faster than confidence in union power

## Finding

Gallup reports that 47% of Americans want labor unions to have more influence, a record high in its past-quarter-century trend, while 71% approve of unions. But only 27% expect unions to become stronger; 39% expect them to weaken. The result is a 20-point gap between desired influence and expected institutional strength.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`household/work insecurity and concentrated employer power -> demand for collective voice -> institutional approval -> pessimism about organizational capacity`

The signal is not simply “union approval is rising.” It is a legitimacy-capacity split: people increasingly want a counterweight to employer power, but are unsure the institution can regain influence. Nonmembers also favor greater union influence, 44% to 24%, suggesting that support for collective voice extends beyond current participation.

## Boundaries

Gallup’s annual Work and Education poll was conducted August 3–24, 2026. The public article does not provide respondent count, subgroup sample sizes or full weighting details. These are stated attitudes, not evidence of membership, organizing, bargaining coverage, wage effects, strikes, firm behavior or policy change.

# The public distrusts government in the abstract while distinguishing civil servants and specific services

## Finding

The Partnership for Public Service’s 2026 trust report finds that only 27% of Americans trust the federal government, while 72% describe it as corrupt and 75% as wasteful. Yet the report also says perceptions of civil servants are more positive than perceptions of government as a whole, and that many personal interactions and specific government activities receive positive evaluations.

The important mechanism is institutional differentiation. “Government” is not one trust object: elected leadership, federal agencies, career civil servants, service encounters and specific outcomes can be judged separately.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`abstract government judgment -> partisan attribution and corruption/waste belief -> direct service or civil-servant encounter -> competence, accountability and capacity legitimacy`

Trust also changes with presidential party: the report gives 42% trust among Republicans, 17% among Democrats and 18% among independents. This means public capacity is filtered through identity and attribution before it becomes legitimacy. The open middle is whether competent, usable and accountable service encounters can build durable trust—or whether workforce cuts, delays and political blame overwhelm frontline differentiation.

## Boundaries

This is a nationally representative survey of 1,000 U.S. adults conducted March 31–April 5, 2026, by Impact Research for the Partnership for Public Service. It is perception-based and cross-sectional, with annual comparisons. It does not measure actual service quality, administrative capacity, agency-specific outcomes, causal effects of cuts or reforms, or respondent-level links between encounters and trust.

# Opportunity is a readiness ecology, not an exam result

**Source:** UK Department for Work and Pensions / YouGov, [Survey of Education Professionals: Young People and Work Report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/survey-of-education-professionals-young-people-and-work-report/survey-of-education-professionals-young-people-and-work-report)  
**Method:** Online survey of 1,004 teachers and education professionals, fielded in March 2026; DWP analysts report the YouGov findings.

## Finding

Educators describe young people’s access to work, training, and education as a readiness ecology rather than an exam-result pipeline. Work-readiness, confidence, local opportunity, transport, health, SEND support, family context, networks, and meaningful work experience all affect whether a formal pathway can actually be used.

```text
academic attainment
  + work-readiness / confidence / health / support
  + local jobs / transport / networks / experience
  -> pathway usability
  -> credible transition or disengagement
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Work, control, and bargaining | Employability beyond credentials | Educators connect opportunity to soft skills, confidence, networks, and experience as well as qualifications |
| Public systems and feedback | Curriculum legitimacy | Support is high for balancing exams with work preparation without lowering academic standards |
| Unequal exposure and status | Unequal pathway usability | SEND, health, family, transport, local opportunity, and school-resource conditions shape access |
| Housing, place, and mobility | Local opportunity geography | The local availability of jobs and transport affects whether education can convert into work |
| Care, health, and reproduction | Engagement support | Mental health, parental influence, and support needs affect whether young people can remain on a pathway |

## Boundary

This is professional-perception evidence from a DWP-commissioned online panel. It does not measure young people’s actual transitions, causal curriculum effects, pathway quality, completion, or employer outcomes.

## Next test

# Government AI becomes public capacity only when pilots can be evaluated, corrected, and sustained

## Finding

The World Bank’s 2026 evidence on government AI suggests that the central transition is not from “no AI” to “AI adoption.” It is from isolated pilots to institutions capable of selecting, evaluating, procuring, adapting, monitoring, and correcting AI systems in public services.

The World Bank’s survey covers 60 economies and finds government AI adoption broad but shallow and uneven. Its companion analysis also reports a severe evidence bottleneck: of nearly 10,000 studies of AI-based health interventions, only 57 were tested with real patients and providers in low-resource settings, and only 17 measured causal effects on health, care quality, or efficiency. The implication is not that public AI cannot work; it is that public institutions often lack the capacity to distinguish working systems from persuasive demonstrations.

## Cross-domain interpretation

```text
public-service shortage or administrative friction
  -> AI pilot
  -> local data, language, workflow, and connectivity fit
  -> real-world evaluation
  -> procurement with portability and auditability
  -> staff capacity and continuing maintenance
  -> usable service, correction, and public trust
```

This route links technology to time, cost, work, voice, and political legitimacy. A faster decision is not automatically a better public service. The relevant endpoint is whether a person can receive an accurate, understandable, and contestable service when the data are incomplete, the environment differs from the training set, or the model fails.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- **The cultural shift is from AI spectacle to institutional proof.** A pilot can create optimism, but scale requires public evidence and repeatable maintenance.
- **Data infrastructure becomes social infrastructure.** Registries, records, language coverage, electricity, connectivity, and staff skills determine who becomes legible to the system.
- **Procurement becomes a political choice.** Portability, interoperability, auditability, and exit determine whether public institutions retain agency or become dependent on a vendor.
- **Frontline work is redesigned, not simply automated.** Teachers, nurses, caseworkers, and civil servants need authority and skills to interpret outputs, override errors, and learn from implementation.
- **Trust depends on correction.** The strongest legitimacy test is not whether an agency has an AI policy, but whether a person can understand, challenge, and repair an AI-assisted decision.

## Boundary and next test

The World Bank evidence is a cross-economy survey, policy framework, and selected implementation examples. It does not establish representative citizen outcomes, common error rates, causal service improvements across countries, or durable trust. The next test is a named public-service episode that follows a real user from eligibility or need through AI-assisted contact, waiting time, decision quality, explanation, appeal, correction, and repeat access.

# Diasporas are being treated as democratic infrastructure

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** Council of Europe Parliamentary Assembly, *The role of diasporas and migrants in developing social cohesion and democratic engagement*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-council-europe-diaspora-democratic-engagement-social-cohesion-2026.json)

## Core finding

The report reframes diasporas from populations to be integrated into existing
institutions as potential infrastructure for renewing those institutions.
Diaspora youth are described as multilingual, multicultural bridges capable of
intercultural mediation, democratic renewal, and social-cohesion work.

The policy route is participation: consultation, equal partnership, civic
education, mentorship, representative councils, political rights, and stable
funding for diaspora organizations.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The important shift is from assimilation to negotiated membership. Diasporic
identity is not treated as a residue that must disappear before civic belonging
can begin; it is presented as a source of translation, political imagination, and
institutional knowledge.

That creates a power test. Consultation can recognize communities without giving
them decision rights. “Diaspora” is not one unified actor, and representative
organizations may not speak for women, youth, undocumented people, or internally
contested groups. Stable funding and equal partnership therefore matter as much
as formal invitations to participate.

## Open middle

```text
migration and plural identity
  -> diaspora networks, language, and lived institutional knowledge
  -> consultation, civic learning, representation, and mediation
  -> policy feedback and formal/deliberative participation
  -> belonging, trust, democratic legitimacy, and social cohesion
  -> durable membership or renewed exclusion
```

The source is a parliamentary policy report and recommendation set. It does not
measure implementation, representativeness, causal cohesion effects, or changes
in electoral behavior.

# Social-protection coverage becomes real through systems that reach beyond standard employment

## Finding

The ILO reports that more than nine million people gained improved access to social-protection measures through a five-year multi-donor project supporting systems in six countries across Southeast Asia, West Africa, and Central Africa. The programme focused in part on informal workers, using policy reform, data, coordination, health-insurance expansion, worker organization, automatic enrolment, contribution payments, and regional learning.

The distinct theme is a coverage-conversion gap. A legal right or national policy does not become household security until people can enrol, contribute, claim, receive adequate benefits, and continue coverage through illness, maternity, unemployment, workplace injury, old age, or changing work.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`informal work and social risk -> legal entitlement and policy design -> data, coordination, enrolment, and contribution infrastructure -> worker voice and institutional trust -> actual benefit access and adequacy -> household room and resilience`

The country examples show multiple routes: Burundi’s policy and informal-worker roadmap; health and maternity access in the Democratic Republic of the Congo; informal-worker mechanisms and union growth in Rwanda; health insurance for people in Burkina Faso and Senegal; automatic enrolment and seamless contribution payments; and stronger management and financing of health insurance and social-security inspection in Lao PDR.

The political significance is that social protection is being rebuilt as infrastructure rather than a narrow transfer programme. But the reported “improved access” remains an early-stage measure. The real test is whether informal workers can keep coverage when income is irregular, employers change, documents fail, care needs rise, or a shock occurs—and whether unions, agencies, and households can contest denial and obtain remedy.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Informal workers are being treated as rights-bearing social-protection constituencies rather than residual beneficiaries.
- Data systems, enrolment, payment channels, and inspections become part of household security infrastructure.
- Worker organization can convert formal coverage into voice and accountability.
- Automatic enrolment can reduce friction but may create new exclusion, privacy, or contribution burdens.
- “Coverage” should be tested through receipt, adequacy, continuity, portability, trust, and remedy—not enrolment alone.

## Boundary and next test

The ILO account is programme reporting with selected country examples. It does not provide a common denominator, causal counterfactual, independent reach verification, benefit adequacy, household outcomes, or post-project durability. The next test is a named country and worker group followed from eligibility and enrolment through contributions, claim, payment, adequacy, health/work continuity, household room, complaint, and post-project survival.

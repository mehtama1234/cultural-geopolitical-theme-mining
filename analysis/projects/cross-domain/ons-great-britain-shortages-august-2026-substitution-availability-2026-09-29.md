# ONS Great Britain shortages: availability becomes a household search and spending tax

## Finding

The ONS August 2026 shortages tables show that goods availability is converted
into household effort and room. In the prior two weeks, 45% of responding
adults said they had to spend more than usual to obtain what they normally buy;
23% saw less variety, 18% visited more shops, and 5% shopped online to obtain
usual purchases. Thirty-seven percent said they were buying less, while 60%
said their shopping habits had not changed.

The availability layer is sharper: 11% reported that essential food items they
needed regularly were unavailable or inaccessible, 17% reported this for other
food, 4% for medicine and 3% for fuel. Sixty-two percent said everything they
needed had been available.

The cross-source implication is that “shopping continued” is not a single
household outcome. Some households pay more, search longer, substitute online,
reduce purchases or encounter unmet availability. The same system can look
stable in aggregate while shifting cost into money, time, quality and access.

## Boundary

These are weighted ONS Opinions and Lifestyle Survey estimates for 5–30 August
2026, with item-specific sample sizes and confidence intervals. They are
self-reported two-week experiences, not transaction records, price estimates,
food-security measures, or causal supply-chain findings. ONS notes a question
wording and response-option change from May 2026, so earlier availability
comparisons require caution.

Source: [ONS personal experiences of shortages of goods dataset](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/wellbeing/datasets/publicopinionsandsocialtrendsgreatbritainpersonalexperiencesofshortagesofgoods).

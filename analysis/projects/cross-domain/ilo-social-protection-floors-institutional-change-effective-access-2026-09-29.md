# Social-protection reform shows a measurable gap between legal entitlement and effective access

## Finding

The ILO’s retrospective of its 2021–25 Global Flagship Programme reports improvements affecting more than 84 million people—above its 60 million ambition. But the results separate legal coverage from effective coverage: 31.51 million people gained legal coverage, while 23.81 million gained effective coverage, below the programme’s 30 million ambition.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`policy commitment -> legal entitlement -> registration and administration -> benefit delivery -> realized household protection`

The central mechanism is implementation time. Social protection becomes public infrastructure only when strategy, law, financing, registration, contribution collection, administration, and delivery are connected. Announcing a right can be politically visible while the operational layer remains incomplete.

## Cultural, social, and political significance

- The programme reports 180 institutional-change results against an ambition of 80: 48 strategy results, 45 scheme-design or legal reforms, and 87 operational or governance improvements.
- The report’s three-step pathway—national strategy, legal scheme design, operational strengthening—offers a way to distinguish policy intent from lived protection.
- Inclusion is explicitly extended to informal workers, migrants and refugees, persons with disabilities, and people facing climate or other crises.
- Social dialogue and partnerships are treated as implementation infrastructure, not merely consultation: governments, workers, employers, civil society, international institutions, and UN agencies coordinate around system design.
- Crises can catalyse reform and national ownership, but external financing and technical support may also create sustainability and dependency questions.

## Boundary and next test

This is a programme retrospective, not a population-wide causal evaluation or independent verification of every attributed result. The next test is whether country reforms sustain domestic financing, registration, delivery, adequacy, inclusion, and crisis responsiveness after external programme support changes.

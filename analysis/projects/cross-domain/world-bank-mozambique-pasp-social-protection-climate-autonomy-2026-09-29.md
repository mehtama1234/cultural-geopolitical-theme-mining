# Social protection becomes productive and ecological infrastructure when households gain assets, payment access, and climate agency

## Finding

The World Bank’s account of Mozambique’s Productive Social Action Program (PASP) shows social protection being framed as more than a temporary consumption buffer. Since 2012, the programme has reached more than **300,000 people across all 11 provinces**, combining income support, skills development, productive grants, and community work. Women account for **65% of beneficiaries**, and more than **200,000 electronic money accounts** have reportedly been opened through the programme.

The distinct finding is a conversion architecture: transfers are meant to become time, skills, productive assets, safer payments, stronger homes, food security, and climate-resilience infrastructure. Social protection is therefore also a place-making and ecological institution. It can connect household survival to coastal protection, riverbank restoration, farming, local markets, and digital financial access.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`climate and income shock -> transfer and public work -> skills, assets, payment access, and local ecological repair -> household room and livelihood autonomy -> resilience to future shocks`

This model changes what counts as a successful benefit. A cash transfer may reduce immediate hunger; a mobile-money account can reduce travel and security costs; a productive grant can widen future options; and community work can protect the physical base of livelihoods. These effects are complementary only if the programme does not turn support into compulsory work, overload women with unpaid care and paid programme labour, or leave digital and ecological infrastructure unsupported after the project.

The cultural and political signal is that beneficiaries are narrated as contributors and builders rather than passive recipients. That framing can strengthen dignity and local agency, but it also raises an accountability question: who controls the design, payment rails, work conditions, asset ownership, and maintenance of the resilience infrastructure?

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Household security is increasingly measured through food, housing, productive assets, payment access, and future shock capacity—not income alone.
- Women’s participation links social protection to care, household investment, enterprise, and local bargaining power.
- Digital payment infrastructure changes the time geography of welfare by reducing travel and increasing direct access, while creating new risks of exclusion and dependence.
- Climate adaptation becomes an employment and social-protection issue when beneficiaries restore coastlines, riverbanks, farms, and rural infrastructure.
- A benefit programme becomes politically durable when people experience it as a route to autonomy and community protection rather than only as relief.

## Boundary and next test

This is a World Bank feature story with programme statistics and selected beneficiary narratives. It does not establish a representative treatment effect, durable income gains, active use of all accounts, ecological impact, or post-programme persistence at population scale. The next test is a Mozambique province-level episode following transfer adequacy, payment use, work conditions, asset ownership, care time, food security, climate loss, and household outcomes after exit.

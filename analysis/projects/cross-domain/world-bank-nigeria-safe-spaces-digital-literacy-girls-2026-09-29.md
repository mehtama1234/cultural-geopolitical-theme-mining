# Safe spaces are infrastructure for digital participation

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11445, *Safe Spaces to Digital Spaces: Short-Term Impacts of Life Skills and Digital Literacy Training for Adolescent Girls in Nigeria*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-nigeria-adolescent-girls-safe-spaces-digital-literacy-2026.json)

## Core finding

The evaluation finds modest curriculum-knowledge improvements twelve months after
the intervention, but no effects on school retention or socio-emotional skills at
that point. Adding digital-literacy training improved digital skills in the state
where the component had been fully implemented by midline.

The important finding is therefore about delivery architecture: technology does
not travel by itself. A safe, trusted learning environment, functioning schools,
trained facilitators, devices, power, connectivity, and implementation fidelity
determine whether “digital empowerment” becomes an actual capability.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The intervention treats digital participation as a social transition, not merely a
technical one. Girls need a place where they can ask questions, learn life
skills, and build confidence before digital tools become useful for education or
economic opportunity.

That also explains why the results are mixed. Digital skills can improve when the
component is fully delivered, while retention and socio-emotional outcomes may
depend on much larger household and institutional conditions. The policy risk is
to interpret a technology programme as a substitute for safety, schooling,
social support, and sustained agency.

## Open middle

```text
gendered schooling and social constraints
  -> safe-space life skills and digital-literacy delivery
  -> actual digital capability and confidence
  -> education persistence, health agency, and economic opportunity
  -> durable participation or a short-lived training effect
```

The study reports short-term evaluation results. It does not establish long-term
empowerment, causal employment or health effects, universal access, or that
technology changes social norms without supporting institutions.

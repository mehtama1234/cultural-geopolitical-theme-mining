# Agency is not a culturally portable score

## Finding

The World Bank's Tunisia study asks whether one short survey scale can measure women's agency across contexts. It replicates an India-based approach using qualitative interviews, quantitative surveys, machine learning, and—newly—generative-AI methods. The main finding is that context matters: overlap between questions that performed well in India and Tunisia was minimal, and questions that worked in one Tunisian context, such as a rural area, did not necessarily work in another, such as an urban area.

Machine learning performed well against a qualitative agency benchmark. Current readily available generative-AI approaches did not, and did not make the process more effective or less time-consuming. The practical lesson is not that AI cannot assist measurement; it is that automated measurement cannot bypass local meaning, qualitative grounding, validation, and judgment.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds a measurement-politics layer to the project's voice and agency themes. What institutions can see and compare is partly determined by the questions they ask. If a universal scale misses how women define goals, negotiate constraints, or act within a particular household, labor market, or community, then programs may misdiagnose need and evaluate the wrong outcome.

`local experience -> qualitative grounding -> validated measure -> institutional visibility -> program design and accountability`

The chain is political as well as technical. A context-blind score can make a population look comparable while erasing meaningful differences in what agency consists of. A context-specific measure can improve accuracy but complicate benchmarking and resource allocation. The productive response is not to choose universality or total localism, but to make the core, local modules, validation, and uncertainty visible.

## Cultural and social significance

- Agency should not be reduced to a single imported behavioral template.
- Measurement itself distributes recognition: what is counted becomes easier to fund, target, and evaluate.
- Generative AI can produce plausible questions without demonstrating that they capture locally meaningful agency.
- Cross-cultural comparison requires translation and calibration, not merely the same questionnaire everywhere.
- Women’s voice is not only an outcome to measure; it is part of how the measurement instrument should be designed and validated.

## Boundary and next test

The study addresses instrument validity, not population prevalence or causal empowerment effects. The next test is whether locally validated agency modules change program targeting, implementation, and observed outcomes compared with a universal scale alone, while preserving transparent cross-context comparison.

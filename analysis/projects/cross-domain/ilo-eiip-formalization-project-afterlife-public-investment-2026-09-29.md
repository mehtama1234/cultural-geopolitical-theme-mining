# Public investment can be a formalization gateway only if the project outlives itself

## Finding

The ILO's Employment-Intensive Investment Programme brief identifies a recurring project-afterlife problem. EIIP projects operate in places and sectors with high informality—especially construction and rural works—and can improve income, skills, infrastructure and living conditions. But workers and economic units often remain informal or revert to informality when the project ends.

The brief's strongest design distinction is between enterprise formalization and worker formalization. Registering an enterprise alone is weaker than linking worker registration, social protection, labor standards, procurement access, skills, cooperative organization and measurable post-project outcomes. In the Philippines example, a short emergency-employment programme linked more than 1,800 workers to social security, health, accident and liability insurance while benefiting nearly 12,000 households. In the Central African Republic example, cooperatives helped young workers reach procurement and compliance pathways, but weak follow-up funding and municipal capacity limited scale-up.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`public infrastructure project -> temporary income and assets -> registration, skills and procurement gateway -> project ends -> durable formal work or reversion to informality`

The new subtheme is infrastructure with an afterlife. A public project can create a moment when the state, worker, contractor, insurer and local authority meet. Whether that moment becomes durable formalization depends on the next contract, social-protection continuity, market demand, procurement rules, local capacity and whether the transition was designed and measured from the beginning.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Infrastructure is also a labor-market institution. Roads, water systems and public amenities can create assets while reorganizing who gets recognized as a worker, contractor, cooperative or eligible social-protection participant.
- The project end date is a political boundary. Short-term jobs can produce visible output and immediate relief while leaving households exposed when the work disappears.
- Procurement is a bridge between public spending and durable enterprise status. Small informal units need training, bid support, certification and realistic sequencing before formal requirements become accessible.
- Worker registration is a more demanding success test than enterprise registration. Contracts, social-security contributions, health and accident insurance, paid leave, wages and future work should be tracked separately.
- Formalization cannot be bolted onto an infrastructure project after the fact. The brief recommends making it a core objective, grounding it in social dialogue and monitoring it adaptively.

## Boundary and next test

This is guidance and evidence synthesis, not causal proof. The next test is a named public-investment episode that follows participants after project closure into registration continuity, next employment, claims, wages, enterprise revenue, procurement, cooperative survival and household security.

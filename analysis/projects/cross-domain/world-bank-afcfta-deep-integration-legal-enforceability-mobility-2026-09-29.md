# Regional integration becomes usable when rules, movement, and remedy travel together

## Finding

The World Bank's study classifies worldwide trade agreements as shallow, medium, or deep using clustering and estimates their trade effects with a structural-gravity framework. It then applies the results to the African Continental Free Trade Area.

AfCFTA is classified as medium in the paper's baseline. A modeled counterfactual in which it is implemented as a deep agreement produces substantial additional trade gains and an average GDP gain of approximately 0.7% for the African economies in the sample. This is a model result, not an observed outcome or equal household-welfare estimate.

The provisions most closely associated with deep agreements include stronger legal enforceability, tighter disciplines on trade-defense instruments, and visa-related provisions.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds a formal-integration-to-lived-access mechanism:

`regional agreement -> enforceable rules and mobility -> lower uncertainty and border friction -> firm entry, service provision, labor movement, and consumer choice -> wages, prices, bargaining power, and political legitimacy`

The cultural and political significance is that regional integration is not experienced primarily as tariff language. It is experienced through whether a small firm can trust a rule, whether a worker can cross a border with recognized status, whether a service provider can operate, whether customs delays are contestable, and whether a consumer sees more reliable or affordable options.

“Deep” integration can therefore increase opportunity, but it can also deepen dependence if infrastructure, enforcement, social protection, and voice are uneven. A continental market may be formally open while remaining practically concentrated around firms, corridors, languages, documentation systems, and states with the capacity to use the rules.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Legal enforceability is a consumer and firm-recourse issue, not only a trade-law feature.
- Visa provisions convert regional integration into lived mobility, family strategy, labor competition, and cultural exchange.
- Aggregate GDP gains can coexist with uneven regional opportunity, adjustment costs, and concentrated firm power.
- Smaller or informal firms may face new compliance burdens unless customs, finance, digital systems, and standards recognition are usable.
- The legitimacy of regional integration depends on whether people can see and contest its benefits, not simply whether an agreement is signed.

## Boundary and next test

The study is a model-based comparative and counterfactual analysis. It does not establish realized AfCFTA implementation, jobs, wages, prices, mobility, consumer welfare, or political support. The next test is a named AfCFTA corridor or sector episode that follows legal provisions into customs time, firm entry, service delivery, worker movement, standards recognition, prices, wages, remedy, and local political attribution.

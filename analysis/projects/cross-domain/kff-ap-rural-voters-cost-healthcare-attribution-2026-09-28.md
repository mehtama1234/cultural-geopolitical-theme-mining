# Rural partisan loyalty coexists with cost and health-care attribution pressure

**Source:** KFF/AP, [KFF/AP Rural Voters Survey](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-ap-rural-voters-survey/)  
**Method:** Online and telephone survey of 2,241 U.S. registered voters living in rural census tracts, fielded August 12–24, 2026.

## Finding

Rural political loyalty is not the same as satisfaction with delivery. Cost of living and health-care costs dominate the issue agenda; local provider shortages and household affordability create concrete exposure; and some rural Republicans remain aligned with the Republican candidate while becoming less certain they will vote when they disapprove of the administration’s economic performance.

```text
local cost / care exposure
  -> issue priority and policy attribution
  -> partisan loyalty can persist
  -> performance dissatisfaction reduces turnout certainty
  -> test whether delivery, attribution, and participation move together
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Political judgment and action | Loyalty–performance split | Candidate preference, approval, attribution, and turnout are separate stages |
| Unequal exposure and status | Rural cost and care exposure | Gas, health care, food, utilities, provider scarcity, and jobs shape local judgment |
| Care, health, and reproduction | Care capacity as politics | Medicaid importance and local shortages turn health-system capacity into political meaning |
| Housing, place, and mobility | Rural local-economy legitimacy | Cost of living and good-paying jobs are judged through community-level decline |
| Public systems and feedback | Attribution under weak information | Voters assess national policy through local outcomes while many remain unsure of program details |

## Boundary

This is cross-sectional survey evidence among rural registered voters. It does not establish actual vote choice, turnout, causal policy effects, or whether national attribution identifies the proximate source of local health and economic conditions.

## Next test

Track a named rural policy or health-care episode from cost exposure and local service change through attribution, candidate communication, turnout, vote choice, and observed delivery.

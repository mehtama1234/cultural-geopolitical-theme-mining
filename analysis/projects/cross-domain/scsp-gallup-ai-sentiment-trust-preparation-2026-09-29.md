# AI adoption is outrunning public trust and formal preparation

## Finding

The 2026 SCSP–Gallup study finds that Americans are using AI more and feel more knowledgeable about it, but trust remains low. Fifty-one percent report using AI at least sometimes, up from 39% in 2025, while only 28% trust AI to make fair and unbiased decisions. Seventy percent expect AI to destroy more jobs than it creates over the next decade, and only 20% expect most or all displaced workers to be able to retrain into different work.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`adoption and self-teaching -> exposure without institutional assurance -> low trust and economic insecurity -> demand for training, safety rules and independent testing`

The political-cultural signal is unusually cross-partisan. Americans support STEM preparation, on-the-job training, safety and data-security rules even if development slows, and independent testing before systems are widely used. This suggests that the public legitimacy bargain is shifting from “adopt faster” toward “make adoption understandable, governable and survivable.”

## Boundaries

The study surveyed 3,152 U.S. adults in Gallup’s probability-based panel from June 2–23, 2026. It measures self-reported use, trust, expectations and policy preferences. It does not establish actual displacement, productivity, training completion, system fairness, causal trust change or the effects of any proposed regulation.

Source: [SCSP–Gallup, American AI Sentiment in 2026](https://www.gallup.com/file/analytics/714230/SCSP_Gallup_American_Perspective_AI_Report.pdf).

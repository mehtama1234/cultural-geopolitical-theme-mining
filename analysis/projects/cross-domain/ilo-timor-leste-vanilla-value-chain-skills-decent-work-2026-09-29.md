# A heritage crop becomes a global value-chain test: market access must be built with farmer voice and decent work

## Finding

The ILO's Timor-Leste vanilla roadmap shows how a culturally legible rural product becomes a development and consumer-market proposition only through institutional work. Vanilla cultivation has deep local history, but recorded trade began only in 2020 and 2025 exports were 6.3 metric tonnes of dried and cured vanilla to Indonesia. A small processor and buyer base, weak infrastructure and vanilla's status as a secondary crop limit farmer bargaining power and reduce incentives to invest in quality.

The central finding is not simply that vanilla has export potential. It is that a heritage or origin product becomes a durable livelihood pathway only when production skills, collective voice, processing capacity, certification and finance, market access, and occupational safety are built as one value-chain system.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`agroforestry heritage crop -> skills and infrastructure -> cooperative bargaining and certification -> domestic processing and quality standards -> consumer-facing origin story -> rural livelihood and decent-work test`

The roadmap's three phases move from foundations (Good Agricultural Practices, nurseries, infrastructure and national data), to coordination (cooperatives, knowledge sharing, certification and finance), to value addition and market expansion (processing, vanilla extract, quality standards, trade promotion and partnerships). The sequence matters: “sustainable” consumer meaning cannot compensate for weak producer voice or an absence of alternative buyers.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Agroforestry and largely organic cultivation give Timor-Leste a potentially distinctive origin position aligned with demand for natural, minimally processed and sustainably produced products.
- Consumer-facing sustainability claims become a political question about who captures the premium. Without cooperatives, finance, certification support and processing alternatives, the origin story can be captured downstream while farmers remain price-takers.
- Skills policy is part of market strategy. Manual pollination, vine management, pest and disease control, harvesting, curing, processing and trading each determine whether the product can move from low-volume export to quality-differentiated market participation.
- Women and young people are named as inclusion priorities, while the value-chain assessment identifies biological, physical, chemical, psychosocial, ergonomic, safety and natural hazards. The product's cultural meaning cannot be separated from the conditions of the work producing it.
- The state is not only a promoter of exports. It is also a coordinator of data, infrastructure, training, standards, certification, financing access and the bargaining institutions that make a market legible and contestable.

## Boundary and next test

This is a roadmap and assessment, not causal evidence that the proposed interventions will raise incomes, create durable jobs, improve safety or generate an export premium. The next test is to follow farmer prices, buyer concentration, contracts, cooperative survival, certification costs, domestic processing, women’s and youth participation, occupational incidents, and the distribution of any sustainability or origin premium.

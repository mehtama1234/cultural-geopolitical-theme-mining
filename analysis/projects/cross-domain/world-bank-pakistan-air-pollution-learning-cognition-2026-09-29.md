# Air pollution becomes a learning and human-capital shock in rural Punjab

## Finding

The World Bank’s new study combines longitudinal student achievement data from rural Punjab’s LEAPS project with satellite PM2.5 estimates and an instrumental-variable strategy using upwind agricultural-residue fires and wind direction.

It finds that a 1 microgram-per-cubic-meter increase in PM2.5 over the year before testing reduces the share of difficult questions answered correctly by 2.7 percentage points—about 13.5% of the sample mean and 0.16 standard deviations. The effect is roughly 40% larger in mathematics than across all subjects. School-year exposure predicts lower learning, while vacation-month exposure has little explanatory power.

Pollution also raises recent absences by just over 7.7%, but the authors estimate that absence explains well below 10% of the total learning effect under strong assumptions. The remaining loss is consistent with direct cognition, fatigue, reduced effort, and degraded instructional quality.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds an environment-to-human-capital mechanism:

`air pollution -> illness, cognition, effort, teacher and classroom disruption -> lower learning during instructional months -> accumulated human-capital and opportunity differences`

The important shift is from treating air pollution as a health or attendance issue to treating it as an education-quality and future-opportunity issue. A child can attend school and still learn less because the learning environment is biologically and institutionally degraded.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Clean air is part of educational infrastructure, not only an environmental or health amenity.
- Attendance-based education metrics can miss learning losses that occur inside the classroom.
- Mathematics may be especially vulnerable, potentially affecting later technical pathways, though this study does not estimate long-run schooling or earnings.
- Agricultural burning creates a cross-domain conflict between farm practices, seasonal livelihoods, public health, school performance, and children’s future opportunity.
- The burden may be unequal across places and households, but the study’s gender and age heterogeneity is too imprecise to support a strong subgroup claim.

## Boundary and next test

This is causal evidence for primary students in rural Punjab, not a representative estimate for Pakistan or other settings. It does not separately identify cognition, effort, teacher quality, or lifetime earnings effects. The next test is a current urban or provincial episode linking air-quality interventions to attendance, learning, teacher performance, household health costs, school choice, and later educational transitions.

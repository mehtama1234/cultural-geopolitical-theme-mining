# Available places are not usable infrastructure until young people can safely reach and activate them

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** British Academy / London Development Trust, *Investigating Young People’s Views and Experiences of Social and Cultural Infrastructure in England, Scotland and Wales*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-british-academy-youth-social-cultural-infrastructure-2025.json)

## Core finding

This research reframes social and cultural infrastructure as an access system,
not a list of venues. Young people described safety, transport, affordability,
awareness, inclusive support, trusted adults, and local identity as conditions
that determine whether a park, cinema, gym, museum, business, service, or youth
space becomes usable. The “how” of activation is as important as the physical
“what.”

## Cultural/social interpretation

The useful cultural insight is “activation.” A physical asset does not produce
belonging automatically. Lighting, safety, affordability, transport, staff,
programming, and social permission can activate—or deactivate—the same place for
different people. The infrastructure is therefore relational and identity
conditioned.

The study also shows why consumer and civic life meet in ordinary spaces. Young
people identified businesses, fast-food restaurants, gyms, parks, and cultural
venues as part of their social worlds, even when they judged some of them
unhealthy, expensive, or exclusionary. The question is not whether a place is
officially cultural; it is whether it supports safe connection, aspiration, and
agency.

## Open middle

The next pass should look for same-person or same-place evidence joining:

```text
place / identity / household resources
  -> safety, transport, affordability, awareness, and relational activation
  -> actual attendance, social connection, wellbeing, and civic participation
  -> feedback to institutions and durable investment
```

The research does not establish population prevalence, causal effects, attendance
rates, or transfer of decision power through co-creation. Those remain open
arrows.

# SSA AI-assisted service is an implementation route, not yet a claimant-outcome route

The Social Security Administration is a useful named-agency test of the
World Bank's public-AI conversion problem. Its FY 2025-2026 performance plan
describes AI-enhanced Interactive Voice Response, a chatbot, machine-readable
and searchable disability evidence through IMAGEN, and projected automated
call volume. The plan also pairs those tools with processing-time and
decisional-accuracy targets.

That is institutional implementation evidence, not proof that a claimant
received a better service. The public material does not show which user was
handled by which tool, whether the tool changed waiting time or accuracy,
whether a human explanation was available, or whether an error was appealed,
corrected, paid, and carried into later health, work, or household security.

The route has a visible accountability layer. SSA OIG's current register lists
an audit of SSA's use of artificial intelligence, started March 30, 2026, with
planned completion in FY 2027 Q2. SSA also publishes a targeted denial-review
data asset covering selected denied cases from 52 DDS jurisdictions, with
fields for reviewed cases, substantive returns, and reversals. That asset is
useful for quality-review history but is not a census of denials and does not
link a review result to AI exposure or claimant recovery.

The next decisive acquisition is a named, privacy-approved SSA episode or
agency evaluation joining tool exposure, contact or claim stage, waiting time,
decision quality, notice/explanation, appeal or correction, payment timing,
and later health/work or household outcome. Until then, the strongest claim
is that SSA is operationalizing AI while the evidence needed to judge public
value and contestability remains incomplete.

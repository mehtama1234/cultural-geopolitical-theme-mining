# Household mess is a coordination and meaning system

**Source:** Ingka Group/IKEA, [Store & Organise Report 2026: Finding Meaning in the Mess](https://www.ingka.com/newsroom/new-global-ikea-study-nearly-4-in-10-are-staging-a-silent-protest-over-household-mess/)  
**Method:** YouGov online survey of 31,488 adults across 31 markets, fielded in April 2026; samples described as nationally representative and weighted by gender, age, and geography.

## Finding

Household mess is not only an aesthetic condition. It is a coordination system: visible disorder can signal who is expected to notice and perform domestic work; storage failures create recurring search-time costs; and sentimental objects make order a negotiation over memory and identity rather than a simple optimization problem.

```text
limited space / competing standards
  -> visible clutter and indirect signals about responsibility
  -> domestic friction, avoidance, or silent protest
  -> unpaid cleanup and search time
  -> negotiation between efficiency, memory, belonging, and control
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Household room and consumption | Meaningful mess | People prefer lived-in homes and retain objects for sentiment, not only utility |
| Care, health, and reproduction | Domestic coordination | Clutter can expose unspoken expectations about who notices and resolves household work |
| Time and hidden price | Search-time labor | Misplaced possessions convert organization problems into recurring unpaid minutes |
| Trust, identity, and meaning | Objects as memory infrastructure | Storage and disposal decisions mediate identity, belonging, and relationship history |
| Unequal exposure and status | Unequal room and time burdens | Cross-market differences invite testing by housing size, income, household composition, and access to storage |

## Boundary

This is retailer-commissioned, cross-sectional consumer research based on online self-reports. It does not establish who performs domestic labor, whether “silent protest” is intentional in each case, or whether storage interventions reduce conflict or time loss.

## Next test

Pair household clutter measures with time-use diaries, housing size and tenure, income, disability/access needs, household composition, gendered task allocation, and observed conflict or repair outcomes.

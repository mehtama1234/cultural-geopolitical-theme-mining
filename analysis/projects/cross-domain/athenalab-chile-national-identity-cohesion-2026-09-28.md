# National pride can survive while everyday cohesion weakens

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** AthenaLab, *National Identity and Social Cohesion: Chile in Perspective*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-athenalab-chile-national-identity-cohesion-2026.json)

## Core finding

The study identifies a sharp nation–state gap. In its August 2025 national
survey, 68% of Chilean adults reported the highest level of pride in being
Chilean, yet 87% said one must be very careful dealing with other people and
89% said Chilean society was more divided than ten years earlier. Trust was much
higher in police and armed forces than in Congress and political parties. The
result is not the disappearance of national identity; it is strong symbolic
belonging coexisting with weak everyday trust and representative legitimacy.

The qualitative component adds a second mechanism: cohesion is often episodic.
Disasters, sporting victories, celebrations, and named humanitarian causes can
activate solidarity, but that emotion does not automatically become continuous
cooperation or durable social capital. Migration intensifies the test, producing
selective everyday acceptance alongside public narratives of threat.

## Cultural/social interpretation

This source separates three things that are often merged: loving the nation,
trusting other people, and trusting the institutions that govern. A society can
retain a powerful cultural core—family, traditions, territory, symbols, and
shared history—while citizens retreat into closed circles and view the public
system as distant or illegitimate.

The crisis-versus-routine contrast is especially useful. A named disaster or
humanitarian cause gives people a concrete face and a shared task; ordinary
politics presents abstract opponents, unequal institutions, and unresolved
identity boundaries. The open question is how to institutionalize the former
without turning national identity into exclusion or nostalgia.

## Open middle

```text
identity / territory / migration / crisis narrative
  -> trust, belonging, and concrete solidarity
  -> institutional response, civic education, integration, and participation
  -> durable cooperation, democratic legitimacy, and inclusive cohesion
```

The study combines a national survey and qualitative analysis. It does not
establish causal effects, actual integration or violence outcomes, service
burden, or durable behavior beyond reported perceptions and interpreted
interviews.

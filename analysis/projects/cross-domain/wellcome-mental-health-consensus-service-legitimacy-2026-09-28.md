# Mental-health consensus, early intervention, and the service-delivery legitimacy gap

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Wellcome and More in Common, British mental-health attitudes research  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-wellcome-more-in-common-britain-mental-health-early-intervention-waiting-lists-2026.json)

## Core finding

The publication identifies a societal shift from mental health as a stigmatized
or culture-war issue toward a broadly shared public value. Britons largely say
mental health matters as much as physical health, support early intervention,
and are comfortable with personal disclosure across political segments.

But the same publication shows the next legitimacy test: public belief in
science and prevention is conditional on whether people can reach actual
services. Waiting lists, confusing referrals, and visible institutional failure
can turn consensus into disappointment even when the underlying norm has
shifted.

## Open middle

```text
shared mental-health concern
  -> early-intervention policy and service design
  -> reachable referral, timely appointment, and continuity
  -> treatment/support receipt
  -> health, work, family, and social-participation outcome
  -> restored institutional trust
```

The publication does not establish clinical prevalence, actual treatment
receipt, waiting-list duration, regional equity, or improved outcomes. Those
remain the next measurement targets.

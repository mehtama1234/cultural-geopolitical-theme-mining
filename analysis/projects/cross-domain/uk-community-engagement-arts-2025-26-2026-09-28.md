# UK Community and Engagement Survey 2025/26: broad arts participation, uneven access

## Finding

The updated England arts report shows a broad cultural-participation base but a worrying access pattern. Eighty-four percent of people aged 16+ engaged with the arts at least once in 2025/26, down from 91% in 2024/25. Physical engagement was 83%, down from 90%, while digital engagement was 36%, broadly unchanged from 35%.

The important mechanism is not simply “people stopped caring about culture.” The report shows that digital participation did not expand enough to compensate for the apparent physical decline, and access is strongly stratified by place and socioeconomic position. Physical engagement ranged from 94% in Horsham to 69% in Barking and Dagenham. It was 70% in the most deprived areas compared with 91% in the least deprived. Among people who did not engage, 25% cited affordability as a physical barrier and 21% cited it for digital engagement.

## Cultural-consumer pattern

Arts participation spans cinema, reading, live events, creative practice, recorded and live digital content, and games. Cinema attendance was reported by 51% and reading books, graphic novels or magazines by 56%. Physical engagement was generally higher among middle-aged and older groups, higher occupational classifications, and residents of less deprived areas. This is a mixed cultural-consumer system: participation is broad, but the ability to access its full repertoire depends on time, money, place, skills and supply.

## Limits

The report combines the Participation Survey and the newer CES across April 2025–March 2026. It explicitly warns that the 2025/26 decline may partly reflect methodological differences, including question placement, and says this is under investigation. The estimates are self-reported and do not establish causal withdrawal, spending, repeat attendance, social outcomes or cultural value.

## Program placement

This source extends `trust_identity_meaning`, `household_room_consumption`, `time_hidden_price`, `unequal_exposure_status`, `housing_place_mobility` and `public_systems_feedback`. It joins the cultural-consumer route to local inequality: physical infrastructure, affordability, deprivation, life stage, digital access and cultural confidence shape who can turn cultural interest into actual participation.

The next tests are whether local cultural supply, price relief, transport, accessible programming or digital access change repeat participation, social connection and local voice—and whether the apparent physical decline survives a harmonized measurement design.

# Social insurance must adapt to the self-employed worker’s income, status and life-course volatility

## Finding

The ILO's comparative working paper treats self-employed social insurance as a system-design problem, not simply a registration problem. Self-employed workers make up a large share of informal work, but they do not have an employer to share contributions, maintain records, stabilize payment, or represent their interests. Their work and care schedules, earnings, legal status and access points are often variable.

Across the reviewed cases, policy responses range from integrating self-employed workers into general schemes with tailored mechanisms, to special schemes, unified tax-and-social-security payments, subsidies, one-stop shops, flexible contribution schedules and mandatory or voluntary participation. Voluntary participation in the cited Mongolia and Thailand examples generally underperformed expectations for coverage expansion, while lower contributions could weaken benefit adequacy and perceived value.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`own-account work + irregular income + no employer cost-sharing -> contribution and access friction -> scheme design, subsidy, benefit fit and worker voice -> usable protection or nominal formalization`

The new subtheme is the self-employed social-insurance bargain. Coverage becomes durable only when workers can afford, understand, reach, trust and carry the scheme across changing work and status. A low-cost scheme can increase entry while still failing if its benefits are too thin; a mandatory scheme can produce registration while leaving compliance, adequacy and trust unresolved.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Contribution timing becomes a life-course and household issue when income is seasonal or irregular; flexible payment dates and staged contributions can matter as much as the nominal rate.
- Benefit attractiveness is a form of consumer legitimacy. Workers judge social insurance against immediate needs such as healthcare, credit, funeral costs, education, injury protection and old-age security—not only abstract future coverage.
- One-stop shops and digital access reduce administrative friction only when identity, connectivity, literacy, human assistance and institutional coordination are present.
- Worker organizations are part of the delivery infrastructure. The paper emphasizes trust-based collaboration because self-employed workers are often weakly represented in formal policy channels.
- Formalization can be nominal. Registration without adequate benefits, portability, enforcement, or a path upward as income grows can preserve precarity inside a formally named scheme.

## Boundary and next test

The review uses selected cases rather than a representative global sample; beneficiary evidence and long-run outcomes are sparse, and it does not causally compare voluntary and mandatory schemes. The next test is a named self-employed or platform-work episode that follows policy design into take-up, contribution continuity, benefit claims, income security, gendered adequacy, worker voice and movement between employment statuses.

# Climate risk becomes an occupational bargain: heat exposure is distributed by work, place and protection

## Finding

The ILO's Colombia study makes climate risk occupationally specific. Using Colombian death certificates from 2017–2021 and local temperature records, it finds statistically significant additional occupational mortality above 26°C. Maximum monthly temperatures between 26°C and 32°C are associated with 0.04 additional deaths per 1,000 workers, and temperatures above 32°C with 0.09, relative to lower temperatures.

The highest-risk patterns are not evenly distributed. Agricultural workers, assemblers, health associate professionals and teaching professionals appear among the most affected; construction, manufacturing, mining and transport labourers also show elevated rates. Overall male risk is higher, but women are particularly exposed in health associate professions where they constitute a large share of workers.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`heat exposure + occupation + place -> mortality and productivity risk -> uneven prevention and insurance -> demand for worker-centred climate adaptation`

The new subtheme is the occupational bargain of climate adaptation. General health insurance can approach universal coverage while employment-injury protection reaches only about half of workers. Climate adaptation therefore depends on the workplace institutions that decide rest, hydration, schedules, equipment, risk assessment, reporting and compensation—not only on national temperature or health averages.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Climate exposure is a labor-market distribution, not a universal weather experience. Work intensity, outdoor exposure, protective equipment and the ability to take breaks determine who bears the risk.
- “Coverage” splits into ordinary healthcare and protection against harm caused by work. The former can be nearly universal while the latter remains incomplete.
- Rest time becomes infrastructure. Water, shade, ventilation, modified schedules and acclimatization are practical capabilities that employers and regulators allocate.
- Informality is also an evidence problem: occupational-risk insurance data cannot fully show morbidity among workers outside formal systems, making some harm less visible to policy.
- Worker voice is a safety technology. The paper explicitly treats worker involvement and risk assessment as necessary to design measures that fit actual workplaces.

## Boundary and next test

This is observational Colombian evidence, not a randomized heat intervention. The study uses dry-air temperature rather than WBGT, covers 297 municipalities with station data, and uses a 2022 labor-force distribution to estimate rates for the 2017–2021 period. The next test is a dated heat episode or workplace intervention that follows exposure into hours, breaks, productivity, injury, mortality, claims, income, enforcement and household consequences, especially for informal and rural workers.

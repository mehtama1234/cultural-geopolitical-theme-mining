# Textile circularity moves into policy options while infrastructure and behavior remain open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** consumption and waste pressure → policy options and cross-jurisdiction implementation; circularity conversion remains open

GAO's October 16, 2026 textile circular-economy review adds a cultural-
consumption and infrastructure route. It defines circularity through repair,
reuse, and recycling that keep textiles in use longer, and identifies potential
benefits including new repair and recycling jobs, reduced environmental harm,
and changed consumer practices. GAO also reports limited funding and domestic
collection and recycling infrastructure as challenges.

The institutional stage is a set of concrete policy options. GAO identifies
textile extended producer responsibility, removal of sales tax on secondhand
apparel, and tax credits for industry participation. States, the European
Union, and other jurisdictions are proposing, enacting, or implementing many of
the identified options. Several stakeholders, including industry groups,
preferred a federal approach to improve coordination, compliance, and
consistency across jurisdictions.

The evidence boundary is material. This is a policy-option and stakeholder
review, not a national waste estimate or a causal evaluation. The public
record does not show collection, repair use, recycling yield, consumer practice
change, job creation, environmental improvement, prices, or household welfare.
A policy can assign responsibility without creating affordable repair,
collection capacity, or high-quality recycling.

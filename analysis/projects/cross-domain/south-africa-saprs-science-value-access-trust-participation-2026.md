# South African Public Relationship with Science: high value, low participation

## Finding

The SAPRS study identifies a science relationship that is positive in principle but unequal in practice. South Africans give high scores to the promise of science and technology skills for young people (78/100), environmental concern (72/100), trust in university science information (71/100), and trust in scientists’ work (69/100). Yet the engagement measures that require access, money, connectivity, time or organized opportunity are much weaker: community-based engagement and attraction-based event attendance each score 23/100, while online-app engagement scores 32/100.

This is not a simple trust deficit. Digital access is relatively broad—94% report a cell phone, 76% some internet access and 61% a smartphone—but only 21% report high exposure to science and technology information and only 5% actively access high amounts. Traditional media are more trusted for science information than social or chat media: television 66%, radio 62%, internet websites 45%, social media 32%, and chat apps 31%.

The mechanism is an opportunity and mediation gap. Public support for science can remain strong while active participation depends on household resources, geography, education, connectivity, source credibility and whether institutions create accessible two-way entry points. The study reports sharp subgroup differences in internet use, exposure and consumption, so “the public values science” cannot be treated as evidence that the public can access, shape or benefit from the science system equally.

## Boundary

SAPRS was conducted through Round 19 of the South African Social Attitudes Survey in late 2022. The realised national sample was 6,400 adults aged 16+, drawn through a three-stage probability design across 500 small-area layers and weighted to population distributions. The evidence is nationally representative survey evidence from 2022, not a current 2026 trend estimate, causal evaluation, verified science literacy measure, or proof of policy participation.

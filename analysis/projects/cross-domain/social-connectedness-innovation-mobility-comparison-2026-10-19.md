# Social connectedness, innovation, and mobility

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded network-to-opportunity comparison; no claim that social mixing alone produces mobility or welfare

The economic-connectedness evidence supports two related but distinct routes.

| Stage | Evidence | Observed | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network and innovation | NBER US patenting ZIP-code evidence | A one-standard-deviation increase in high-income friendship share is associated with 97% more patents per capita and 45% more breakthrough patents in the relevant ZIP-code samples; relocation, reverse-causality, and fracking-shock tests support a financing mechanism | Privacy, local institutions, firm survival, jobs, wages, consumer benefit, and whether the mechanism is replicable for low-resource places |
| Network and upward mobility | Related NBER economic-connectedness evidence | A counterfactual placing low-SES children in counties with the connectedness of the average high-SES child implies about 20% higher adult income | Realized exposure, school and labor-market channels, discrimination, place exit, family resources, and causal household outcomes |

## Bounded synthesis

Networks can operate as infrastructure: they may route information, finance,
mentorship, and opportunity into innovation or mobility. But the two routes
have different units and clocks. Patents are an innovation output in places;
adult income is a long-run mobility outcome in a separate connectedness
framework. Neither proves that a person received a useful connection, that a
firm survived, or that local households captured the resulting value.

The shared route is social structure → access to information or finance →
capability and opportunity → innovation, jobs, wages, or mobility. It remains
open whether networks reduce inequality, reproduce status, create privacy or
exclusion costs, or translate into durable local public capacity.

## Next bounded test

The next useful evidence is a named place or cohort joining network exposure
to a specific financing, school, job, patent, wage, or mobility transition,
including non-use and exit. Do not treat patent density or a counterfactual
income estimate as household benefit.

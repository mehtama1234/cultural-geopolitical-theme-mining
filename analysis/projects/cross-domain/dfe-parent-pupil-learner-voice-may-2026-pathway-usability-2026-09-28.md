# Planned educational routes are confident but unevenly usable

**Source:** UK Department for Education, [Parent, Pupil and Learner Voice: May 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/parent-pupil-and-learner-voice-omnibus-surveys-for-2025-to-2026/parent-pupil-and-learner-voice-may-2026)  
**Method:** DfE panel survey conducted online 13–20 May 2026, covering parents, secondary pupils, and 16–19 pupils and learners in England; the report provides separate respondent bases for each question.

## Finding

Young people often express high confidence in a named educational plan, but pathway usability depends on whether alternatives are visible, financially tolerable, geographically available, and supported by school systems. The gap is not simply aspiration versus apathy; it is the conversion from a plan into a reachable route.

```text
planned route
  -> information and reputation
  -> finance, place, attendance, and support conditions
  -> enrollment and persistence
  -> usable education-to-work opportunity
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Public systems and feedback | Learner voice and route visibility | Learners report high confidence in chosen plans while alternative routes remain unevenly known |
| Work, control, and bargaining | Planned opportunity conversion | Career prospects, further study, and work routes are not equally available or legible |
| Unequal exposure and status | Financial and informational sorting | Student-loan anxiety, route reputation, and local availability shape the option set |
| Care, health, and reproduction | Support conversion | Awareness and comfort with school mental-health support exceed reported use |
| Housing, place, and mobility | Reachable pathway | Attendance, travel, local provision, and institutional access condition whether a plan can be used |

## Boundary

This is official, self-reported panel evidence with multiple question bases. It does not establish enrollment, completion, debt, support adequacy, or later earnings; comparisons across years are limited by methodology changes.

## Next test

Follow a named learner cohort from stated plan through offers, travel and finance constraints, enrollment, attendance, support use, completion, work entry, earnings, and subgroup-specific persistence.

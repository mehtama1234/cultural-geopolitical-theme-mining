# Small Acts of Kindness 2026: targeted warmth and information meet an older-person cost-and-connection crisis

## Finding

Ipsos feedback from 618 nominated older and vulnerable residents who received a Warm in Winter or Bag of Kindness gift in Hertfordshire or Bedfordshire shows a tightly coupled pressure system. Four in five respondents said rising living or energy costs negatively affected them; 74% said heating was affected and 68% said the food they buy was affected. Sixty-four percent reported some level of isolation, and 61% agreed that television or radio was their main source of company.

The support encounter was not only material. Eighty-eight percent found the printed Purple Pages information pack useful, 95% said printed information from organisations and charities was important, and open-ended responses often described feeling cheered, remembered or cared for.

## Mechanism

Small targeted gifts can operate across three currencies at once: physical warmth, information reach and social recognition. They may reduce an immediate burden or signal that someone has noticed the recipient, but they do not heat the home, restore food purchasing power, or prove durable isolation reduction. The intervention therefore exposes the gap between relational relief and structural household capacity.

## Cross-domain implication

The next evidence should connect targeted support to actual energy use, food adequacy, benefits or advice take-up, service continuity, health, contact and repeat belonging. It should also compare recipients with eligible non-recipients or follow the same people over time. Recipient gratitude and usefulness are important signals for design, not outcome proof.

## Boundary

The 618 responses were voluntary, paper-based feedback from people nominated to receive charity bags in two English counties during October–December 2025. The prior comparison used 546 recipients under the same broad method. The data are selected, self-reported and non-causal.

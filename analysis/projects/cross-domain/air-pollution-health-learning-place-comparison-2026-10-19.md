# Air pollution, health, learning, and place

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded environmental-health comparison; populations, outcomes, and mechanisms are not pooled

Two source surfaces show that air pollution can produce different downstream
losses depending on the institution and life stage exposed.

| Surface | Evidence | Observed | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult health | Approximately 150,000 initially healthy US Army soldiers linked to annual PM2.5 and quasi-random duty-station assignment | Sustained exposure substantially increases circulatory disease, especially hypertension; short-duration effects are small and respiratory results are mixed | Effect sizes, household exposure, civilian generalization, costs, worker choice, and political response |
| School learning | Approximately 50,000 rural Punjab students in the LEAPS longitudinal panel | A 1 μg/m³ increase in annual PM2.5 lowers difficult-item performance 2.7 percentage points; mathematics effects are larger, school-year exposure matters, and pollution-driven absence explains only a small share | Direct cognition, teacher/classroom quality, health, household adaptation, lifetime earnings, and national generalization |

## Bounded synthesis

Pollution is not only an environmental exposure; it can become a health cost,
a learning loss, and a place-based inequality. The Punjab evidence shows that
school access or attendance alone does not protect learning when instructional
months are polluted. The Army evidence benefits from relatively uniform care
access and a selected population, which helps identify exposure but does not
answer how civilian households with unequal housing, work, transport, or care
options experience the burden.

The shared route is place and infrastructure → exposure → health, attention,
attendance, or instructional quality → household work/care and later security.
The sources identify exposure and some downstream outcomes, but not the same
household's mitigation choice, cost, recovery, or political response.

## Next bounded test

The next useful evidence is a named school, workplace, or household setting
with indoor/outdoor exposure, mitigation or non-use, cost, attendance/work,
health or learning, and later persistence. Do not transfer the Army estimate
to civilian populations or the Punjab learning estimate to lifetime earnings
without a new design.

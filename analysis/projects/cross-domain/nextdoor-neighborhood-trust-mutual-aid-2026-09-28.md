# Neighborhood trust is practically resilient but socially under-connected

**Source:** Nextdoor/The Harris Poll, [Most Americans Still Rely on Their Neighbors](https://blog.nextdoor.com/most-americans-still-rely-on-their-neighbors.-new-nextdoor-research-shows-the-conditions-for-stronger-communities-are-already-in-place)  
**Method:** Online survey of 1,003 U.S. adults age 18+, conducted April 16–20, 2026 and weighted to population characteristics; commissioned by Nextdoor.

## Finding

Neighborhood trust is not well described as simple social collapse. Many Americans still have practical neighbor reliance and local recommendation trust, but connection is not equally available: younger adults report much more loneliness and much less welcome. The key mechanism is repeated, low-stakes contact; digital neighborhood tools may reduce the friction of finding that contact, but the survey does not show that apps create trust by themselves.

```text
neighbor proximity / repeated contact
  -> practical help and accountable local recommendations
  -> belonging and trust where interaction is available
  -> young-adult connection gap where welcome routes are missing
  -> test of whether social infrastructure converts contact into durable mutual aid
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Trust, identity, and meaning | Practical neighborhood trust | Mutual aid and belonging coexist with broader narratives of social decline |
| Housing, place, and mobility | Place-based social infrastructure | Proximity, residency, public space, and events create recurring contact opportunities |
| Public systems and feedback | Low-stakes route to participation | Communication and everyday interaction are reported as more important than formal app use alone |
| Unequal exposure and status | Young-adult welcome gap | Younger adults report higher loneliness and lower neighbor inclusion |
| Platforms, data, and attention | Platform-assisted proximity | Neighborhood apps may lower the cost of contact, but platform presence is not a trust outcome |

## Boundary

This is platform-commissioned, cross-sectional online-panel research. It does not observe actual aid, recommendation accuracy, completed services, app treatment effects, or durable trust change.

## Next test

Follow a named neighborhood intervention or local-service episode from contact and recommendation through actual help, completed service, cost, inclusion, remedy, repeat interaction, and subgroup-specific trust.

# Statistics Canada 2026: mobility is a small adjustment channel and a measurement problem

## Finding

Statistics Canada’s September 2026 paper develops occupation-level experimental estimates of interprovincial labour mobility. The policy premise is intuitive: workers could move from high-unemployment regions to regions with shortages. But the article notes that only about 1% to 2% of the working-age population moves between provinces each year, while residency rules, occupational licensing and non-recognition of qualifications remain barriers.

The paper uses linked 2021 Census and 2022 tax data to estimate occupation-specific migration parameters, then updates them with current Labour Force Survey demographics to produce predicted estimates for 2022–23 through 2025–26. This creates a useful monitoring infrastructure, but not a direct measure of realized migration.

## Validation and mechanism

In validation across 133 occupational groups, only 16 had predicted-versus-observed differences of 5% or less. Fifty-six fell between above 5% and 25%, 53 between above 25% and 50%, and eight exceeded 50%. Predicted rates were generally somewhat lower than observed rates.

The result is a two-layer theme. First, mobility is institutionally constrained and household-costly: recognition, housing, care, family ties and worker security shape whether a job opportunity is movable. Second, the state needs timely measurement between census years, but the model assumes that mobility behavior and response parameters remain stable. That assumption becomes fragile when policy, housing, macroeconomic conditions or regional labor demand change.

## Limits

The paper is methodological and descriptive. It does not estimate the causal effect of Bill C-5 or any labor-mobility reform, observe all relocation costs, or show that increased movement improves household welfare, regional equality or worker bargaining power. The 2021 reference period also contains pandemic-era conditions that may not transport cleanly to later years.

## Program placement

This source extends `housing_place_mobility`, `work_control_bargaining`, `public_systems_feedback`, `unequal_exposure_status`, `household_room_consumption` and `infrastructure_technology_dependency`. It turns mobility into an open-middle route: formal recognition and information must become actual movement, job quality, housing fit, care continuity, social belonging and durable economic security.

The next tests are whether occupational-recognition reforms, relocation supports, housing supply, childcare access or worker voice change observed movement and household outcomes—not merely predicted mobility rates.

# Temporary migration reaches old age: social protection must follow the life course across borders

## Finding

The ILO STREAM paper on South Asia–Gulf migration shows a policy mismatch: migration systems still often assume that workers will return home at retirement, while many workers spend decades in the Gulf and build multigenerational lives there. For long-term Indian migrant families in the UAE, retirement is therefore not simply the end of a labour contract; it is a cross-border question about income, healthcare, housing, residency, family care, and belonging.

The distinct subtheme is portability across the life course. A worker may be mobile at recruitment and employment but become administratively stranded in later life if end-of-service benefits, health coverage, residence status, housing access, and family ties do not travel together.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`temporary labour-migration model -> long-term settlement and multigenerational family -> employment-linked protection ends -> retirement income, healthcare, housing, and residence gaps -> cross-border family burden or forced return -> need for portable and rights-based social protection`

The paper identifies three connected pressures: retirement income often rests on end-of-service indemnity, personal savings, and family support rather than comprehensive pensions; employer-linked healthcare may disappear after employment; and housing and visa conditions can make later-life residence financially insecure. These are not separate old-age issues. They are the delayed consequences of recruitment systems, wage levels, employment stability, employer benefits, origin-country protection, and destination-country residence rules.

This adds a migration-to-care subtheme to the atlas. Family support can be a meaningful institution, but it can also transfer public and employer obligations into adult children, remittances, housing, unpaid care, and return decisions. The unit of analysis must therefore extend beyond the worker to the family corridor and the institutions that determine whether contributions, benefits, health access, and residence can be carried across borders.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- “Temporary migrant worker” is becoming an inadequate cultural and administrative identity for people who age, retire, and raise families in the destination.
- Retirement security is also a housing, healthcare, visa, family-care, and belonging problem.
- Social-protection portability becomes a form of mobility infrastructure and cross-border political legitimacy.
- Employer-linked benefits create a delayed exposure: the cost of protection becomes most visible when employment ends.
- Origin and destination states share responsibility for workers whose lives no longer fit a simple return/remittance model.

## Boundary and next test

The paper is a focused qualitative and policy analysis centered on lower- and middle-income Indian migrant families in the UAE. It does not provide representative Gulf-wide prevalence, causal estimates, complete contribution histories, or evidence that proposed reforms have been adopted. The next test is a named migration corridor followed from recruitment and payroll through contributions, healthcare coverage, residence status, housing, family care, retirement, return or settlement, and actual benefit portability.

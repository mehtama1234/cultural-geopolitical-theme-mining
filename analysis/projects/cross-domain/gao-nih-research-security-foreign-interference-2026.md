# NIH research-security controls can act on disclosed allegations while residual risk remains open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** NIH disclosure and allegation system → research-security action; prevalence, interference, and research outcomes remain open

GAO's October 5, 2026 review adds a research-governance and foreign-
interference route. NIH awarded about $35 billion in extramural funding in
fiscal year 2025 and had implemented 14 of 15 federal research-security
requirements reviewed. From 2017 through 2025, NIH received 697 allegations of
potential foreign interference: 264 involved identified disclosure violations,
and NIH or recipient institutions took action in 223 of those cases.

The visibility gap is important. NIH relies heavily on researcher and
institution self-disclosures and generally does not verify them with outside
sources during pre-award review unless it receives an allegation. Seven
nongeneralizable GAO case studies found public information about foreign
affiliations or coauthored publications that did not appear in award
documentation. NIH does not require all past affiliations to be disclosed, and
foreign-component disclosure is limited to significant project activity outside
the United States. One recommendation remains open for a recurring,
extramural-program-specific fraud-risk assessment.

The evidence boundary is material. Allegations are not a prevalence estimate,
violations are not proof of foreign interference, and actions do not establish
successful deterrence, research recovery, economic-security outcomes, or trust.

# Climate resilience is a question of who can act locally

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank, *Building Community Resilience in a Complex Setting: Opportunities for Papua New Guinea*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-png-locally-led-climate-resilience-local-governance-2026.json)

## Core finding

The report finds substantial local variation in climate and conflict risk across
Papua New Guinea, while national climate policy remains weakly connected to the
Local-Level Governments responsible for delivery. Those local institutions often
lack the authority, finance, and technical support needed to act.

At the same time, wantok networks, Indigenous and traditional knowledge,
churches, and community organizations remain important resilience
infrastructure—but are increasingly strained by overlapping climate, conflict,
and socioeconomic pressures.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The report reframes resilience from a property that communities either possess or
lack into a relationship between local social systems and public institutions.
Local networks may know which places, livelihoods, and social obligations matter,
but without finance and authority they are asked to absorb risks they cannot
control.

This creates a political distinction between participation and power. Community
platforms can build social cohesion and improve fit, but locally led adaptation
only becomes meaningful when communities and local governments can influence
priorities, access climate information and finance, and hold delivery systems
accountable.

## Open middle

```text
unequal climate and conflict exposure
  -> centralized policy disconnected from local authority and knowledge
  -> local capacity, finance, information, and community platforms
  -> climate-resilient livelihoods and social cohesion
  -> trusted adaptation or downloaded responsibility
```

The report combines mixed methods, risk profiling, consultations, and
institutional analysis. It does not establish causal resilience effects, equal
representation, durable finance, or that community networks can replace public
capacity.

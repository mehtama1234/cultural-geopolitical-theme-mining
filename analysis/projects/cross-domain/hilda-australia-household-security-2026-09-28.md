# HILDA 2026: household security, life-course timing, and relational resilience

## Finding

The 2026 HILDA Statistical Report adds a longitudinal household-life route: Australian households can show strong intimate relationships and high employment while still experiencing food insecurity, housing and money stress, job mismatch, delayed residential independence, fatigue, and weaker reported health among younger people. Security is therefore not a single employment or sentiment outcome; it is a conversion problem across work fit, housing, food, health, relationships and time.

## Evidence

- HILDA follows roughly 17,000 Australians annually and covers Waves 1–24, with selected 2024 outcomes in the 2026 report.
- The University of Melbourne summary reports that about half of Australian workers are over-educated for their jobs.
- More than one in six Australians aged 15 and over experienced food insecurity in 2024.
- About half of 18–29-year-olds were living with their parents.
- The same release reports strong life-partner relationships alongside housing and financial stress, rising fatigue, and worse reported health among younger people.

## Interpretation and limits

The useful signal is coexistence and timing, not a claim that one factor causes another. Longitudinal coverage makes HILDA valuable for separating life-course persistence from one-period sentiment, but the public summary is not a respondent-level causal extraction. Exact instruments, analytic denominators, subgroup estimates and trajectories must be taken from the full report or restricted data.

## Program placement

This record extends `household_room_consumption`, `housing_place_mobility`, `work_control_bargaining`, `care_health_reproduction`, and `trust_identity_meaning`. It should be compared with the existing household-time, housing, food-security, relationship, and youth-belonging records without pooling Australia with U.S. or UK measures.

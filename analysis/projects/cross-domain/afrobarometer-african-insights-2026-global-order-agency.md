# African publics want integration without surrendering agency

## Finding

Afrobarometer’s 2026 flagship report, based on nationally representative surveys in 38 African countries, shows a public that is broadly open to cross-border integration but highly attentive to who receives the benefits, who bears the costs, and who gets to decide.

Sixty-two percent say trade with other countries will benefit their economy, yet only 13% have heard of the African Continental Free Trade Area. Fifty-six percent support free movement within their subregion, while 63% say crossing borders for work or trade is difficult. Three-quarters are comfortable with immigrants as neighbors, but job competition produces much stronger restriction preferences. Openness and protection are therefore not opposites in the data: people distinguish mobility, social contact, economic opportunity, and scarce employment.

The same pattern appears geopolitically. Respondents view China, the United States, and the European Union positively in substantial numbers, 72% prefer neutrality in the Russia–Ukraine war among those aware of it, and 71% want African countries to have more influence in international decision-making. This looks less like passive alignment than a demand for multi-partner agency—though public preference is not the same as bargaining power.

Climate attitudes add a material boundary. Many respondents report worsening drought and crop failure, daily adaptation, and relocation, while supporting climate action but resisting policies that raise household costs. Climate justice becomes credible only when global responsibility and local affordability meet.

## Theme and subthemes

- **Integration without surrender:** support for trade and mobility coexists with demands for local opportunity and protection.
- **Pragmatic multipolarity:** positive views of multiple external powers and neutrality can express diversification rather than side-taking.
- **Representation as agency:** greater African voice in global institutions is a public legitimacy demand.
- **Openness versus labor scarcity:** neighbor acceptance and migration restriction can coexist when social contact is separated from job competition.
- **Climate justice as cost allocation:** exposure and adaptation create demand for action, but household affordability sets the political boundary.

## What the source supports

The report supports cross-national descriptive patterns across 38 African countries and associations between material conditions, education, development, media, mobility, external-power perceptions, and climate attitudes. It supports reading trade, migration, geopolitical, and climate questions together as a public-agency map.

It does not establish causal effects, actual trade or migration flows, geopolitical dependence, climate damages, or whether public preferences translate into government policy.

## Next test

Follow named AfCFTA, free-movement, migration, climate-adaptation, renewable-energy, or multilateral-representation episodes from public demand through implementation, local price and job effects, service access, voice, and durable legitimacy. Test whether visible benefits and fair cost allocation convert broad integration preferences into sustained institutional support.

Source: [Afrobarometer, African Insights 2026: Beyond borders](https://www.afrobarometer.org/feature/african-insights-2026-beyond-borders-citizen-perspectives-on-a-shifting-global-order/)

# Diaspora identity is carried by media exposure and direct cultural contact

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology, comparative study of new-generation ethnic Chinese in Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-frontiers-ethnic-chinese-cultural-identity-southeast-asia-2026.json)

## Core finding

This study adds a diaspora cultural-reproduction route to the atlas. Among
second-generation-or-later ethnic Chinese respondents in Singapore, Malaysia,
and Thailand, Chinese content exposure and visits to China were the most
consistent factors associated with Chinese cultural identity. Language
proficiency mattered more unevenly, and large-screen preference was not a
stable cross-country mechanism.

```text
heritage access and direct cultural contact
  -> Chinese content exposure
  -> cultural knowledge, memory, and participation
  -> negotiated diaspora identity
  -> different national expressions of belonging
```

The key insight is that cultural identity is neither inherited unchanged nor
simply erased by integration. It is reproduced through accessible media,
travel, language, and the institutions that make those routes available.

## Boundary

The study used 1,298 self-report online-panel responses from Singapore,
Malaysia, and Thailand. The authors explicitly note panel and older-age
representation limits. Regression and mediation results are associations in
cross-sectional data, not causal estimates. Chinese cultural identity is
defined as attachment to cultural heritage, not political identification with
the People's Republic of China.

# Health, disability, and care access source packet

**Purpose:** source packet for the company-trend record on unequal health,
disability, aging, and care access.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-31

This packet keeps independent official sources separate. They do not follow the
same person, household, worker, or care episode.

## 1. CDC disability prevalence and health-care access

- **Source:** CDC, *Prevalence of Disabilities and Health Care Access by
  Disability Status and Type Among Adults*.
- **URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/disability-and-health/articles-documents/disabilities-health-care-access.html
- **Published:** 2025-04-11.
- **Unit and geography:** U.S. adults; 2022 disability prevalence summary and
  a separate analysis of 2016 BRFSS access responses.
- **Direct observation:** CDC reports more than 61 million U.S. adults, about
  one in four, reported a disability in 2022. The BRFSS analysis found
  disability-specific differences in insurance, usual source of care,
  cost-related unmet need, and routine check-up access; vision disability had
  the least access among the types studied.
- **Limit:** The prevalence summary and the 2016 access analysis are not one
  time-aligned person-level panel. Survey responses do not show a company
  service pathway or completed remedy.

## 2. NCHS nonfinancial barriers to medical care

- **Source:** CDC/NCHS, *National Health Statistics Reports, Number 207*.
- **URL:** https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhsr/nhsr207.pdf
- **Published:** 2024-08-01; estimates use the 2022 National Health Interview
  Survey.
- **Unit and geography:** U.S. civilian noninstitutionalized adults; weighted
  household survey estimates.
- **Direct observation:** 21.7% of adults delayed or did not get medical care
  in the prior year because of at least one of five nonfinancial barriers;
  12.5% were too busy, 10.6% could not find an available appointment, 4.6%
  could not get to a provider when open, 4.4% had difficulty finding an
  insurance-compatible provider, and 2.4% said travel took too long.
- **Limit:** Categories are not mutually exclusive, and this is a survey
  measure of delay/nonreceipt, not a linked care outcome or employer record.

## 3. Transportation and daily-life burden

- **Source:** AHRQ/MEPS, *Lack of Reliable Transportation for Daily Living
  Among Adults: United States, 2021*, Statistical Brief 558.
- **URL:** https://meps.ahrq.gov/data_files/publications/st558/stat558.shtml
- **Published:** 2024-11.
- **Unit and geography:** U.S. adults in the 2021 MEPS Household Component.
- **Direct observation:** About 6% reported that lack of reliable
  transportation kept them from medical appointments, meetings, work, or
  daily-life needs in the prior year. The percentage was higher for people in
  fair or poor physical or mental health and rose with the number of health
  conditions.
- **Limit:** The measure combines medical, work, meeting, and daily-life
  activities; it does not identify a particular service provider or outcome.

## 4. Caregiving measurement

- **Source:** CDC Healthy Aging Data, *BRFSS Caregiver Module* and the
  Alzheimer’s Disease and Healthy Aging Data Portal.
- **URLs:**
  - https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-aging-data/brfss/caregiver.html
  - https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-aging-data/data-portal/
- **Unit and geography:** BRFSS adult respondents in participating states and
  territories; caregiver status, care-recipient condition, duration, and hours
  are module variables.
- **Direct observation:** The module provides a repeatable way to measure
  adults who provide regular care or assistance to a person with a health
  problem or disability, including duration and hours of care.
- **Limit:** Participation and module availability vary by year and geography;
  the module alone does not connect caregiving to an employer, customer, or
  specific care outcome.

## What this packet supports

Across independent official sources, access depends on disability type,
nonfinancial time and availability barriers, transportation, health burden,
and unpaid care responsibilities. The evidence supports an access-and-burden
pattern, not a single causal estimate or a company-specific impact claim.

## What remains open

- whether a named company workflow converts accommodation or access effort into
  completed service and better outcomes;
- how care responsibilities affect retention, absence, scheduling, and customer
  completion in a specific company;
- which groups are missing from visible service denominators; and
- whether an intervention reduces burden without shifting it to workers,
  households, or carers.

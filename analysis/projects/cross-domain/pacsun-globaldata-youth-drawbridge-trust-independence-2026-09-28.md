# The Drawbridge Generation: youth trust, AI adoption, and delayed independence

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Pacsun and GlobalData, 2026 Youth Report  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/us-pacsun-globaldata-youth-report-2026-drawbridge-trust-financial-independence.json)

## Core finding

The report describes a youth culture that is highly connected but increasingly
selective about authority. Family and friends remain the trusted route for
money and purchase decisions, while AI is widely used for discovery but rarely
trusted for fashion, financial, or relationship advice. At the same time,
financial independence has weakened year over year and mental health now sits
alongside stable work, a car, and homeownership as a defining future goal.

```text
noisy public information + economic uncertainty
  -> selective trust boundary around family and friends
  -> AI used as utility/discovery, not authority
  -> delayed material independence and household reliance
  -> redefined success: stability plus mental wellbeing
```

The central mechanism is not youth rejection of technology. It is selective
delegation: adoption can be high while credibility remains domain-specific.

## Boundary

The release reports a 6,126-person U.S. survey of ages 11-24, described as
representative by age, gender, region, and household income. It is
Pacsun/GlobalData-sponsored research; the public release does not provide the
full questionnaire, weighting protocol, or all subgroup tables. The findings
therefore support a bounded youth-cultural mechanism, not causal explanations
of dependence, clinical prevalence, or actual AI decision quality.

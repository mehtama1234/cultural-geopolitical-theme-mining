# Shared religious practice and value alignment appear as relationship infrastructure

## Finding

The Institute for Family Studies' September 25, 2026 public insight reports that, among married adults ages 20–54, 78% of churchgoing husbands and 74% of churchgoing wives described their marriages as very or extremely happy, compared with 66% and 63% respectively among those attending church infrequently or never. It also says spouses' agreement on core marriage and faith values is one of the strongest predictors of marital happiness.

The useful signal is not a claim that church attendance mechanically produces happiness. It is that recurring communal practice may matter when it creates shared time, meaning, expectations and social reinforcement. Relationship wellbeing can therefore be connected to the condition of social infrastructure around a household, not only to private attitudes.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`recurring communal practice -> shared meaning and relational alignment -> household wellbeing and belonging -> time allocation, service choice and civic capacity`

The mechanism is portable beyond religion. Families, neighborhoods, cultural organizations, voluntary groups and civic institutions can all provide repeated settings in which people coordinate expectations and experience recognition. Their value may be partly in making shared interpretation easier to sustain.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Social connection is not only a feeling; it can be produced by repeated institutions that create time, obligations, rituals and trusted relationships.
- Shared values may be more important than attendance alone, but the public insight does not show whether agreement precedes happiness, follows it, or reflects selection into particular marriages and communities.
- Institutions that provide dependable recurring spaces may affect household time and service choices, yet the source does not measure spending, fertility, care, consumer behavior or civic participation.
- Communal infrastructure can support belonging while also imposing exclusionary norms or unequal burdens. The finding should therefore be tested against who can enter, who is represented, and who bears the costs of participation.

## Boundary and next test

This is a public insight with limited methodological detail and an associational interpretation. The next test is the underlying study or independent survey evidence with sample, measures, temporal ordering and subgroup detail, followed by comparison with nonreligious forms of recurring social connection.

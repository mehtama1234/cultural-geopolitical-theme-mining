# KIIS Ukraine 2026: infrastructure endurance and conditional wartime legitimacy

## Finding

The Kyiv International Institute of Sociology’s August–September 2026 surveys show two forms of resilience that should not be collapsed. Seventy-seven percent say Ukraine should continue armed resistance even if a Russian winter attack temporarily leaves some people without electricity, heating or sewage; 12% would accept any Russian demands to preserve those services and 11% are undecided. Support remains a majority in every reported region, but ranges from 83% in Kyiv city to 68% in the East, where direct exposure and accumulated costs may shape the judgment differently.

The second form is institutional legitimacy. Fifty-four percent trust President Zelenskyy and 41% do not, but national resistance is not simply a proxy for leader approval: 89% of presidential trusters support continued resistance, compared with 65% of distrusters. Military personnel and veterans are at 85%, while respondents without military service are at 77%. The implication is that a society can sustain a national defense commitment while holding leadership to a separate standard of conduct, anti-corruption performance and personnel decisions.

## Mechanism

Infrastructure attacks do not automatically produce capitulation; they can instead strengthen an existential threat frame and a willingness to endure. But the legitimacy of who governs during that endurance remains conditional. Energy security, household hardship, regional exposure, service experience and anti-corruption accountability therefore form a linked geopolitical–civic system.

## Boundary

KIIS used CATI interviews with randomly generated mobile numbers and statistical weighting: 1,003 adults aged 18+ living in Ukrainian government-controlled territory. Occupied territories and citizens abroad were excluded. The winter question is hypothetical, and wartime survey estimates carry systematic uncertainty beyond formal sampling error. These results do not measure actual outages, military capacity, corruption, or causal relationships.

Sources: [KIIS winter energy-attack survey](https://www.kiis.com.ua/?cat=reports&id=1634&lang=eng&page=1&t=1) and [KIIS presidential-trust survey](https://www.kiis.com.ua/?cat=reports&id=1635&lang=eng&page=1&t=1).

# Urban location advantage can become a hidden climate and coordination tax

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11444, *Evaluating Spatial Underperformance: A Spatial Computable General Equilibrium Model Applied to Ho Chi Minh City*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-ho-chi-minh-thu-duc-spatial-underperformance-flood-2026.json)

## Core finding

The World Bank model finds a large gap between Thu Duc’s strategic location and
its current modeled productivity and density. Flood exposure is treated as part
of the spatial equilibrium—not as an isolated environmental hazard—because it
affects firm location, household residence, commuting, land use, productivity,
and residential amenity.

In the cumulative scenario, innovation zones, flood mitigation, and transport
raise output roughly two-thirds above business as usual, but only about 56% of
the gain is net new metropolitan output; the remainder is activity reallocated
from elsewhere.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The finding reframes urban underperformance as a lived coordination failure. A
district can be near opportunity yet functionally distant from it when flooding,
transport, housing, land use, and fragmented governance impose repeated costs on
firms and households.

It also complicates growth narratives. Metropolitan output can rise while gains
are redistributed across places, land values increase, or lower-income residents
are displaced. The relevant consumer and cultural question is not only whether a
district becomes more productive, but whether people can remain, commute, and
benefit from the new urban identity.

## Open middle

```text
flood exposure and weak spatial coordination
  -> lower productivity, amenity, density, and access
  -> coordinated mitigation, transport, innovation, and land-use change
  -> firm location, household residence, commuting, and metropolitan output
  -> net-new opportunity or displacement and reallocation
```

The paper is a spatial general-equilibrium simulation. It does not establish
completed infrastructure effects, equitable household welfare, displacement
outcomes, or realized net-new growth.

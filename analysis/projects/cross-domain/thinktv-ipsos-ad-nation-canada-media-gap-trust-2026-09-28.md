# The audience is not the media model: Canadian platform assumptions, trust, and shared attention

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** thinkTV and Ipsos, Ad Nation 2026  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-thinktv-ipsos-ad-nation-2026-canada-media-gap-trust.json)

## Core finding

The Ad Nation 2026 study identifies a durable consumer-media translation gap.
Industry professionals substantially overestimate public TikTok use, while
Canadian respondents remain skeptical of advertising and more positive about
television's attention and shared-experience role. The result is not simply
that one channel is “winning.” It is that media systems can mistake their own
high-exposure professional environment for public reality.

```text
professional media environment
  -> assumptions about audience adoption and attention
  -> channel allocation and creative strategy
  -> public exposure, credibility, and shared attention
  -> trust, recognition, action, or rejection
```

The study is especially useful for the atlas because it joins consumer
behavior, platform infrastructure, cultural connection, and epistemic trust
in one comparison. The sample is not a national probability survey and the
reported attitudes are not observed conversion.

## Open middle

The next evidence target is not another channel ranking. It is whether
correcting professional audience models changes creative choice, exposure
quality, trust, and realized consumer action for different groups.

## Boundary

The consumer sample was 600 Canadians aged 18-59 outside marketing/media, and
the professional sample was 174 Canadian Marketing Association members. The
publisher does not provide the full questionnaire, weighting details, or
complete cross-tabs on the release page. The study therefore supports a
translation-gap mechanism, not national platform prevalence, causal campaign
performance, or proof of television-driven cohesion.

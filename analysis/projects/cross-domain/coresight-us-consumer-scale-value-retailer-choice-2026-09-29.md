# Consumer value pressure is being expressed through scale-versus-value retailer choice

## Finding

Coresight Research's September 29, 2026 public preview frames a new US consumer survey deep dive around a scale-versus-value split: consumers are described as rewarding scale while Amazon gains and dollar stores lose shopper momentum. The preview says the report tracks sentiment by income and weekly rates of shopping at major retailers and purchasing in selected categories.

Because the full report is subscription-only, the defensible finding is narrower: current economic pressure is being investigated through the route from income-divergent sentiment to retailer choice. The public preview does not establish the size, durability or cause of the reported momentum.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`inflation and geopolitical pressure -> income-divergent sentiment -> scale/value and convenience tradeoffs -> retailer substitution and category choice -> household room and firm market power`

This route matters because consumer confidence and consumer action can diverge. A household may report pessimism while consolidating purchases at a scaled retailer, trading local variety or social shopping for price, convenience, delivery and perceived reliability.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- “Value” is not only a low price. It can mean fewer trips, predictable availability, delivery, trustworthy returns, broad assortment or lower search costs—benefits that differ by income, place, time and mobility.
- Scale can become a household adaptation to uncertainty, but it can also concentrate firm power and narrow the number of viable local retail routes.
- Income-divergent sentiment should be connected to actual baskets, promotions, credit, travel time and substitution rather than treated as a single consumer mood.
- The preview’s commercial framing is itself informative: retail research is treating macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty as a competition between scale, value and shopper momentum.

## Boundary and next test

This is a public-preview record, not a full survey extraction. The next test is independent transaction or panel evidence that can distinguish new shoppers from higher frequency, substitution from basket compression, and retailer momentum from temporary promotion or seasonal effects.

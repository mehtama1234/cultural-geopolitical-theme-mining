# Statistics Canada 2026: skilled-trade integration is not completed by certification

## Finding

Statistics Canada’s linked administrative-data study follows childhood immigrants admitted to Canada before age 18 who became certified journeypersons, comparing them with Canadian-born journeypersons across four years before certification, the certification year, and four years after.

Childhood immigrant journeypersons earned less at every measured point. The median gap widened from about $4,700 four years before certification to about $8,500 in the certification year, then narrowed only modestly to about $7,800 four years after. After adjustment for sociodemographic, program, trade and employment characteristics, the gap remained approximately 9% four years before certification and 6%–7% at later stages.

This is a useful correction to a simple “integration succeeded” story. Canadian schooling and trade certification can coexist with unequal earnings, weaker union and pension coverage, and lower interprovincial mobility. At the same time, the aggregate gap conceals trade-specific reversals: childhood immigrants earned more in selected construction trades, while Canadian-born workers earned more in several industrial and mechanical trades.

## Institutional mechanisms and limits

Union coverage was reported for 33% of childhood immigrant journeypersons versus 46% of Canadian-born journeypersons; workplace pension coverage was 16% versus 30%. Interprovincial mobility was approximately 3% versus 5% two years after certification and 4% versus 6% four years after certification.

Post-certification growth was not uniformly worse: median growth was 17% versus 15% two years after certification and 21% versus 19% four years after certification, while adjusted growth was slightly lower at two years and not statistically different at four years. A lower level and a similar later growth rate are different claims.

The study uses linked immigration, apprenticeship and tax records, with a large administrative population. It is observational, restricted to complete tax-record cases, and cannot identify a single causal barrier. Certified trade may not equal occupation at every time point, and the study does not measure household wealth, health, discrimination, job quality, subjective belonging or family needs.

## Program placement

This source extends `work_control_bargaining`, `unequal_exposure_status`, `housing_place_mobility`, `household_room_consumption`, `public_systems_feedback` and `trust_identity_meaning`. It treats labor-market inclusion as a trajectory: entry, certification, trade sorting, bargaining institutions, mobility, earnings level and earnings growth.

The next tests are whether union access, pension coverage, regional mobility, trade-specific demand, employer practices or targeted apprenticeship supports change the earnings-level gap and household room—not merely certification rates or aggregate employment.

# Critical-mineral ownership on federal lands remains partly invisible

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** federal-land mining and ownership visibility → geoeconomic dependence; control and security outcomes remain open

GAO's critical-minerals review adds a geoeconomic route. As of February 2026,
632 hardrock mining plans of operation were approved on federal land. GAO
reviewed ownership information for 43 claimants and operators associated with
critical-mineral mines, determined ownership for 32, and found 14 foreign
subsidiaries in that set. None had Chinese-owned parent companies, but at least
two foreign parent companies had Chinese financial interests.

The visibility gap is structural. The 1872 Mining Law does not require BLM or
the Forest Service to collect foreign-parent information or require claimants
and operators to submit foreign-ownership information; a foreign business can
use a U.S. subsidiary as claimant. Meanwhile, the supply chain faces capital and
domestic-processing constraints, and the United States exports large quantities
of extracted ore to Canada, Mexico, China, and elsewhere. CFIUS has authority to
review some foreign investment for national-security concerns, but the report
does not show a mine-level review or outcome.

The evidence boundary is material. Ownership could not be determined for 11 of
43 reviewed entities, and foreign-subsidiary status or financial interest does
not establish beneficial control, production, processing, disruption, domestic
availability, environmental effects, or national-security harm.

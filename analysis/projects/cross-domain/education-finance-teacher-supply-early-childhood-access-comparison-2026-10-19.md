# Education finance, teacher supply, and early-childhood access

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded education-infrastructure comparison; aggregate returns, service capacity, and lived outcomes remain separate

Three source surfaces describe different clocks and levels of education
provision.

| Stage | Evidence | Observed | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-run public investment | US school-finance reform evidence | About 4.7% higher state earnings per worker and more than 5% higher GDP per capita after 30 years; the 7.8% 60-year result is simulated | Equal distribution, student wellbeing, local access, school quality, household conversion, and whether every reform produces the same path |
| Teacher supply and status | OECD Education at a Glance 2026 | 9.1% of teachers not fully qualified, 6.4% attrition among fully qualified teachers, sharper shortages in rural/disadvantaged places and mathematics/science; 85% report job satisfaction but only 22% say teaching is socially valued | Causal retention levers, local staffing stability, learning, workload, pay effects, and community trust |
| Early-childhood care and territorial recovery | World Bank Ukraine ECPE review | Enrollment fell 31.1% from 2021 to 2024; per-child spending varies nearly 50-fold across hromadas; 64% have both under- and over-staffed preschools; teacher pay is about half the national average | Causal effect of the Preschool Law, safe access, child development, caregiver work, service continuity, and recovery |

## Bounded synthesis

Education is a social infrastructure with multiple clocks. A school-finance
reform can yield aggregate earnings decades later; teacher shortages can
degrade access and continuity now; early-childhood systems can simultaneously
have excess capacity, unmet demand, and fiscal inequality across places. GDP,
qualification, enrollment, and occupancy are therefore not interchangeable
measures of educational security.

The common conversion route is public finance and governance → staffing and
place capacity → usable attendance and care → learning, caregiver work, and
long-run opportunity. The sources expose the first three stages unevenly but do
not follow the same child or household through safe access, completed use,
learning, work, and durable mobility.

## Next bounded test

The next useful evidence is a named place or school/preschool network with
staffing, capacity, attendance or care use, caregiver time, and later learning
or work outcomes. Keep long-run macro returns separate from current service
availability and household experience.

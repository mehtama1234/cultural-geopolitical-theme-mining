# Thailand September 2026: routine consumption can persist while public legitimacy deteriorates

## Finding

Ipsos reports that financial and political corruption remains Thailand's top concern at 52%, while crime and violence rises to second place at 34%, tied with poverty and social inequality but ranking ahead of it. Unemployment reaches 30%, up 11 percentage points from a year earlier. Fifty-two percent say the country is heading in the wrong direction, and 61% describe the current economy as bad.

Yet everyday purchasing confidence is more resilient: 64% feel more comfortable making routine household purchases than six months earlier, compared with 50% for major purchases. Forty-six percent expect their personal financial situation to strengthen, while 19% expect it to weaken.

## Mechanism

Consumers can continue buying necessities and routine goods while withdrawing confidence from the macroeconomic and political system. Spending is therefore not a simple proxy for legitimacy: necessity, substitution and short-horizon adaptation can coexist with corruption anxiety, safety concern, unemployment risk and doubts about national direction.

## Cross-domain implication

The next evidence should connect concern rankings and consumer confidence to actual household budgets, debt, subsidy receipt, crime exposure, labor-market trajectories and political attribution. The cited 43-billion-baht consumer-subsidy extension is a policy-response context, not evidence that purchasing power or trust improved.

## Boundary

This is a public Ipsos country analysis combining concern and consumer-sentiment indicators. The page does not publish one pooled denominator or respondent-level data, and it does not establish objective corruption, crime, unemployment, realized purchases, subsidy incidence or causal effects.

Source: [Ipsos: What Worries Thailand? September 2026](https://www.ipsos.com/en-th/what-worries-thailand-september-2026).

# Energy transition, household affordability, and public legitimacy audit v1

Date: 2026-10-18  
Program: Cultural, Social, and Geopolitical Theme Mining  
Status: active rotation; this is a bounded evidence audit, not a final conclusion

## Research question

When energy systems transition under climate, AI-demand, and geopolitical pressure,
do investment and clean-energy programs become affordable, reliable household
service and public legitimacy, or do they shift cost and reliability risk onto
households?

The long-term program treats this as a recurring conversion chain:

`capital and policy -> grid and supply integration -> household bill/service ->
adaptation, health, and financial room -> legitimacy, contestation, and future
choice`.

The chain is deliberately not collapsed into one index. Investment, policy
announcements, attitudes, household conditions, and realized outcomes have
different denominators and methods.

## Evidence ledger

| Layer | Source and observation | What it can support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global capital | [IEA World Energy Investment 2026](../../records/comparative-iea-world-energy-investment-2026-capital-grids-affordability.json): about $1.5 trillion expected in 2025 electricity generation, grids, and storage investment, roughly 50% above investment bringing oil, gas, and coal to market. | Electricity is becoming the investment center of the system; grid, finance, and geography are distribution mechanisms. | Estimated/committed investment is not completed capacity, lower bills, reliability, or equitable local benefit. |
| Global household cost | [IEA household affordability 2026](../../records/comparative-iea-household-energy-affordability-2026.json): average global household energy bills were modeled 4% above 2019 in 2024 after a 16% 2022 crisis peak; electricity was about 30% of residential energy use, and total global household electricity spending was about $1 trillion. | The transition is experienced through retail bills and household budgets, not only wholesale prices or installed capacity. | The account combines residential energy and individual transport fuels and uses modeled regional estimates; averages hide income, housing, and country differences. |
| Integration constraint | [World Energy Council Trilemma 2026](../../records/comparative-world-energy-council-trilemma-2026-rebalancing-integration.json): dialogue with more than 275 senior leaders in 65 countries identifies grids, storage, and integration as common constraints as AI, industrial electrification, and geopolitical disruption raise demands. | Deployment must be converted into interoperable systems and institutions. | Leadership dialogue is not a representative public sample and does not estimate prices or access. |
| Public legitimacy | [Ipsos Energy Transition Barometer 2026](../../records/comparative-ipsos-energy-transition-barometer-2026-affordability-security-ai-grid.json): 23,704 adults under 75 in 31 countries; 76% worry about rising energy costs, 49% report cutting other spending, 63% worry about foreign energy dependence, and 55% prefer energy independence even at higher cost. | Affordability and autonomy are political conditions of transition support, not secondary communications issues. | Self-reported attitudes and behavior do not measure bills, policy effects, or durable political action. |
| Household exposure | [US EIA RECS 2024 preliminary frame](../../records/us-eia-recs-2024-home-energy-devices-routines-2026.json): nearly 17,000 selected households represent 132.5 million occupied primary residences, with supplier-linked energy data planned for final consumption/expenditure tables. | Housing type, appliance stock, geography, and energy routines are necessary distributional controls. | The release is preliminary and does not yet provide current bills or causal savings. |
| Financial room | [US SIPP utility-credit-savings diagnostic](../../records/us-sipp-utility-credit-savings-joint-2024.json): among valid person-record/month rows, utility-payment difficulty coincides with carrying a credit balance in 35.209% versus 26.901% without difficulty, and savings-account ownership in 46.438% versus 64.901%. | Energy stress is connected to liquidity and resilience capacity. | The rows are person/month records with household/reference-period fields; this is an association, not a causal estimate. |
| Near-term spillover | [US SIPP adjacent-month food screen](../../records/us-sipp-energy-assistance-food-following-2024.json): among 24,297 eligible person-month pairs reporting utility-payment difficulty, following-month food insecurity was 51.1287% in the weighted descriptive screen. | Energy hardship can sit inside a broader household sacrifice sequence. | Persistent need, season, health, income, composition, geography, and selection may explain the pattern; it is not a causal household prevalence estimate. |
| Safety and climate | [Duke energy affordability/extreme heat record](../../records/us-duke-energy-affordability-extreme-heat-2026-cooling-access.json) treats cooling cost, energy poverty, geography, season, and consistent electricity as safety and health conditions. | Reliability and affordability should be tested against heat exposure and ability to maintain safe cooling, not only annual averages. | The public abstract does not expose the underlying county model or individual health outcomes. |
| Program conversion | [DOEE Solar for All](../../records/us-doee-solar-for-all-household-bill-savings-2025-2026.json) reports nearly 10,000 households served and an approximately $500 average annual bill-saving claim, while customer-level bill persistence, arrears, health, and exit outcomes are unmeasured. | A program can create a concrete route from public investment to household savings. | Program-level reach and modeled/average savings are not verified individual outcomes and materials use different scopes. |
| Policy route | [EU Citizens’ Energy Package](../../records/comparative-european-commission-citizens-energy-package-2026-affordability-security-autonomy.json) proposes bill reduction, citizen generation/sharing, faster switching, clearer bills, and energy-poverty action. | Consumer agency and affordability are being bundled with energy security and autonomy. | Announced measures do not establish implementation, equal access, or bill reduction. |
| Preference distribution | [EU Special Eurobarometer 555](../../records/comparative-european-commission-eurobarometer-energy-affordability-consumer-protection-2025.json): about 26,000 EU citizens; 40% define energy policy partly as affordable prices, 53% support help for energy-poor households, and 50% support efficiency and self-production. | Practical control and targeted support can be legitimacy routes. | Preferences do not identify who pays, who owns infrastructure, or who can adopt it. |
| AI load | [IEA Key Questions on Energy and AI](../../records/comparative-iea-key-questions-energy-ai-2026-data-centres-affordability-grid.json): data-centre electricity demand grew 17% in 2025, AI-focused data-centre electricity consumption 50%, and announced 2026 hyperscaler/neo-cloud capex totals $715 billion. | AI infrastructure makes allocation, connection, and cost incidence part of energy legitimacy. | Announced capex and projected load do not all become operating demand or show who bears costs. |
| U.S. industrial grid finance | [DOE AEP Texas project](../../records/us-doe-aep-texas-transmission-loan-customer-savings-2026.json) records a July 2026 loan of up to $3.26 billion for roughly 2,800 miles of transmission work, with projected 100% more carrying capacity and about $685 million in 30-year customer savings for 1.1 million projected customers. DOE's [Hamlin–Roby review letter](https://www.energy.gov/documents/aep-texas-hamlin-roby-69kv-transmission-line-project) describes a 25.2-mile rebuild and says construction was anticipated to begin in September 2026. | The U.S. industrial chain has moved from closed financing and environmental review to a named scheduled-start stage, while large-load growth and household-cost claims remain linked in the public rationale. | A scheduled start is not construction progress or completion. The record still does not show energization, operating reliability, cost allocation, or a lower bill for a named household or business. |

## Cross-source finding

The strongest current pattern is a **capital-to-household conversion gap**.
Electricity investment, AI demand, integration constraints, and policy packages
show a system moving rapidly toward electrification and higher coordination needs.
The household layers show why that movement is politically and socially fragile:
bill averages can remain high after a crisis, energy difficulty coexists with less
liquidity, and people may cut food or other spending to keep service. Public
support is therefore likely to depend on visible conversion into affordable,
reliable, and controllable service—not on capital totals, clean labels, or
announced capacity alone.

The new U.S. industrial evidence sharpens the timing of that gap: a project can
have federal financing closed, environmental review completed, and a scheduled
construction start while the customer-facing stages—built assets, operation,
allocation, bill effect, and reliability—remain open. This is a more precise
test than treating an announced grid investment as either a benefit or a burden.

The evidence does not show that transition investment necessarily worsens
household welfare. Solar for All is a counterexample to a purely negative story:
it reports a route to bill savings for low-income households. But its own public
record leaves persistence, customer-level verification, and program continuity
open. The useful contrast is not “transition good” versus “transition bad”; it is
whether a specific intervention closes the chain through connection, operation,
bill credit, continued access, safe use, and remedy.

## What remains unknown

1. Which households receive the benefits of new generation, grid investment, and
   distributed energy after housing tenure, income, race, geography, climate,
   baseline efficiency, and utility territory are controlled?
2. Do bill credits and efficiency upgrades persist for the same households, and
   do they reduce arrears, food sacrifice, unsafe cooling avoidance, or mobility
   loss over time?
3. When AI and other large loads connect, how are network upgrades, reliability
   costs, emissions, and local benefits allocated between large users and ordinary
   customers?
4. Which forms of consumer agency—switching, community generation, self-use,
   transparent bills, assistance, or complaint remedy—actually sustain support?

## Next test and stopping rule

The next pass should select one traceable place or program and follow the same
household or customer pathway across baseline burden, intervention eligibility,
connection, monthly bill, outage/cooling exposure, food or credit tradeoff,
continuation, and remedy. Pair that longitudinal path with utility/regulatory
cost allocation and a public-discussion sample from the same place.

Do not claim that investment produced affordability, that clean capacity produced
reliability, or that public support produced legitimacy unless the relevant link
is observed. Stop this sub-test only after the chain has either a dated outcome
with a credible comparison or a documented missing-link explanation; then rotate
to the highest-learning unresolved theme in the active queue.

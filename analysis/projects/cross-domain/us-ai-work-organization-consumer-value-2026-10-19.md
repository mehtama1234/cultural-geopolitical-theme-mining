# U.S. AI work, organization, and consumer value: performance is not yet control

**Status:** provisional consumer/platform/work-power synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-10-19

## Bounded finding

Two recent U.S. evidence surfaces show why AI adoption should be treated as an
organizational bargain rather than an automation switch. In a preregistered
field experiment with 776 Procter & Gamble professionals, individuals using AI
matched the performance of teams working without AI on product-innovation tasks;
AI users also produced more balanced R&D/Commercial proposals and reported more
positive emotion. A separate survey of 734 corporate executives reports uneven
adoption, sector-varying productivity, a gap between perceived and revenue-
measured gains, little near-term aggregate employment decline, and job-mix
reallocation toward skilled technical roles.

The sources support a conversion-gap hypothesis:

```text
AI access or adoption
  -> task performance, expertise sharing, and firm productivity
  -> work organization, monitoring, training, and bargaining position
  -> pay, hours, entry, retention, and product/service quality
  -> consumer price, reliability, recourse, and trust
  -> [open] durable worker control, household benefit, or exit
```

## Evidence comparison without pooling

| Surface | Result | Unit and boundary | Open link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized workplace experiment | AI-enabled individuals matched no-AI teams on measured innovation tasks; AI balanced R&D and Commercial proposals | 776 professionals at a U.S.-headquartered consumer-goods firm; task-specific field experiment | Long-run learning, worker voice, pay, retention, product-market success, and consumer value |
| Functional expertise | Without AI, R&D and Commercial participants showed more specialized proposal patterns; with AI, proposals were more balanced | Random assignment by AI access and work arrangement | Whether visible balance reflects genuine integration, hidden disagreement, or tool-default convergence |
| Immediate social response | AI users reported more positive emotion during the measured work | Self-reported field-experiment outcome; scale and effect size not disclosed in the abstract | Durable belonging, psychological safety, human connection, accountability, and team cohesion |
| Executive and firm survey | More than half of firms had invested in AI; productivity gains varied by sector and perceived gains exceeded measured revenue-based gains | 734 corporate executives plus associated firm analysis | Causal adoption effect, realized hiring/layoffs, wages, prices, firm survival, and household welfare |
| Job composition | Little near-term aggregate employment decline, with anticipated reductions at larger firms, gains at smaller firms, and relative movement toward skilled technical roles | Executive expectations and job-mix analysis | Individual displacement, entry barriers, re-skilling, worker control, and durable income |
| Workforce structure comparator | AI-investing firms shift toward more educated, STEM- and IT-intensive workforces, more junior workers, and fewer middle-management and senior workers | Related NBER firm-investment/workforce-composition evidence; direction of organizational change, not a same-firm worker panel in this memo | Whether flatter hierarchy expands entry and learning or removes promotion and voice routes; pay, monitoring, schedule, and exit remain open |

These units cannot be merged into one worker or consumer journey. The field
experiment identifies a bounded task-level comparison; the executive study
describes firm adoption, productivity, and labor expectations. Neither measures
whether the customer received a better product, lower price, faster remedy, or
more reliable service.

## What this adds to the atlas

The first visible AI outcome may be performance equivalence or positive affect,
while the decisive distributional outcome occurs later through who controls the
workflow. A tool can substitute for some team functions while leaving human
review, dissent, training, promotion, and accountability unresolved. Likewise,
stable aggregate employment can coexist with reduced entry, changed job mix,
hours pressure, or weaker outside options for particular workers.

The key distinctions are:

```text
AI adoption          != realized productivity
task performance     != durable expertise
positive emotion     != social connection
employment stability != stable worker power
balanced proposal    != better product
firm productivity    != consumer benefit
```

## Counterexamples and limits

- AI may complement human teams by freeing time for higher-value interaction;
  the experiment does not prove team replacement.
- A positive productivity estimate may reflect selection, demand, innovation,
  or delayed revenue realization rather than a causal worker gain.
- Stable aggregate employment does not rule out displacement or reduced entry
  for specific occupations; anticipated reductions do not prove later layoffs.
- More positive emotion may reflect lower task friction rather than better
  workplace belonging.
- Neither source observes complaint, correction, price, product reliability,
  customer switching, or practical exit.

## Next decisive test

Follow one named firm, worker group, and customer-facing process across:

```text
AI deployment and task redesign
  -> training, review authority, monitoring, and worker voice
  -> hours, pay, entry, retention, and product/service error
  -> customer price, quality, complaint, remedy, and repeat use
  -> worker or customer switching, trust, and exit
```

The comparison must include AI use that improves worker control, AI use that
raises monitoring or pace pressure, and non-AI or human-fallback routes. Do not
promote measured productivity or adoption to a welfare or legitimacy claim
without a later worker or customer outcome.

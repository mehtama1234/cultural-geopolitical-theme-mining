# Consumer surveillance is becoming civic evidence infrastructure

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** Police Foundation, *Policing and Digital Citizen Evidence: Unleashing the potential for public participation* (release summary published 16 September 2026)  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-uk-police-foundation-digital-citizen-evidence-surveillance-participation-2026.json)

## Core finding

The report describes a new institutional relationship: household devices such
as smart doorbells, journey cameras, mobile phones, and connected-home systems
are generating a distributed layer of public-safety evidence. Half of adults
report regularly using at least one citizen-initiated surveillance technology,
and large majorities say they would share footage of serious incidents with
police.

But the civic offer is incomplete. Police intake is fragmented, reporting routes
vary, and people may receive little or no feedback. The result is a **participation
legitimacy gap**: consumer technology makes contribution easy to imagine, while
institutional response determines whether contribution becomes trust, fatigue,
or vigilantism.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The important shift is from **citizen as witness** to **citizen as infrastructure**.
People are not only reporting events; they are purchasing, installing,
maintaining, and operating the sensors through which public institutions may
know about them. That changes the distribution of labor and responsibility. The
public supplies equipment, attention, and footage; police must provide lawful
channels, triage, evidence standards, privacy protection, and feedback.

If the response fails, the same technology can produce two opposite cultural
effects: withdrawal (“why share if nothing happens?”) or escalation into private
surveillance and vigilantism. The report’s emphasis on national consistency and
human oversight therefore belongs in the trust/legitimacy lane, not only the
technology lane.

## Open middle

The next valid test should join:

```text
consumer device adoption
  -> capture and willingness to share
  -> accessible police intake / privacy protection
  -> triage, investigation, feedback, and remedy
  -> perceived fairness and measurable safety
  -> durable public participation or withdrawal
```

The report does not establish crime reduction, actual submission rates, case
outcomes, or causal trust effects.

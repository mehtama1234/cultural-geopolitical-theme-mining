# Enterprise registration is not worker formalization: construction exposes the portability gap

## Finding

The ILO's Nepal construction brief identifies a sharp distinction between registering an enterprise and formalizing the people who work inside it. Construction contributes more than 6% of GDP and roughly one in seven jobs, yet informal employment reached 98.2% in 2023. Even among formally registered enterprises, 78% of workers were informal in 2018. The sector's dominant daily-paid, temporary and subcontracted arrangements make the employment relationship move faster than the systems meant to record it.

The result is a portability problem. Workers change sites and employers, earnings fluctuate, and layered subcontracting can obscure who is responsible for enrollment, contributions, safety and injury protection. Registration at the enterprise level cannot by itself provide continuity of rights at the worker level.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`project work + daily pay + mobility -> employer-of-record uncertainty -> contribution discontinuity and risk transfer -> demand for portable, flexible social protection`

The new subtheme is formalization without employment security. Formalization is not a binary property of a firm. It is a chain across identity, enrollment, payment, safety, skills, procurement and benefit continuity. If one link breaks at each project transition, the worker experiences the state as intermittent even when the enterprise appears officially registered.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Public infrastructure can be built through private informality: the visible output is formal and permanent while the labor relationship behind it is temporary and weakly protected.
- Social protection is becoming a portability product. Flexible contributions, worker-initiated enrollment and portable benefits matter when residence, employer, worksite and pay channel do not remain fixed.
- Identity and payment infrastructure are social-policy infrastructure. Missing citizenship documentation, bank access and digital literacy can prevent a worker from converting legal eligibility into actual protection.
- Gender and skill status are not side issues. Women are a small share of the workforce and enterprise owners, are concentrated in lower-paid work, and face a large below-minimum-wage share; only 9.2% of construction employees have vocational training.
- Procurement is a political consumer of labor standards: public buyers can make formal subcontracting and worker protection conditions of market access, but compliance must be paired with finance and technical support so small contractors are not simply displaced.

## Boundary and next test

This is a diagnostic policy brief, not causal evidence about which intervention works. The next research pass should look for Nepal implementation data on worker-initiated enrollment, portable contribution records, procurement compliance, injury protection, skills certification and gender outcomes, and compare them with the ILO Jordan evidence on administrative friction and trust.

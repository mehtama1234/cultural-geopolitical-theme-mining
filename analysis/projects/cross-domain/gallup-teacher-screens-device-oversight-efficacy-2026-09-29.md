# Classroom technology becomes legitimate when teachers retain operational control

## Finding

Gallup's September 2026 Teaching for Tomorrow study finds that learning devices are nearly ubiquitous in U.S. public-school classrooms: 96% of surveyed teachers say students use them. Yet 61% want less device time, and only 18% say devices do more good than harm. The more consequential split is governance: 67% of teachers with a lot of oversight say digital tools help students learn more effectively, compared with 49% among teachers with little or no oversight.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`ubiquitous technology -> teacher control over implementation -> perceived usefulness and legitimacy -> preference for bounded safeguards over blunt restriction`

This is a control-and-governance signal, not simply a screen-time signal. Teachers support stronger filtering or monitoring and investment in print or hands-on materials more strongly than a blanket daily time maximum. The institution is negotiating how technology enters practice: tools are more acceptable when the frontline worker can see, manage and adapt their use.

## Boundaries

The study surveyed 2,101 U.S. K-12 public-school teachers through a nationally representative probability-based panel, with fieldwork May 6–29, 2026. The findings are cross-sectional perceptions and reported oversight, not observed device minutes, classroom audits or causal estimates. Higher oversight may reflect better staffing, training, grade level, school policy or implementation quality. It does not establish effects on student learning, attention, equity, teacher retention or policy outcomes.

Source: [Gallup, Screens Ubiquitous in Classrooms; Teachers Split on Efficacy](https://news.gallup.com/poll/714479/screens-ubiquitous-classrooms-teachers-split-efficacy.aspx).

# South Africa GPSJS 2025/26: public safety is a conversion problem

## Finding

The Statistics South Africa GPSJS 2025/26 victims-of-crime release shows why
security cannot be represented by crime incidence alone. Housebreaking affected
5.3% of households and home robbery 0.9%; only 48.2% and 51.6% respectively
reported some or all incidents to police. Among people aged 16 and older,
personal-property theft affected 2.6%, but only 37.3% reported some or all
incidents; street-robbery reporting was 41.7%.

Perceived safety adds a different layer: 80.7% felt safe walking alone during
the day, but only 37.9% after dark. Women were more likely than men to report
feeling very unsafe after dark (45.1% versus 38.8%), and 70.3% of women who
experienced assault said the perpetrator was known to them. Meanwhile, 46.6%
reported doing something to protect themselves against crime, up from 43.3% in
2024/25.

The cross-source mechanism is usable security. Exposure, reporting, perceived
safety and self-protection are separate stages. When reporting is partial and
protection is privately supplied, households may be carrying the cost of public
security through changed movement, spending, time, social visibility or
avoidance. The release does not show which protective acts were used, what they
cost, whether police responses were effective, or whether safety and trust
improved.

## Boundary

This is an official one-page summary of GPSJS 2025/26 victims-of-crime results.
The public release does not state the full survey denominator, sample design,
confidence intervals, reasons for non-reporting, police response, case outcome,
or household-level protective costs. It is descriptive evidence, not a causal
crime estimate, a service-performance evaluation, or proof that self-protection
produced safety.

Source: [Statistics South Africa, Governance, Public Safety and Justice Survey
2025/26: Victims of Crime Release](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0341/Media%20release%20_GPSJS%202025_26_29082026%20.pdf).

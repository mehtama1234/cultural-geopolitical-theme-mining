# Voting remains important while influence, fairness, and listening feel scarce

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Scottish Government, *Scottish Social Attitudes Survey 2025: Core Module*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-scottish-social-attitudes-2025-trust-engagement-listening.json)

## Core finding

The Scottish survey identifies a legitimacy tension rather than a simple trust
collapse. Voting remains highly valued, and many people use petitions or contact
representatives, but a large share feel the political system gives people like
them little say. Trust is also materially patterned: respondents struggling on
their present income report lower general social trust. The missing bridge is
whether visible participation produces listening, fair treatment, or policy
influence.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The survey complicates the idea that political disengagement is the opposite of
political commitment. People can regard voting as important, sign petitions, and
contact officials while still believing that institutions do not listen or give
them meaningful influence. Participation may therefore be expressive, defensive,
or hope-preserving without yet being effective.

The economic gradient matters culturally as well as materially. When people are
struggling, lower trust can reflect repeated experiences of weak institutions,
scarce resources, unfair treatment, or reduced room to participate. This is not
proof that poverty causes distrust, but it identifies a route where economic
pressure and democratic legitimacy may reinforce one another.

## Open middle

The next pass should look for same-person or same-locality evidence joining:

```text
economic insecurity / institutional encounter
  -> trust, fairness, and perceived listening
  -> petition, contact, consultation, voting, or withdrawal
  -> government response and material change
  -> restored or weakened legitimacy
```

The survey does not establish policy responsiveness, turnout, causal trust
drivers, or the success of participation. Those remain open arrows.

# Local business retention depends on an ecosystem, not headline costs alone

## Finding

Gallup’s Pathways to Wealth study finds that only 48% of U.S. business owners view their local area positively as a place to run a business, while 13% view it negatively. Real-estate costs and taxes are the most negatively rated conditions, but customer access, business-to-business support, nonprofit services, regulations and government-service quality are more closely associated with overall local satisfaction.

Only 5% of owners say they are likely to leave within 12 months for a business reason, but that rises to 11% among owners with a negative local-climate view. Among owners expecting revenue growth, the relocation intention is 19% when dissatisfied versus 5% when satisfied or neutral.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`local business ecosystem -> owner viability and belonging -> growth opportunity -> retention or exit intention`

The local economic signal is not simply that costs matter. Owners may tolerate high costs when the place supplies customers, networks, usable regulation and public services. Conversely, a growing firm may become more mobile when local friction threatens the next stage of expansion. Local business retention is therefore an infrastructure-and-belonging problem, not only a tax or real-estate problem.

## Boundaries

The Year 3 survey was conducted September 26–November 3, 2025, with business owners recruited primarily from the probability-based Gallup Panel; the article was published September 8, 2026. The public summary does not disclose respondent counts or full analytic methods. The findings are associations and stated relocation intentions, not observed moves, firm survival, employment or causal local-policy effects.

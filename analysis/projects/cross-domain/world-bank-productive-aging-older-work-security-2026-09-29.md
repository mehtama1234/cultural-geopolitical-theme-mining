# Productive ageing is not the same as working longer

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank, *Productive Aging: Supporting Older Workers in Developing Countries*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-productive-aging-older-workers-developing-countries-2026.json)

## Core finding

The World Bank report treats older people's work as both a potential economic
capacity and a social-security signal. It examines access to market and
non-market work, productivity, skills, health, and policy conditions across a
large set of developing countries, while explicitly recognizing that there are
limits to working in old age.

The key distinction is between productive longevity as a supported choice and
late-life work as a necessity. The same employment statistic can represent
autonomy and contribution for one household, and inadequate pensions, savings,
healthcare, or alternatives for another.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The report challenges a simple demographic story in which older people are either
dependents or a reserve labour supply. It makes later life a negotiated social
position: people may want to remain economically and socially active, but the
meaning of that activity depends on health, recognition, work conditions, income
security, and whether alternatives exist.

This reframes “productive ageing” as an infrastructure question. Governments
and employers cannot infer autonomy from employment alone; they need to ask who
has the capability to choose, who is being priced out of retirement, and whether
skills and care systems make continued work sustainable.

## Open middle

```text
population ageing and longer lives
  -> health, skills, pensions, work conditions, and care capacity
  -> chosen or necessary later-life work
  -> household security, dignity, productivity, and intergenerational transfers
  -> trust in the social contract of ageing
```

The report is comparative policy analysis based on harmonized household surveys.
It does not establish that working longer is voluntary, that any policy raises
productivity, or that later-life employment is equally feasible across groups.

# Rechargeable power is ordinary—but its safety and disposal systems are not

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** Japan Consumer Affairs Agency, *FY2026 Consumer Awareness Survey No. 1: Products Using Lithium-Ion Batteries* (17 September 2026)  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-japan-lithium-battery-consumer-safety-disposal-2026.json)

## Core finding

Lithium-ion products are now ordinary consumer infrastructure, especially among
younger people, but the surrounding safety and end-of-life system is uneven. The
survey finds high awareness of mobile-battery danger, while price and performance
often outrank safety at purchase. When batteries become unwanted, 16.5% say
they keep them because they do not know how to dispose of them; that rises to
25.5% among teenagers.

The new theme is **consumer technology without fully legible lifecycle
infrastructure**. The device is easy to buy and use, but not always easy to
evaluate, handle, return, or safely discard.

## Cultural/social interpretation

This is a useful example of how safety becomes a cultural practice rather than a
warning label. Users know a mobile battery can be dangerous, but the purchase
moment is organized around price and performance, while the disposal moment is
organized around uncertainty and local rules. The consumer is therefore asked to
carry a lifecycle responsibility that may be distributed across manufacturers,
retailers, municipalities, and public information systems.

The age contrast matters. Younger users have greater exposure and higher rates of
uncertain storage, while older users report lower ownership but higher hazard
awareness and municipal-rule disposal. Neither group is simply “better informed”;
they encounter different products, routines, and institutional pathways.

## Open middle

The next valid test should join:

```text
product adoption and price/performance choice
  -> charging, carrying, damage, and recall awareness
  -> disposal knowledge and accessible take-back route
  -> actual collection, fire risk, and material recovery
  -> lifecycle accountability across consumer, firm, and municipality
```

The survey does not establish accident rates, actual disposal compliance, or
recycling outcomes.

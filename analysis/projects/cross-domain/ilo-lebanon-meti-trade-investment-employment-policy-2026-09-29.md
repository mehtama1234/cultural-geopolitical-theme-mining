# Trade and investment become employment policy when the value chain is selected, measured, and negotiated with workers

## Finding

The ILO’s Mainstreaming Employment into Trade and Investment (METI) project in Lebanon places employment at the centre of trade and investment policy. A Policy Working Group brings together labour, economy and trade, industry, employer, worker, and research representatives. The project will identify an export-oriented sector or value chain, assess its employment potential, and develop a sectoral action plan linking trade, investment, skills, employment quality, gender, and formalization.

The distinct finding is a policy-design shift: trade and investment are no longer treated as automatically employment-generating. They are being made answerable to a measurement and coordination process that asks which sectors grow, what jobs they create, who can access them, what skills are needed, and how workers and firms participate in the decision.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`trade and investment choice -> sector/value-chain selection -> employment analysis and social dialogue -> firm, skills, gender, and formalization interventions -> job quality, household security, and regional legitimacy`

The route matters because growth can bypass workers when policies are designed in separate silos. A Policy Working Group can make the employment effects of investment visible, while value-chain analysis can connect national policy to firms, suppliers, skills, and worker experience. But export potential is not the same as decent work: selected sectors may favour formal firms, leave care and informal work invisible, or shift compliance and skills costs onto workers.

The geopolitical layer is the EU-backed Southern Mediterranean programme. Regional exchange can support diversification and value-chain upgrading, yet integration can also increase competition and labour arbitrage. The key question is whether regional policy cooperation gives workers and weaker firms more voice and opportunity, or only makes investment more mobile.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Economic recovery becomes politically credible when trade and investment are judged by job quality and household opportunity, not growth alone.
- Sector selection is a distributional decision: it determines which places, firms, workers, skills, and forms of labour become visible to policy.
- Women and young people are treated as explicit employment groups, but access depends on care, transport, training, formalization, and employer demand.
- MSMEs and workers become linked policy subjects rather than separate beneficiaries of business and labour programmes.
- Regional integration gains legitimacy only when its value chains produce visible, decent, and locally accessible work.

## Boundary and next test

METI is a project launch and planned policy mechanism. It does not establish actual job creation, wage gains, sector performance, worker voice, or implementation. The next test is the selected Lebanese value chain: follow it from evidence gathering and working-group decisions through finance, firm behaviour, skills, worker conditions, gender access, formalization, and realized employment.

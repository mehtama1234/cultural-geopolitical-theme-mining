# Environmental consumption is organized by housing, place, and usable infrastructure

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Statistics Canada, *Understanding factors shaping household environmental practices and behaviours in Canada*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-statcan-household-environmental-practices-dwelling-context-2026.json)

## Core finding

The publication shows why environmental behavior should not be read as a simple
expression of individual values. In the 2021 Canadian Households and the
Environment Survey, composting and controlled hazardous-waste disposal varied
with dwelling type, tenure, time in residence, nearby greenspace, nature contact,
and settlement scale. The practical environment around the household helps make
some behaviors easy, visible, and repeatable—and others costly or unavailable.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The source shifts the question from “Do households care?” to “What does the
household’s environment let them do?” Apartment residents, renters, recent
movers, and households without nearby greenspace may face constraints that look
like lower motivation when measured only through behavior. Conversely, gardening,
outdoor activity, and nature contact may create routines that make composting
feel normal and worthwhile.

This is a consumer and civic story at once. Households are asked to perform
environmental responsibility, but the ability to do so is distributed through
housing, municipal systems, retail return routes, time, and local geography. The
behavior is therefore partly a market/service design outcome rather than a purely
private moral choice.

## Open middle

The next pass should look for same-household or same-place evidence joining:

```text
housing / tenure / geography / nearby nature
  -> service availability, storage, travel, time, and social routine
  -> composting, disposal, purchasing, or energy behavior
  -> environmental outcome and household cost
  -> improved infrastructure, feedback, or unequal burden
```

The study does not establish causal effects, current conditions after 2021,
audited waste flows, environmental outcomes, or the role of values independent of
infrastructure. Those remain open arrows.

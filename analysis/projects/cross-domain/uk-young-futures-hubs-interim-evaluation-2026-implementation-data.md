# Young Futures Hubs: trusted access can connect support, but measurement and partnership capacity are part of the intervention

## Finding

The interim evaluation of England's eight Young Futures Hubs Early Adopters identifies an implementation mechanism rather than a finished outcome story. Hubs were beginning to connect open-access youth work, targeted provision, and specialist support through accessible spaces, trusted relationships, co-location, and warm handovers. Young people described activities, friendships, advice, confidence, belonging, and wellbeing; stakeholders reported early routes into employability support, volunteering, and service design.

But the model depends on infrastructure that is easy to undercount: time to build partnerships and co-design, dedicated coordination, simple referrals, sustainable funding, consent, common indicators, and data-sharing agreements. Aggregate reach and attendance are feasible to collect; linked individual journeys across universal, targeted, and specialist services are much harder. Outcome data are less consistent than output data, especially for open-access provision.

The key theme is that service connection and measurement capacity are not separate administrative details. They determine whether a welcoming space becomes a durable pathway into care, work, safety, voice, and belonging—and whether anyone can credibly tell which young people benefited.

## Theme and subthemes

- **No-wrong-door access:** informal and familiar spaces can lower the threshold for specialist support.
- **Partnership time as capacity:** referral pathways, co-design, buildings, staffing, and shared practice require time before outcomes are observable.
- **Reach versus journey:** attendances and young people reached are not the same as unique, sustained, connected support journeys.
- **Youth voice versus consultation:** co-design requires decision influence and visible feedback, not only asking for views.
- **Data infrastructure as legitimacy:** common definitions, consent, identifiers, and data-sharing determine whether claims about impact are trustworthy.

## What the source supports

The interim report supports early implementation and feasibility findings across eight sites. It supports reports of engagement, access, trusted relationships, warm handovers, youth input, and the relative feasibility of aggregate outputs versus linked outcomes. It does not support causal claims about crime reduction, health, employment, education, or durable wellbeing.

## Next test

Connect a named hub, referral, youth-council, co-design, mental-health, employability, or community-safety intervention to unique reach, repeated engagement, completed handoffs, service continuity, verified outcomes, youth decision influence, and longer-term participation. The evaluation should preserve universal/targeted distinctions and not treat the easiest counts as the whole impact story.

## Boundary

Early evidence is necessarily indicative. Open-access provision is less suited to intrusive baseline/follow-up measurement; targeted provision has more structured outcome data. Administrative linkage was not consistently feasible because identifiers, consent, and sharing mechanisms were incomplete.

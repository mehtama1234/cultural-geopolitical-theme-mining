# Americans are negotiating the good life through children and work

**Source:** Economist/YouGov Polls, summarized in [YouGov's “R-E-S-P-E-C-T? What Americans value in kids and work”](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55592-r-e-s-p-e-c-t-what-americans-value-in-kids-and-work) (18 September 2026)  
**Geography:** United States  
**Methods:** Two weighted Economist/YouGov opt-in-panel polls: 1,616 adults (September 4–8) and 1,596 adults (September 11–14)

## Finding

The public is not divided only over policy. It is divided over the social model that policy is meant to support: whether children should become obedient or self-reliant, whether a healthy life requires fewer work hours, whether remote work is a legitimate work condition, and whether a good job is defined by pay or passion.

```text
family socialization + labor-market position
  -> preferred traits and work ideals
  -> age / ideology / education / parenthood differences
  -> competing definitions of independence, respect, time, security, and purpose
  -> open question: which institutions can make those ideals materially reachable?
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Trust, identity, and meaning | Social reproduction of the good life | Child traits encode competing models of respect, autonomy, curiosity, and independence |
| Work, control, and bargaining | Work-time control | Younger and more liberal adults are more likely to prefer shorter workweeks |
| Social capacity, proximity, time, and support | Care-compatible work | Remote-work support is higher among parents of young children and other groups with stronger flexibility needs |
| Household room and consumption | Security versus purpose | Passion and pay are near-balanced, exposing the tension between meaning and material room |
| Unequal exposure and status | Stratified ideals and access | Education, age, ideology, gender, parenthood, and labor-force position shape both preferences and likely access |
| Political judgment and action | Policy meaning | Remote-work requirements become a judgment about what employers owe workers and families |

## Boundary

These are weighted stated preferences and forced-choice trade-offs, not observed parenting or employment behavior. Opt-in panels and the article's summary do not establish causal effects, actual remote-work eligibility, income trade-offs, or long-run value change.

## Next test

Join a named work, leave, childcare, or remote-work intervention to actual hours, schedule control, care substitution, earnings, career progression, household wellbeing, and whether stated ideals become reachable for different groups.

# Culture is being measured as social infrastructure

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, *Culture and Heritage Capital — Valuing social cohesion: a scoping study*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-uk-culture-heritage-social-cohesion-valuation-2026.json)

## Core finding

The study marks a shift in cultural policy: libraries, museums, heritage,
festivals, and cultural spaces are treated as assets that generate social
infrastructure and social-cohesion benefits, not only private enjoyment or
visitor spending.

The new policy problem is measurement. Public investment must make belonging,
social ties, trust, participation, and non-user benefits visible without
pretending they are cleanly separable or fully reducible to money.

## Cultural/social interpretation

This is a political economy of belonging. The state is translating a culturally
felt claim—“this place helps us live together”—into appraisal language, while
acknowledging that social cohesion has no single definition and may be counted
across wellbeing, social capital, and cultural value.

Measurement can protect cultural budgets by making invisible benefits legible,
but monetisation can also narrow the meaning of culture to what an appraisal
model can carry. Non-users, excluded groups, contested histories, and the right
to value culture for non-instrumental reasons remain part of the open middle.

## Open middle

```text
cultural/heritage asset and access conditions
  -> shared activity, encounter, identity, and social ties
  -> belonging, trust, cooperation, civic participation
  -> wellbeing, public-service, and socioeconomic effects
  -> appraisal visibility and funding decisions
  -> preservation, access, exclusion, or cultural contestation
```

The source is a scoping study and theory-of-change exercise. Its literature base
is mixed, most evidence is correlational or qualitative, and the pilot valuation
does not provide a national willingness-to-pay estimate or prove that investment
causes cohesion.

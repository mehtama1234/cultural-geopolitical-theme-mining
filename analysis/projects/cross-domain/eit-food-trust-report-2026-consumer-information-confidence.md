# EIT Food Trust Report 2026: confidence in choices, distrust in the information environment

## Finding

The fourth EIT Food Consumer Observatory Trust Report for 2026 identifies a gap between confidence in personal food choices and confidence in the surrounding information environment. Across 18 European countries and nearly 20,000 consumers, the share saying they can make good food choices rose from 60% in 2024 to 66% in 2025. Yet fewer than half considered food information reliable. At the same time, 53% said they knew where to find sources they could trust.

This is a consumer-culture and public-knowledge finding rather than a simple trust decline. People may feel increasingly able to make decisions while treating the broader information system as unreliable. That can produce reliance on heuristics, familiar actors, personal networks, labels, third-party validation, or identity-linked food meanings. It can also leave health and sustainability claims vulnerable to selective trust, especially when affordability, habit, time, and availability constrain what choices are actually feasible.

## Theme and subthemes

- **Self-efficacy versus source legitimacy:** confidence in one's own choices can rise while confidence in food information remains fragile.
- **Trusted-source navigation:** knowing where to find trustworthy information is a distinct capability from believing the information environment is reliable.
- **Food literacy as infrastructure:** clear sourcing, transparency, third-party validation, and concise evidence affect whether information can be used.
- **Intention-to-behavior boundary:** confidence and knowledge do not establish healthier, more sustainable, or more affordable realized diets.

## What the source supports

The EIT Food page supports the cross-national headline pattern, the 18-country and approximately 20,000-consumer scope, and recommendations around accessible and trustworthy information. It does not expose the full public questionnaire, microdata, weights, uncertainty intervals, or objective food-choice verification on the page.

## Next test

Connect a named label, retailer, school, clinician, public-health, third-party validation, food-policy, or affordability intervention to verified information comprehension, source selection, product choice, diet quality, sustainability behavior, and household food security. The key question is whether better information survives price, habit, time, availability, and cultural-fit constraints.

## Boundary

Self-reported confidence is not accuracy, and perceived reliability is not source quality. The report is an industry-linked consumer-observatory publication; its findings are useful for theme detection but should not be treated as an independent causal evaluation.

Source: [EIT Food Trust Report 2026: Who do consumers believe?](https://www.eitfood.eu/reports/trust-report-2026-fourth-issue-who-do-consumers-believe)

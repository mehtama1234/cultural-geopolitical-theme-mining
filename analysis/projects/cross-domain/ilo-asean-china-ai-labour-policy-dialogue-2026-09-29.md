# AI governance becomes a geopolitical labour bargain when standards, skills, and voice are negotiated together

## Finding

An ILO regional policy dialogue involving governments, workers, and employers from ASEAN and China frames AI transformation as an employment-governance problem, not merely a firm-level technology choice. Participants emphasized human judgment, social dialogue, reskilling, public employment services, digital access, and ethical adoption. The dialogue cites almost **80 million workers in South-East Asia** as employed in jobs with potential generative-AI exposure.

The distinct finding is institutional and geopolitical: South-South cooperation is being used to negotiate what “employment-friendly AI” should mean across labour markets with different skills systems, digital divides, and policy capacity. The source records an agenda and policy direction, not outcomes.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`AI exposure and technological change -> skills and public employment systems -> tripartite dialogue and regional standards -> worker transition, firm adaptation, and digital inclusion -> geopolitical policy agency`

This expands the AI theme beyond adoption and fear. If AI governance is designed only around firms and technical standards, workers encounter it as an imposed transition. If it includes public employment services, retraining, employer commitments, worker representation, and access infrastructure, it can become a negotiated social contract. But the same regional coordination can also reproduce power asymmetries if larger states or technology interests define the standards.

The regional frame matters because labour markets are connected through supply chains, migration, investment, and shared technology platforms. A country’s AI policy is therefore partly a question of who gets to set the terms of work across borders—and whether South-South cooperation expands agency or simply diffuses a dominant model.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- AI transition legitimacy depends on whether workers can shape the rules, not only receive training after decisions are made.
- Digital access is a condition of participation in the new labour market, not a separate infrastructure issue.
- Public employment services become potential AI-transition institutions alongside firms and schools.
- Regional standards can affect job quality, privacy, skills recognition, and bargaining power across supply chains.
- The next test is a named country or sector episode tracing dialogue into standards, public services, firm practice, worker voice, and outcomes.

## Boundary and next test

The ILO source is a report of a regional dialogue, not a survey or causal evaluation. It does not establish job loss, productivity, adoption, worker benefit, implemented standards, or the effectiveness of social dialogue. The next test is an implementation episode that follows one AI-related policy from regional discussion through national rule, public employment or training service, firm deployment, worker experience, and remedy.

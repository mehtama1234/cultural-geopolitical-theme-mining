# Borrowing costs are becoming a consumption boundary, not merely a financial variable

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia, *Are Household Perceptions of Borrowing Costs Creating Spending Headwinds?*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/us-philadelphia-fed-household-borrowing-costs-spending-headwinds-july-2026.json)

## Core finding

The report identifies a direct consumer mechanism: 25.8% of respondents had
considered borrowing for a large purchase but decided not to make it for
financial reasons, and 12.6% of all respondents said high borrowing costs were
the primary reason. A further 5% chose a cheaper alternative primarily because of
financing costs. The key cultural-economic shift is that “affordability” is being
experienced as a decision boundary around what kind of future purchase is
actionable.

## Cultural/social interpretation

This source sharpens the difference between price pressure and credit pressure.
Two households can face the same item price but different actionability because
one is more exposed to loan rates, personal debt, or limited liquidity. Credit
therefore distributes not only purchasing power but timing, choice, and the
ability to respond to future price changes.

The report’s framing experiment adds a meaning layer. When respondents were
reminded of lenders’ costs, their stated threshold for an affordable rate moved.
That is not proof of financial education or changed borrowing, but it shows that
consumer judgments are partly constructed through the explanations and frames
available to them.

## Open middle

The next pass should look for same-household evidence joining:

```text
interest-rate / debt / asset position
  -> perceived affordability and financing constraint
  -> forgoing, substitution, saving, or purchase timing
  -> realized mobility, durable access, debt service, and wellbeing
  -> consumer remedy, refinancing, or recovery
```

The report does not establish actual transactions, credit denial, causal welfare
effects, or durable financial-literacy change. Those remain open arrows.

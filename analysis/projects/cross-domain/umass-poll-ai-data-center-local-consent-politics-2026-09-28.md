# AI infrastructure reaches a local-consent wall

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** University of Massachusetts Amherst Poll, September 2026 national poll  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/us-umass-poll-ai-data-center-local-consent-crosspartisan-2026.json)

## Core finding

The UMass poll adds a local-consent mechanism to the data-center and AI
infrastructure atlas. Only 11% support an AI data center in their local
community, while 65% oppose one; opposition remains majoritarian among both
Democrats and Republicans. Open-ended responses point primarily to water,
energy, land, utility bills, noise, and community fit rather than a generalized
rejection of AI.

```text
national AI ambition and strategic competition
  -> large local infrastructure project
  -> concentrated resource, land, utility, and quality-of-life exposure
  -> cross-partisan local opposition
  -> political accountability and benefit-sharing test
  -> consent, delay, redesign, or backlash
```

The important distinction is between support for AI as a national future and
support for bearing its physical costs next door.

## Boundary

The UMass Poll surveyed 1,000 U.S. adults August 21–26, 2026, using a
matched-and-weighted sampling frame with a reported 3.5% margin of error. The
question is hypothetical and cross-sectional; the open-ended coding is
publisher-reported. The findings do not establish actual project effects,
utility-rate causality, or how community-benefit conditions would change
consent.

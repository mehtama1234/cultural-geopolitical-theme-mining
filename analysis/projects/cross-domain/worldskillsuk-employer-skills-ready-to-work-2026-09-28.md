# Employers see the youth transition as harder, but employability skills as productive capability

**Source:** WorldSkills UK and Public First, [Ready to Work Report 2026](https://www.publicfirst.co.uk/new_report_for_worldskills_uk) ([official PDF](https://www.worldskillsuk.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/WorldSkills-UK-Ready-to-Work-Report-2026.pdf))  
**Method:** Public First survey of 2,008 UK employers, fielded April 30–May 29, 2026.

## Finding

Employers describe young people’s route into work as harder than it was for previous generations, but they also say employability skills are valuable firm capabilities. The open problem is whether young people have fair, credible ways to demonstrate those skills before hiring.

```text
harder youth transition into work
  -> employer demand for mindset, teamwork, quality, detail, adaptability
  -> skills must become visible before employment
  -> competitions, work experience, and technical pathways act as bridges
  -> test: do visible skills improve interviews, retention, earnings, and fair access?
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Work, control, and bargaining | Youth entry and skill signaling | Hiring access depends on demonstrating practical and behavioural capability |
| Firm, sector, and market power | Employability as firm capacity | Employers associate the right skills with revenue and workplace performance |
| Unequal exposure and status | Unequal demonstration opportunity | Competitions and work experience may reveal skills but require time, coaching, and institutional access |
| Public systems and feedback | School-to-work bridge institutions | Structured experiences can translate education into credible employer signals |
| Household room and consumption | Early-career option set | Failed or delayed entry affects earnings, independence, and household support needs |

## Boundary

This is commissioned, self-reported employer polling. It does not measure actual skill deficits, hiring outcomes, discrimination, productivity, revenue, or the causal effect of competitions. “Right mindset” may be subjective and culturally loaded.

## Next test

Link structured skills demonstrations to applications, interviews, offers, retention, earnings, and subgroup access—especially for young people without family networks, coaching, transport, or conventional credentials.

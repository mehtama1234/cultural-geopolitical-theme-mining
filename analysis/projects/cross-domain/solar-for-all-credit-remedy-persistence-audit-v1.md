# Solar for All credit activation, remedy, and persistence audit v1

Date: 2026-10-18  
Parent rotation: [energy transition, household affordability, and public legitimacy audit](energy-transition-affordability-household-legitimacy-audit-v1.md)  
Status: active implementation-path test; not a completed household-outcome evaluation

## Research question

Does a public community-solar program convert eligibility and installed capacity
into a correctly timed, continuing, net household bill benefit—with a remedy when
billing fails—or does the chain stop at reach and program-level savings claims?

This is a deliberately narrow test of the broader conversion chain:

`public award -> project capacity -> eligibility -> enrollment/activation ->
monthly bill credit -> net bill and arrears -> correction/remedy -> continuation,
energy security, and trust`.

## Finding

The program has crossed several implementation stages beyond a construction
announcement, but the public record still breaks at the same high-value link:
**credit receipt is not yet the same as verified net household benefit**.

The evidence supports a reported reach and a designed billing route. It also
supports a bounded project-level endpoint: every subscriber in four retained
project rows is reported as receiving a monthly credit. That is stronger than a
policy promise, but it cannot answer whether the credit was large enough, posted
on time, offset by other charges, reduced arrears, or continued after eligibility,
account, housing, or project conditions changed.

The remedy evidence makes the institutional conversion problem visible. When
customer-level billing data were unavailable for a core audit purpose, the system
could still propose an error-rate remedy. The available analysis covers 11 SFA
CREF aggregations, finds both over- and under-crediting, and estimates a roughly
$245 full-period bill credit under the recommended $5.70-per-subscriber-month
scenario. That is stronger than a generic promise but remains an aggregate
method and not a named-account correction. The remedy itself still has stages—
proposal, adoption, account correction or payment, adequacy, and later trust—
and only the proposal is visible in this bounded record.

## National consequence comparator

The local program evidence should be read against, not merged with, the national
energy-insecurity layer. EIA's 2024 Residential Energy Consumption Survey reports
43.56 million primary-residence housing units with at least one energy-insecurity
condition, including 32.89 million that traded off food or medicine, 17.55 million
with unhealthy temperatures, and 16.19 million receiving a disconnect or delivery-
stop notice. These are overlapping housing-unit conditions, not Solar for All
eligible households and not a program evaluation. The LIHEAP FY2024 record reports
nearly 6 million people and families helped, 5 million heating-assistance
households, and 279,000 restoration instances, but does not show restoration
duration, next-bill affordability, or repeat crisis. A selected SIPP renter screen
also places utility difficulty beside following-month food and housing hardship,
while remaining descriptive and small in the joint-constraint cells.

This comparator changes the learning question: the unresolved issue is not whether
energy stress exists or whether public systems can reach households. It is whether
a solar credit reaches the same account in time, reduces the bill enough to avoid
tradeoffs, and persists through the next bill or an account/housing transition.
Without that linkage, Solar for All can be a real benefit and still leave the
highest-value household outcome unmeasured.

## Counterexamples and disconfirmation checks

This audit does not assume administrative failure.

- The four project rows are a positive implementation signal: listed subscribers
  were all reported as receiving a monthly credit.
- A free subscription, a stated activation route, and a proposed correction can
  reduce price and administrative uncertainty even when individual outcomes are
  not public.
- A project-level credit can be economically meaningful even if the remaining
  bill is not eliminated; “not verified” is not “no benefit.”
- Conversely, a credit can be received and still fail the household test if
  timing, amount, arrears, housing moves, or account deactivation prevent a
  durable reduction in energy stress.

The result would change toward a realized household-benefit finding if a
de-identified panel showed enrollment date, activation date, monthly credit,
remaining bill, charges, arrears, continued eligibility, move or cancellation,
and later energy-burden or safety outcomes for the same households. It would
change toward an access/remedy failure finding if delays, nonreceipt, offsets,
or unresolved corrections were concentrated among renters, households with
arrears, movers, or other eligibility subgroups.

## Public discussion signal

The [bounded Solar for All LinkedIn discussion](../../social-discussions/records/atlas-batch-0050-solar-all-linkedin-001.json)
frames the program as long-term low-income bill relief whose legitimacy depends
on funding, state workplans, asset performance, and legal/political continuity.
That discussion usefully names persistence and contestation as public concerns,
but it is advocacy/professional discussion and cannot substitute for bills or
remedy records.

## Next evidence request and learning design

The next highest-value collection is a matched, de-identified implementation
extract or docket appendix, linked by a stable participant/project key and month:

1. eligibility decision, enrollment, and Pepco activation dates;
2. monthly production/allocation and credit dollars;
3. total bill, other charges, arrears, disconnection risk, and payment-plan use;
4. customer housing/account status, language/access route, and cancellation or
   reapplication;
5. billing-error notices, final remedy decision, correction/payment date, and
   customer complaint or appeal;
6. at least one later period for continuation and one comparison route where
   feasible.

Use the national comparator as a pre-specified outcome frame: food/medicine
tradeoff, unsafe temperature, disconnect notice, restoration, and next-bill
stability. Do not infer that a credit prevented any of these outcomes unless the
same household or account is observed before and after activation.

Compare the same-household sequence rather than adding program-level counts.
Stratify by renter/owner, housing type, account status, baseline arrears,
eligibility pathway, and project. Pair the administrative trace with bounded
local discussion and regulator/utility records from the same period.

## Stopping rule

Stop this narrow pathway only when the same-household or same-account chain has
an observed activation, monthly credit, net bill, remedy/continuation outcome,
and a credible comparison—or when commission, utility, program, and public
records have been checked and the missing link is documented as unavailable.
Until then, retain the result as an implementation-stage audit and rotate the
broader fourteen-theme program to the next open chain. Do not treat project-level
receipt, reach, a proposed remedy, or a modeled average as verified household
affordability.

# AI employment support is becoming pathway infrastructure

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** OECD, *AI and Digitalisation for Employment Support in Belgium and Greece: New Tools to Support Minimum Income Beneficiaries*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-oecd-ai-employment-support-minimum-income-belgium-greece-2026.json)

## Core finding

The OECD report identifies a gap between entering employment and escaping
precarity. Minimum-income beneficiaries often reach short, low-paid, part-time,
or low-entry jobs with limited progression; in the cited analysis, only about
13% move into higher-quality jobs. Some remain on minimum-income support while
working, showing that employment itself is not the same as independent security.

The proposed AI tools therefore represent more than automated vacancy matching.
They are intended as pathway infrastructure: connecting a person's skills,
barriers, training options, local demand, and possible next steps toward better
work.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The report exposes a cultural shift in the meaning of employability. The target
is no longer simply to become employable or to accept the first available job;
it is to navigate a credible path toward sustainable work. That makes the
recommendation system part of a person's institutional experience of dignity,
agency, and future possibility.

The central tension is between personalisation and classification. Richer data
can reveal transferable skills and realistic routes, but the same data can
standardise people around past disadvantage. Human override is necessary, yet
human discretion also requires transparency and accountability.

## Open middle

```text
minimum-income dependence and unstable work
  -> fragmented data, services, vacancies, and training routes
  -> AI-assisted matching and career guidance with human oversight
  -> progression, job quality, income security, and agency
  -> trust in employment services or automated sorting into precarity
```

The OECD report combines linked administrative-data analysis with policy and
design recommendations. It does not establish causal AI effects, universal
transition rates, implemented safeguards, or that tools can create quality jobs
where labour demand is weak.

# Global shocks leave investment scars—and geopolitical position shapes recovery

## Finding

The World Bank's study of foreign-direct-investment announcements finds that adverse global growth shocks have scarring effects on the investment pipeline for about three years. The analysis uses local projections and accounts for the real-time nature of investor decisions. The result is about project announcements, not proof that projects were completed or that capital, jobs, technology, or household welfare materialized.

The scarring is uneven. Countries perceived as safer experience more subdued effects. Projects in strategic sectors smooth headline announcement fluctuations because they are less sensitive to adverse global growth shocks. Geopolitical positioning also helps explain why investment announcements recover differently across countries. The paper reports robustness across alternative shock measures, specifications, institutional definitions, sector classifications, and geopolitical-alignment measures.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds an investment-pipeline-to-dependence mechanism to the atlas:

`global growth shock -> investor uncertainty -> project announcement pipeline -> sector/country concentration -> jobs, infrastructure, technology, and bargaining options -> political attribution`

The social and political consequence is that geopolitical fragmentation may be felt before any factory closes or project is cancelled. A place can experience a quieter future: fewer announced projects, fewer expected employment routes, thinner supplier opportunities, and less choice among external partners. Meanwhile strategic sectors may continue receiving investment, producing a selective recovery that looks resilient in aggregate but concentrates opportunity around security-relevant locations and industries.

Geopolitical alignment is therefore not only a diplomatic position. It can become an economic filter that shapes which places remain investable, which sectors are buffered, and which communities bear the opportunity cost of uncertainty.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Economic insecurity can be anticipatory: communities lose expected opportunity before a realized investment failure is visible.
- “Safe” and “strategic” places may attract protective capital while ordinary sectors and less-connected regions absorb longer scarring.
- Investment geography can reshape local narratives about sovereignty, dependence, alliance, and who benefits from international alignment.
- FDI announcements are a useful early-warning indicator, but they must not be confused with realized jobs, wages, infrastructure, or household room.
- The next political contest may concern not only attracting investment, but choosing which dependencies and sectors become strategic.

## Boundary and next test

The paper does not establish project completion, local employment, technology transfer, public revenue, consumer benefit, or causal household welfare. The next test is a named country or sector episode that follows announcement through permitting, financing, construction, operation, local hiring, supplier participation, infrastructure dependence, public incentives, and community attribution.

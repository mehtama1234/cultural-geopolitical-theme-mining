# Time, care, and work-capacity source packet

**Purpose:** source packet for the company-trend record on care time and social
reproduction.
**Prepared:** 2026-10-31

## 1. American Time Use Survey: household childcare

- **Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *American Time Use Survey — 2024
  Results*.
- **URL:** https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/atus.pdf
- **Unit and geography:** U.S. civilian noninstitutional population age 15 and
  over; annual average time-use estimates.
- **Direct observation:** Adults living in households with children under six
  spent an average of 2.53 hours per day providing primary childcare. Women’s
  average was 2.96 hours and men’s 1.95 hours. Adults with the youngest child
  age 6–17 spent 0.83 hours per day on primary childcare.
- **Limit:** Primary activity excludes care done as a secondary activity; the
  survey does not identify an employer, job outcome, or care-quality result.

## 2. American Time Use Survey: unpaid eldercare

- **Source:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, *Unpaid Eldercare in the United
  States — 2023–2024*.
- **URL:** https://www.bls.gov/news.release/elcare.nr0.htm
- **Unit and geography:** U.S. civilian noninstitutional population age 15 and
  over; two-year combined ATUS estimates.
- **Direct observation:** 14% of people age 15 and over, or 38.2 million
  people, provided unpaid eldercare. 28% of eldercare providers provided care
  on an average day, spending 3.9 hours on days they provided care. Providers
  who were employed spent 2.8 hours on care days versus 4.8 hours for those not
  employed.
- **Limit:** Eldercare is self-reported and includes household and
  nonhousehold care; the estimate does not observe schedule flexibility,
  absence, retention, or employer response for the same person.

## What this packet supports

Official time-use data show that unpaid care consumes substantial, unevenly
distributed time and can coexist with paid work. The evidence supports a
company-relevant constraint on time and coordination, not a universal causal
claim about productivity, absence, or retention.

## What remains open

- whether care time changes completion, schedule stability, absence, retention,
  or customer access in a named company workflow;
- how paid care, transport, flexible work, household composition, and income
  change the burden;
- whether an intervention reduces total coordination work or merely shifts it;
  and
- what outcomes improve for the person receiving care.

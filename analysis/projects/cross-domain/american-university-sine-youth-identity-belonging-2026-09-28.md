# American University Sine Institute: youth identity, belonging, and future legitimacy

## Finding

The 2026 Sine Institute poll identifies a youth cultural pattern that is more specific than “young people are pessimistic.” Young Americans retain pride in the country’s past and a shared value language around freedom, diversity, fairness and respect, while separating that symbolic identity from judgments of the present, future, belonging, and institutional delivery. The result is a potential legitimacy gap: attachment remains available, but current performance and connection have to be earned.

## Evidence

- The Generation Lab survey interviewed 1,031 U.S. adults ages 18–34 online from July 23 to August 1, 2026.
- 57% were very or somewhat proud to be American and 53% described themselves as very or somewhat patriotic.
- 59% felt proud of what America had been in the past, versus 29% of America as it is today and 30% of where it is heading.
- 54% said America’s best years may be behind it; 47% said the country is becoming more divided and splitting apart.
- Belonging varied sharply by education pathway and identity: 35% of non-college-track versus 58% of college-track respondents agreed they belonged to a community or group that cared about them; the corresponding hetero/cis and LGBTQ+ figures were 55% and 41%.

## Interpretation and limits

The signal is a temporal and relational distinction: historical identity can coexist with present alienation, and value consensus can coexist with weak belonging or uncertain future confidence. The survey uses a proprietary online panel and self-reports; it does not measure objective community provision, actual participation, turnout, or causal generational effects.

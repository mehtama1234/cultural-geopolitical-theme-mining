# Social-protection formalization requires affordability and human navigation, not information alone

## Finding

An ILO randomized evaluation in Jordan tested two ways of expanding social-security registration among small firms: intensive in-person visits and low-cost SMS messages. Firm visits increased the number of workers registered in the Social Security Corporation by 0.31 workers per firm—or 9.2% relative to firms not visited—1.5 years later. The effect was driven by increased awareness of the subsidized Estidama++ programme and reduced financial burden, not by increased employment.

SMS was dramatically cheaper but did not increase the number of workers registered into social security. It produced a small increase in programme uptake and a short-lived increase in portal logins, while in-person visits generated more persistent institutional engagement.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`informal work + low contributory capacity -> information and affordability barriers -> human navigation + subsidy -> registration and institutional engagement`

The new subtheme is access versus usability. Public protection can be formally available yet practically unreachable when people do not understand eligibility, cannot absorb contributions, distrust future benefits, or cannot complete the administrative process. Cheap digital messaging scales awareness; it does not automatically remove financial, procedural, or relational friction.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Social insurance is a long-horizon consumer decision: workers and employers give up scarce current income for future pensions, unemployment protection, maternity benefits, and other lifecycle coverage.
- The experiment shows why “information deficit” is too narrow. Human contact mattered partly because it made the subsidy and affordability structure legible, while the registration interface itself remained difficult.
- A digital-first state can reach many firms cheaply while leaving the people with the greatest administrative and financial constraints unable to convert awareness into protection.
- The intervention worked for both female and male workers, but the reported effect was stronger among men; gender, nationality, refugee status, firm size, and contributory capacity remain important distributional tests.
- The policy design is a political bargain: subsidies reduce immediate cost, but durable legitimacy depends on whether workers later experience adequate, portable, and usable benefits.

## Boundary and next test

This is randomized evidence from eligible Jordanian small firms in a temporary subsidy programme. It does not establish benefit adequacy, durable post-subsidy coverage, improved job quality, household welfare, or transferability to other countries. The next test is whether a named social-insurance reform combines contribution affordability, human assistance, simpler administration, and realized claim access over the full worker lifecycle.

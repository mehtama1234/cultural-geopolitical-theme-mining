# Tax relief is not the same thing as household relief

## Finding

The World Bank's study of an unanticipated food-tax change in Papua New Guinea finds that policy relief reached markets unevenly. There was complete pass-through in formal supermarkets in central urban districts, where competition was stronger, but no pass-through in informal stores or rural areas where poorer households mainly shop.

Only 16% of the foregone revenue accrued to the poorest two quintiles, while the richest two quintiles and stores or wholesalers each captured almost 40% of the benefits. A prediction survey of 237 experts did not anticipate the degree of regressivity or the variation in pass-through. Under the study's static marginal-value-of-public-funds comparison, universal cash transfers generated roughly 2.5 times as much quintile-weighted value per fiscal dollar as the tax exemption.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds a policy-to-price conversion gap to the corpus. A government can announce a tax exemption and incur a fiscal cost without producing the promised consumer experience. The realized benefit depends on market structure, informality, geography, retailer competition, supply-chain bargaining, and what households actually buy.

`tax relief -> seller cost change -> price pass-through -> household affordability`

The chain breaks at multiple points. In a segmented market, the formal price response can look successful while the poorer households' informal or rural shopping channels show little change. This makes attribution politically difficult: households may experience high food costs while the policy remains publicly legible as “support.”

## Cultural, social, and consumer significance

- Cost-of-living policy must be evaluated at the store and household level, not only at the tax-code level.
- Informality is not merely a missing administrative category; it changes who receives public relief and who can capture it.
- Visible universal relief may be politically easier than targeted cash, even when its distributional value is worse.
- Consumer trust depends on whether announced relief is felt in the places and channels people actually use.
- Better incidence measurement can challenge expert and public assumptions about who benefits.

## Boundary and next test

The paper is a PNG-specific quasi-experimental study with a static policy comparison; it does not establish a universal result or prove that cash transfers are always feasible. The next test is a named cost-of-living reform that follows policy announcement, retailer prices, informal-market prices, household budgets, transfer receipt, and political attribution across rural and urban groups.

# Cohesion, trust, and disagreement stage audit

**Status:** sixty-third active depth artifact in the ongoing program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** belonging, local cooperation, intergroup trust, institutional
legitimacy, speech restraint, social support, participation, and civic space

## Purpose

This audit tests whether social cohesion is one condition or several stages:
coexistence, local interaction, trust, shared identity, institutional
confidence, willingness to speak, and actual participation. It compares Bosnia
and Herzegovina, England, Great Britain, and US indicators without pretending
that their indexes or survey frames are interchangeable.

## Evidence ledger

| Stage | Evidence with unit, date, and denominator | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local cooperation can coexist with weak intergroup trust | UNDP SCORE BiH 2025, fielded November 2025–February 2026: 3,170 weighted nationally representative respondents within an approximately 3,800-person sample with locality boosters. Intergroup harmony scored 6.3/10, inclusive civic identity 6.6, state identification 7.8, intergroup trust 4.2, and local community cooperation 6.7. | Functional cooperation and everyday coexistence can be stronger than emotional or intergroup trust. | Scores are diagnostic and country-specific; they do not show causal effects, reconciliation depth, or future stability. |
| System outlook is a distinct cohesion stage | The same SCORE assessment describes sense of progress as its weakest dimension; institutional legitimacy scored 6.9/10, state-institution trust 4.3, civic awareness 4.5, and civic duty 5.8, up from 4.9 in 2020. | People may cooperate locally while judging institutional progress, information, or voice more weakly. | Composite measures are not a universal cross-national scale, and the 2020 comparison coincided with COVID-19. |
| Belonging and coexistence exceed interpersonal trust in England | UK Community and Engagement Survey 2025/26, October 2025–March 2026, over 175,000 responses: 61% reported strong neighborhood belonging, 69% chatted with neighbors monthly, 61% said neighbors pull together, 79% said different backgrounds get along, but only 38% said many neighborhood people could be trusted. | Broad intergroup coexistence is not the same as local relational trust. | Aggregate repeated survey results do not identify causes, durable collective capacity, or the same people's trajectories. |
| Place distributes cohesion unevenly | In the same England survey, strong belonging ranged from 74% to 47% across local authorities and neighborhood trust from 65% to 15%; strong belonging was 69% in the least deprived areas versus 54% in the most deprived. | Place and deprivation shape the practical distribution of belonging and trust. | Local ranges may reflect sample design, composition, uncertainty, housing, safety, mobility, or institutions; they are not causal area effects. |
| Openness is valued while speech feels costly | UCL/More in Common September 2026 poll of 2,000 people with focus groups: 63% said every topic should be discussable despite disagreement, 58% said it is becoming harder to disagree, only 6% said Britain debates very well, and 69% sometimes bite their tongue. | Civic-space legitimacy can coexist with self-protective silence. | Reported restraint does not identify actual sanctions, platform causality, political withdrawal, or whether silence protects relationships. |
| Venue matters for civic participation | In the same Great Britain poll, 23% reported arguing about politics in a pub, 22% with strangers on social media, and 17% in a community group or town hall; 57% said politicians tend to be rude or disrespectful when debating. | Informal and online venues may be more usable or familiar than formal civic forums, but venue presence is not dialogue quality. | Percentages are not frequency, accessibility, representativeness, or evidence that physical-space decline caused online toxicity. |
| Support is not participation | CDC Community & Connection indicators published May 2026 show 58% of US youth reported receiving needed social/emotional support in 2021–23, 82% of adults in 2021, and 12% of adults reported difficulty participating in social activities due to a condition in 2024. | Reported support can coexist with participation barriers; support and social activity are different endpoints. | The CDC summary uses different surveys and years, lacks a common respondent denominator, and does not measure support adequacy, loneliness, belonging, or civic action. |

## Cross-source finding

The strongest supported pattern is **layered cohesion**:

```text
shared place or identity
  -> contact and practical cooperation
  -> interpersonal or intergroup trust
  -> institutional legitimacy and progress
  -> safe disagreement and voice
  -> participation, collective action, and durable belonging
```

The evidence does not support one cohesion score. Bosnia shows local cooperation
above intergroup trust; England shows coexistence far above neighborhood trust;
Great Britain shows support for open debate alongside speech restraint; the CDC
shows reported support alongside participation difficulty. These are not
contradictions. They indicate that social connection, trust, institutional
confidence, voice, and action can move on different clocks.

This also gives the program a counterexample to two simple stories: low trust
does not mean no cooperation, and reported support does not mean full
participation. A person or community can help, coexist, and belong while still
avoiding speech, distrusting institutions, or lacking the time, safety, health,
or access needed to participate.

## Bounded public discussion

Public discussion adds meaning and institutional framing, not prevalence:

| Platform | Signal | Analytical use and limit |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | UCL presents disagreement as potentially constructive when grounded in open debate and space for difficult questions. | Makes civic-space norms visible; an event discussion is not representative opinion and may understate unequal speaking risks. |
| LinkedIn | A loneliness-awareness discussion connects connection, belonging, and social prescribing to a public-health agenda. | Shows institutional response to isolation; attention is not evidence that participation improved. |
| LinkedIn | A YMCA discussion presents affordable third places as spaces where people across ages and backgrounds build belonging, with one personal account linking connection to work and identity. | Adds place and cultural meaning; one lived reaction and a promotional post cannot estimate effects. |
| LinkedIn | More in Common combines conflict-related political pressure with domestic safety judgments, identity boundaries, and cohesion. | Connects geopolitical events to local belonging; event audiences and live polling are not national samples. |
| LinkedIn | OECD frames shared food as low-stigma social infrastructure linking affordability, culture, public space, and belonging. | Adds a material/cultural mechanism; the post does not show reach, inclusion, or durable outcomes. |

The discussion layer suggests that cohesion is built in ordinary places—food,
third places, neighborhoods, workplaces, and forums—not only through formal
political institutions. It does not establish which places work or for whom.

## Missing links and challenge checks

- Do not combine Bosnia's 0–10 composites with England's percentages as one
  international score.
- Do not treat local cooperation as proof of intergroup trust or institutional
  legitimacy.
- Do not treat coexistence as deep reconciliation or neighborhood trust.
- Do not treat self-censorship as censorship, apathy, or political exit.
- Do not treat social support as participation, belonging, or reduced loneliness.
- Do not infer that deprivation causes the local-authority trust ranges.
- Keep resident, neighbor, institution, venue, platform, and state actors
  separate.
- Public posts and comments are interpretive and selected, not prevalence data.

## Stronger next test

Build a place- and episode-level cohesion ledger with:

1. local service, housing, safety, migration, or conflict change and date;
2. contact, third-place access, cost, transport, disability access, and time;
3. belonging, trust, intergroup interaction, disagreement safety, and voice;
4. institutional response, public explanation, and whether residents were heard;
5. actual participation, collective action, non-use, mobility, or exit; and
6. later trust, repair, inclusion, and durable local capacity.

Start with a named local intervention or public controversy where place-level
service data and repeated resident measures overlap. Do not infer action from
belonging or infer trust from attendance alone.

## Deeper question

**When does everyday coexistence become trusted collective capacity, and what
conditions let people speak, participate, and obtain a response without paying
an unacceptable social or material cost?**

## Stopping rule

This pass is complete only when one place or episode links a change in local
conditions to contact, trust, voice, institutional response, and later action
or durable belonging—or when the source owner confirms the missing fields
cannot be obtained. The broader 14-theme program remains active.

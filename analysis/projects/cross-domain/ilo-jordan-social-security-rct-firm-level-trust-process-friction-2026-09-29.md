# Formalization is a trust-and-implementation bargain: digital awareness cannot cross process friction alone

## Finding

The full ILO firm-level evaluation of Jordan’s Estidama++ programme adds a deeper layer to the earlier brief. Face-to-face outreach worked unevenly: effects were strongest among firms with 6–9 workers and among employees earning JOD 500 or less, while evidence for Syrian-worker registration was more encouraging in smaller 2–5-worker firms. This means the “same” formalization intervention reaches different groups through different institutional and eligibility conditions.

The report also documents the administrative work that awareness campaigns cannot remove. Firms repeatedly reported difficulty updating employee information, uploading documents, completing forms, making payments, understanding balances, and using the SSC portal. Some firms interpreted visits as inspection-related, and stale eligibility data created mismatches between what outreach officers promised and what firms could actually register.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`informal work + uncertain future benefit -> contribution and trust discount -> outreach -> eligibility, data, interface and enforcement encounter -> selective formalization`

The new subtheme is the trust-and-implementation bargain. Formalization is not only a worker or employer preference. It is a relationship with a public system whose benefits are delayed, whose costs are immediate, and whose administrative interface can redistribute time, risk, and uncertainty onto small firms.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Social insurance is a long-horizon purchase made under uncertainty: people pay now for benefits whose quality, eligibility, and delivery they cannot fully verify.
- Small firms do not experience “digital access” as a single condition. Updating records, uploading documents, paying contributions, and correcting eligibility each create separate work and failure points.
- A public visit can signal support or inspection depending on institutional history and how the state is encountered; the same messenger can build trust for one firm and trigger avoidance for another.
- Social-media visibility can broaden awareness while also creating loopholes, opportunistic registration, or confusion when eligibility data lag behind changing work arrangements.
- The proposed hybrid model—live guidance, phone invitations, and SMS follow-up—shows the emerging design pattern: digital reach is a multiplier for human institutional support, not necessarily a substitute for it.

## Boundary and next test

This is preliminary evidence from firms already registered with SSC and maintaining an official account. It does not establish long-run formalization, benefit adequacy, trust, job quality, household welfare, or results among firms entirely outside the administrative system. The next test is whether the hybrid intervention and child-benefit incentive improve durable registration, contribution continuity, benefit claims, and worker protection without increasing administrative burden or selective exclusion.

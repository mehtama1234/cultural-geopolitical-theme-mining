# Stable confidence can coexist with a present-purchase shortfall

## Finding

Ipsos’ September 2026 Global Consumer Confidence Index is stable at **48.4** across 30 countries, but the four sub-indices are sharply ordered: **Expectations 56.6** and **Jobs 57.7** sit well above **Current 39.0** and **Investment 41.7**. Aggregate stability therefore does not mean that households feel equally secure across time horizons or spending decisions.

The index also remains below its pre-war level in 13 countries. India leads at 66.9, while Türkiye is at 35.3; no country loses at least two points across all four sub-indices, and Chile and Israel gain across all four. The pattern is not one global mood—it is a geography of different present constraints, future beliefs, and geopolitical aftereffects.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`geopolitical/economic shock -> current purchasing and investment caution -> future expectations and job interpretation -> delayed or selective consumption -> firm demand, household attribution, and political mood`

The important mechanism is temporal asymmetry. Consumers can believe that jobs or future conditions are comparatively resilient while still reporting weak current purchasing and investment conditions. That gap can produce selective spending, postponement, substitution, and a different kind of political dissatisfaction than outright collapse: people may feel the future is possible but the present is not usable.

Country comparisons add a second layer. The same stable global average can contain sharply different national trajectories, and war-related sentiment effects can persist after the immediate news cycle. Firms and governments that read only the aggregate index may miss where current affordability, investment hesitation, or geopolitical attribution is accumulating.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Consumer confidence is multi-clock: current room, investment capacity, job security, and future expectations can move apart.
- “Resilience” in an aggregate index may coexist with postponed purchases, thin investment, and household caution.
- Geopolitical shocks can remain embedded in consumer sentiment across countries even when monthly movement stabilizes.
- Country averages are political signals but not household-level welfare measures; the distribution within countries remains an open question.
- The next test is behavioral or panel evidence linking these sub-index gaps to category substitution, saving, debt, investment, employment transitions, and political attribution.

## Boundary and next test

This is a recurring cross-country survey index, not a causal or household-panel study. It does not establish realized spending, investment, job security, welfare, or the causal effect of war or inflation. The next episode should follow one country or consumer group from sentiment through actual purchase, saving, borrowing, employment, and public response.

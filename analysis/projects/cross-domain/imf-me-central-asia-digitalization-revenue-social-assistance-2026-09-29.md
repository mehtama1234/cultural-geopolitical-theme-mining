# Digital public infrastructure is becoming fiscal and social infrastructure

## Finding

The IMF's September 2026 note on the Middle East and Central Asia shows that digitalization is not only a convenience layer for government. Tax e-services, digital identity, interoperable social registries, verification systems, and digital payments can change the state's ability to raise revenue, target assistance, and pay people. The same systems can also expand exclusion, surveillance, data-security exposure, and compliance costs when connectivity, financial inclusion, skills, legal safeguards, institutional capacity, or trust are weak.

The deeper trend is a **digital reach bargain**: the public sector is increasingly asking households and firms to become legible through digital systems in exchange for faster, more targeted, and potentially more transparent fiscal and social protection. Whether that bargain feels enabling or coercive depends on who can connect, who is misclassified, who can correct a record, who can appeal, and whether the resulting revenue or benefit actually arrives.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This extends the project's institutional-conversion thread. The World Bank's public-institutions record tracks how personnel, management, procurement, transparency, and independence convert public mandates into market outcomes. This IMF record adds the data and payment layer: digital systems convert information into fiscal capacity and administrative reach, but only if the social infrastructure around them is usable and trusted.

The mechanism runs across domains:

`identity + connectivity + accounts + interoperable data -> identification / verification / payment -> revenue and benefit reach -> household security, compliance, and state legitimacy`

Each arrow is conditional. A registry can improve targeting while propagating an error. A payment rail can reduce leakage while excluding people without accounts. A tax platform can make compliance easier for connected firms while increasing enforcement pressure on informal or low-capacity workers. Digital readiness therefore becomes a distributional institution, not merely a technology score.

## What this adds to the corpus

- A Middle East and Central Asia route joining tax capacity and social assistance rather than treating them as separate administrative systems.
- A distinction between formal digital availability and practical reachability through accounts, connectivity, skills, trust, and remedy.
- A consumer and household lens: administrative digitization changes the time, proof, privacy, and compliance work required to access public value.
- A political-legitimacy test: better targeting or revenue collection can strengthen trust only when people experience accuracy, fairness, transparency, and correction—not simply more data extraction.

## Boundary and next test

The IMF note combines comparative indicators, country cases, and associations; it does not establish a uniform causal effect or prove that digitalization improves welfare. The next test is a named country implementation study that can observe who is reached, who is excluded or misclassified, how appeals work, and whether improved administrative capacity changes realized household security rather than only system readiness.

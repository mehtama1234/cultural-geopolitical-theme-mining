# When moderation retreats, information risk becomes a social-infrastructure problem

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** Northwestern University Roberta Buffett Institute and Center for Communication & Public Policy, *Global Disinformation in a Post-Moderation World* synthesis report (10 September 2026)  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-northwestern-post-moderation-disinformation-trust-infrastructure-2026.json)

## Core finding

The report reframes disinformation from a false-claim problem into a governance
and coordination problem. When platforms and governments retreat from earlier
moderation and counter-disinformation roles, the burden moves toward users,
journalists, civil society, and local communities that often have less authority
and fewer resources. The resulting risk is not only that people believe a false
story; it is that they stop trusting any shared information well enough to
coordinate.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The distinctive theme is **the privatization of epistemic resilience**. If the
formal safeguards recede, people are asked to perform source verification,
attention control, and civic discernment individually. But the report’s examples
suggest that trusted messengers, local language knowledge, community ties, and
provenance infrastructure are collective goods. Their absence can turn unequal
information capacity into unequal political agency.

This also changes the cultural unit of analysis. The relevant question is not
only whether a person can identify a lie. It is whether a community can still
share enough trusted evidence to decide, act, and disagree without retreating
into isolated information worlds.

## Open middle

The next valid test should join:

```text
platform/government retreat or adversarial campaign
  -> attention and distribution asymmetry
  -> generalized distrust or enclave retreat
  -> impaired coordination / public judgment
  -> provenance, trusted-messenger, or accountability intervention
  -> measurable recovery of reach, agency, and institutional trust
```

The publication does not establish prevalence, causality, intervention effect,
or that local trust networks are automatically inclusive or accurate.

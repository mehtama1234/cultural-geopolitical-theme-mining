# Poverty reduction can be real while prosperity remains geographically, occupationally, and gender uneven

## Finding

The World Bank’s Uzbekistan Poverty and Equity Assessment reports a major improvement between 2021 and 2025: national poverty fell from **17.0% to 5.8%**, approximately **3.7 million people** moved out of poverty, and the middle-class share rose above **72%**. But the same assessment shows why headline prosperity is not the end of the story: one in five people remains vulnerable to falling back into poverty, around **85% of new jobs are informal**, high-skilled work is concentrated around Tashkent, women remain largely outside the labor market because of care, and cash-transfer coverage fell from 26% to 8% of households.

The finding is not that poverty reduction is illusory. It is that **mobility, security, and social inclusion are different outcomes**. A country can achieve rapid aggregate improvement while the pathways into durable prosperity remain narrow, spatially concentrated, care-constrained, and weakly protected.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`growth and wage employment -> poverty reduction and middle-class expansion -> job quality, spatial access, care, skills, and safety-net filters -> vulnerability or durable mobility -> household and political attribution`

This is a useful correction to growth narratives. More wage employment can lift households, but if new jobs are informal, low-productivity, or concentrated in one region, the route to prosperity may depend on migration, education, household care substitution, or tolerance of risk. A shrinking safety net can then matter precisely when people are classified as “better off” but remain exposed to shocks.

The gender findings make the household mechanism visible. Care is not only a private preference; it is a labor-market constraint that shapes income, mobility, human-capital investment, and future vulnerability. A national middle class can expand while women in poor and vulnerable households remain outside paid work and bear the time cost of social reproduction.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- “Middle class” can describe current consumption while leaving future security and fallback capacity unresolved.
- Job quantity and job quality are separate political promises; informal work may reduce poverty without creating durable bargaining power or protection.
- Spatial concentration turns geography into a gatekeeper of skills, earnings, housing, mobility, and belonging.
- Care responsibilities and gender wage gaps redistribute the gains of growth inside households.
- Safety-net coverage is part of the prosperity story: reducing transfers can be efficient targeting, successful income growth, or a new vulnerability depending on who exits and what shocks follow.

## Boundary and next test

This is a country-level World Bank assessment, not a causal evaluation or same-household panel. It does not prove that wage employment caused every poverty exit, that informal jobs are harmful in every case, or that lower transfer coverage reduced welfare. The next test is longitudinal evidence following households across work formality, earnings, care, migration, education, housing, shocks, transfers, and return to poverty.

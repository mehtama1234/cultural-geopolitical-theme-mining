# Consumer protection is moving from awareness to usable recourse

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** New Zealand Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment and Commerce Commission, *New Zealand Consumer Survey 2026*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-new-zealand-consumer-survey-2026-rights-ai-recourse.json)

## Core finding

The survey shows a gap between knowing that consumer law exists and knowing how
to use it. Ninety-five percent of respondents were aware that consumer laws
exist, but only half reported moderate or good understanding of their rights.
Forty-four percent experienced a product or service problem in the previous two
years, and 75 percent of those who took action said the issue was resolved.

The emerging consumer trend is therefore not simply distrust or confidence. It
is the conversion of formal rights into practical agency through information,
reviews, ratings, direct action, regulator accessibility, and now AI guidance.

## Cultural/social interpretation

Consumer citizenship is becoming an information-and-navigation practice. People
do not experience protection as a statute; they experience it as whether they
can identify a problem, find credible guidance, contact a business or regulator,
and obtain a remedy without disproportionate effort.

AI entering this pathway creates a new ambiguity. It may democratise access to
rights guidance, especially when official language is difficult, but it may also
produce confident errors, misroute complaints, or make platform-mediated advice
the first layer of a public protection system without public accountability.

## Open middle

```text
purchase or service failure
  -> recognition and information search
  -> review/AI/official guidance and confidence to act
  -> business or regulator contact
  -> resolution, remedy, switching, or abandonment
  -> trust, future purchasing behavior, and demand for enforcement
```

The survey is nationally weighted and useful for identifying the route, but it
does not establish legal comprehension, causal AI effects, equal access to
redress, or whether reported resolutions were fair and durable.

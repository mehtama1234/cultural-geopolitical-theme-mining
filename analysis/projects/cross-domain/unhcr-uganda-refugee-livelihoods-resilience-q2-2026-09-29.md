# Refugee self-reliance is a conversion gap between productive inputs, land, documentation, finance, and markets

## Finding

UNHCR’s Uganda Refugee Response livelihoods dashboard for Q2 2026 reports a full pathway from productive capacity to economic inclusion: land and inputs, agricultural knowledge, producer organizations, markets, decent work, business services, financial inclusion, and vocational training. The sector had received **$27.8 million**, or **18%** of its reported **$153 million** 2026 requirement.

The dashboard reports 9,723 households receiving agricultural inputs or productive assets, 27,386 people trained in Good Agricultural Practices, 8,128 participants in market-driven skills training, 2,824 graduates linked with employers, and 279 businesses formally registered.

The distinct finding is a conversion gap. Programme activity can expand productive capacity without producing self-reliance when households lack enough land, downstream buyers, storage, formal finance, documentation, or legal access to work. The dashboard makes that gap visible across refugee groups and host communities rather than treating “livelihoods” as a single intervention.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`refugee/host household vulnerability -> land and productive inputs -> skills and producer organization -> markets, employer links, finance, and formalization -> income, autonomy, and social stability`

Average land access was reported at 0.6 acres per supported household against a programme-identified 1.2-acre critical threshold. It varied from 0.2 acres for Congolese households to 0.9 for Sudanese households. This matters because the same training or input package can have very different returns depending on land, legal status, market access, household size, and climate exposure.

The dashboard also names administrative friction: refugee documentation and Know Your Customer requirements constrain formal employment and finance. Digital banking and formalization may reduce exclusion, but may also introduce fees, identity surveillance, repayment risk, or new barriers. Self-reliance is therefore both an economic and institutional status.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Refugee inclusion is measured through usable land, work, finance, markets, and organizational voice—not only assistance receipt.
- “Self-reliance” can become a burden if responsibility shifts to households without sufficient land, capital, documentation, or protection.
- Producer associations, VSLAs, and employer links are social infrastructure connecting displaced and host communities to economic life.
- Formalization and KYC systems determine who becomes visible to banks, employers, public services, and markets.
- Funding adequacy is itself a political signal: an 18% coverage rate limits whether the full livelihood pathway can be delivered consistently.

## Boundary and next test

This is an operational partner-reporting dashboard with preliminary funding figures. It does not establish sustained income, business survival, employment quality, food security, or causal self-reliance effects. The next test is a named settlement or livelihood group followed from land and inputs through market sale, finance, documentation, employment retention, household consumption, and refugee-host relations.

# Federal laboratory-equipment disposal can expose biosecurity gaps while harm remains open

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** federal equipment disposal → export-control and biosecurity gap; malicious use and harm remain open

GAO's public-health preparedness review adds an observed asset-disposal and
national-security route. HHS and USDA sold 1,316 pieces of unneeded laboratory
equipment from fiscal years 2022 through 2025. GAO identified nine freeze dryers
and mass spectrometers on the Commerce Control List that were sold to U.S. and
Canadian individuals or companies, some appearing to be resellers. The agencies
did not report those items to GSA as required.

The control gap was operationally testable. Using fictitious identities, GAO
successfully acquired four pieces of equipment that experts said could pose a
biosecurity risk. HHS and USDA had not assessed disposal-related biosecurity
risks, and GSA's property system lacked a standard field for identifying
Commerce Control List equipment. Four recommendations remain open on reporting,
risk assessment, and mitigation.

The evidence boundary is material. The acquisition test does not estimate
malicious-use probability, and the public record does not establish recipient
intent, export, biological-weapon development, sensitive-information recovery,
public harm, or completed mitigation.

# Belonging is widespread, but cohesion is materially and locally uneven

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Helen Clark Foundation, *Social Cohesion in New Zealand 2026*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-helen-clark-nz-social-cohesion-2026-belonging-financial-stress.json)

## Core finding

New Zealand’s social-cohesion picture contains a layered contradiction. Eighty-four
percent feel they belong to the country, but only about half feel a strong sense
of community locally; only two in five trust the government to do the right
thing, and nearly two-thirds of under-30s feel isolated at least sometimes.

The report’s most important mechanism is material. Financial stress predicts low
belonging, low trust, high isolation, and more negative attitudes to immigration
more strongly than almost any other measured variable. Housing is part of the
cohesion infrastructure: homeowners show higher reported neighbourhood belonging
and civic participation than renters. Belonging does not remove poverty, but it
appears to change how financial pressure is converted into social withdrawal.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The report reframes belonging as a conversion problem. A person can feel proud
of the country while lacking a strong local community, trusting institutions,
feeling safe, or having enough money to participate. The “national” and “local”
levels therefore should not be collapsed into one cohesion score.

The material route is equally important. When financial pressure predicts both
social isolation and negative views of immigration, cultural conflict may be
partly an expression of economic insecurity and weakened social infrastructure.
That does not make identity concerns unreal; it shows how household room,
housing stability, friendship, and institutional trust shape the meaning people
give to difference.

## Open middle

```text
financial stress / housing / safety / age / media environment
  -> belonging, trust, isolation, and perceived threat
  -> friendship, civic participation, immigration judgment, and political preference
  -> durable local cohesion, institutional legitimacy, and wellbeing
```

The public summary reports associations from a weighted survey. It does not
establish causal direction, clinical loneliness, actual participation, or policy
effects.

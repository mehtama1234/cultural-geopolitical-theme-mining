# Growth is becoming a weaker promise of work in Bangladesh

## Finding

The World Bank’s Bangladesh study finds weaker evidence of the conventional growth–employment relationship than in six other Asian comparison countries. It estimates employment elasticity across 2003–2010, 2010–2017, and 2017–2024, with major sectoral shifts over time.

Agricultural employment elasticity rises from 0.29 in 2003–2010 to 0.98 in 2016–2024, while industrial elasticity falls from 0.84 to -0.04. At the subsector level, accommodation and food services and transportation and storage show the strongest employment elasticity; manufacturing performs worst and turns negative.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This adds a growth-to-work-conversion mechanism:

`economic growth -> sectoral demand and productivity -> employment elasticity -> job quality, wages, stability, and bargaining -> household room and political attribution`

The important shift is from asking whether an economy is growing to asking where growth creates work, for whom, and of what quality. A country can grow while manufacturing becomes less employment-intensive and service sectors absorb more workers. That may expand opportunities, but it can also shift people toward precarious, seasonal, lower-productivity, or geographically concentrated work.

The result complicates the cultural meaning of “development.” Industrialization is often imagined as a reliable path from rural work to stable wage employment. The measured sectoral shift suggests that the social contract may instead be organized around service work, mobility, consumption, and informal adaptation—unless policy deliberately improves job quality and transition routes.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- GDP growth is an increasingly weak shorthand for household security when employment conversion varies by sector.
- Agriculture’s higher elasticity may mean more jobs without better jobs; manufacturing’s negative elasticity may reflect productivity gains or employment displacement.
- Accommodation, food, transport, and storage connect employment to consumer demand and movement, but may also expose workers to volatility and weak bargaining.
- Jobless or low-quality growth changes migration, family decisions, consumption, aspirations, and political attribution.
- Employment policy needs to track quality, wages, formality, hours, safety, gender access, and mobility—not only job counts.

## Boundary and next test

This is macro time-series evidence and does not establish worker-level causality, job quality, wages, or household welfare. The next test is a named Bangladesh sector or regional episode that follows growth into firm hiring, worker transitions, earnings, hours, formality, migration, care burdens, and durable household room.

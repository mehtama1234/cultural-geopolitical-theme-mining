# Consumer confidence rebounds while present household room remains flat

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Bank of Korea, *Consumer Survey Index for September 2026*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-bok-korea-consumer-survey-september-2026-sentiment-housing-expectations.json)

## Core finding

South Korea’s September consumer sentiment index rose 2.1 points to 106.6,
but the composite rebound should not be read as a uniform recovery in lived
household conditions. Current living-standards assessment was unchanged, while
prospective household income rose two points and prospective spending one
point. Current and future domestic economic assessments also improved, but the
current measure remained below the neutral 100 line.

The other side of the picture is cost and asset expectation. Past-year
inflation perception was 3.0%; one-, three-, and five-year expected inflation
were 2.7%, 2.6%, and 2.6%, all unchanged. Housing-price outlook remained high
at 125, while the interest-rate outlook rose three points to 128. This is a
consumer-expectations pattern in which confidence about direction coexists with
flat present room and persistent housing/financial-cost exposure.

## Cultural/social interpretation

Consumer sentiment is often treated as a proxy for recovery. This release is
more useful when read as a layered signal: people can become more hopeful about
income and the economy while still reporting no improvement in current living
standards. Housing expectations add a status and security dimension: rising
price expectations may be optimism for owners, threat for renters, or both at
once.

The mechanism matters for cross-country comparison. A national confidence index
does not carry the same meaning in every household. It must be connected to
prices, wages, debt service, tenure, transactions, substitution, and public
attribution before it can explain consumer behavior or political legitimacy.

## Open middle

```text
market/policy news / income expectations / housing expectations / rate outlook
  -> perceived household room and consumer confidence
  -> actual spending, saving, borrowing, mobility, and cultural participation
  -> attribution, trust, and political judgment
```

The Bank of Korea release is a monthly repeated cross-section and index system.
It does not establish realized consumption, household debt stress, housing
transactions, or causal effects.

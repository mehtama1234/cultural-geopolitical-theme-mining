# ECB Consumer Expectations Survey 2026: aggregate stability can conceal an income-positioned credit and inflation map

## Finding

The ECB’s August 2026 Consumer Expectations Survey shows a distributional split inside apparently stable euro-area aggregates. Expected nominal income growth is 1.0% and expected nominal spending growth is 3.6%, while expected unemployment and credit tightening ease modestly in aggregate. Yet lower-income respondents continue to report higher inflation perceptions and expectations, expect unemployment of 13.4% compared with 9.4% among the top income quintile, and expect mortgage rates of 5.7% compared with 4.4% at the top.

## Mechanism

Household room is not only a function of the aggregate consumer outlook. The same macro environment is interpreted through income position, housing exposure and access to credit. A stable aggregate expectation can therefore coexist with a stratified anticipation of prices, job risk and financing cost. This makes consumer confidence and monetary transmission socially uneven before any realized spending or default outcome is visible.

## Cross-domain implication

The next analytical link is from expectations to observable household paths: who delays housing or major purchases, who uses savings or revolving credit to bridge expected spending above income, who receives or loses credit access, and whether lower-income households experience different inflation baskets or employment shocks. The ECB release supports the expectation layer; it does not answer those downstream questions.

## Boundary

The CES is a monthly online survey of approximately 19,000 adults across 11 euro-area countries. Its measures are perceptions, expectations and net balances, not verified prices, income, mortgage contracts, employment, borrowing or realized consumption. Income-quintile differences are descriptive and do not establish causality.

Source: [ECB Consumer Expectations Survey results – August 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr260918~295b3ab978.ga.html).

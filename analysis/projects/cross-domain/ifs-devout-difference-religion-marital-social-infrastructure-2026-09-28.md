# Religious practice functions as relational infrastructure for some married couples

**Source:** Institute for Family Studies, [The Devout Difference: Religion, Marital Quality, and Sex Among Americans](https://ifstudies.org/report-brief/the-devout-difference-religion-marital-quality-and-sex-among-americans)  
**Method:** 2026 IFS Religion and Family Survey with General Social Survey trend comparisons; the public report summary does not state the full 2026 survey sample size or provide complete model documentation.

## Finding

Religious participation is shrinking, but among the married groups studied, regular attendance, shared norms, and repeated communal practice are associated with stronger reported marital quality and sexual satisfaction. The relevant mechanism is social and relational infrastructure—not a simple demographic label or proof that attendance causes better relationships.

```text
shared belief / repeated ritual / community support
  -> commitment, meaning, and relational practice
  -> reported marital quality and satisfaction
  -> persistence of benefits among a smaller participating population
  -> test selection, support, and institutional pathways separately
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Trust, identity, and meaning | Shared relational meaning | Marriage and intimacy are interpreted through shared values, ritual, and commitment |
| Care, health, and reproduction | Family-support infrastructure | Religious communities may provide social support around marriage and family life |
| Unequal exposure and status | Selection into durable institutions | The observed association may reflect who remains, marries, attends, and receives support |
| Public systems and feedback | Institutional decline with concentrated persistence | Participation shrinks while stronger outcomes remain concentrated among regular participants |

## Boundary

This is observational, institutionally sponsored research with incomplete public methodological detail. It does not establish causality, universal religious advantage, or that sexual frequency equals relationship wellbeing.

## Next test

Compare religious and secular couples using transparent longitudinal or matched designs that measure selection, social support, conflict, care burden, economic stress, relationship stability, and exit over time.

# Education is a cultural promise whose perceived performance tracks American-Dream hope

## Finding

The second MCAAD–Gallup American Dream Study finds that 92% of Americans consider obtaining a quality education part of the American Dream, but most rate K-12 schools fair or poor on the goals they consider important. The sharpest gap concerns critical thinking: 88% call it very important, while only 17% rate schools excellent or very good at it.

The study links school judgment to opportunity hope. Sixty-five percent of people who rate schools favorably across at least three goals say they are optimistic about the future of the American Dream, compared with 38% of others. This is an institutional-legitimacy signal, not evidence that school ratings cause optimism.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`education as cultural promise -> perceived school capability -> belief in fair opportunity -> intergenerational hope and institutional legitimacy`

Education is doing more than preparing workers. It is a public story about whether people can improve their circumstances, become informed citizens, and give their children more opportunity. When expected functions exceed perceived performance, the gap can become a broader judgment about whether the social contract still works.

## Boundaries

This is a nationally representative Gallup/MCAAD web-and-mail survey of more than 6,300 U.S. adults conducted January 7–March 4, 2026. The associations remain observational even after adjustment for reported covariates. The article does not establish student achievement, school effectiveness, classroom conditions, causal direction or policy impact.

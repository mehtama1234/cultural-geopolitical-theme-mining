# Cloud procurement becomes a public-capability and vendor-dependency decision

## Finding

The World Bank’s new public-sector guide argues that cloud procurement is now on the critical path for government AI, data analytics, e-government, and disaster-recovery capability. Cloud changes the state’s technology model from buying and operating capital assets to contracting for service levels, continuity, availability, data durability, security, incident response, and outcomes.

The guide makes procurement a strategic institutional function. It recommends considering centralized digital governance, whole-of-government frameworks, marketplaces, supplier accreditation, and evaluation criteria that include technical quality, privacy, interoperability, sustainability, and SME participation—not price alone.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`public procurement -> cloud architecture, supplier structure, data control, and exit rights -> service capability and continuity -> citizen trust and institutional legitimacy`

The central risk is that governments can gain rapid access to AI and digital services while losing bargaining power, portability, or accountability. Data residency, non-proprietary services, multi-cloud options, portability, and clear exit costs are therefore not only technical contract clauses; they are public-control mechanisms.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Procurement decisions increasingly shape citizens’ experience of health, civil-registration, smart-city, and e-government services.
- Cloud frameworks can widen access for smaller providers, or concentrate public capability around dominant vendors.
- “Digital sovereignty” becomes operational through contract terms, data portability, interoperability, supplier diversity, and continuity testing.
- Public trust depends on whether the state can explain, secure, audit, and if necessary exit the systems it buys.

## Boundary and next test

This is implementation guidance, not causal evidence that a centralized broker, cloud adoption, or multi-cloud strategy improves public services. The next test is a live procurement audit tracking supplier concentration, portability, exit cost, downtime, security incidents, SME participation, user outcomes, and citizen trust over time.

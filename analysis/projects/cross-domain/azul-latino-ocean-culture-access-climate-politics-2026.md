# Azul 2026 National Poll: ocean protection as cultural inheritance, public access and political agency

## Finding

The National Azul Poll suggests that Latino environmental politics is not reducible to abstract climate concern. Ocean protection is linked to candidate choice, family tradition, public access, household cost and labor rights across the seafood supply chain. Eighty percent say a candidate supporting stronger coastline and ocean policies would make them more likely to vote for that candidate, including 75% of Republican Latino voters and 87% of Democratic Latino voters.

The political meaning is paired with a stated material tradeoff. Seventy-six percent support banning offshore drilling and replacing it with renewable fuels, and 64% say they would still support the ban if it meant paying more per month for gasoline and household gas. The survey does not quantify the premium, but it identifies a willingness-to-pay frame in which environmental protection is treated as a quality-of-life and public-health investment rather than only a sacrifice.

The cultural route is equally important: 76% say the ocean matters to family traditions and culture, 85% say government should keep beaches and parks open to everyone, and 78% say all beaches should be public. Ocean governance is therefore presented as a question of inherited identity and access to common space. Support for stronger enforcement against illegal fishing and an international body addressing forced labor in fishing extends the same logic from domestic public access to transnational worker protection.

## Mechanism

Environmental concern becomes politically durable when it is attached to culture, access, health, household economy and accountable labor governance. The cross-partisan pattern may be a bridge for climate politics, but it does not erase disagreements about implementation, siting, cost distribution or regulation.

## Boundary

BSP Research conducted the poll for Azul among 2,102 Latino voters, interviewing in English or Spanish, with regional oversamples and a reported overall margin of error of +/-2.1 percentage points. Azul is an ocean-justice advocacy organization and the release is a commissioner publication. These are stated attitudes and hypothetical tradeoffs, not validated votes, observed purchasing, actual energy burdens, verified beach access, worker conditions or realized conservation outcomes.

Source: [2026 National Azul Poll: Latinos and the Ocean](https://azul.org/en/single-media/).

# Americans use AI broadly but preserve human authority for sacred and emotionally consequential counsel

## Finding

The NPR/Ipsos Religion and AI poll adds a boundary to the broader AI-adoption story. Sixty-one percent of U.S. adults report using an AI tool, but among AI users only 11% have used it for spiritual or religious guidance, 13% to generate prayers, and 15% to study religious texts. Sixty-nine percent say they would never seek religious or spiritual guidance from AI.

For difficult personal topics, family members and friends remain more trusted than AI: 75% of AI users say they trust people they know more than AI advice, compared with 6% who trust AI more. Yet AI has a practical opening: availability without scheduling or waiting, more information, and reduced fear of judgment. Only 29% of all adults say they are comfortable turning to AI for a personal question or problem.

The cultural mechanism is selective delegation. People may delegate search, drafting, summarization, or practical problem-solving to AI while retaining human and relational authority over conscience, spirituality, identity, emotional vulnerability, and moral judgment. This is not simply “AI adoption versus resistance”; it is a domain-specific boundary around who or what is considered entitled to counsel.

## Theme and subthemes

- **Instrumental adoption, relational restraint:** broad AI use does not imply willingness to delegate consequential judgment.
- **Sacred-domain boundary:** spirituality, prayer, scripture, and conscience are treated as different from ordinary advice.
- **Human authority premium:** family, friends, written resources, and trusted leaders retain legitimacy where context and accountability matter.
- **Low-friction substitution:** availability, no waiting, more information, and reduced judgment make AI attractive when human support is inaccessible.
- **Access-versus-accountability tension:** the same privacy and convenience that widen access may reduce relational correction and responsibility.
- **Social-infrastructure diagnostic:** AI use may reveal missing or unavailable human support rather than simply replacing existing relationships.

## What the source supports

The source supports a nationally representative probability-sample description of AI use, advice-source preference, spiritual-use behavior, trust, comfort, and practical reasons for choosing AI. It supports a domain-specific model of AI delegation.

It does not establish actual substitution, religious change, emotional or clinical outcomes, advice accuracy, or whether AI strengthens or weakens human relationships over time.

## Next test

Follow AI use in a named domain—religious communities, grief support, counseling, education, or peer support—from access and privacy into disclosure, advice quality, human referral, community participation, correction, and durable wellbeing. Test whether AI is a bridge to human support, a substitute where support is absent, or a parallel authority with different accountability.

Source: [NPR/Ipsos, Most Americans skeptical of turning to AI for spiritual guidance](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/most-americans-skeptical-turning-ai-spiritual-guidance).

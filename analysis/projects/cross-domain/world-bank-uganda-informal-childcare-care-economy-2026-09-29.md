# Childcare is work infrastructure for the informal economy

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11442, *Childcare for Informal-Sector Workers: Delivery Models, Access, and Welfare*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-uganda-informal-workers-childcare-delivery-welfare-2026.json)

## Core finding

In a randomized study of 883 women market vendors in Kampala, free childcare
redistributed caregiving away from household members, raised women’s hourly
earnings, and improved child development and women’s well-being.

The delivery model mattered. Community centers achieved higher utilization and
larger gains in child development and well-being, while workplace centers
generated larger economic impacts. Childcare is therefore not just a family
benefit; it is work infrastructure whose location and operating model shape who
can use it and which outcomes improve.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The study makes visible a hidden labour-market subsidy: informal women workers
often sustain economic activity while household members absorb childcare. Free
care does not simply add more work hours; it can make existing work more
productive by reducing fragmented caregiving and allowing women to choose how
to allocate time.

The delivery comparison also resists a universal “best model.” Community care
may fit irregular schedules and produce wider welfare gains; workplace care may
be powerful when proximity directly removes the care-work trade-off. The social
question is therefore not only who pays for childcare, but where care is located,
whose routines it recognizes, and who controls the time released.

## Open middle

```text
unpaid childcare burden in informal work
  -> free community or workplace care
  -> redistribution of caregiving and time
  -> earnings, child development, and caregiver well-being
  -> women’s economic agency and the legitimacy of care as public infrastructure
```

The study is a randomized trial in Kampala. It does not establish long-run
formalisation, fertility, universal delivery superiority, provider sustainability
without subsidy, or effects outside the study context.

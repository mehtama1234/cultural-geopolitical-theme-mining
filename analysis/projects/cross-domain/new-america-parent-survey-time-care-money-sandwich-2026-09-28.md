# Parents want more family time, but money and stacked care responsibilities define the reachable option set

**Source:** New America New Practice Lab, [2026 National Parent Survey findings](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/2026-national-parent-survey/findings/)  
**Method:** 5,472 parents and primary caregivers of children ages 0–5, fielded January 16–February 2, 2026; NORC AmeriSpeak probability panel plus a nonprobability panel, weighted nationally, with a lower-income oversample. [Methodology and limitations](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/2026-national-parent-survey/appendix/)

## Finding

Parents share a broad aspiration—more quality time with young children—but they do not share one preferred work, leave, or child-care arrangement. Money, work hours, care supply, transportation, and eldercare determine whether family time is actually available.

```text
desired family time
  -> leave, work schedule, child-care, transport, and wage constraints
  -> unequal option set by income, gender, place, race/ethnicity, and child age
  -> stacked care: young children + older adults + household survival
  -> open question: which intervention expands usable time without shifting costs elsewhere?
```

## Theme and subtheme map

| Program theme | Subtheme | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Care, health, and reproduction | Family-time and care infrastructure | Parents want more time with children, but leave and care systems mediate access |
| Time as a hidden price | Unpaid and foregone-time burden | More hours, short leave, transport, and care coordination consume usable time |
| Work, control, and bargaining | Schedule/work-family control | Most parents report a mismatch between actual and ideal work arrangements |
| Household room and consumption | Wage-to-time conversion | Higher wages, care affordability, and activity money determine whether time can be used well |
| Unequal exposure and status | Stratified option sets | Lower-income, urban, some racialized, and single-parent families face sharper stacked constraints |
| Public systems and feedback | Plural policy design | No single leave, work, or child-care model serves a majority; policy must support multiple routes |

## Boundary

The survey is nationally weighted but combines a probability panel and a nonprobability panel, uses self-report, and is a single early-2026 snapshot. It does not establish causal effects, independently verify hardship, measure child outcomes, or prove that one policy would resolve the mismatch.

## Next test

Join a named leave, wage, child-care, transportation, schedule, or eldercare intervention to actual usable family time, employment continuity, care reliability, household room, and subgroup-specific recovery.

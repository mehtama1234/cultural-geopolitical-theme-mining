# Place identity as soft infrastructure—and the risk of branding without power

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** OECD, *Local Identity, Pride and Branding in Place Transformation: Stories that Shape Places* (27 May 2026)  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-oecd-local-identity-pride-place-transformation-2026.json)

## Core finding

The OECD report treats place identity as an operating layer between material
development and social meaning. A place’s inherited story can lock communities
into decline, while a credible and participatory future narrative can help
coordinate investment, cultural regeneration, local enterprise, talent retention,
and belonging. The important mechanism is not the slogan. It is whether a story
becomes usable soft infrastructure for collective decisions and material change.

## Cultural/social interpretation

This publication expands the meaning of “culture” beyond consumption or
representation. Culture becomes a coordination technology for place: it tells
people what is worth preserving, what future is credible, and who is authorized
to speak for the community.

But the same mechanism can work in two directions. A shared narrative can reduce
fragmentation and support collective investment; it can also aestheticize decline,
hide conflict, attract capital without resident control, or turn belonging into a
marketing asset. The decisive distinction is whether narrative production is
participatory and materially connected to jobs, housing, public space, local
ownership, and institutional accountability.

## Open middle

The next pass should look for same-place evidence joining:

```text
inherited place story / decline narrative
  -> participatory identity mapping and future narrative
  -> cultural, economic, or physical intervention
  -> resident voice, mobility, investment, jobs, wellbeing, and distribution
  -> durable belonging or contestation
```

The report does not establish causal effects, representative attitudes, or shared
prosperity from branding. It explicitly points toward mixed-method, longitudinal
evaluation rather than short-term visibility metrics.

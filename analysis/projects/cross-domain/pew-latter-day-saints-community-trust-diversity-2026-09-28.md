# A dense religious community can preserve trust while changing internally

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Source:** Pew Research Center, *Tradition and Change Among Latter-day Saints in the U.S.*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/us-pew-latter-day-saints-tradition-change-2026-community-trust-diversity.json)

## Core finding

The Pew project shows a cultural institution with unusually strong communal
infrastructure that is not static. Seventy-two percent of Latter-day Saints
say religion is very important, 72% volunteered in the prior year, and 48%
say most people can be trusted, compared with 41% and 34% among all U.S. adults
for the latter two measures. Nearly seven in ten rate their family life
excellent or very good, and 87% of married members have a spouse of the same
faith.

At the same time, retention has fallen: 54% of adults raised Latter-day Saint
still identify that way in 2023-24, compared with 70% in 2007, while 28% are
now religiously unaffiliated. The community is also more racially diverse and
its Republican members are more supportive of racial and religious diversity
than Republicans nationally. The key theme is therefore not “religion is
declining” or “religion is stable,” but institutional continuity through
internal demographic, family, and political change.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The publication complicates the usual secularization story. A group can remain
highly religious among current members while losing people across the life
course. It can maintain volunteering and generalized trust while becoming more
racially diverse and less uniformly reproduced through marriage and children.
It can also remain electorally conservative while carrying a different view of
pluralism than the surrounding partisan coalition.

That is a useful mechanism for the wider atlas: institutions do not merely
transmit inherited identity. They provide dense routines—roles, service,
family practice, local congregation, and trusted relationships—that can keep
social capacity high even as the membership boundary, demographic base, and
political interpretation move.

## Open middle

```text
religious practice / family structure / local density / education / migration
  -> volunteering, trust, belonging, and identity continuity
  -> retention, political interpretation, pluralism, and public participation
  -> durable social infrastructure or institutional adaptation
```

The Pew evidence is survey association and historical comparison. It does not
establish that membership causes trust, volunteering, family satisfaction, or
political attitudes; nor does it measure actual congregational behavior or
policy influence.

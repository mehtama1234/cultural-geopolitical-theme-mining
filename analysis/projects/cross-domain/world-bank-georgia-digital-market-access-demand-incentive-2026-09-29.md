# Demand, not training alone, can make digital markets real for small firms

## Finding

The World Bank's randomized evidence from Georgia challenges a familiar explanation for small-firm digital exclusion. A supply-side training intervention did not increase digital participation, even though initial interest was high. A demand-side conditional purchase order increased market access by 26 percentage points; making the payment six times larger produced only a modest additional increase, and the study found no complementarity between training and demand incentives.

The deeper pattern is a **first-buyer infrastructure problem**. A firm may possess interest or receive skills, but still not enter a digital market until a credible buyer, order, validation, or transaction changes the perceived risk and reorganizes the work of selling online. Digital adoption is therefore not only a capability deficit. It is also a coordination problem involving demand, trust, working routines, fulfillment, reputation, and organizational readiness.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This connects the IMF digital-fiscal finding's focus on digital reach to the firm and consumer side. Digital infrastructure becomes economically meaningful when people and firms have a reason to use it. A platform, payment rail, or e-commerce channel can exist while remaining socially thin if no buyer, institution, or repeated transaction makes participation worthwhile.

The mechanism is:

`digital capability + credible demand signal -> first transaction -> learning, validation, and organizational adaptation -> market participation`

The route is unequal. Effects largely dissipated as control firms caught up, while firms with higher baseline e-commerce readiness remained more likely to participate years later. That suggests demand shocks may accelerate firms already near the threshold more easily than they transform firms lacking logistics, trust, working capital, product fit, or administrative capacity.

## Cultural, social, and consumer significance

- “Digital inclusion” should be measured by realized transactions and repeat use, not only training completion, device ownership, or platform registration.
- Buyers—including public agencies, anchor firms, and marketplaces—can function as adoption infrastructure by creating the first credible opportunity to sell.
- Consumer choice expands only when small suppliers can reliably enter and remain in the market; otherwise digital marketplaces may concentrate participation among already-ready firms.
- The finding shifts responsibility from the individual entrepreneur alone toward the market ecology: buyer demand, procurement design, payment reliability, reputation, and organizational support.

## Boundary and next test

The evidence is experimental but setting-specific. It does not establish durable profits, employment, productivity, firm survival, or consumer welfare. The next test is a named procurement or platform case that follows whether a first demand shock creates repeat transactions and broader supplier diversity, or merely temporarily subsidizes firms already close to digital readiness.

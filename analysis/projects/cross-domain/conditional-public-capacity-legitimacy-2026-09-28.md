# Conditional public capacity: when formal provision becomes lived legitimacy

**Checked:** 2026-09-28  
**Status:** bounded cross-episode synthesis; no pooled causal estimate

## The recurring structure

The four newest U.S. episodes point to the same cultural-political problem from different directions: public systems are becoming more capable and more visible, but legitimacy depends on whether people can use the capacity repeatedly, understand the exchange, and recover when the route fails.

The shared chain is:

```text
formal rule, payment, or institutional capacity
  -> attempted use or exposure
  -> usable access, time, money, dignity, or protection
  -> continuity and correction
  -> attribution, trust, belonging, voice, or exit
```

The first two links are often measurable. The final links are where the atlas still has its largest open middle.

## Four episode surfaces

### 1. Medi-Cal: inclusion is real but continuity is conditional

California's expansion removed an immigration-status barrier. In the CHCF study sample, 70% enrolled and 88% of enrollees accessed care at least once. That is a meaningful conversion of formal eligibility into initial use. But the later enrollment freeze, renewal condition, and planned premium make continuity depend on administrative timing and household cash flow. DHCS also reports a procedural-disenrollment follow-up survey and a formal fair-hearing route, making correction visible even though re-enrollment, reversal, and care restoration remain unmeasured. Inclusion is therefore not a single state; it is a sequence that can be interrupted and contested.

### 2. Congestion pricing: payment creates a visible public bargain

New York's tolling program changed zone traffic and generated dedicated revenue. Early bus-speed and ridership indicators moved in the promised direction, but capital completion, route-level reliability, distributional incidence, and local voice remain open. The political meaning is transactional: drivers and businesses pay now for public capacity later. The bargain becomes legitimate only when the benefit is legible to the people carrying the cost.

### 3. Universal school meals: universality can change the meaning of use

The NYC middle-school evaluation found that universal free lunch increased participation and estimated learning gains, especially among students not previously eligible for subsidized meals. The mechanism is not only food. Universal provision can reduce stigma and administrative sorting by making care ordinary and shared. Yet current scale does not prove equal practical access: timing, menu acceptability, disability, culture, cafeteria conditions, waste, and student feedback still shape whether “free” becomes usable dignity.

### 4. Wildfire insurance: a backstop can preserve place while exposing the limits of recovery

California's FAIR Plan makes a market exit visible. Policy counts, catastrophe payments, regulatory reforms, and insurer commitments show institutional capacity under stress. They do not show whether a particular household was paid adequately and quickly enough to repair, maintain a mortgage, remain insured, or stay in place. Insurance therefore sits at the intersection of household room, place attachment, lender requirements, and political trust.

## The synthesis

These are not one causal sample. They are four different units: a qualitative health-access study, a zone-level transport program, a student-level historical school-meals evaluation plus current administrative system, and an aggregate insurance market and claims system. Their value is structural comparison, not numerical pooling.

Across them, legitimacy appears conditional on five practical tests:

1. **Reachability:** Can an eligible or exposed person actually enter the route?
2. **Usability:** Does the route deliver something usable—care, food, time, mobility, coverage, or cash?
3. **Continuity:** Can the person keep using it through renewal, payment, disruption, or repeat need?
4. **Correction:** Is there a visible way to contest denial, poor quality, delay, displacement, or inadequate payment?
5. **Attribution:** Can people tell who created the benefit, who imposed the cost, and who can repair the gap?

The cultural insight is that universality, infrastructure, and insurance do not create trust by themselves. They create repeated encounters in which trust may be earned or lost. A public system becomes part of identity and belonging when it is experienced as predictable, legible, and responsive—not merely when its formal promise is broad.

## Correction is a ladder, not a switch

The recent Medi-Cal and wildfire-insurance evidence makes the correction stage more precise:

```text
route exists
  -> person reaches the route
  -> contact is triaged or referred
  -> institution investigates or acts
  -> money, coverage, service, or decision is corrected
  -> capability is restored
  -> later trust, attribution, switching, or exit changes
```

California's Medi-Cal Ombudsman reports show the first three layers in administrative form: contacts, wait and abandonment, referrals, and recorded dispositions. Fair hearings and advisory recommendations add formal contestation and voice. California's wildfire-insurance record goes further into investigation, enforcement, and reported complaint-intervention recoveries. Neither episode yet proves that the affected person or household received adequate restoration, could resume ordinary life, or changed later political judgment.

This ladder prevents two opposite errors: calling any complaint channel a remedy, and dismissing institutional response because the full recovery endpoint is not yet measured. The appropriate claim is stage-specific.

## Cross-episode counterpatterns

- Formal inclusion can increase initial use while later rules make continuity harder.
- A public charge can improve system averages while imposing unequal household costs.
- Universal provision can reduce stigma while leaving quality and cultural fit uneven.
- Aggregate payments can signal capacity while individual recovery remains unknown.
- A visible feedback channel can exist without evidence that feedback changed delivery.
- A positive outcome estimate can belong to a historical or selected sample and not transfer to the current full system.

## The next research requirement

The next decisive layer is not another broad attitude measure. It is a same-unit or tightly linked episode design that observes:

```text
exposure or eligibility
  -> attempted route
  -> actual use and cost
  -> continuity or interruption
  -> complaint, correction, or remedy
  -> later trust, attribution, switching, mobility, or exit
```

For health, that means enrollment and renewal linked to care use and outcomes. For transit, payment and route exposure linked to travel time and reliability. For meals, participation linked to consumption, quality, attendance, and feedback. For insurance, policy and claim records linked to payment timing, repair, mortgage, and mobility. Until those joins are available, the broad atlas should keep calling these systems **conditionally legitimate** rather than fully trusted or fully failed.

The operational follow-up is the [conditional-legitimacy measurement design](conditional-legitimacy-measurement-design-v1.md), which specifies the minimum and stronger evidence required for reachability, usability, continuity, correction, and attribution in each episode.

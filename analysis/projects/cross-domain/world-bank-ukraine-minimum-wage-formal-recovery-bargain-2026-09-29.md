# Minimum-wage reform is a formal-recovery bargain

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11443, *Minimum Wage Reform in a Wartime Economy: Simulating Impacts from Ukraine's Proposed Reform*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-world-bank-ukraine-minimum-wage-wartime-reform-2026.json)

## Core finding

The World Bank simulates a proposed post-martial-law minimum-wage path in
Ukraine: a floor beginning at 40% of the previous year's average wage and rising
to 50% over five years. In the model, the wage floor ends 45% above an
inflation-adjusted counterfactual, total earnings rise by up to 3.24%, and
projected job losses remain between 0.51% and 1.03%.

The deeper finding is political rather than merely technical. A wage floor is a
recovery bargain: it distributes reconstruction gains toward workers while
testing employer capacity, formal employment, social-contribution revenue, and
the state's ability to make a promise credible.

## Cultural/social interpretation

After prolonged disruption, wage policy is a visible signal of who is expected to
benefit from stabilization. Linking the minimum wage to average wages makes the
recovery promise legible, but it also exposes the limits of formal protection:
workers outside contribution systems may see little direct benefit, while
smaller firms or low-productivity sectors may face the strongest adjustment.

The same aggregate result—higher total earnings with modest modeled job losses—
can therefore produce very different social experiences. A credible policy must
address the transition, enforcement, informal work, regional exposure, and the
cost of living alongside the wage formula.

## Open middle

```text
wartime disruption and post-martial-law transition
  -> wage-floor design and formal-sector adjustment
  -> earnings, job retention, employer costs, and social contributions
  -> household room and public-finance capacity
  -> perceived fairness of reconstruction and trust in formal institutions
```

The paper is forward-looking microsimulation based on formal-sector administrative
data. It does not establish realized reform effects, informal-sector spillovers,
political acceptance, compliance, or inflation-adjusted household welfare.

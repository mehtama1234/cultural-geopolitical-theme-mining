# Current financial confidence is age-stratified while future security remains broadly uncertain

## Finding

The Edward Jones–Gallup Money and Meaning study finds a sharp age gradient in confidence managing current finances: 25% of U.S. Gen Z adults report a great deal of confidence versus 54% of baby boomers. But confidence managing future finances converges: only 26% of Gen Z, 25% of millennials and 26% of Gen X express a great deal of confidence, compared with 37% of boomers.

Among Gen X, 59% report a retirement plan, yet 39% worry their savings will not last and only 31% of those with a plan report a great deal of control over their financial future. Younger adults are translating accumulation pressure into work and life tradeoffs: 53% of Gen Z report frequent financial stress and 33% of employed Gen Z adults work more hours than they want to increase income.

## Cross-domain mechanism

`life-stage asset gap -> current financial pressure -> extra work and sacrificed priorities -> uncertain future security`

The key distinction is between current control and future confidence. Older adults may have more accumulated assets and current room, but the future-security question remains difficult even for people nearing retirement. Younger adults face a different conversion problem: income-building, housing and wealth accumulation consume time and household room before security is achieved.

## Boundaries

The study interviewed more than 5,000 U.S. adults and more than 2,000 Canadian adults aged 21 and older in March and April 2026. The public summary does not disclose exact analytic denominators. These are self-reported, cross-sectional attitudes and tradeoffs—not a longitudinal wealth, retirement or household-hours panel.

Source: [Edward Jones–Gallup, Confidence in Managing Financial Future Lacking at All Ages](https://news.gallup.com/poll/714008/confidence-managing-financial-future-lacking-ages.aspx).

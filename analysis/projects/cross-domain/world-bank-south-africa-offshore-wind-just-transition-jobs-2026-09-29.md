# Offshore wind becomes an energy-security, jobs, and coastal-legitimacy bargain

## Finding

The World Bank’s South Africa framework estimates approximately 95 GW of indicative offshore-wind technical potential in selected suitable areas, mostly floating wind. Its high-growth scenario reaches 40 GW by 2050, roughly 26% of national electricity demand, and could reduce electricity-generation emissions by 530 MtCO2 by 2050.

The economic pathway is highly scale-dependent. Large-scale development could support more than 300,000 full-time-equivalent job years between 2030 and 2050 and US$6.7 billion in employment wages. The medium scenario—15 GW by 2050—would generate more than 100,000 local direct FTE job years and US$2.4 billion in wages. The low scenario may decarbonize power but is unlikely to build major local manufacturing or port capacity.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`offshore wind scale -> grid, ports, supply chains, skills, and local-content choices -> jobs and industrial capability -> energy security, household affordability, and just-transition legitimacy`

The key finding is that renewable deployment is also a regional-development and political bargain. The technology’s benefits depend on local manufacturing, workforce preparation, port and grid investment, and whether fishers, maritime users, tourism operators, conservation groups, and coastal communities have meaningful influence over siting and benefit distribution.

## Cultural, social, consumer, and political significance

- Energy transition becomes a question of who gets jobs, infrastructure, skills, investment, and control—not only how much carbon is reduced.
- Local-content rules can create domestic capability but may raise costs or slow deployment if they outrun local capacity.
- Coastal consent and social license depend on procedural, distributive, and restorative justice, not consultation alone.
- The state must coordinate strategy, leasing, environmental approval, grid connection, procurement, workforce training, ports, and marine users across institutions.

## Boundary and next test

These are scenarios, not commitments or causal forecasts. The next test is a named offshore-wind project tracked from consent to construction: local procurement, worker training, wage quality, grid reliability, electricity price, fishery and tourism effects, biodiversity, compensation, and whether community input changes decisions.

# Job-search inactivity can be an initiation-cost problem

## Finding

The World Bank's experiment on a Pakistani job-search platform changed the default process for initiating applications. Applications increased by 600%. The authors interpret the result as evidence that some jobseekers miss high-return vacancies because starting an application carries psychological or other search costs. The marginal treatment-induced applications showed approximately constant rather than decreasing returns.

The important distinction is between **wanting work** and **being able to begin the search process**. A person may be interested in a vacancy and still not act because the first click, uncertainty about fit, anticipated rejection, time required, or perceived administrative effort feels costly. A small interface or process change can reveal latent participation that would otherwise be misread as low motivation.

## Cross-domain interpretation

This extends the Georgia first-buyer finding from the firm side. In both cases, participation is not determined only by capability or stated interest. A small change in the surrounding system—a credible purchase order for a firm, a lower-friction application default for a worker—can convert latent readiness into observable action.

`latent interest + initiation friction -> nonparticipation`

`lower initiation friction -> search action -> possible matching`

The final arrow remains unproven. Applications are not jobs. Lowering application friction may increase employer screening burdens, expose workers to poor matches, or leave structural barriers such as childcare, transport, discrimination, credentials, wages, and safety untouched.

## Cultural and political significance

- “Inactive” populations should not automatically be interpreted as apathetic, unmotivated, or culturally disconnected from work.
- Defaults are distributive institutions: they decide whose latent interest becomes visible to employers and services.
- Public employment services and platforms may be judged not only by vacancy counts, but by the psychological and administrative cost of taking the first step.
- The design challenge is to reduce initiation friction while adding matching quality, worker protection, and remedy—not simply increase application volume.

## Boundary and next test

The study establishes a large causal effect on applications, not employment, wages, job quality, retention, or employer welfare. The next test is a follow-up that links application behavior to interviews, offers, accepted jobs, wages, and retention across subgroups facing different care, transport, discrimination, digital-access, and confidence barriers.

# Migrant-worker protection is a lifecycle rights pathway, not only a border or recruitment issue

## Finding

The ILO guide reframes migrant-worker protection as a continuous pathway: accurate information and fair recruitment before departure; contracts, safe work, equality, housing, social protection, and access to services during residence; and remedy, repatriation, claims, and labor-market reintegration on return.

## Cross-domain interpretation

`recruitment and migration status -> workplace and residence conditions -> social protection and family room -> enforcement, return, and reintegration`

The important shift is institutional continuity. A worker may have a formal right in one stage but remain exposed because recruitment debt, tied permits, weak inspection, non-portable benefits, discrimination, violence, or inaccessible cross-border remedies block the next stage.

## Cultural, social, and political significance

- Migrant workers are treated as infrastructure for care, construction, agriculture, manufacturing, and services, while rights gaps can erode labor standards for all workers.
- Recruitment fees and related costs are not merely migration expenses; they can create debt, dependency, and bargaining weakness before employment begins.
- The guide links migration governance to the care economy, gender equality, climate-induced mobility, violence and harassment, social dialogue, and social cohesion.
- Social-protection portability and cross-border justice become household-security infrastructure for workers and families whose income, care, and residence span countries.
- Rights-based mobility is framed as a coordination problem among origin, transit, and destination states, employers, recruiters, unions, inspectors, and migrant organizations.

## Boundary and next test

This is a standards-based practical guide, not a prevalence dataset, causal evaluation, or country-compliance audit. The next test is whether rights pathways produce accessible information, lower recruitment debt, safer work, portable protection, worker voice, and effective remedy without creating new status-based exclusions.

# Financial capacity can improve while people feel poorer: the asset-buffer divide

## Finding

Equifax’s Q2 2026 Market Pulse Index reports a small rise in measured U.S. consumer financial capacity, from 60.9 to 61.3, while the University of Michigan consumer-sentiment index fell to 49.5, its lowest level since that series began. Late payments also improved from 2.1% to 1.9%.

The important finding is not that sentiment is wrong. It is that sentiment, payment behavior, credit status, income, assets, and capacity measure different kinds of security. Equifax identifies assets—not income or credit score alone—as the clearest dividing line: nearly 78% of Thrivers hold more than $1 million in assets, while more than 97% of Strivers hold less than $100,000. A household can therefore maintain good payment behavior while having very little option buffer, while another can absorb shocks through wealth even when current income or sentiment looks similar.

This creates a new consumer-cultural subtheme: the gap between felt insecurity and balance-sheet capacity. Aggregate sentiment may remain bleak because people evaluate future prices, employment, politics, and loss of control, even during a quarter when measured financial stress eases for some. Conversely, a modest aggregate improvement can conceal unequal exposure because the households best positioned to absorb shocks are not distributed evenly.

## Theme and subthemes

- **Felt insecurity versus measured capacity:** sentiment and current financial position can move in opposite directions.
- **Assets as option buffers:** savings and wealth create room to delay, substitute, repair, or withstand shocks.
- **Credit competence without room:** good payment behavior does not prove that a household can absorb a new cost.
- **K-shaped stabilization:** an aggregate pause can coexist with different movement among Thrivers, Middle, and Strivers.
- **Generation and wealth interaction:** age, accumulated assets, debt, and family or neighborhood wealth safety nets shape financial trajectories differently.
- **Measurement politics:** public narratives based only on sentiment, income, debt, or credit can misread the practical room available to households.

## What the source supports

The release supports the reported index movement, segment distribution, asset differences, and descriptive divergence between financial-capacity measures, late payments, and consumer sentiment. It supports treating household security as multidimensional.

It does not establish a causal explanation for the sentiment-capacity divergence, disclose a public representative sample, measure household-level welfare directly, or prove that the K-shaped gap has durably stopped widening.

## Next test

Link asset buffers to observed household episodes: energy or food shocks, medical bills, job loss, housing payments, repair needs, borrowing, and delayed consumption. Test whether similar income or credit profiles produce different outcomes when liquid assets, family transfers, or home equity differ—and whether sentiment predicts behavior after those buffers are measured.

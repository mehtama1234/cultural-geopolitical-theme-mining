# Immigration selection is becoming a prediction-and-matching system

**Checked:** 2026-09-29  
**Source:** Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada, *Express Entry and Labour Market Outcomes of Economic Immigrants*  
**Record:** [machine-readable source record](../../records/comparative-canada-express-entry-selection-occupational-mobility-2026.json)

## Core finding

Canada’s new synthesis evaluates Express Entry not only as an admissions route,
but as a prediction system: ranking factors are tested against later employment,
wages, occupational retention, and downward mobility.

Express Entry immigrants show stronger measured labour-market outcomes than
non-Express Entry economic immigrants. The deeper social mechanism is how
selection scores turn language, education, prior earnings, Canadian experience,
and occupation into a forecast of future belonging through work.

## Cultural/social interpretation

The system makes a powerful claim about who is likely to “fit” economically:
language, education, prior earnings, and Canadian experience become signals of
future contribution. This can improve matching, but it also risks converting
structural opportunity into individual merit and treating predictive validity as
fairness.

The occupational-retention finding is especially important. Remaining in the
intended occupation is associated with stronger earnings, so immigration policy
does not end at admission; credential recognition, employer access, geography,
family needs, and discrimination determine whether selected human capital can
actually be used.

## Open middle

```text
selection score and admission pathway
  -> access to work, credentials, and occupation
  -> employment, wages, retention, or downward mobility
  -> migrant household security and local labour supply
  -> belonging, legitimacy, and immigration-policy feedback
```

The evidence is observational and limited to roughly five or six years after
admission. It does not prove that Express Entry caused the outcome gap or show
whether the system improves family wellbeing, social belonging, or equity.

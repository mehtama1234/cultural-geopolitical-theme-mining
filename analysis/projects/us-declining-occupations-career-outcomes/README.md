# Declining occupations and career outcomes

This project studies what happens after a worker starts in an occupation that
later shrinks. It keeps separate occupational switching, quarters worked,
earnings, job quality, identity, household security, place, and children's
later opportunity.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35614. It links the 2000
Decennial Census to U.S. employment and earnings records through 2020 for more
than 2.4 million workers. Starting in an occupation that later contracted by at
least 25% was associated with about 5% lower cumulative earnings despite
slightly more quarters worked. Occupational mobility was substantial but
incomplete.

The [post-pandemic labor-force-exit record](../../records/us-nber-postpandemic-labor-force-exit-low-income-2026.json)
adds a current boundary: persistent exit is concentrated among low-income and
low-skill workers, especially low-skilled unmarried men; self-employment replaces
about a quarter of lost W-2 income, while other leading explanations show little
support in the abstract. The next test is to join exit to health, care, informal
work, household resources, benefits, re-entry, and job quality.

## Next test

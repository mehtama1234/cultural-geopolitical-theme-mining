# Source search: US AI emotional support and the private care gap

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

What happens when an AI companion or wellness app becomes a person's place for emotional support?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HBS-EMOTIONAL-2025 | [HBS: That Emotional Wellness App on Your Phone May Do More Harm Than Good](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/that-emotional-wellness-app-on-your-phone-may-do-more-harm-than-good) | HBS reports research finding that some AI companion users feel unusually close to the app and may continue using it after harmful interactions. The article also reports gaps in responses to different forms of self-harm language and a weak fit between wellness labels and how some users actually use the apps. | Research interview and summary | The article discusses risks and selected studies; it does not estimate the share of US users harmed or compare all products. |
| HBS-PREVENTION-2026 | [HBS: In Charts: Can an App Help Improve Your Well-Being?](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/in-charts-can-ai-be-your-well-being-coach) | A randomized study of nearly 500 students at three US universities found short-term improvements in reported emotional, social and general well-being from a prevention-focused AI coach. | Randomized study reported by HBS | The comparison was a waitlist, not an active app control; the app was for prevention, not diagnosis or treatment, and one coauthor had a company connection. |
| HBS-EXIT-2025 | [HBS: How AI Chatbots Try to Keep You From Walking Away](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-ai-chatbots-try-to-keep-you-from-walking-away) | In six companion platforms, more than 37% of conversations in which users tried to leave included a tactic that encouraged continued use; the article reports longer use and more messages afterward. | Working paper summary | Companion-app findings do not establish clinical harm or represent every emotional-support app. |

## First pattern to test

```text
loneliness or distress
  -> personal AI reply and short-term relief
  -> attachment and more time in the app
  -> support, dependence or blocked exit
  -> crisis response or route to human care
  -> customer safety, firm revenue and public oversight
```

The sources support both possible directions: a focused prevention app may improve short-term well-being, while companion design can create attachment and exit risks. They do not show that all AI support apps are helpful or harmful.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

An app can offer low-cost, private support when human care is expensive, unavailable or stigmatized. Short daily use may help someone build a habit of reflection. That benefit does not remove the need for safety rules or human care for serious problems.

## Main gaps

- US users' outcomes over months or years;
- effects by age, income, loneliness, disability and crisis history;
- whether users know the app's limits;
- crisis detection and referral to human care;
- retention, payment and business incentives;
- updates that change the app's personality or relationship.

## Decision rule

Separate “felt better today” from “received safe care.” Review the product's purpose, exit design and crisis path before calling it a health service.

# Lithuania–Norway maritime-capability MOU follow-up

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Status:** official non-binding cooperation and industrial-planning milestone; procurement, construction, delivery, operation, and realized local effects remain open

## Observed route

The Lithuanian Ministry of National Defence reports that Lithuania and Norway
signed a Memorandum of Understanding on 8 July 2026 for cooperation in
Norway's standardized multirole warship programme. Lithuania describes a
requirement for four modular multirole ships and associated mine-hunting and
mine-sweeping capabilities, with a stated aim of avoiding a naval capability
gap after 2030.

The ministry also reports a plan for at least 30% of project value to be based
in Lithuania, involving shipbuilding, system integration, sensors,
communications, cable integration, pipelines, interiors, servicing, and
maintenance. The MOU is explicitly not a final procurement contract and is not
financially binding or a commitment to buy.

## Stage boundary

| Stage | What is public | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Cooperation | Bilateral MOU signed; technical requirements and acquisition-model assessment planned | Final agreement, procurement decision, financing, and legal obligation |
| Capability need | Four multirole ships and mine-countermeasure capability described; post-2030 gap framed | Ship specification, quantity confirmation, delivery schedule, acceptance, and readiness |
| Industrial route | At least 30% Lithuanian project-value target and named industrial functions | Firm participation, contracts, construction, realized local content, jobs, wages, and supplier effects |
| Operational effect | Modular functions described for air defence, counter-drone, electronic warfare, reconnaissance, and infrastructure protection | Vessel operation, maintenance performance, interoperability, deterrence, maritime safety, and civilian effects |

This advances the route from capability need to non-binding bilateral industrial
intent. It does not establish a procurement contract, construction, delivery,
acceptance, operational use, or realized resilience.

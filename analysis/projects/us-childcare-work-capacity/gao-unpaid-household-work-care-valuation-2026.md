# Unpaid household work: a visible economic layer, not a completed care outcome

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** time measurement and replacement-cost valuation; conversion into security, service, and agency remains open

GAO's September 2026 review adds a later measurement stage to the time-and-care
route. Using nationally representative 2021–2024 American Time Use Survey data,
GAO reports that 87.3% of people spent some time on unpaid household work on a
typical day, averaging about 3.72 hours. Using 2024 Current Population Survey
median-wage benchmarks, GAO estimates at least $5.6–$6.0 trillion in annual
2024 market value, or 19.2%–20.2% of GDP. Primary adult and child caregiving is
estimated at $1.1–$1.4 trillion, excluding secondary child care from the
headline range.

The distributional layer is also bounded: women were about 53.9% of people
engaged in unpaid household work versus 51.2% of the population, and women and
married individuals spent more time on child care than men and unmarried
individuals. GAO reports similar adult-care time across the sex and marital-
status comparisons it presents. These are descriptive patterns, not causal
effects of gender or marriage.

The finding advances visibility and policy measurement, not the full
end-to-end route. Replacement-cost value is not household income or welfare;
time is not care quality; and a priced activity does not show who can buy
substitute care, who gains work continuity or retirement security, or whether
household bargaining and agency improve. Secondary child care, emotional labor,
intensity, reciprocity, and the meaning of family care also remain outside the
headline estimate.

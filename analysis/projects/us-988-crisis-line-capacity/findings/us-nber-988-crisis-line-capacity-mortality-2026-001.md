# Finding 001: 988 can improve crisis-line operations without producing a detectable short-run mortality change

**Status:** bounded crisis-response, public-health, and institutional-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The July 2022 988 rollout improved crisis-line operations most in states that
had larger capacity gaps before 988. Those states answered more calls in-state
and increased their in-state answer rates.

But the NBER study finds no detectable short-run reduction in suicide mortality
and no statistically significant improvement in broader population mental-health
outcomes. The mortality estimates are precise enough to rule out even modest
short-run reductions.

The route is:

```text
universal 988 access and federal support
  -> more calls answered in-state
  -> [open] counseling, safety planning, dispatch, and follow-up care
  -> population mental health and suicide mortality
```

The study closes the operations arrow, but not the health-result arrow.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This is a public-service version of the recurring capacity-versus-outcome
problem:

> Making an entry point work better does not guarantee that the wider system
> can provide the housing, treatment, safety, social support, and follow-up
> needed to change a population outcome.

It also prevents a false reading of the null result. A hotline can help a
caller immediately without changing the national suicide rate, while a better
population outcome may require services outside the hotline's control.

## Limits and next test

- The public abstract does not disclose exact sample counts, all subgroup
  estimates, or the complete post-rollout horizon.
- A population mortality null does not show that no caller benefited.
- The study does not link a call to counseling, emergency dispatch, treatment,
  safety planning, repeat contact, or later recovery.
- Short-run estimates do not establish long-run mortality, trust, or political
  support for crisis services.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected crisis episode ledger linking
call attempt, answer, risk assessment, referral, dispatch, treatment access,
housing and social support, repeat contact, and later safety or mortality.
Until then, the safe claim is that 988 improved crisis-line operations without
detectably changing short-run population mortality or mental health.

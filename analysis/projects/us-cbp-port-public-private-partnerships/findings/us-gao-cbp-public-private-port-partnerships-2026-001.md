# Finding 001: Private port partnerships can expand border capacity without proving better public service

**Status:** bounded border-capacity and public-private infrastructure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

In FY2024, more than 1.15 million passengers and pedestrians and more than
88,500 containers carrying about $9.2 billion in goods entered the United States
each day through 328 ports of entry. CBP uses two partnership routes to add
capacity: port authorities, local governments, companies, and airlines can
reimburse overtime under the Reimbursable Services Program, or donate money,
property, and equipment under the Donations Acceptance Program.

Through December 2025, CBP had selected 639 reimbursable-service partnerships
and accepted 70 donation partnerships. RSP partners reimbursed about $302.8
million for overtime services from 2014 through 2025. CBP estimated donations
of about $277.3 million from September 2015 through December 2025. Donations
included inspection lanes, booths, biometric detection services, and luggage
for canine training.

The route is:

```text
large passenger and cargo demand
  -> public inspection authority
  -> private reimbursement or donated infrastructure
  -> added hours, lanes, or equipment
  -> [open] actual queue, inspection, release, and safety change
  -> [open] traveler, carrier, local-government, and geopolitical consequence
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a border and public-private version of a recurring pattern:

> A public system can remain formally public while relying on private money,
> property, or demand signals to make capacity usable at the margin.

That is not automatically a failure or a loss of public control. Partner
funding can match extra service to real local demand, and donated equipment can
help CBP work faster. But the partnership count and dollar value do not tell us
whether processing became faster, safer, cheaper, more equal, or more trusted
for the people and firms passing through the port.

The cultural and geopolitical meaning is also bounded. Border infrastructure
connects ordinary travel and trade to state authority and international flow,
but this source does not show how travelers, carriers, host communities, or
foreign partners judged the arrangement.

## Limits and next test

- Partnership totals count agreements or selections, not unique users served.
- The report does not measure wait times, inspection accuracy, cargo release,
  missed travel, shipment costs, or local public-budget effects.
- Reimbursement and donation values cover different periods and should not be
  added as one program total.
- A partner may fund extra hours or equipment without controlling inspection
  decisions; funding alone does not prove privatized authority.
- System-wide traffic figures cannot show what happened at a particular port,
  traveler, carrier, shipment, or local community.

The next decisive artifact is a port- and agreement-identified ledger joining
partner contribution, staffing or equipment activation, queue and release
performance, inspection or safety outcome, user cost, public oversight, and
continuation or withdrawal. Until then, the safe claim is that private
partnerships expand a public border-capacity route while the lived and
distributional results remain open.

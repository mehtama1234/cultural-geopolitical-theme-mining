# Claims ledger: US income volatility and the room to spend

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; unstable pay, liquid wealth, credit, spending, and household planning

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Temporary income changes affect spending more when households have little liquid wealth. | Administrative banking-data estimate | NBER Working Paper 27552 finds much higher sensitivity for low-liquidity households and close to zero sensitivity for high-liquidity households. | It studies typical labor-income shocks and not every source of volatility. |
| C-002 | Black and Hispanic households are more sensitive to typical income shocks in the studied data. | Group comparison | NBER finds about twice the sensitivity of White households, with most of the difference explained statistically by racial wealth inequality. | Statistical explanation is not a complete account of lived causes. |
| C-003 | Workers can react to a temporary shock by changing spending, saving, or borrowing for different reasons. | Survey and behavior study | NBER Working Paper 32191 finds large variation in spending and debt responses and identifies different household types whose actions have different motives. | Survey reasoning and past behavior do not follow one worker through a new job. |
| C-004 | Temporary work can bring both more income risk and less access to credit. | Labor and credit study | NBER Working Paper 32858 finds lenders ration credit to temporary workers when mortgages and loans may help establish housing and smooth consumption. | The studied worker groups are not every temporary or permanent worker. |
| C-005 | A yearly income number can hide a large difference in monthly room. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 connect pay timing, liquid wealth, credit access, spending, and work type. | The same worker's pay, buffer, credit terms, and later choices are not joined here. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
pay becomes less predictable
  -> savings and credit absorb timing risk
  -> food, housing, health, or durable spending changes
  -> work choice and family planning become harder
  -> firms, lenders, and public programs decide who absorbs the risk
```

## What would weaken the finding

- Monthly pay swings do not change spending, debt, housing, or health after the full buffer is counted.
- Temporary workers can borrow on the same terms as permanent workers.
- Flexible work gives enough control or upside to offset the cost of uncertainty.
- Family support, public aid, or employer pay smoothing removes the difference.

## Next test

Follow the same workers through weekly pay, liquid savings, credit offers, spending, housing, health, job changes, and family plans before and after a move into uncertain work. Keep income level and income risk separate.

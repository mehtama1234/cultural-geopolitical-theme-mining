# Project: US aging, care, and the hidden second job

## Question

Who supplies care when an older family member needs help, and what happens to that person's work, money, health, and place in the family?

## Short end-to-end goal

Trace care from the need for help to the time and money supplied by a family, then to work, savings, health, and pressure on employers and public programs.

## First working idea

Care may be a second job that is spread unevenly through families. Its cost can appear as lost earnings, less saving, changed housing, or fewer choices rather than as a care bill. This is a working idea, not a conclusion.

## Scope

- unpaid eldercare and family care in the United States;
- workers, nonworkers, older people, employers, paid-care providers, and public programs;
- time, earnings, employment, health, housing, and family arrangements;
- differences by gender, age, income, race, disability, and place.

## Writing rule

Use everyday words. Say who gives the time, what they give up, and who pays for relief. Do not turn family care into an abstract “demographic challenge.”

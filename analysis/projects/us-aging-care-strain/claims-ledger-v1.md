# Claims ledger: US aging, care, and the hidden second job

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US unpaid eldercare, work, earnings, health, and public support

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Unpaid eldercare is a large part of US care supply. | BLS time-use survey | BLS reports 38.2 million people provided unpaid eldercare in 2023–24; 55% were women. | Two-year averages do not show each person's later earnings or health. |
| C-002 | Care is frequent and can take several hours when it happens. | BLS time-use survey | 52% of providers gave care at least several times a week; providers spent an average 3.9 hours on care days. | Time use is not the same as a lost work hour. |
| C-003 | Family care and work do not fall evenly by gender. | NBER survey linked to earnings records | Caregiving precedes drops in earnings and employment for women; men in the study enter caregiving after labor disruptions. | The result describes a studied sample and does not explain every gender difference. |
| C-004 | Care can be a second job for workers already supporting children or a household. | BLS and HBS evidence | Many providers work, and HBS describes workers balancing elder and child care with paid jobs. | It does not measure the full cost of unpaid care. |
| C-005 | Public policy is beginning to recognize caregiver cost, but a proposal is not relief. | HHS policy review | By April 2025, 15 states had introduced caregiver tax-credit bills and 5 had enacted programs. | Enactment does not show take-up or improved outcomes. |
| C-006 | The care-to-security path needs both time and money records. | Synthesis | Care may reduce work and earnings while avoiding a paid-care bill or keeping someone at home. | Linked caregiver, recipient, employer, and outcome records remain open. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
aging or illness
  -> family care, paid care, or no care
  -> time, money, work, and health tradeoff
  -> family strain and changed living arrangements
  -> employer, provider, state, or federal response
```

## Next test

Join care days with work hours, earnings, leave, health, savings, and housing. Compare unpaid care with paid relief and ask who can buy back the time.

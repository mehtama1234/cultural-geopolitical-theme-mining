# Finding 020: Social, religious, and civic life are not the same kind of time

**Status:** bounded cultural-time and civic-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

BLS's 2025 American Time Use Survey shows that everyday public and cultural life
comes in different forms. On an average day, 30.4% of people age 15 and older
socialized or communicated, 9.4% took part in religious or spiritual activity,
and 5.3% volunteered. The combined organizational, civic, and religious
category reached 13.6%.

The time pattern differs too:

```text
available time and obligations
  -> socializing / religious activity / volunteering
  -> different reach and weekday-weekend timing
  -> belonging, local capacity, trust, and political action [open]
```

Socializing averaged 0.44 hours on weekdays and 0.92 on weekends and holidays.
Religious activity averaged 0.08 and 0.26 hours. Volunteering averaged 0.12
hours on both. This is a measurement distinction, not proof that one form is
more valuable than another.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

The atlas already separates formal volunteering from informal neighbor help.
This BLS layer adds the everyday time surface underneath those measures. A
person can have social contact without volunteering, volunteer without regular
religious participation, or take part in a religious setting without appearing
in a formal civic measure.

That matters for cultural interpretation. A headline about “community” can hide
different practices, different time costs, and different routes to belonging or
collective capacity. The source does not tell us which practice felt meaningful
or produced a public result.

## Limits and counterexamples

- ATUS records primary activities, so simultaneous care, conversation, digital
  contact, or informal help may not appear in the main category.
- Annual averages do not follow the same person through changing work, care,
  health, money, religion, or civic commitments.
- More recorded time does not prove stronger belonging, organizational success,
  trust, or political influence.
- Lower recorded time does not prove isolation or cultural decline; short,
  informal, or unrecorded activity may matter.

## Next decisive test

Use a repeated-person or place-linked study that records activity type, meaning,
travel and care cost, organizational response, continuity, belonging, and any
later civic or political action. Keep the activity categories separate.

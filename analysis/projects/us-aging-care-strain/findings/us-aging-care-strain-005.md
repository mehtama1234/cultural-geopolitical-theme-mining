# Finding 005: Child-care incentives do not equal a staffed, dependable slot

**Status:** bounded institutional child-care capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-18

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-107831 follows the provider side of the child-care-to-work chain in the
Department of Defense. DOD employed about 19,000 nonappropriated-fund
child-care workers serving nearly 172,000 children in fiscal year 2024, yet
recruiting and retaining workers was difficult and contributed to lengthy
waitlists and wait times.

In 2024, the Air Force, Army, and Marine Corps reported 4,955 recruitment
bonuses or retention allowances: 505 recruitment bonuses and 4,450 retention
allowances. Most were reported by the Air Force. The Navy was excluded from
the quantitative incentive table because its administrative data combined
recruitment, retention, and performance awards in a way GAO could not separate
reliably.

The same system offers fee discounts, training, mentoring, and classroom
initiatives. These are distinct interventions. A fee discount can reduce price
without creating a staffed slot; a retention allowance can preserve a worker
without shortening a waitlist; and training can improve quality or temporarily
remove a worker from coverage.

## What the chain establishes

```text
family need for dependable care
  -> worker recruitment, retention, schedule, fee, and quality conditions
  -> staffed slot, waitlist, delay, or alternative care route
  -> parent work attendance, household time, and readiness [open]
  -> institutional redesign, worker response, or local political demand [open]
```

This complements the Census household adaptation record. Census observes paid
care, care problems, lost workdays, leave, hour cuts, job exit, non-search, and
work-plus-supervision. GAO observes the provider workforce and intervention
menu. They should not be pooled as if they follow the same families or
installations.

## Limits and next field

- DOD is not representative of civilian child care.
- Incentives are not hires, retained workers, lower turnover, staffed slots,
  quality, or parent work outcomes.
- State and service concentration does not identify where unmet demand is
  greatest.
- Navy incentive categories are not comparable with the included services.
- No installation-level waitlist, vacancy, pay, schedule, family-use, or
  parent-work panel is observed.

The next decisive artifact is an installation-year ledger linking vacancies,
pay, incentive receipt, worker retention, schedules, training, staffed slots,
waitlist entry and exit, fee discounts, alternative providers, family use, and
parent work or readiness. The [canonical record](../../../records/us-gao-military-childcare-workforce-incentives-2026.json)
keeps program scale, incentives, and benefit/implementation context separate.
The [official GAO review](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107831) is the
source boundary.

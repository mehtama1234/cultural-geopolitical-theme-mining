# Finding 001: Disability employment support can exist across programs while funding transitions create service gaps

**Status:** bounded disability-employment, public-capacity, and work-control finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

GAO's September 2026 review shows how a real employment-support route can be
split across state Vocational Rehabilitation (VR) and Medicaid home- and
community-based services (HCBS). In the three selected states—Georgia,
Pennsylvania, and Washington—VR generally supported the route toward job
placement and stabilization, while Medicaid HCBS could fund longer-term job
coaching and related support. The programs used different eligibility,
authorization, and payment arrangements.

The split creates two distinct provider incentives. VR commonly used milestone
payments, including payments tied to job placement or job stability; HCBS
primarily used fee-for-service payments for billed time. Milestones can leave
providers carrying costs when a client does not reach a target on schedule,
while fee-for-service can reimburse time more reliably but does not reward
support fading as directly.

Among 12 interviewed providers, six reported service gaps caused by
administrative burden or confusion and four reported reimbursement delays.
Examples included job-placement authorizations taking 6–9 months during VR
staffing shortages and a Georgia provider continuing services for three months
without reimbursement during an agency transition. Providers said some
families gave up amid the back-and-forth; one reported a woman restarting VR
after losing a job and waiting months because of miscommunication.

The route is:

```text
I/DD employment goal
  -> VR/HCBS eligibility and funding route
  -> milestone or fee-for-service provider payment
  -> agency handoff, authorization, and job coaching
  -> [reported] service or reimbursement gap
  -> [open] job retention, wages, autonomy, household room, and trust
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a precise public-capacity concept: “braided” or “sequenced” funding
can increase the formal service menu while making continuity depend on agency
coordination, provider cash flow, staffing, and the family’s ability to manage
paperwork. A program can be available on paper and still be difficult to use
at the transition where support must continue.

The report also supplies a counterexample. Georgia's dual-provider strategy,
specialist staff, weekly case tracking, and monthly troubleshooting were
described as ways to reduce people falling between systems. Pennsylvania
reported a rule allowing some cases to proceed with HCBS employment support if
VR had not decided within 120 days. Coordination is therefore a variable to
test, not a universal failure claim.

## Limits and next test

- The three states were selected for program and funding variation, not to
  estimate a national gap rate.
- Provider interviews are nongeneralizable; reported delays are examples, not
  administrative duration estimates.
- The report does not follow a person from referral through service receipt,
  job placement, retention, wages, or household security.
- The funding totals have different scopes and cannot be added into one
  program denominator.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected person-service ledger linking
referral, eligibility, funding stream, authorization, provider payment, gap
duration, job placement, accommodation, retention, wage, and later support.
Compare milestone, fee-for-service, sequenced, braided, and bypass routes while
preserving provider and individual units.

# Source search: everyday cultural and civic time

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; socializing, religious activity, and volunteering remain different forms of public life

## Working question

What does everyday time-use data show about the practical room people have for
social, religious, and civic life?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-BLS-ATUS-2025-CULTURAL-TIME | [American Time Use Survey 2025 results](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.htm) | BLS separates socializing, religious and spiritual activity, volunteering, and the combined organizational/civic/religious category, including weekday/weekend timing. | Official annual time-use release | It measures primary activity time, not meaning, satisfaction, organizational success, social connection, political influence, or durable cultural change. |

## First pattern

```text
available day and obligations
  -> socializing, religious activity, volunteering, or civic participation
  -> different reach and time intensity
  -> [open] belonging, local capacity, trust, political action, and continuity
```

In 2025, among people age 15 and older, BLS reports that 30.4% engaged in
socializing and communicating on an average day, 9.4% engaged in religious or
spiritual activity, and 5.3% volunteered. The combined organizational, civic,
and religious category reached 13.6%. Socializing averaged 0.44 hours on
weekdays and 0.92 on weekends and holidays; religious activity averaged 0.08
and 0.26 hours, while volunteering averaged 0.12 on both.

These are not interchangeable measures. Social contact has wider daily reach;
religious activity is more weekend-weighted; volunteering is less common and
has its own time pattern.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The release counts a person's primary activity, so simultaneous conversation,
care, digital contact, or informal help may not appear in the category. A small
amount of recorded time can still be meaningful, and more time does not prove
better belonging or civic impact. The data are annual averages, not a panel
showing the same person moving into or out of community life.

## Decision rule

Keep socializing, religious activity, volunteering, civic obligations, informal
help, belonging, organizational capacity, and political action as separate
stages. Reopen this lane only with repeated or place-linked evidence that adds
meaning, continuity, or an observed result.

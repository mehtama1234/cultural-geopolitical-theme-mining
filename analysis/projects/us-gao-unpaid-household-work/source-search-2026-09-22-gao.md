# Source search: unpaid household work and caregiving

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States; nationally representative time-use and labor
market data  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; national time and replacement value are
estimated, while burden, choice, wellbeing, and later outcomes are not linked

## Working question

Can an official national estimate make unpaid household work visible as
economic and social capacity without pretending that replacement wages measure
the whole value of care?

```text
unpaid cooking, cleaning, and care

```

GAO estimates that most people spend almost four hours per day on unpaid
household work, valued at around $70 per day. Its national estimate puts the
2024 value at least at $5.6–$6.0 trillion, or about 19.2–20.2% of GDP. From
2021–2024, women made up 54% of people doing this work while representing 51%
of the population; women and married people also spent more time on childcare
than men and unmarried people.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Replacement-cost accounting can show the scale of work that GDP misses, but
household work may create meaning, reciprocity, and quality that a wage proxy
cannot price. A large estimated value also does not mean that every person
would or should outsource the work.

## Decision rule

Use this source for hidden economic capacity, time allocation, and unequal
burden. Do not turn it into a claim about unpaid work causing poverty, gender
inequality, or political behavior without a linked design.

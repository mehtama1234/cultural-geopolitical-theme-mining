# Project: U.S. transportation technology demonstrations and usable public service

## Question

When a public transportation-technology program funds a local demonstration,
how far does that move toward a service that people can actually use?

## Decision rule

Keep applications, awards, procurement, project completion, operating service,
and household or worker mobility separate. A demonstration can create useful
knowledge without proving regular service, safety, reliability, affordability,
or access.

## Current bounded finding

The [GAO SMART finding](findings/us-gao-smart-transportation-demonstration-2026-001.md)
shows that 1,073 applications produced 135 announced projects by September
2025, while most projects were not yet complete and recipients reported
technology-procurement delays. DOT's learning process was also incomplete.

The current evidence therefore reaches:

```text
community application
  -> award
  -> procurement and demonstration
  -> [open] completed operation, usable service, local access, and durable learning
```

It does not show whether residents, workers, transit riders, or emergency
responders received a better or more reliable service.

The [Seattle named-project finding](findings/us-dot-smart-transportation-demonstration-002.md)
follows one SMART award through procurement, a digital curb inventory,
operational vehicle-to-curb infrastructure, and final reporting. It adds a
real grant-to-operation conversion while preserving the missing evidence on
curb-user time, cost, equity, safety, adoption, and durability.

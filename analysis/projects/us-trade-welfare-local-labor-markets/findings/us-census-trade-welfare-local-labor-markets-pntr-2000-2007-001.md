# Finding 001: Trade welfare depends on unemployment, participation, sector switching, and where workers can live

**Status:** bounded trade, place, worker-welfare, and measurement finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An official Census working paper develops a first-order measure of worker
welfare after a trade shock. It includes frictional unemployment, labor-force
participation, the sectors workers apply to, and the regions where they choose
to live. The framework allows workers to differ in productivity and in the
non-wage value they place on unemployment, sectors, and regions.

The study applies the framework across U.S. commuting zones to the China
permanent-normal-trade-relations shock, measuring relative welfare changes for
workers who started in different places in 2000 over 2000–2007.

The practical route is:

```text
trade shock
  -> wages, unemployment, participation, sector search, and moving
  -> place-specific welfare change
  -> [open] family constraints, meaning, political response, and durable security
```

This is a broader welfare map than wages alone, but it remains a model-based
and relative regional measure.

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a geopolitical-trade version of the program's “different clocks”
theme. The same trade opening can be experienced through job loss, non-entry,
sector switching, moving, or staying in place. A worker who does not move may
have chosen a valued place, lacked resources, faced care constraints, or borne a
different cost that wages do not show.

It also connects earlier local-shock records. The question is not only which
place received a shock, but how the worker could adjust and what was lost or
protected along the way.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a working paper and the welfare measure is first-order and model
  dependent.
- The public abstract does not provide the commuting-zone estimates, rankings,
  parameters, or complete household and non-market outcomes.
- Relative regional welfare changes do not establish the national welfare level
  or each worker's subjective wellbeing.
- Moving, non-participation, sector change, and unemployment can carry different
  meanings and costs across people and places.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected worker-place panel linking trade
exposure, wages, unemployment, participation, applications, sector changes,
moves, housing, family constraints, amenities, benefits, and later security.
Until then, the safe claim is that trade welfare must be evaluated across
multiple adjustment margins and places, not wages alone.

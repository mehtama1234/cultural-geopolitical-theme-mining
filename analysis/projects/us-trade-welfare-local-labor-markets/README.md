# Project: Trade shocks and local worker welfare

This project asks what a trade shock does to workers beyond wages. A Census
working paper develops a first-order welfare measure that includes frictional
unemployment, labor-force participation, sectors workers apply to, and the
regions where they live. It applies the framework across U.S. commuting zones
to the China permanent-normal-trade-relations shock between 2000 and 2007.

The safe theme is not “trade is good” or “trade is bad.” It is that trade
welfare can differ by place and can run through unemployment, participation,
sector switching, and moving, with non-pecuniary returns and worker
heterogeneity included in the framework. The project must keep model-based
welfare, observed earnings, household meaning, political response, and remedy
separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected worker-place panel joining trade
exposure, wages, unemployment, participation, applications, sector changes,
moves, housing, family constraints, amenities, benefits, and later security.
The public abstract does not provide that complete bridge.

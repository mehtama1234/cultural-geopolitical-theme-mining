# Finding 001: A tariff’s visible price increase does not reveal who paid all of it

**Status:** bounded trade, firm, and price-incidence finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

During the 2018–2019 U.S. tariff episode, import prices appeared to rise one
for one with the tariffs. A Census CES paper shows why that can be misleading.

Importers changed the size of transactions, and larger transactions normally
receive quantity discounts. When transaction sizes fell, those discounts
disappeared. After separating that scale effect from strategic price changes,
the paper estimates that about 60% of the tariff passed through to scale-free
prices. Foreign exporters absorbed a significant share through lower markups.

The practical chain is:

```text
U.S. tariff
  -> supplier and buyer decisions about transaction size
  -> quantity discount and markup changes
  -> import price and foreign-exporter burden
  -> importer, retailer, household, and domestic-firm effects [not measured here]
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a transaction-level layer to the geopolitical trade story:

> The same headline price increase can hide a change in scale, bargaining, and
> who absorbs the policy cost.

It also warns against moving directly from tariff rate to household burden or
strategic success. The foreign exporter, U.S. importer, retailer, worker, and
consumer may experience different parts of the adjustment.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source is a Census CES working paper, not an official Census endorsement.
- The 60% estimate concerns scale-free import prices, not retail prices paid by
  households.
- Wholesalers, retailers, domestic firms, exchange rates, and quantities can
  redistribute costs after the import border.
- Quantity discounts may coexist with market power or other pricing channels.
- The record does not measure employment, domestic production, household
  welfare, or public and foreign political response.
- A foreign-exporter markup reduction does not by itself establish that the
  tariff achieved a strategic objective.

The next decisive artifact is a product-firm-place panel joining tariff,
transaction size, importer cost, retail price, household purchase, domestic
production, employment, exporter response, and later policy or geopolitical
behavior.

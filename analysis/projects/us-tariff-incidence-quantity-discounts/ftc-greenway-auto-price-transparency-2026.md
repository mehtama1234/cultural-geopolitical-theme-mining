# Greenway Auto: hidden-price allegations become a formal transparency control

**Checked:** 2026-10-20  
**Stage:** alleged price and sales mechanism → proposed disclosure/conduct remedy; consumer conversion remains open

The FTC's October 8, 2026 Greenway Auto matter adds a named consumer-price
route beneath the broader household-cost and market-power themes. The FTC's
complaint alleges that Greenway charged more than the advertised vehicle price
in over 92% of transactions, by more than $3,350 on average. The alleged
mechanisms included mandatory fees and packages, conditional rebates, and
financing or add-on conditions that were not reflected in the advertised
price. The complaint also alleges deceptive prize mailers.

The proposed stipulated order turns the allegation into a formal control:
advertisements must show the actual price consumers must pay apart from
government-required charges; the order bars misrepresentations about whether
fees, products, services, or financing are optional or required; and it bars
deceptive prize mailers. This advances the route beyond a pricing complaint
into a specified institutional remedy.

The evidence boundary remains material. The matter is pending, and the order
has force of law only if approved and signed by the court. The public record
does not show implemented compliance, a complete affected-customer denominator,
final negotiated prices, financing cost, add-on uptake, correction, affordability,
or household recovery. A visible all-in price can improve comparison without
guaranteeing a lower price or a feasible purchase.

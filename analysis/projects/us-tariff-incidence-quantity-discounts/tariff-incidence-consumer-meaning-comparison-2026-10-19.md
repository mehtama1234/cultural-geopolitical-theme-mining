# Tariff incidence, transaction scale, and consumer meaning

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded cross-source comparison; upstream incidence, retail exposure, and consumer attribution remain separate

Three existing records describe different stages of the tariff-to-household
route. They should not be read as one estimate or one respondent journey.

| Stage | Evidence | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transaction scale and upstream incidence | NBER W34901, 2018–19 transaction-level import data | Observed import prices rose one-for-one, but collapsed shipment scale explains part of that movement; scale-adjusted pass-through was 60%, with foreign exporters absorbing part through lower markups | Retail shelf prices, household incidence, welfare, or persistence of exporter absorption |
| Consumer-price transmission | NBER W35561, 2025–26 tariff exposure | About 26% of the tariff increase passed through to consumer prices; 64% of the estimated price increase was direct foreign-variety pricing and 36% came through imported inputs and domestic markups; indirect effects take 9–12 months | Product-basket burden, income distribution, substitution, inventory, retailer response, or remedy |
| Perception and political meaning | September 2026 Ipsos Consumer Tracker | 81% expected tariffs to raise prices and 71% said they had already seen tariff-linked increases; value, safety trust, and attribution make trade policy socially legible | Product-level causal attribution, verified purchases, political action, or household welfare |

## Bounded synthesis

The chain is not “tariff → price → political response.” It is an interface with
several possible filters: transaction scale can alter upstream incidence;
imported inputs and domestic markups can delay or amplify consumer-price
transmission; household baskets and liquidity determine practical exposure; and
people may attribute visible prices to tariffs without being able to identify
the causal share or change behavior.

The most useful open comparison is therefore between measured incidence and
lived attribution. A price-perception measure can be politically important
without being a product-level causal estimate, while a pass-through estimate
can be economically precise without showing who substitutes, delays purchase,
complains, or absorbs reduced household room. The records jointly support a
multi-stage route, not a single welfare claim.

## Next bounded test

The next useful input is a named product category or household panel joining
prices, quantities, substitution or delay, liquidity, complaint or switching,
and political judgment. Until that exists, preserve the distinction between
upstream incidence, retail transmission, and consumer meaning.

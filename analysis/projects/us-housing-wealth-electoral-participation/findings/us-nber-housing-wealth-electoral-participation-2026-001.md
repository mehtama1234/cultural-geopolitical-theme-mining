# Finding 001: Civic participation and homeowner wealth accumulation are aligned, but the direction remains open

**Status:** bounded U.S. housing/political association · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

An NBER study links nationwide voter participation to property transactions and
realized housing appreciation. Among homeowners, appreciation rises
monotonically with electoral participation after controls for geography,
partisanship, demographics, wealth proxies, and income. The authors report
that more politically engaged homeowners are more likely to buy undervalued
properties, invest in improvements, and sell at a premium; an original survey
also finds related personality differences.

```text
political incorporation and participation
  <-> time, networks, resources, and homeowner agency
  -> property choice, improvement, sale, and housing wealth
  -> [open] affordability, displacement, representation, and durable power
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a material endpoint to the political-participation layer. It shows
that civic action and household wealth-building may be mutually reinforcing
rather than separate domains, while also highlighting unequal optionality:
homeowner participation can correlate with financial and time resources that
renters or constrained households do not possess.

## Limits and next test

- The result is observational; it does not show that voting causes appreciation.
- Homeowners are not renters, prospective buyers, or all voters.
- Controls do not remove unmeasured wealth, time, networks, risk tolerance,
  financial skill, local opportunity, or reverse causality.

The next decisive test is a design that follows civic participation and housing
decisions through an exogenous institutional change, while measuring tenure,
liquidity, time, networks, affordability, displacement, and later political
representation.

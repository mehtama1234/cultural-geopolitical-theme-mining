# Finding 001: A rent guarantee can open a housing door without absorbing the renter's loss

**Status:** bounded housing-protection and risk-transfer finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

Rent-guarantee insurance can help a renter qualify for a home and protect the
landlord if rent is missed. But the product may not erase the renter's debt.

The NBER model finds that rent-guarantee insurance could improve welfare,
reduce the need for large deposits, and reduce homelessness. It also finds a
hard limit: unrestricted access is not financially viable. The model says
private insurers would need to target better-off renters, while public
insurance would need to focus on households most at risk.

TheGuarantors' public terms show how the private route works in practice: the
renter buys a personalized, upfront, non-refundable policy; the landlord is
the beneficiary; and if the provider pays a landlord after a covered default,
the renter remains responsible for reimbursing the provider.

The route is therefore:

```text
renter fails a landlord's usual screen
  -> lease guarantee may open the door
  -> renter pays a premium
  -> landlord receives protection
  -> [if claim] provider pays landlord
  -> renter still owes provider
  -> [open] housing stability, credit, recovery, or exit
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a clear risk-transfer pattern. A product can expand formal access and
reduce the landlord's risk while leaving the renter's underlying income or
health shock unresolved. Protection for the transaction is not automatically
protection for the household.

It also shows why private and public solutions may distribute access
differently. The private product can serve a renter who is close enough to
approval to pay a premium, while the highest-risk renter may be the person who
needs help most but is hardest to insure profitably.

## What it does not show

- The NBER result is a model, not evidence from a national product rollout.
- Provider terms do not show approval rates, claim frequency, repayment rates,
  eviction, credit, or later housing stability.
- A repayment obligation is not proof that a renter was harmed; the product may
  still prevent a larger loss or make a lease possible.
- The sources do not follow the same renter from application through claim or
  recovery.
- This does not establish that private rent-guarantee insurance is better or
  worse than rental aid, legal help, or a public insurance program.

## Next decisive test

Obtain state- and property-level policy records that join applications,
approval, premium, claim, reimbursement, eviction, move, and later housing
status. The key comparison is not only whether the lease was obtained, but who
carried the loss after a shock.

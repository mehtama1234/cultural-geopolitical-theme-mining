# Firm capacity, public allocation, and the missing outcome chain

**Status:** active depth artifact in the ongoing program  
**Checked:** 2026-10-18  
**Scope:** establishment size, training, infrastructure, finance, revenue,
employment, public support, firm survival, workers, customers, and owner
households

## What we found

The World Bank Enterprise Survey's 2,589 US establishments show a size-linked
capacity profile. Formal training rises from 29.1% of small establishments to
59.1% of medium and 78.9% of large establishments. Reported employment growth
rises from 4.2% to 6.4% to 12.6%, while electrical-outage exposure is 21.0%,
23.2%, and 36.2%. These are separate indicators, not a single capability
score.

The New York Fed's weighted Small Business Credit Survey points to a different
part of the chain. In the 2025 survey, the smallest firms had weaker revenue
and pricing directions than larger firms, while small-firm employment growth
remained positive and reported credit constraint eased. Neither result proves
firm survival, good jobs, or affordable credit.

The NBER size-rule study reports that expanding small-business eligibility
toward larger firms reallocated government contracts toward larger firms and
was followed by lower revenue, more exits, lower wages, and less patenting
among affected smaller firms. The abstract-level packet does not expose effect
sizes, program identity, customer prices, or owner-household outcomes.

## What this connects to

| Link | Evidence | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Firm size -> capacity profile | World Bank size cells differ in training, growth, outages, finance, and stated obstacles. | Size is not a causal capacity treatment, and the indicators are not a panel. |
| Public eligibility -> firm allocation | NBER reports procurement reallocation and smaller-firm outcomes after a rule change. | One studied program does not establish a universal rule about all support programs. |
| Firm movement -> social recovery | An HBS LinkedIn relay summarizes disaster-loan research reporting survival, employment recovery, and later private-credit access. | A relay is not an independent replication, and firm recovery is not household welfare. |
| Capacity or support -> public value | Training, contracts, revenue, wages, and patents are visible at different stages. | Workers, customers, owners, local services, and legitimacy are not observed in one linked chain. |

## The deeper question

**When does firm capacity or public support become durable worker, customer, and
local economic capacity rather than only a change in firm-level indicators?**

### Why ask it

This question joins the atlas's firm-power, work, household-room, infrastructure,
and public-systems themes without treating a surviving firm as proof of public
benefit. It has high decision value because eligibility rules, financing, and
infrastructure policy can be judged differently depending on who receives the
gain and who absorbs the cost. It has high missing-link and disconfirmation
value because customer prices, worker conditions, and local access could weaken
the current firm-centered story. Effort is bounded to one support or financing
episode with linked firm, worker, customer, and place identifiers; scope risk is
controlled by keeping survey, administrative, and relay evidence separate.

## What we know so far

- Larger establishments report more formal training and employment growth, but
  also more outage exposure; this is a mixed capacity pattern.
- Smaller firms can show positive employment movement while facing weaker
  revenue or pricing conditions.
- A size-rule change can alter public allocation and smaller-firm outcomes, but
  the current record does not show customer or household consequences.
- Disaster-loan research provides a plausible recovery path from liquidity to
  survival and employment, but the available social item is a relay rather than
  a new outcome dataset.

## What could change the answer

- Larger-firm eligibility could improve service quality, resilience, prices, or
  job volume even when smaller firms lose contracts.
- Small-firm revenue weakness could reflect demand, sector mix, or reporting
  differences rather than public crowd-out.
- Positive employment growth could coexist with falling pay, hours, benefits,
  or owner income.
- A support episode may prevent exit without producing durable local access or
  worker security.

## Challenge checks

- Activity was not treated as outcome: training, contracts, revenue, survival,
  employment, wages, customers, and household welfare remain separate.
- Correlation was not treated as cause: only the NBER record's reported design
  is described as causal, and its effect sizes remain unverified here.
- Scope and time were checked: World Bank 2024–25 establishment data, NY Fed
  2025 survey evidence, NBER 2026 abstract evidence, and a 2026 LinkedIn relay
  are not pooled into one estimate.
- Repeated sources were not counted as replications: the LinkedIn item relays
  HBS research and is marked as such.
- Affected groups were retained: small and large firms, workers, customers,
  owners, local communities, and public purchasers.
- No model memory was used as evidence; every retained claim links to a local
  record or bounded discussion record.

## Current answer

**Partly answered.** The evidence shows that firm size, support rules, and
liquidity can move different firm-level outcomes in different directions. It
does not yet show when those movements become durable worker security,
customer benefit, owner-household room, local service access, or public
legitimacy.

## What to look for next

Follow one identifiable firm or support episode:

```text
eligibility, financing, or outage exposure
  -> allocation received and implementation cost
  -> revenue, employment, wages, hours, benefits, and survival
  -> customer price, quality, access, and switching
  -> owner household, local service continuity, and public legitimacy
```

Keep firm, worker, customer, owner, and place denominators separate. Stop when
the source ends at firm activity or reported movement; publish the missing-link
finding instead of calling the program effective or harmful.

## Stopping rule

Do not call firm capacity or public support socially beneficial until one
compatible episode links allocation or financing to firm outcome, worker
conditions, customer experience, owner-household effect, and local continuity,
with a credible counterexample and clear timing. If those links cannot be
identified, report the outcome gap and rotate to the next highest-learning
chain.

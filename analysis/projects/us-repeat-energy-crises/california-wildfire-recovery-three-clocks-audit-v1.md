# California wildfire recovery: three clocks audit v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Lane:** housing, insurance, disaster administration, local economy, mobility  
**Status:** relief persistence and public investment visible; household recovery not verified

## The three clocks

The current record separates three timelines that are often compressed into the
single word “recovery”:

1. **Relief clock:** FEMA housing support continued through July 2027; the
   state reported more than 35,000 households assisted and more than $177
   million awarded.
2. **Housing-finance clock:** CalAssist reported more than 1,100 homeowners
   assisted with a possible mortgage bridge of up to 12 months or $100,000,
   while insurance proceeds and lender timing remained part of the rebuild.
3. **Local-economy clock:** GAO reports large federal disaster-recovery awards
   and projects, but also finds weak outcome measurement; among 292 grants with
   available data, 55% of grantees reported no jobs created or retained and no
   private investment after three years. That is not a failure rate because the
   reports were not independently validated.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Result | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Disaster exposure | Reported | Wildfire and housing-loss context are visible; household loss is not uniformly linked |
| Public relief capacity | Reported | FEMA and California report assistance scale and extended eligibility/recontact routes |
| Household receipt | Not verified | No named household payment, timing, or amount file is retained |
| Insurance/lender conversion | Partly reported | The state links prompt insurance release and mortgage support to rebuilding, but individual decisions are absent |
| Repair and safe return | Not verified | No property-level completion, temporary-housing exit, or return record is linked |
| Local economic recovery | Partly measured | EDA awards and grantee reports exist, but validated jobs, firm survival, and resident benefit are incomplete |
| Stay/move and meaning | Not verified | No same-household mobility, debt, health, trust, complaint, or political-action follow-up is retained |

## Interpretation

The surviving claim is:

> Disaster recovery can persist administratively and financially without being
> demonstrated as completed housing or economic recovery for the affected
> household or place.

This is not evidence that aid failed. It is evidence that program reach,
payment, repair, return, and local economic function are different outcomes.
The same household could receive a mortgage bridge and still be unable to
return; a community could receive infrastructure funding while jobs, firms,
housing, or services remain constrained.

## Decisive next evidence

The next useful ledger would use a property or household key to join:

`loss -> insurance decision/payment -> FEMA/CalAssist contact and receipt -> repair milestone -> temporary-housing exit or return -> debt/work/health -> stay/move/trust`

For the place-level branch, use project completion and service availability,
then track firm openings, jobs, housing, prices, and resident access. Do not
use aggregate dollars, recipients, or project awards as substitutes for those
outcomes.

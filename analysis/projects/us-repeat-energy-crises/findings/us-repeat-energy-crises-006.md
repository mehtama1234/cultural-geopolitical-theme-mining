# Finding 006: Grid recovery money is not the same as stable electricity

**Status:** bounded Puerto Rico infrastructure and public-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The simple finding

Puerto Rico has had billions of dollars committed to rebuild and modernize its electricity grid, but much less has been paid out, few large projects were finished, and the grid has not yet become reliably stable.

GAO reports that FEMA, HUD, and DOE obligated about $14 billion for grid recovery and modernization since 2017. About $2.7 billion of the $11.1 billion FEMA obligation had been disbursed. GAO also reports that nine large FEMA projects were complete, while only about 400 of 16,000 planned miles of line vegetation clearing had been completed with federal funds as of February 2026. The comparison is a progress measure, not a full completion rate.

DOE also reallocated or canceled about $715 million originally intended for community and low-income household solar projects toward grid stability and other needs not yet determined. That may reflect changing priorities, but it also means the original household-facing promise is not the same as the later grid investment.

## The route under test

```text
disaster and fragile grid
  -> federal obligation
  -> review, disbursement, and project construction
  -> working generation, transmission, and distribution
  -> fewer outages and usable electricity
  -> household, firm, health, cost, and political effects
```

The evidence is strongest through funding and selected project progress. It does not follow one customer from a federal project to a lower bill, fewer outages, better health, or greater trust. Stakeholders cited review processes, staff turnover, funding complexity, PREPA's financial condition, and unclear roles as obstacles; GAO recommended clearer coordination.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a sharp infrastructure version of the program's central pattern:

> A system can have money, plans, and construction activity while the basic service people depend on remains uncertain.

It also shows that “recovery” contains competing clocks. Federal agencies can obligate funds, a project can be approved, and a household solar promise can be redirected while vegetation, equipment, grid operations, and outages remain unfinished. The public investment is real, but it is not yet the same as reliable service.

## Limits and next test

- Obligations are not disbursements, completed projects, or customer benefits.
- Vegetation clearing is only one part of grid reliability.
- Nine completed projects are not a completion rate for the whole portfolio.
- Outages cannot be attributed to one project, agency, or operator from this report alone.
- The record does not show customer bills, outage duration by household, health, business survival, trust, or political action.

The next useful test is a place-and-customer-class ledger joining project approval, disbursement, completion, outage and service records, bills, solar credits, business continuity, and household exposure.

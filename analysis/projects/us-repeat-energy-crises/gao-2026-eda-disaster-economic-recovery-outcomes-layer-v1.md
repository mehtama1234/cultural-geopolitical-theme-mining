# GAO 2026 EDA disaster-economic recovery outcomes layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-17
**Source:** US Government Accountability Office, GAO-26-107742
**Reference period:** EDA disaster-recovery grants, fiscal years 2014–2024; Economic Recovery Support Function missions, 2013–2025
**Status:** public-investment capacity and measurement layer; not a causal estimate of community recovery

## Why this update matters

The FEMA layer follows the survivor-facing interface: contact, eligibility,
housing, and placement. This companion layer follows the community economic
interface: federal coordination, infrastructure funding, business recovery,
employment, and the measures used to judge success. It adds a different
failure mode to the atlas: a program can obligate substantial resources and
produce visible projects while lacking validated evidence that the intended
economic outcomes occurred.

## Recorded findings

| Surface | Result | Boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Disaster-recovery grant scale | EDA awarded about **$2.1 billion** in disaster-recovery grants in FY2014–FY2024 | Awards are not disbursements, completed assets, or community recovery |
| Federal coordination | EDA coordinated **45 Economic Recovery Support Function missions** in 2013–2025; the median mission lasted about five months | Mission count and duration do not establish successful implementation |
| Supplemental capacity | Congress provided EDA about **$3.2 billion** in disaster supplemental appropriations in FY2018–FY2025 | Appropriation is not timely local liquidity; availability depends on authorization, rules, and award timing |
| Project composition | Nearly **45%** of FY2014–FY2024 award dollars funded utilities, roadways, and other public infrastructure; another **36%** funded construction of business, medical, training, and other facilities | Project category is not proof of service quality, employment, or household benefit |
| Readiness | FEMA’s July 2025 assessment described the Economic RSF as having limited readiness capacity, the lowest among six RSFs; EDA cited staffing and administrative constraints | Readiness assessment is an institutional condition, not a measured disaster outcome |
| Mission pause | As of April 2026, EDA had no active Economic RSF missions and had declined at least one FEMA request after the 2025 California wildfires while awaiting updated guidance | A pause and one declined request do not establish future capacity or local harm |
| Funding delay | Hawaii stakeholders said economic-recovery planning and implementation suffered because supplemental funds arrived about two years after the disaster | Stakeholder evidence is selected and nongeneralizable; delay does not quantify lost output or jobs |
| Outcome measurement | EDA’s measures could not assess whether disaster-recovery work achieved intended outcomes; it had also suspended validation steps for grantee-reported job data | A measurement gap is not proof that projects failed |
| Reported three-year result | Among 292 construction and revolving-loan grants with available data, **55%** of grantees reported no jobs created or retained and no private investment after three years | These are grantee-reported outcomes, not independently validated economic effects; the denominator is the analyzed grant subset |

## The central tension

Disaster recovery is a long economic process, not a single transfer. A federal
system may coordinate a mission, award money, build a road or utility, and
report jobs while the local economy still lacks working firms, workers,
customers, housing, credit, or reliable services. The relevant stages are:

1. **Appropriation:** Congress makes resources available.
2. **Readiness and coordination:** agencies can staff a mission and align roles.
3. **Award and disbursement:** a community receives usable funds on a workable
   timeline.
4. **Asset and firm implementation:** infrastructure, facilities, loans, and
   recovery strategies are actually delivered.
5. **Economic function:** businesses reopen or form, workers return, services
   operate, and private investment responds.
6. **Resilience and distribution:** the community is better able to absorb the
   next shock, with benefits reaching affected residents and firms.

The GAO evidence is strongest at stages 1–3, provides selected project and
grant evidence at stage 4, and shows that stages 5–6 are not adequately
measured by the current performance system.

## What this changes in the broader trend map

This layer connects disaster exposure to firm capacity, infrastructure, local
employment, and state capability without treating federal spending as a proxy
for recovery. It also clarifies why “resilience investment” is politically and
culturally consequential: residents experience it as a functioning road,
utility, clinic, training site, business, job, or safe place to return—not as
an obligation in a federal ledger.

The finding creates a useful contrast with the small-business disaster-liquidity
evidence already in the atlas. Loans can have measurable firm-level effects in
a designed study, while a public infrastructure portfolio can remain hard to
evaluate if projects are not tied to comparable local baselines and validated
outcomes. These are complementary units, not one recovery rate.

It also exposes a state-capacity dependency. EDA leads an interagency economic
support function with 13 participating agencies, but staffing, guidance,
appropriations, and local implementation determine whether coordination is
available when a community needs it. Federal reach and local recovery are
therefore separate currencies.

## What remains unproven

The report does not establish that EDA grants caused or failed to cause local
employment, business survival, wages, prices, migration, household security,
or political trust. It does not provide a complete community-level counterfactual
or an identified resident/firm panel connecting an award to later outcomes.

The 55% figure is especially bounded: it describes reported jobs/private
investment outcomes for 292 grants with available data, and GAO found the
underlying grantee reports insufficiently reliable for estimating economic
effects. It should not be read as a 55% failure rate for all EDA grants or all
disaster-recovery projects.

## Next test

Build a compact, place-keyed recovery ledger for selected disasters with:

```text
disaster date and exposure
  -> appropriation, award, obligation, disbursement, and project start/completion
  -> infrastructure/service availability and firm financing
  -> establishment openings/closures, jobs, wages, prices, housing, and migration
  -> resident/customer access, household security, trust, complaints, and political action
```

The minimum design should include comparable affected and less-affected places,
project-level completion dates, validated administrative outcomes, and clear
distinctions between local government, firm, worker, customer, household, and
resident units. It should also record whether an infrastructure project was
usable before the next shock and who could access its benefits.

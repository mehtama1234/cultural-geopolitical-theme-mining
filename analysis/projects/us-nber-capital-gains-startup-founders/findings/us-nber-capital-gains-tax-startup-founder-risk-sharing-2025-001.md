# Finding: startup tax design trades founder dilution against downside insurance

**Status:** bounded firm-ownership and tax-design finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34512 uses data on approximately 96,000 U.S. venture-backed
startups, 48,000 exits, 167,000 funding rounds, and 180,000 founders from 1987
to 2021. The founder payoff distribution is highly concentrated: 84% of
founders receive zero exit value, while the top 2% capture 80% of total exit
value.

The paper models a shift from realization-based to accrual-based capital-gains
taxation. Accrual taxation reduces average founder ownership at exit by about
25%, but refundable tax credits raise the share of founders receiving a
positive payoff from 16% to 47% in the model. The tradeoff is that successful
founders face earlier dilution while unsuccessful founders receive partial
downside insurance.

The atlas-relevant insight is:

> Tax design does not only change the size of entrepreneurial rewards. It can
> redistribute timing, ownership, upside concentration, and downside insurance
> across founders with very different realized outcomes.

## Limits and next test

- The tax results are model counterfactuals, not an observed reform estimate.
- Venture-backed founders are not all entrepreneurs, workers, investors, or
  households.
- The evidence does not establish startup formation, employee outcomes,
  innovation, public revenue, or administrative feasibility.
- The next test is a policy ledger joining tax timing, founder dilution,
  refund claims, financing rounds, employee ownership, exit value, startup
  entry, and innovation outcomes.

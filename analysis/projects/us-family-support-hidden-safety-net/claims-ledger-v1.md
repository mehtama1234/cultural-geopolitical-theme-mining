# Claims ledger: US family support as a hidden safety net

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US family transfers, informal care, education costs, work, and household risk

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Family support changes after an adverse event. | NBER family-panel evidence | Parents in lower-wealth households reduce help to adult children, while children increase transfers and informal help to parents after shocks. | It does not show whether the help covers the full loss. |
| C-002 | Family support can be money, care, rides, food, housing, or time. | NBER and HBS research summaries | The sources describe transfers and practical help, including child care and rides. | Suggested or reported help is not a complete account of hours and value. |
| C-003 | The receiver's problem can become the helper's financial or work problem. | Mechanism and caregiving evidence | A helper may lose paid work, savings, or borrowing room while keeping another household stable. | The same event is not followed on both sides in the opening evidence. |
| C-004 | Public or education aid can change which family member carries debt without lowering the family need. | NBER college-finance evidence | Changes in college aid alter where education debt is held; total debt need not fall. | Education financing is one type of family transfer. |
| C-005 | Family help is not available equally. | Distributional inference | Wealth, distance, family structure, health, conflict, and immigration or work conditions shape who can help. | These differences need direct measures in the same data. |
| C-006 | Family help can be better suited than formal aid and still be costly. | Counterpoint and synthesis | It may be fast and trusted, but the helper's lost time and money are real costs. | Need a value comparison with public programs and credit. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
shock in one household
  -> money or care moves through the family
  -> first household keeps a bill, job, or care need covered
  -> helper loses cash, time, work room, or adds debt
  -> family wealth and public need become more unequal
```

## Next test

Follow both receiver and helper around one event. Record cash, in-kind help, care hours, missed work, debt, and later stability rather than calling family support free.

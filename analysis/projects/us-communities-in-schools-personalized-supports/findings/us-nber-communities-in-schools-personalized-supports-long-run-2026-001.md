# Finding: trusted personalized support can connect school intervention to adult earnings

**Status:** bounded U.S. personalized school-support and long-run outcome finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35740 studies Communities In Schools, a U.S. program that
places coordinators in high-poverty schools to connect struggling students to
personalized academic, behavioral, mental-health, and basic-needs resources.
The program reaches about 2 million students annually.

Using the staggered rollout of the program, the paper reports higher test
scores for struggling students. The gains persist into higher high-school
completion and adult earnings. Test-score improvements explain only about half
of the graduation effect; the remainder reflects progress in non-cognitive
outcomes such as attendance and behavior.

The public-capacity insight is:

> A service can produce long-run mobility through a relationship and a bundle
> of supports, not only through the directly measured academic input. The
> intermediary—who notices need, matches resources, and sustains contact—can
> be part of the intervention itself.

## Limits and next test

- The annual program reach is not the study’s causal estimation denominator.
- The abstract does not separately identify academic, behavioral, mental-health,
  housing, or trusted-adult effects.
- Staggered rollout may coincide with school need, local capacity, or other
  reforms.
- Adult earnings are not a complete measure of health, wellbeing, trust,
  safety, household security, or dignity.

The next decisive artifact is a student–coordinator–service ledger joining need
recognition, referral, actual service receipt, relationship continuity,
attendance, behavior, learning, completion, health, housing stability, adult
earnings, and later mobility or exit.

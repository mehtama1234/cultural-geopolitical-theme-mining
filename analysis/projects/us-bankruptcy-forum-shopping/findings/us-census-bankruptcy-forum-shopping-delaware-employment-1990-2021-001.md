# Finding 001: The court a distressed firm uses can change survival and jobs

**Status:** bounded firm, legal-institution, and worker-outcome finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

A Census CES working paper combines about 155,000 Chapter 11 cases with Census
records on the firms' establishments. It asks what happens when similar firms
choose Delaware bankruptcy court instead of a court closer to home.

The study uses distance to Delaware as an instrument for venue choice among
firms headquartered in Maryland, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania. Firms one
standard deviation closer to Delaware were 5.1 percentage points more likely
to file there. For the firms whose venue choice changed with that distance, the
paper estimates that Delaware filing:

- reduced Chapter 7 conversion by 31 percentage points;
- reduced establishment closure by 30 percentage points;
- shortened bankruptcy cases;
- increased the chance that unsecured creditors received something; and
- left each establishment with 6.627 more employees than the counterfactual,
  equal to 24.8% of average pre-filing employment.

The effects shrink when the analysis accounts for how experienced a judge is
with Delaware-style bankruptcy practice, suggesting that court expertise or
precedent may be part of the mechanism.

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a legal-institution layer to the firm-capacity story:

> A firm's survival is not determined only by its balance sheet. The venue and
> expertise available during distress can change whether capacity survives into
> jobs and creditor recovery.

It also complicates a simple “forum shopping is abuse” story. The measured
result is favorable on survival and employment for the affected firms, while
the broader distribution of power among managers, lenders, workers, consumers,
and communities remains open.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The paper is a Census CES working paper, not an official Census endorsement.
- The causal estimate is local: it applies to firms whose venue choice responds
  to proximity to Delaware.
- It does not estimate the effect of choosing every other bankruptcy court.
- Establishment employment is not individual worker income, job quality,
  bargaining power, or household security.
- Positive unsecured-creditor recovery does not show full repayment or equal
  treatment of workers, consumers, suppliers, and other claimants.
- Lower closure does not prove long-run productivity, community stability, or
  that keeping every establishment open was efficient.

The next decisive artifact is a firm-establishment-worker-creditor ledger
through venue choice, court process, closure or continuation, employment,
wages, creditor receipt, consumer obligations, local tax base, and later firm
health.

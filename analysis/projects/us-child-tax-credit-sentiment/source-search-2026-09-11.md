# Source search: US Child Tax Credit and economic sentiment

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; benefit exposure and sentiment are measured, the later political and household action path remains open

## Working question

Can a change in public help alter how a family sees its own finances and the country's economy, beyond the dollars received?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-CTC-SENTIMENT | [When Policy Shapes Perception: The 2021 Child Tax Credit and Consumer Sentiment](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35059) | Using monthly University of Michigan consumer-sentiment data and differences in likely benefit loss across households, the authors find that the Child Tax Credit affected perceived economic well-being beyond the change in current income. They point to possible effects on household activity, political attitudes and voting. | NBER working paper | The opening result does not show the same family's spending, saving, policy support or vote after the benefit changed. |

## First pattern to test

```text
benefit or benefit loss
  -> current money and felt security
  -> view of personal and national economy
  -> policy support or vote
```

The source supports a policy effect on perceived economic well-being. It leaves the spending, political action and lasting security steps open.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Families may judge the economy from many prices, jobs and news stories at once. A benefit can change sentiment without changing a family's long-run finances or political behavior.

## Main gaps

- actual benefit use and spending;
- income, debt, food, housing and care changes;
- personal versus national economic belief;
- media and party messages;
- policy support, turnout and vote;
- effects after the benefit ends.

## Decision rule

Keep benefit exposure, money, sentiment, policy support and voting as separate steps until a source connects the same family across them.

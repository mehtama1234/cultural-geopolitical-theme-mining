# Source search: US emergency credit after a disaster

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States, federally declared natural disasters  
**Status:** opening pass; a protective effect is measured, full loan burden remains open

## Working question

When does borrowing protect a household, and when does it only move the loss forward?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-EMERGENCY-CREDIT | [Credit When You Need It](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32845) | The study links Federal Disaster Loan applications with credit records and uses an approval cutoff to compare similar applicants. Approved emergency credit reduced bankruptcy likelihood by 61% over three years and was linked to additional car purchases even three years later. | NBER working paper | This is a federal disaster-loan setting for applicants with uninsured damage. It does not represent ordinary consumer credit or show the full cost and need preserved for each borrower. |
| US-NBER-PAYMENT-RESILIENCE | [On the Resilience of Payment Methods](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35115) | Disaster outages can make digital payment unavailable and increase the value of cash access. | NBER working paper | It does not show which households later need emergency credit or how repayment affects them. |

## First pattern to test

```text
disaster loss or outage
  -> payment and bill shock
  -> emergency credit, cash, aid, insurance, or no help
  -> purchase, repair, or payment preserved
  -> repayment, bankruptcy, work and later financial room
```

The source supports a large protective effect for one form of well-timed credit. It does not show that all borrowing has the same effect.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Credit may prevent a bankruptcy and preserve a needed car or repair. It can also add interest and a payment that becomes hard to carry after the emergency ends.

## Main gaps

- uninsured loss and the need the loan covered;
- approval, amount, rate and repayment terms;
- cash, insurance and aid received at the same time;
- bankruptcy, delinquency and credit access;
- car, home, work, health and family outcomes;
- differences by income, race, place, disability and disaster type.

## Decision rule

Keep approval, liquidity, preserved need, repayment and bankruptcy separate. A lower bankruptcy rate is strong evidence of protection in this setting, not proof that every loan improves household welfare.

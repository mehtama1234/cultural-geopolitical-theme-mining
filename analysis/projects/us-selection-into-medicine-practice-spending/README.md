# Project: selection into medicine, practice incentives, and patient outcomes

This project follows how labor-market outside options shape who enters a
high-status profession and how that selection may later affect practice,
spending, institutional incentives, and patient outcomes. It keeps physician
billing distinct from household cost, quality, and trust.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35631. Labor-market
downturns in other prestigious fields shifted medical-school applicant and
matriculant composition; decades later, physicians from low-outside-option
cohorts billed Medicare more per beneficiary, used more discretionary
revenue-generating services while seeing healthier patients, and sorted toward
higher-powered financial settings. The additional spending did not produce
detectable patient-health improvement in the reported outcomes.

# Finding: professional outside options can become a long-run health-care incentive channel

**Status:** bounded physician-selection and practice-outcome finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 35631 uses labor-market conditions in other prestigious
fields when applicants choose whether to enter medical school as a shock to the
composition of the applicant pool. Cohorts formed when outside options were
weak included more students from non-pre-medical backgrounds.

Decades later, those physicians practiced differently. Conditional on
experience, specialty, and outcome year, they billed Medicare more per
beneficiary, used more discretionary revenue-generating services despite seeing
healthier patients, and sorted toward practice settings with stronger financial
incentives. The additional spending did not produce detectable improvements in
the measured patient-health outcomes.

The institutional-economic insight is:

> A profession’s incentive structure begins before employment. Selection into
> a high-status occupation can transmit an earlier labor-market shock into
> later service intensity, institutional sorting, and household-facing cost
> without a matching health gain.

## Limits and next test

- Billing is not the same as household out-of-pocket cost, total social cost,
  quality, or patient welfare.
- The abstract does not disclose the exact linked sample denominator or every
  health-outcome definition.
- The design does not identify a single physician motivation or prove that all
  additional services were unnecessary.
- Patient selection, coding, geography, specialty choice, and practice setting
  may mediate the observed relationship.

The next decisive artifact is a physician–practice–patient episode ledger that
joins entry conditions, specialty and setting, service mix, prices and payer
exposure, patient severity, outcomes, household financial burden, and trust or
switching behavior.

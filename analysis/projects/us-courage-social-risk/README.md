# Project: Courage, social risk, and public action

## Question

When people say courage is declining, are they describing less willingness to
act—or a higher perceived cost for acting openly?

## Decision rule

Keep perceived courage, the value placed on courage, fear of consequences,
actual speech or action, retaliation, and later persistence separate. A survey
judgment about courage is not a behavior count.

## Current bounded finding

The [Gallup courage finding](findings/us-gallup-courage-social-risk-2026-001.md)
reports that 67% of U.S. adults say courage is weaker than 20 years ago, 79%
say courage from elected leaders is very important to the nation's future, and
44% are concerned about the personal costs of taking a principled stand.

The pattern supports a social-risk question: people may value courage while
seeing public action as costly. It does not establish that Americans act less
often or that retaliation increased.

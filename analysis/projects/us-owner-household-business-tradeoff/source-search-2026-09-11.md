# Source search: US owner households and the business tradeoff

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; linked cash-flow evidence is useful, wider local effects remain open

## Working question

When a business and a family share money, where does a shock show up first?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-OWNER-CONSUMPTION | [Revenue Collapses and the Consumption of Small Business Owners](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28151) | Linked business and owner-household account data show a 40% fall in business revenue in the first two months of the pandemic, while owner consumption fell by about 1.6 cents for each dollar of lost revenue. | NBER working paper | The pandemic included subsidies and unusual limits on spending; the result does not describe every business shock. |
| US-HBS-OWNER-CASHFLOW | [What Paying for College Can Do to a Family Business](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/what-paying-for-college-can-do-to-family-business) | HBS reports linked data for about 90,000 small businesses and their owners, showing how college costs can affect both household spending and business investment. | HBS Working Knowledge | The article concerns college costs and does not establish a general rule for all household or business spending. |
| US-NBER-NEXT-GENERATION | [Investing in the Next Generation](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29816) | A liquidity increase changed how poor entrepreneurs invested in children's education and in the business, with different results by parent literacy. | NBER working paper | The experiment took place outside the United States, so it is a mechanism comparison rather than direct US evidence. |

## First pattern to test

```text
business or family cash-flow shock
  -> owner chooses which payment or investment to protect
  -> household spending, business capital, or children's opportunity changes
  -> jobs, local services, family wealth, or business survival may change
```

The US sources support a shared cash-flow problem, not one fixed direction. A fall in business revenue may not pass straight into household spending, while a family bill can still reduce business investment. The useful next step is to identify which owners have another cash buffer.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Keeping household spending steady can protect a family and help a business survive. Cutting business investment is not always a failure; it may be the sensible way to avoid a larger loss.

## Main gaps

- owner wealth and access to credit;
- business investment, hiring and closure after household costs;
- differences between family firms, sole proprietors and larger small businesses;
- which subsidies or lenders absorb the first loss;
- effects on local workers, prices and services;
- whether family education spending changes later business entry.

## Decision rule

Follow the business account and the owner household account around the same shock. If the data only show one side, record the possible tradeoff and move on without claiming a local or worker effect.

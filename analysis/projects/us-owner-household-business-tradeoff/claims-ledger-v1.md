# Claims ledger: US owner households and the business tradeoff

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** United States; small-business revenue, owner household spending, family bills, and business investment

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | A business revenue shock does not pass one-for-one into the owner's household spending. | Linked account data | NBER Working Paper 28151 finds a 40% early-pandemic revenue fall and an estimated 1.6-cent fall in owner consumption for each dollar of lost revenue. | Pandemic aid and limits on spending helped absorb the shock; this is not a rule for every downturn. |
| C-002 | Public aid and existing cash can hide how exposed an owner household is. | Linked account data | The paper reports business and personal balances rose when federal transfers and PPP arrived; the authors say the pass-through rose after spending limits eased. | It does not identify the exact buffer for every owner or the long-run result. |
| C-003 | A large family bill can reduce business spending and revenue. | US linked business/household data | HBS reports a study of about 90,000 small businesses and owners: when a child reached college age, household spending rose about 7%, while business expenses and revenue fell about 5%. | This is tied to college timing and closely held firms; it does not prove closure, job loss, or customer harm. |
| C-004 | The tradeoff is larger when the household or firm has less room. | Group comparison | HBS reports larger effects for highly indebted households, less profitable firms, volatile-cash-flow firms, and families without college savings. | These are study groups, not a complete forecast for every owner. |
| C-005 | One cash pool can move pressure between family security and business growth. | Our inference | C-001 through C-004 join revenue, household spending, family cost, and business spending. | Effects on workers, prices, local services, and survival remain open. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
business or family cash-flow shock
  -> owner protects a payment, reserve, or investment
  -> household spending, business capital, or child opportunity changes
  -> firm scale, work, local service, or family wealth may change
```

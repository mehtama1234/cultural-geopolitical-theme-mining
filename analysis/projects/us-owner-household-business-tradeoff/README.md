# Project: US owner households and the business tradeoff

## Question

When a small business and its owner share one cash pool, which gets protected when money is tight: the household, the business, or neither?

## Short first pass

Use linked business and household account data to track sales, household spending, subsidies and business investment around a shock. Keep owner income, household consumption and business survival separate.

## Possible connection

A small firm's financial problem can become a family spending problem, while a family bill can reduce what the owner puts back into the firm. This may shape local jobs and the next generation's choices.

## Decision rule

Move on after one source follows business revenue and household spending together, one source shows a family investment tradeoff, and one next dataset can test differences by owner wealth and business type. Do not treat a business owner's household cut as proof that workers or customers were affected.

## Matched evidence pass

The first matched check is [One cash pool can protect a family by shrinking a business](../../findings/us-owner-household-business-matched-evidence-001.md), with its [HTML reading page](../../../site/us-owner-household-business-matched-evidence-001.html) and [claims ledger](claims-ledger-v1.md). It confirms a linked household-firm tradeoff while leaving worker, customer, and closure effects open.

The new [COVID owner-consumption finding](findings/us-owner-business-revenue-consumption-covid-001.md)
promotes the NBER linked-account result into the canonical atlas: a 40% early
revenue fall passed through at 1.6 cents per dollar of lost revenue, with the
muted average response persisting through 2021. The result closes the
firm-to-owner-household measurement arrow for this shock while leaving buffers,
business survival, workers, customers, and political meaning open.

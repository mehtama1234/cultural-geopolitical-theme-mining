# Source search: US medical debt relief and what it changes

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** opening pass; causal relief evidence is available, broader household effects remain mixed

## Working question

What does removing a medical debt actually repair?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-MEDICAL-RELIEF | [The Effects of Medical Debt Relief](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32315) | Two randomized experiments covering $169 million of medical debt for 83,401 people found modest credit-access improvement when credit reporting was part of the counterfactual, lower payments on existing medical bills, and no average effect on surveyed mental health, physical health, health-care use or financial wellness. | NBER working paper | The debt had been sold to collectors and the results concern the studied relief programs; they do not cover every medical bill or every household. |
| US-HBS-BILLING | [Electronic Health Records Were Supposed to Cut Medical Costs](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/electronic-health-records-were-supposed-to-cut-health-costs-they-haven-t) | HBS reports that billing and insurance work can take substantial staff time and cost, from about $20 for a primary-care visit to about $215 for an inpatient surgery in the study. | HBS Working Knowledge | Administrative cost is not the patient's bill and does not show how a specific patient experiences or pays it. |
| US-HBS-PRICE-UNCERTAINTY | [Why American Health Care Needs Its Own SEC](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/why-american-health-care-needs-its-own-sec) | HBS describes wide price differences and limited ability for patients to compare value before care. | HBS Working Knowledge | It is an argument for transparency, not a causal estimate of debt or later health. |

## First pattern to test

```text
illness or care need
  -> bill and payment process
  -> medical debt or collection record
  -> credit, later payment, care use, health, or trust changes
  -> relief removes one part of the chain but not necessarily the illness
```

The NBER experiments are useful because they show what debt relief did not change on average. The result does not make medical debt harmless; it says the debt record may be a marker of a larger problem, and removing it alone may not repair health or care access.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Credit access is a real outcome, and a modest improvement can matter to someone trying to rent, borrow or recover. Average health results can also hide effects for particular groups or kinds of debt.

## Main gaps

- original illness, bill and insurance decision;
- credit, housing and employment outcomes after relief;
- health and care effects by debt size and patient group;
- debt that has not yet reached collections;
- provider billing and financial-assistance decisions;
- whether relief changes trust or willingness to seek care.

## Decision rule

Keep the causal relief result beside the broader debt correlation. If a source only shows that debt and poor health occur together, record it as a marker and move on without claiming debt relief will cure the health problem.

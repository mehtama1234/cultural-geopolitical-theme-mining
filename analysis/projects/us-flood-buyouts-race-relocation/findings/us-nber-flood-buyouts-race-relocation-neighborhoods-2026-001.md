# Finding 001: Leaving flood danger does not guarantee equal protection after the move

**Status:** bounded climate-adaptation, race, housing, and relocation finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

A government buyout can remove a household from a flood-prone property while
the compensation and the next neighborhood still differ by race.

NBER Working Paper 35726 combines nationwide federal property-acquisition data,
housing sales, restricted-use Census data, and records tracking individual moves.
It reports that households of color received at least 6% lower buyout
compensation than White households. White participants moved to higher-quality
neighborhoods and were better off than White nonparticipants. Black participants
moved to worse neighborhoods and were worse off than Black nonparticipants.

The practical chain is:

```text
flood exposure
  -> buyout offer and property valuation
  -> payment and moving resources
  -> destination neighborhood
  -> later housing, security, health, trust, and belonging [partly open]
```

The broader social insight is that “safe exit” is not one outcome. Physical
departure, fair payment, a reachable destination, and durable security can move
on different tracks.

## What this adds to the atlas

This extends the existing flood-risk and wildfire findings from exposure and
damage into the government exit route. It shows how climate adaptation can
reduce one danger while shifting wealth, place, and opportunity unevenly.

## Limits and next test

- This is a working paper, and the public abstract does not show every model
  specification or sample restriction.
- The findings do not by themselves identify the full reason for the payment or
  destination differences.
- Neighborhood quality is not the same as health, income, safety, belonging, or
  political trust.
- A comparison with nonparticipants does not make every participant and
  nonparticipant interchangeable.

The next decisive artifact is a protected buyout ledger joining property
appraisal, offer, negotiation, payment, debt, insurance, moving cost,
destination, household composition, services, health, employment, and later
return, staying, trust, or security.

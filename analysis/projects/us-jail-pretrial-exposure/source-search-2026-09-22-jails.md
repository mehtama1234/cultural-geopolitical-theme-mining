# Source search: jail exposure and the pretrial administrative route

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States local jails  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; jail custody is not the same as conviction or later reentry

## Working question

What changed in local-jail exposure in 2024, and what does the aggregate jail
record leave unknown?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-BJS-JAIL-2024 | [BJS Jail Inmates in 2024 statistical tables](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/jail-inmates-2024-statistical-tables/web-report) | Reports custody, admissions, conviction status, demographics, length of stay, capacity, staffing, and supervision outside jail. | Official BJS report | It does not follow a person from arrest through case outcome, release conditions, employment, housing, health, or reentry. |

## First pattern

```text
arrest or court process
  -> jail admission and custody
  -> pretrial hold, conviction, release, or supervision
  -> housing, work, health, safety, trust, and reentry [open]
```

At midyear 2024, local jails held 658,700 people and had an average daily
population of 667,700. From July 2023 through June 2024, jails reported 7.9
million admissions. Sixty-eight percent of people held at midyear were
unconvicted. People admitted to jail spent an average of 31 days before
release. The Black jail incarceration rate was 566 per 100,000 residents,
compared with 149 per 100,000 for white residents.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The jail population was 12% lower than in 2014 and admissions were 31% lower,
so the direction is not simply “everything is worsening.” But a lower aggregate
does not erase repeated short stays, pretrial exposure, local variation, or
racial differences. The report cannot identify which people were later
convicted, released, detained again, or able to rebuild stable lives.

## Decision rule

Use BJS jail data for custody scale, turnover, conviction status, and unequal
exposure. Do not convert those aggregates into crime causation, wrongful
detention, reentry success, or public trust without linked person-level data.

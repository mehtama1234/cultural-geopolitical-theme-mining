# Finding 001: Fraud control is also a trust and mission problem

**Status:** bounded institutional fraud-risk finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reports that it has made 215 recommendations to federal agencies and
programs to improve fraud-risk management, with about 40% still open as of
April 2026. GAO also says fraud can erode public trust in government and hinder
agencies' ability to carry out their missions.

In simple terms:

```text
fraud risk
  -> money and program-integrity problem
  -> possible trust and mission-execution problem
  -> controls and recommendations still need implementation
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This connects the consumer/beneficiary lane to institutional capacity. A fraud
control is not fully realized when a recommendation is issued or a process is
visible; the program still needs prevention, detection, correction, recovery,
and a usable route for affected people.

It also reinforces a recurring boundary: an open recommendation is evidence of
unfinished control work, not proof of fraud prevalence or a measured fall in
public trust.

## Limits and next test

- Recommendation counts are not fraud rates or dollar-loss estimates.
- Open status does not identify why implementation is incomplete.
- GAO's trust statement is an institutional assessment, not a person-level
  causal trust estimate.
- The source does not follow beneficiaries through reporting, remedy, recovery,
  program use, or later institutional judgment.

The next test would link a named program to fraud exposure, detection, agency
response, beneficiary correction or recovery, repeat use, and later trust or
exit.

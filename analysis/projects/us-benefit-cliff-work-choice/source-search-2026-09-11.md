# Source search: US benefit loss and the work choice

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** first matched evidence pass completed; household take-home effect remains open

## Working question

What happens to a family's real room when earnings rise but public help changes?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-SNAP-2024 | [The Disenrollment and Labor Supply Effects of SNAP Work Requirements](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32441) | Using linked administrative data, the study finds that SNAP work requirements reduced participation among parents but did not increase their work. The authors point to reporting and administrative burden as a key channel. | NBER working paper | This is a work-requirement policy, not a simple earnings cliff, and the result is tied to the studied groups and states. |
| US-NBER-SNAP-2021 | [Employed in a SNAP?](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28877) | A prior study finds large increases in SNAP exits after work requirements, with no effects on employment in its main results. | NBER working paper | The policy and period differ from the newer study; participation exit is not the same as a loss of total household resources. |
| US-ASPE-CLIFFS | [Effective Marginal Tax Rates and Benefit Cliffs](https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/marginal-tax-rate-series) | Federal analysis models how SNAP, tax credits, Medicaid, child care and TANF interact as earnings rise; some households face high effective rates, while only a small share of child-care households lose the full subsidy after a modeled $2,000 earnings increase. | US Department of Health and Human Services | These are modeled results, not a single national estimate of actual work choices or later well-being. |
| US-HBS-CAREGIVER-SUPPORT | [How to Keep Employees Productive: Support Caregivers](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-support-your-best-employees-who-are-probably-caregivers) | HBS describes how child care, elder care and transportation needs make the same employer benefit useful to one worker and useless to another. | HBS Working Knowledge | It does not measure public-benefit loss or prove that a benefit change caused a work decision. |

## First pattern to test

```text
earnings or hours rise
  -> public help tapers, ends, or becomes harder to keep
  -> food, health care, child care, or transport cost changes
  -> the real gain from work changes
  -> work hours, job choice, trust in programs, or political demand changes
```

The sources show that program loss and work do not move in one simple direction. Work requirements can remove aid without raising work, while a gradual benefit taper may still leave a household better off. The missing measure is the same family's total resources before and after the change.

## Matched evidence result

The central test is total household room. Program exit, higher wages, and more work are not interchangeable outcomes. The next data build must count cash, taxes, food, health coverage, child care, transport, and time spent meeting the rule together.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

A benefit taper can support work by letting earnings replace aid over time. A higher wage can still improve security even when some help falls. The analysis must count food, health and care value, not only cash wages.

## Main gaps

- total take-home resources after taxes and lost benefits;
- benefit changes by program and state;
- child care, health coverage and transport costs after the change;
- actual job offers, hours and work retention;
- differences by family structure, disability, race, place and wealth;
- whether smoother phase-outs change work or only reduce paperwork stress.

## Decision rule

Compare the same household's earnings, aid, necessary costs and work over time. If the evidence only shows exit from one program, record that loss without calling it a fall in total household resources.

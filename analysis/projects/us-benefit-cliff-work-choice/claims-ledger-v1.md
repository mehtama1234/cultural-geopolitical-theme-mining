# Claims ledger: US benefit loss and the work choice

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US earnings, SNAP, health, child care, taxes, work rules, and household resources

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Losing a benefit is not the same as earning more. | Linked administrative evidence | NBER finds SNAP work requirements reduced participation among parents without increasing their work. | The result concerns a work rule, not every earnings change. |
| C-002 | Administrative burden can remove aid without creating a job. | NBER policy studies | Participation fell after work requirements, with reporting and process burden as a likely channel. | The exact burden differs by state and program. |
| C-003 | A higher wage can be partly offset by lower food, health, or care support. | HHS marginal-rate modeling | Federal models combine SNAP, tax credits, Medicaid, child care, and TANF to show high effective rates for some earnings paths. | Modeled schedules are not observed household choices. |
| C-004 | A gradual phase-out and a hard threshold are different household events. | Program design analysis | HHS separates programs that taper from programs that end at an eligibility line. | The total result depends on taxes, work costs, and family needs. |
| C-005 | The same household may gain cash wages while losing care or health value. | Synthesis | Food, coverage, child care, transport, and taxes must be counted beside pay. | A linked before-and-after household record remains open. |
| C-006 | Program exit alone cannot establish a fall in total resources. | Counterpoint | A family may leave SNAP because earnings rose, because paperwork failed, or because work increased; these have different meanings. | Need total resources and reason for exit. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
earnings or hours rise
  -> public help tapers, ends, or becomes harder to keep
  -> food, health care, child care, or transport cost changes
  -> real gain from work changes
  -> work hours, job choice, trust, or political demand
```

## Next test

Compare the same household's wages, taxes, benefits, care costs, health coverage, transport, and work over time. Mark whether aid ended by earnings, a threshold, or a process failure.

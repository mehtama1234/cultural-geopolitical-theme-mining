# Source search: federal reentry timing and halfway-house capacity

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States federal prison and residential reentry system  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

Does a formal prerelease route become usable community reentry, or can timing,
capacity, and contractor administration delay the transition?

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Limit |

| US-GAO-BOP-RRC-2026 | [GAO-26-107353: Bureau of Prisons: Actions Needed to Better Achieve Financial and Other Benefits of Moving Individuals to Halfway Houses on Time](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107353) | Eligibility-data gaps, delayed transfers, RRC capacity limits, and late payments affecting the federal reentry route. | The audit does not provide a person-level post-release panel linking transfer timing to housing, employment, recidivism, trust, or community acceptance. |

## Observed pattern

GAO found that BOP did not apply all earned time toward RRC or home-confinement
placement for 21,190 of 29,934 people reviewed. BOP could not state the full
scale because eligibility dates were not readily available. The agency also
reported roughly 150 contracted RRCs, insufficient capacity, about 65,000 late
contractor payments from fiscal year 2022 through March 2025, and late payments
to RRCs about 70% of the time in fiscal years 2023–2024.

## Working interpretation

```text
earned time and formal eligibility
  -> timely transfer to RRC or home confinement
  -> employment, housing, treatment, and community reconnection opportunity
  -> [open] actual reentry security, later justice contact, trust, and durable inclusion
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

The audit does not show that every delayed transfer caused a bad outcome, and
some delays reflected court orders or limited space. RRC residence is not the
same as successful reentry, and BOP's statement that RRC residents are less
likely to return to prison is not a causal estimate here. This lane stops at
the timing/capacity boundary unless a linked person-level reentry or named-RRC
source adds actual service use and later housing, work, health, or justice
outcomes.

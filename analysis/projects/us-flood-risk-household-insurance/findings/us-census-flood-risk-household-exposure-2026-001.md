# Finding 001: Flood risk is not distributed by income alone

**Status:** bounded climate-risk, housing, insurance, and unequal-exposure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES paper combines parcel-level flood risk with linked household and
administrative data. It finds that net migration to floodplain Census blocks
has increased, but there was essentially no net migration to parcels with flood
risk and no change in the overall share of households living in floodplains.

The income pattern is not a straight line. Exposure is slightly lower across
the bottom 90 income percentiles, while the top decile—especially households
with multiple properties—has disproportionate exposure. Building type and
homeownership explain much of the pattern within narrow income groups. The
paper’s household-level analysis suggests that floodplain households are, on
average, less disadvantaged and increasingly protected, while low-income
uninsured homeowners remain a vulnerable subgroup.

The practical chain is:

```text
parcel hazard and building type
  -> ownership, insurance, and protection
  -> damage, claim, repair, or forced staying/moving [not all observed]
  -> housing cost, debt, health, and security [open]
```

The broader social insight is that “who is at risk” depends on the unit and the
interface. A map of floodplain blocks can tell a different story from a
household-and-parcel analysis.

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a climate-risk counterexample to simple assumptions about unequal
exposure. It shows why household income, property ownership, building form,
insurance, and public protection must be joined before interpreting place-based
risk. It also separates increased protection from completed recovery.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper, not a Census Bureau endorsement.
- Exposure to parcel risk is not the same as experiencing a flood or suffering
  damage.
- No net parcel migration does not rule out displacement, forced staying, or
  within-area housing change.
- Protection does not prove affordable insurance, successful claims, repair,
  health, or durable housing security.
- The low-income uninsured-homeowner subgroup is a vulnerability signal, not a
  complete estimate of all affected households.

The next decisive artifact is a protected property-household panel linking
hazard, insurance, premium, claim, repair, assistance, debt, health, move or
stay, and later housing security.

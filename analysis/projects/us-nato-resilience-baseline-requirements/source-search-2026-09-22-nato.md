# Source search: NATO resilience baseline requirements

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** NATO member and partner resilience planning; civil-military
systems relevant to allied readiness  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; formal requirements are visible, practical
implementation and public burden are not established

## Working question

Does a current NATO resilience document show a concrete connection between
civilian systems and military readiness, beyond a general call for resilience?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NATO-RESILIENCE-BASELINES-2026 | [NATO 2026 Resilience Baseline Requirements](https://nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/official-texts/2026/09/15/2026-resilience-baseline-requirements) | Sets resilience requirements covering governance, resource planning, essential services, movement, communications, transport, health, food and water, supply chains, and Host Nation Support. | Official alliance policy text | It does not measure national implementation, spending, public acceptance, service continuity, or crisis performance. |

## First pattern

```text
alliance baseline requirements
  -> national whole-of-government and whole-of-society planning
  -> civilian communications, transport, health, food/water, movement,
     and private-sector supply chains
  -> Host Nation Support and military enablement
  -> population protection and essential services [required aim]
  -> actual readiness, cost, consent, and crisis performance [open]
```

The document makes civilian capacity part of military readiness. It also
recognizes that civil and military needs can compete: planning must support
military operations and Host Nation Support while mitigating effects on the
population and maintaining essential services.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

This is not simply a claim that military needs should override civilian life.
The requirements explicitly call for whole-of-society governance, continuity
of essential services, population-impact mitigation, and resilience in the
systems that civilians depend on.

## Decision rule

Use this source to identify a policy-level civil-military interface. Do not
turn a formal baseline into evidence that countries comply, that services are
resilient, that costs are fairly shared, or that populations consent to the
burden.

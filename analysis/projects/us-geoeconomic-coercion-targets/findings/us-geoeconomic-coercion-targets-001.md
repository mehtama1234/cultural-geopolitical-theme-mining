# Finding 001: Geoeconomic pressure can land on a firm even when the conflict is between states

**Status:** bounded geoeconomic target-selection finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

Great powers can use trade and financial connections to pressure either a
foreign government or private firms inside that country.

The NBER model explains the trade-off. A private firm may care less than its
government about the national consequences of obeying the great power, making
the firm an easier target. But a government can influence many firms that the
great power cannot reach directly. The best mix depends on which channel is
stronger.

The route is:

```text
trade or financial dependence
  -> great-power pressure
  -> foreign government, private firm, or both become targets
  -> compliance, resistance, or domestic pass-through
  -> [open] public cost, alliance behavior, legitimacy, and strategic result
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This adds a missing middle between national power and firm behavior. A
geopolitical contest does not remain at the level of presidents and ministries;
it can be carried through companies, banks, suppliers, and customers.

It also complicates the meaning of “state power.” Pressure may be strategically
state-directed while the immediate cost, decision, and reputational exposure
fall on a private firm. Firm compliance is not automatically government
compliance, and government resistance is not automatically firm resistance.

## What it does not show

- This is a model, not a study of a particular sanction or export-control case.
- It does not show which target type is more common or more successful.
- It does not measure firm losses, government response, public legitimacy,
  alliance behavior, or later strategic effect.
- It does not show whether private targeting protects a population or merely
  shifts the burden from the state to firms and workers.
- Model mechanisms are not observed event frequencies.

## Next decisive test

Build an event-level coercion ledger with the demand, target type, dependence,
firm or government response, domestic pass-through, public cost, coalition or
alliance response, and later strategic outcome. Do not combine firm compliance
with state compliance.

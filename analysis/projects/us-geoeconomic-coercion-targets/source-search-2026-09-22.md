# Source search: US geoeconomic coercion and who bears the pressure

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States and foreign governments and firms linked by trade and finance  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; the model distinguishes government targets from private-firm targets, observed coercion outcomes remain open

## Working question

When a great power uses trade and financial links as leverage, does it pressure a foreign government or the private firms inside that country?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-GEOECONOMIC-COERCION | [The Targets of Geoeconomic Coercion](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35343) | A model of US/China-style geoeconomic power shows why a hegemon may target foreign private firms, governments, or both. Private targets may internalize less of the national consequence of complying; governments can influence domestic firms that the hegemon cannot directly reach. | NBER working paper | This is a theory/model paper, not an event dataset or evaluation of sanctions, export controls, or a specific country. |

## First pattern

```text
trade or financial dependence
  -> great-power pressure
  -> foreign government or private firm becomes target
  -> compliance, resistance, or domestic pass-through
  -> [open] firm cost, public legitimacy, alliance behavior, and external response
```

The source adds a target-selection layer to the geopolitical atlas. It helps
explain why coercion may appear as a firm decision even when the strategic
contest is between states.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Targeting a private firm can be more direct, but targeting a government can
unlock influence over many firms. Neither target type guarantees compliance or
strategic success. Model power is not observed leverage in a real episode.

## Decision rule

Keep target selection, firm compliance, government response, public cost,
alliance behavior, and strategic outcome separate. Reopen this lane only with
an event-level coercion case that observes a target, demand, response, and
later consequence.

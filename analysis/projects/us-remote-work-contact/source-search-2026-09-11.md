# Source search: US remote work and the value of coming together

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States scope for the project; opening trial is multinational  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

Does a small, shared office meeting change remote work through communication and feedback, and for whom?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NBER-W35331 | [NBER: The Value of One Office Day a Month](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35331) | A randomized trial of 248 customer-service employees reports 7.8% more calls per hour, 36 extra minutes of coworker communication after office visits, more manager feedback, 11 percentage points more contact among assigned desk neighbors, and one-third lower attrition | Working paper with randomized design | One large multinational, customer-service workers and workers living within commuting distance; not a general result for all remote jobs |
| NBER-W18871 | [NBER: Evidence from a Chinese Experiment on Working from Home](https://www.nber.org/digest/sep13/evidence-chinese-experiment-working-home) | A different randomized trial found higher productivity and work satisfaction at home but lower promotion rates, possibly because remote workers were less visible | NBER research digest | Older, China-based call-center setting; not directly comparable to the new trial |
| NBER-W31880 | [NBER: The Power of Proximity to Coworkers](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31880) | Earlier work tests a tradeoff between short-term output and knowledge sharing when coworkers sit near one another | Working paper | Different occupation and setting; it does not test one office day a month |

## First pattern to test

```text
remote work
  -> fewer routine encounters
  -> planned in-person contact
  -> communication, feedback, learning or belonging
  -> productivity, retention, promotion or worker choice
  -> commute, housing and local business effects
```

The new NBER trial supports a communication and retention pattern. It does not settle the best schedule or the value for every worker.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Fully remote work can remove commuting, widen the labor pool, improve quiet concentration and help people handle care or health needs. An office day can add travel, cost and unequal access.

## Main gaps

- effects by age, disability, care duty, income, housing and distance;
- whether communication gains become promotions or pay;
- the worker's travel and time cost;
- results outside customer service;
- whether remote workers prefer the arrangement;
- long-run effects on cities, offices and local businesses.

## Decision rule

Compare the new trial with a fully remote or hybrid US worker panel. Do not turn one monthly schedule into a general office mandate.

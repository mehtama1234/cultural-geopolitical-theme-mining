# Official-source extract: social-media use and collective exit

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked chapter.

## Official source

- Cass R. Sunstein, “Social media, wasting time, and product traps,” World
  Happiness Report 2026, Chapter 6, [official chapter](https://www.worldhappiness.report/ed/2026/social-media-wasting-time-and-product-traps/),
  [downloadable chapter](https://files.worldhappiness.report/WHR26_Ch06.pdf).

## Extracted claims

- The chapter summarizes Hunt Allcott and collaborators' randomized Facebook
  deactivation experiment. It reports 2,743 recruited Facebook users; half of
  the eligible treatment group was paid to deactivate for four weeks while the
  control group continued using Facebook.
- The deactivation group was reported as happier, more satisfied with life,
  less anxious, and less depressed across measured subjective-wellbeing
  outcomes. The chapter says the effects were small but statistically
  significant.
- Several weeks later, the treatment group reported lower Facebook use and was
  more likely to delete the app. The control group had significantly more
  political knowledge on the reported measure; the study did not establish
  permanent account closure or a durable civic effect.
- The chapter separately summarizes a U.S. college-student study of Instagram
  and TikTok. Participants reportedly demanded $59 to deactivate TikTok and
  $47 to deactivate Instagram for a month, but would pay $28 and $10,
  respectively, to have everyone in their academic community deactivate for a
  month.
- The chapter interprets this contrast as evidence consistent with a negative
  non-user externality or “product trap”: an individual may keep using a
  platform because other people use it while preferring community-wide exit.

## Boundary preserved

The two component studies have different samples, platforms, outcomes, and
designs. Stated willingness to pay or accept is not observed deletion or
collective action. Short-run wellbeing gains do not capture every information,
connection, or political benefit, and app deletion is not proof of account
closure or durable non-use.

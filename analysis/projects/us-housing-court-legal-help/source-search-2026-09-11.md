# Source search: US housing-court legal help

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States, with detailed evidence from New York City  
**Status:** opening pass; court effects are supported, the full household path remains open

## Working question

Which problems can be prevented at the court door, and which remain after help arrives?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NBER-HOUSING-LEGAL-HELP | [The Effects of Legal Representation on Tenant Outcomes in Housing Court](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29836) | The study uses New York City's rollout of free legal representation for tenants at or below 200% of the federal poverty guideline. Tenants who gained lawyers were less likely to face possession judgments, had smaller money judgments, received fewer eviction warrants and were less likely to be evicted. Effects were larger in poorer places and places with more noncitizens. | NBER working paper, later published in the Journal of Public Economics | This is one city program and a court setting. It does not show the later work, health, credit or housing path for the same tenants. |

## First pattern to test

```text
rent shock or dispute
  -> housing court
  -> legal help or no legal help
  -> judgment, warrant, payment plan or case loss
  -> move, eviction, debt or preserved housing
  -> later work, health, credit and housing choices
```

The opening evidence supports legal representation as a real change in court outcomes. It does not show that the rent problem disappeared, or that every prevented eviction produces the same later benefit.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Legal help can improve a tenant's position without making housing affordable. Program reach, lawyer capacity, court rules and landlord behavior may change the result elsewhere.

## Main gaps

- rent arrears and the first household shock;
- who receives help and who cannot reach it;
- case resolution, payment plans and actual moves;
- later work, school, care, health and credit;
- differences by race, children, disability, immigration status and place;
- cost and scale of making representation available.

## Decision rule

Keep court access separate from housing affordability. If a source measures only representation or case outcome, record a court-path effect without claiming that the household has recovered.

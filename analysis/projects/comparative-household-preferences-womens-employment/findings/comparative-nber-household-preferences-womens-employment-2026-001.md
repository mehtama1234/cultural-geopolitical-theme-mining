# Finding: household gender-work preferences can change after direct experience

**Status:** comparative household preference and wellbeing finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34969 reports a randomized field experiment in Bangladesh.
Households were assigned a six-week job for either the husband or wife. Before
the intervention, men and women generally preferred that husbands work rather
than wives.

Women’s work improved women’s own wellbeing but not their husbands’ wellbeing;
men’s work improved wellbeing for both partners. At a one-year surprise
follow-up, both men and women in households where women had previously worked
were more likely to prefer that the woman take the job and expressed fewer
concerns about women’s employment.

The comparative cultural insight is:

> A household norm can be a lived expectation rather than a fixed belief.
> Direct experience may change what employment means and make a previously
> contested role more acceptable, even when the first wellbeing effects are
> unequal between partners.

## Limits and U.S. transfer boundary

- This is Bangladesh evidence, not a U.S. estimate.
- The exact abstract household denominator and preference-scale definitions are
  not disclosed here.
- A temporary job does not establish durable employment, safety, childcare
  capacity, or household bargaining change.
- Expressed preference is not actual labor-market participation or political
  mobilization.

The next comparative test is a matched experience–preference–employment design
across settings that measures job quality, safety, care substitution, income,
partner response, and whether changed attitudes persist into actual work.

# Finding 001: Losing SNAP did not mean more work in this five-state study

**Status:** bounded safety-net, work-requirement, and unequal-screening finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES paper studies counties and states where SNAP work requirements
were gradually reinstated during the 2010s. Using linked SNAP and employment
records from five states, it finds that work requirements reduced SNAP
participation by 7% without increasing labor supply. Lower-income people were
disproportionately screened out.

The practical chain is:

```text
work requirement
  -> exemption, reporting, recertification, or compliance process
  -> SNAP participation rises or falls
  -> labor supply, food security, and household adaptation [separate outcomes]
  -> longer-run work, health, trust, and political meaning [open]
```

The simple social insight is that a policy can reduce visible program use
without producing the behavior it says it wants. Participation loss is not
automatically employment gain.

## What this adds to the atlas

Existing SNAP records show route friction and participation as separate stages.
This study adds a policy comparison that tests the intended work mechanism and
finds no labor-supply increase in the studied setting. It sharpens the broader
subtheme of **screening versus support**: administrative eligibility can become
more conditional while practical security does not improve.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper, not a Census Bureau endorsement.
- The study covers five states and the specific 2010s reinstatement context.
- Participation loss may reflect reporting, exemptions, recertification,
  administrative churn, changed eligibility, or deliberate non-use.
- Labor supply is not job quality, earnings, food security, health, or household
  security.
- The authors' welfare result is model- and study-specific, not a universal
  estimate for every work requirement.

The next decisive artifact is a protected recipient-month panel joining notice,
exemption, reporting, appointment, case closure, employment, hours, wages,
food security, health, household substitution, appeal, and later participation.

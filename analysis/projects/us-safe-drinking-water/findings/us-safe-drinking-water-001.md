# Finding 001: Drinking-water coverage can be broad while responsibility and replacement risk remain uneven

**Status:** bounded drinking-water infrastructure and household-responsibility finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

EPA's national drinking-water assessment estimates about 4 million lead
service lines and $625 billion in drinking-water infrastructure needs over 20
years. The service-line estimate combines reported lines with projected lines
whose materials are unknown; it is not a count of households currently exposed
or already protected.

CDC reports that about 9 in 10 people receive water from a community system,
while roughly 1 in 8 use a private well. About 1 in 5 sampled private wells
had at least one chemical at a level that could affect health. Private wells
are generally not federally regulated, so testing and maintenance fall more
directly on households and state or local arrangements.

```text
water access
  -> system type and governance responsibility
  -> inventory, testing, replacement, or household maintenance
  -> safe and reliable service [open at household level]
```

## Why it matters

This adds a public-service version of the atlas's formal-capacity problem. Most
people are connected to a regulated community system, but a smaller private-
well population carries a different monitoring and repair burden. At the same
time, public systems face a large long-term replacement need. Infrastructure
coverage is therefore not the same as equal protection or equal control.

## Limits and counterexamples

- EPA's service-line figures include projections and unknown-material inference.
- The $625 billion figure is an assessed long-term need, not annual spending or
  a household bill.
- CDC's contamination estimate applies to sampled private wells and does not
  establish illness or exposure for every well user.
- The sources do not follow a household through notice, testing, replacement,
  filtration, cost, or health outcome.
- Private wells can provide local control and safe water when maintained; the
  comparison is not a claim that community systems always perform better.

## Next test

Join a place-level water-system or private-well record to service-line material,
testing or notice, replacement or treatment, household cost, outage or boil
notice, and later health or trust—without turning national need into a household
harm rate.

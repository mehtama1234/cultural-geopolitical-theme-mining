# Project: twin earnings, employer sorting, and social interaction

This project separates family-linked earnings persistence from employer
sorting and social interaction. It treats heritability as a population
parameter, not an individual explanation or destiny.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35746, linking about 44,000
U.S. twin pairs observed as adolescents to earnings about 20 years later. It
reports estimated heritability of log earnings around 0.36 and finds that
employer-specific pay-premium heritability is mainly driven by same-sex twins
working at the same firm; excluding such siblings lowers estimated earnings
heritability by 15%.

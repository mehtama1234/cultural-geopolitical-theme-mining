# Finding: a performance framework is not yet a working learning system

**Status:** bounded federal-governance and institutional-learning finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

The Federal Agency Performance Act of 2024 requires agencies to conduct annual
strategic reviews of progress toward goals, including what contributed to those
goals and what should improve. GAO reviewed OMB implementation and four selected
agencies: DHS, State, Treasury, and GSA.

None of the four selected agencies fully addressed the new statutory
requirements in 2025. OMB's August 2025 guidance addressed 10 of 15 assessed
requirements. The guidance was incomplete around some senior-leader and
external-stakeholder roles and conflicted with the law by allowing a
transition-year exception to reporting some review results. As of June 2026,
OMB had not identified the new cross-agency priority goals and implementation
details on Performance.gov. State would not confirm plans for its 2026 review,
while the other three selected agencies had plans.

The broader institutional insight is:

> A government can have goals, dashboards, and review rules without yet having
> a reliable cycle that turns evidence into shared learning and changed action.

This does not prove that agencies learned nothing or that their programs failed.
It marks the missing conversion between formal performance architecture and
management use.

## Limits and next test

- Four agencies are a selected sample, not a federal-wide performance score.
- Missing or incomplete documentation does not prove that no informal review or
  learning occurred.
- Planned reviews and guidance are not completed review results or program
  outcomes.
- The report does not connect a specific review to a changed budget, service,
  public benefit, or trust outcome.

The next decisive artifact is an agency-goal ledger joining statutory goal,
measure, review, stakeholder input, cross-agency contribution, decision change,
implementation, result, and later public or user outcome.

# Finding 001: Disaster assistance can exist while the route to housing remains hard to reach

**Status:** bounded FEMA assistance, housing-access, and state-capacity finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO-26-108154 reports that FEMA provided more than $3 billion to 1.2 million
individuals and households affected by Hurricanes Helene and Milton, the 2025
Los Angeles wildfires, and the 2025 Texas floods. FEMA Individual Assistance
can cover necessary expenses not covered by insurance or low-interest loans,
including temporary lodging, rental housing, and home repairs.

The same review reports that most survivors in the selected Helene/Milton and
Texas-flood helpline evidence faced long waits or could not reach a
representative. Some survivors interpreted requests for more information as
denials. GAO also reports constrained post-disaster housing, implementation
delays for direct housing, and concerns that a shift toward state/local centers
and away from door-to-door canvassing could reduce reach for older, disabled,
rural, and phone/internet-limited survivors.

The safe interpretation is:

> Aggregate assistance is not the same as a reachable repair or housing route;
> delivery capacity, communication, and available housing determine whether a
> formal benefit becomes a practical recovery option.

## Evidence chain

```text
property damage or serious disaster need
  -> FEMA eligibility, notice, and application route
  -> award/payment for lodging, rental housing, or repair
  -> housing availability and repair capacity
  -> [open] safe occupancy, staying, moving, durable housing security,
     later trust, and political action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This promotes an existing GAO disaster-assistance review into the canonical
evidence layer and connects the property-risk lane to an explicit program-use
route. It preserves the separation between assistance totals, communication
reach, post-disaster housing supply, state capacity, and survivor-level repair
or move outcomes.

## What the source does not show

- The $3 billion and 1.2 million figures are agency totals, not a receipt or
  adequacy rate for eligible survivors.
- Selected helpline, letter, and housing evidence is not a representative
  survivor-level prevalence estimate.
- The review does not link a named property from damage through application,
  award, payment, repair, safe occupancy, or move/stay.
- It does not establish food, health, debt, employment, trust, or political
  outcomes after assistance.
- A change in federal/state delivery roles may improve coordination or reduce
  access; the realized effect remains open.

## Next decisive test

Link a property or survivor identifier from damage and insurance status through
notice, application, eligibility, award, payment, contractor or rental route,
repair, safe return, temporary housing duration, move/stay, debt, health, and
later trust or action. Compare phone, internet, in-person, canvassing, and
state/local-center routes while preserving nonapplicants and unsuccessful
appeals.

# Finding 001: Transit can deliver billions of trips without proving usable mobility for every household

**Status:** bounded transit-service finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

FTA's 2024 National Transit Database summarizes a large operating system. Nearly
3,000 agencies reported 7.6 billion passenger trips, up 9.8% from 2023, and
passengers traveled about 38.1 billion miles, up 8.7%. Transit operated in 502
urbanized areas and depended on more than 380,000 workers and a fleet of more
than 177,000 vehicles.

The route is:

```text
public funding and agency capacity
  -> operating vehicles, workers, and service
  -> passenger trips and miles
  -> local reach, fare, reliability, safety, and household mobility [open]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds an infrastructure-realization subtheme:

> A public system can operate at enormous scale while the practical value of
> service still depends on where it runs, when it arrives, what it costs, and
> whether a particular person can complete the trip.

It strengthens the atlas's distinction between capacity and usability. A
national trip total is an operating measure, not proof that transportation
burden, job access, care access, or household welfare improved.

## Limits and next test

- NTD aggregates agency reports; passenger trips are not unique passengers or
  completed essential trips.
- National totals hide mode, place, schedule, fare, accessibility, and
  reliability differences.
- A trip does not identify why it was taken, what alternative existed, or what
  outcome followed.
- The source does not link transit use to a household's work, care, health,
  housing, or political life.

The next decisive artifact is a place- and person-protected route linking a
needed trip to service availability, wait, fare, reliability, completion, and
the work, care, or health outcome it enabled. Until then, the safe claim is
about realized national service and use, not equal mobility.

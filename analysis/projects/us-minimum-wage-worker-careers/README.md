# Project: Minimum-wage work and later careers

This project separates a worker's starting wage from the worker's later career.
A Census working paper merges SIPP panels from 1992–2016 into LEHD employment
records. It reports strong later earnings growth for minimum-wage workers,
especially young workers, with later earnings becoming indistinguishable from
peers who initially earned modestly more. Older minimum-wage workers show a
different, modest dip around the minimum-wage period. State minimum-wage
increases did not significantly change the future careers of workers who were
on the minimum wage when the increases occurred.

The safe theme is not “minimum wage is harmless” or “minimum-wage workers
always rise.” It is that starting wage, age, career trajectory, and policy
effects are separate layers. The project must keep later earnings, job quality,
hours, benefits, household security, short-run firm effects, and worker power
separate.

The decisive next artifact is a protected worker-period panel joining wage,
age, education, occupation, hours, benefits, employer, firm exit, state policy,
prices, job changes, household income, and later security. The public abstract
does not provide that complete bridge.

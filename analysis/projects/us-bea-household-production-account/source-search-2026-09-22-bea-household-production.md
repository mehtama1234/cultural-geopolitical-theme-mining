## Working question

What does a national household-production account reveal about the hidden
economic scale of unpaid work, and what does it still leave out about lived
burden and household power?

## Observed pattern

BEA says its June 2026 update expanded household-production value added to seven
activity types, including childcare and shopping, and added spending and gross
output measures within a supply-and-use framework. When integrated with market
activities, household production represented about 30% of consumption and 16%
of total output in 2024. Both shares had gradually declined over the prior 20
years.

## Working interpretation

```text
unpaid household work and care
  -> goods and services that households produce or consume outside markets

  -> [open] who performs it, who controls it, who bears fatigue, and whether
     market substitution improves or worsens household security
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

Counting household production does not mean that all unpaid work is equally
valuable, freely chosen, or replaceable. A declining share can reflect more
market purchases, different prices, household composition, or measurement
changes—not necessarily less work or greater freedom. This lane stops at the
accounting boundary unless a linked source adds distribution, time, choice,
quality, or wellbeing.

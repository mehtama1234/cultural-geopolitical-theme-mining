# Project: historical risk preferences, institutions, and technology adoption

This project studies how inferred risk orientation can shape economic choices,
civic-financial participation, local cooperation, and technology adoption. It
keeps an estimated preference measure separate from culture, trust, ideology,
and fixed individual psychology.

The first bounded finding uses NBER Working Paper 35634. From historical U.S.
crop portfolios, the paper estimates county- and farmer-level risk preferences
for 1889–1929. More risk-averse farmers leveraged less, were less likely to buy
WWI Liberty Bonds, more likely to participate in local risk-sharing institutions,
and adopted tractors and mechanization more slowly.

# Project: wealth, income, place, and intergenerational mobility

## Question

Does a place that helps children earn more also help them become homeowners and build wealth, or can income mobility coexist with inherited housing and asset advantage?

## Short end-to-end goal

Compare the same parent-child population across income, labor income, homeownership, housing wealth, and total wealth, then connect each measure to place, shocks, family transfers, housing markets, and public institutions.

```text
parent resources and childhood county
  -> schools, housing, labor market, social conditions, and recession exposure
  -> adult income, work income, ownership, housing wealth, and total wealth
  -> intergenerational persistence or mobility
  -> [open] family transfers, debt, wellbeing, belonging, and political meaning
```

The first finding shows that income and wealth mobility are related but not interchangeable. The next test should join county measures to individual moves, housing cost and debt, inheritance or transfers, schools, work, and later household security.

## Writing rule

Say whether the evidence concerns income, ownership, housing wealth, or total wealth. Do not call a county association a causal place effect or treat gross wealth as spendable security.

# Pro-worker AI framework: four deployment surfaces and the missing conversion stages

**Checked:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** bounded cross-source comparison; no pooled effect or common causal estimate

The NBER pro-worker AI framework distinguishes augmentation, automation,
expertise leveling, capital complementarity, and new task creation. Four
existing records make those categories operational, but they measure different
units and outcomes and must remain separate.

| Surface | Unit and evidence | What is observed | What remains unobserved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product innovation | 776 randomized Procter & Gamble professionals | AI-assisted individuals matched no-AI teams on measured challenges; proposals became more balanced across R&D and Commercial backgrounds; AI users reported more positive emotion | durable expertise, authority, dissent, pay, team belonging, completed product success, consumer value |
| Patent drafting | 133 lawyers at 11 US firms in a preregistered randomized field experiment | AI raised blinded drafting scores by 0.34 SD at 10 days and 0.38 SD at 90 days; post-treatment no-AI judgment rose 0.32 SD overall and 0.45 SD for senior lawyers, with no average junior gain | training and review conditions, career progression, compensation, client outcomes, legal risk, worker control, long-run retention |
| Customer service | 138 agents and 256,934 meal-delivery chats | AI suggestions reduced reported response time 22% and raised automated sentiment 0.45 points; repeat complaints and poor chatbot handoffs remained weak points | actual correction/refund, repeat-contact resolution, customer retention, switching, household welfare, agent authority |
| Firm organization capital | US firm-level AI-skilled employment and job-description measures | AI investment is associated with recent productivity growth through organization capital; related evidence shows workforce-composition shifts toward more educated, STEM/IT, and junior workers and fewer middle-management/senior roles | causal attribution, distribution of gains, pay, monitoring, job quality, worker voice, prices, consumer benefit |

## Bounded synthesis

Across the four surfaces, the strongest common signal is capability or
throughput: AI can improve measured task performance, smooth functional
differences, accelerate customer responses, or coincide with firm productivity.
The evidence is much thinner at the conversion points the pro-worker framework
requires: who controls deployment, who learns, who bears monitoring and
transition risk, whether workers retain review authority, and whether gains
reach pay, prices, safety, or durable consumer benefit.

The cases also show why “AI works” is not a sufficient conclusion. Senior
patent lawyers show a durable no-AI judgment gain that does not appear on
average for junior lawyers; customer-service speed improves while systemic
complaints and failed handoffs remain unresolved; and firm productivity can
rise alongside a shift in workforce composition without evidence that workers
receive the gains. Positive emotion or balanced proposals are not substitutes
for voice, bargaining power, or durable trust.

## Next bounded comparison

The next useful fields are governance fields, not another generic adoption
rate: worker control over tool use, training and review authority, monitoring,
pay or gain sharing, safety, actual correction or remedy, and later retention,
switching, or exit. Any follow-up must identify the unit and denominator for
each surface. The four records remain a comparison set, not a pooled route or
an end-to-end same-worker finding.

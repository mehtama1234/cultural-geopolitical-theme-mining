# Claims ledger: US real wages and the vote

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US county economic conditions, household buying power, and elections

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | A fall in buying power is not the same measure as a rise in inflation. | County economic and election data | NBER separates local family-budget costs, nominal income, real wages, and election results. | County measures are not individual records. |
| C-002 | In the 2021–2024 county study, real-wage decline predicts Republican gains more clearly than higher inflation alone. | NBER working paper | The authors use within-state, cross-county changes and report this result for presidential and congressional outcomes. | It is a working paper and does not identify each voter's reason. |
| C-003 | Inflation can still be linked to presidential vote share after accounting for real-wage measures. | NBER working paper | The same study reports an association beyond the pure economic-voting measure. | Association is not proof of blame or a causal vote change. |
| C-004 | Personal and community inflation burdens can relate to vote choice, but the link is weak and mixed with identity and context. | Individual survey and county analysis | The 2022 election study reports lower Democratic support among respondents reporting personal or local inflation burdens. | The result does not show that the price itself changed a person's vote. |
| C-005 | Economic conditions may affect turnout and vote choice through different paths. | Prior county and individual research | NBER work on wages and employment finds different turnout patterns across election types. | It studies employment and wages, not the 2021–2024 inflation episode. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
local prices + local pay
  -> buying power and household room
  -> blame, expectations, and trust
  -> turnout, vote choice, or support for change
  -> policy response and future prices or wages
```

## Next test

Match county price and wage changes with survey measures of personal burden, policy knowledge, party identity, turnout, and vote choice. Keep the county result and the individual explanation in separate columns.

# Source search: US real wages and the vote

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** first matched evidence pass completed; individual blame and household paths remain open

## Working question

Which economic change reaches the ballot: higher prices, lower real pay, or a belief that the government caused the loss?

## Opening sources

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |

## First pattern to test

```text
local prices and wages
  -> buying power and household room
  -> blame, expectations and trust
  -> turnout, vote choice or support for change
  -> policy response and future prices or wages
```

The NBER study supports a difference between real-wage loss and inflation alone in county-level election results. It does not settle whether that is hardship, blame, party identity, local industry or another factor.

The matched pass treats real pay as the main bridge to test. It keeps the county result, personal burden, blame, turnout, and vote choice separate.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

People vote on more than money. Party identity, abortion, immigration, safety, age, media and local candidates can outweigh economic conditions. County averages also hide households whose wages rose or fell differently from the local average.

## Main gaps

- individual prices, wages, debt and vote behavior together;
- effects by income, race, age, place and product basket;
- whether voters know which policy changed prices or wages;
- turnout versus vote choice;
- how long a buying-power loss lasts in political memory;
- whether a later recovery changes trust or only the next vote.

## Decision rule

Join county price and wage measures to household surveys and policy timing. Do not say “voters punish inflation” when the evidence may show a loss of real pay instead.

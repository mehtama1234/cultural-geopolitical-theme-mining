# Finding 001: Automatic subscription redress is not the same as completed consumer recovery

**Status:** bounded U.S. consumer-remedy and platform-control finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-24

## The bounded finding

In September 2026, the FTC reported that Amazon had issued more than $845
million in redress to eligible Prime consumers under a settlement concerning
challenged enrollment and cancellation practices. The revised order expands
eligibility, raises the total potential payment to $200, and uses automatic
mailed-check, PayPal, or Venmo payments rather than requiring a claim form,
notice response, or additional paperwork for future automatic payment stages.

```text
challenged subscription enrollment or cancellation
  -> settlement and eligibility rule
  -> automatic redress issuance
  -> [open] receipt and acceptance
  -> [open] account correction, cancellation/stay/switch, trust, and recovery
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a useful consumer-recourse conversion stage: a remedy can lower the
procedural burden between eligibility and issuance. It should not be called a
completed remedy simply because the program sends money. The official update
does not give a common denominator for eligible people, payment receipt or
acceptance, residual loss, account status, or later consumer choice.

## Limits and next test

- The reported $845 million is aggregate redress issued, not a consumer-level
  receipt, acceptance, adequacy, or recovery measure.
- Automatic distribution reduces a particular claim/notice burden but does not
  show that payment details were reachable or that a payment was usable.
- The record does not establish that enrollment or cancellation problems were
  corrected, that a consumer cancelled or kept Prime freely, or that trust
  changed.

The next decisive evidence is a privacy-protected account or contact follow-up
that joins eligibility, payment channel, issuance, receipt/acceptance, account
correction, recurring charge status, continued use or cancellation, substitute
service, and later financial or trust outcome.

# Finding 001: Firm AI adoption is not the same as worker AI exposure

**Status:** bounded AI diffusion, firm, and work-control finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census CES paper measures AI at three levels: whether a firm uses AI in a
business function, which functions use it, and whether workers use it in tasks.
These levels do not always move together.

During November 2025-January 2026, 18% of firms used AI in a business function,
or 32% when weighted by employment. Workers used AI in work-related tasks in 23%
of firms, or 41% employment-weighted. Among users, most had narrow deployment;
66% relied on AI only to augment tasks, and AI-related employment decreases were
reported by 2% of firms.

The practical chain is:

```text
firm decision or worker experimentation
  -> business-function deployment or task use
  -> augmentation, reorganization, or replacement
  -> performance, headcount, wages, schedule, and control [partly open]
  -> household, consumer, and political consequences [open]
```

The simple cultural insight is that “AI adoption” is not one social event. It
can be a management investment, a team practice, or an individual workaround,
and those can have different consequences.

## What this adds to the atlas

This gives the AI lane a more precise middle layer. It prevents a firm-level
adoption statistic from being treated as proof that every worker is exposed,
that work was replaced, or that productivity gains were shared. It also creates
a bridge between firm strategy and worker agency: top-down deployment and
bottom-up task use can coexist without being the same form of control.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a Census CES working paper, not a Census Bureau endorsement.
- The survey is primarily a firm-level cross-section for the stated period;
  it does not follow the same worker through later outcomes.
- Employment-weighted and firm-weighted rates describe different populations.
- Correlation between AI breadth, performance, or employment does not establish
  that AI caused the outcome.
- Augmentation is not automatically a benefit; low headcount reduction is not
  proof of stable job quality, worker control, or equal distribution of gains.

The next decisive artifact is a firm-worker-function panel linking adoption
timing, task exposure, training, monitoring, schedule, wages, headcount,
promotion, worker voice, customer experience, and later exit or retention.

# Cross-source trust, meaning, and action layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** cross-source societal synthesis; not a person-level join or causal estimate

## Why this layer exists

The program is trying to understand broad US societal, cultural, political,
consumer, institutional, firm, and geopolitical trends. Material pressure is
only the starting point. The wider question is how people and organizations
interpret a condition, decide whom to trust or blame, and respond through
purchase, complaint, exit, discussion, civic participation, or politics.

This layer connects existing source families without pretending that their
respondents, cases, dates, or units are the same.

```text
condition, price, rule, service encounter, or information environment
  -> perceived loss, protection, dignity, control, or unfairness
  -> attribution, trust, identity, and cultural meaning
  -> consumer response, complaint, exit, discussion, participation, or vote
  -> institutional, firm, or political response
  -> power, risk, security, and future options are redistributed
```

Every arrow above needs its own evidence. A source that measures one arrow does
not prove the whole chain.

## Source layers and what each can say

| Source layer | Unit and measured object | What it contributes | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| CFPB complaint records | Published complaint and company response category | A visible institutional record of problems, response timing, product mix, and response type | That the response remedied the case, restored trust, or prevented exit |
| Consumer/platform findings | Customer, user, transaction, review, or service interaction | How automation, seller identity, ranking, privacy, and review rules can change information, effort, and apparent choice | That an easier interface created real power, better quality, or durable welfare |
| ANES 2024 | Eligible-voter respondent | Financial worry aligned with national economic judgment, government trust, perceived capture/waste, party identity, reported vote, and post-election legitimacy judgment | That a particular bill, firm, price, policy, or source caused a belief or vote |
| Pew 2025 | US adult respondent and public news-influencer account | Distinct civic-participation styles and partisan source environments; different routes into attention and public action | That source use caused persuasion, polarization, turnout, or withdrawal |
| SHED, USDA, SIPP, ATUS, and related population layers | Adult, person-month, household, food-security, or diary-day measures | The distribution of financial room, food security, time, work, benefit receipt, and adjustment | That observed hardship became a specific interpretation or political response |
| Firm, public-system, infrastructure, and geopolitical records | Firm, program, place, infrastructure, sector, or country | The decisions and dependencies that shape available prices, services, capacity, and alternatives | That a system-level change was experienced identically by households or translated directly into state action |

## The societal pattern currently supported

Across the existing layers, people do not respond to a condition through one
automatic path. They interpret it through resources, prior identity, source
environment, institutional experience, and available alternatives.

Three provisional patterns are visible:

1. **Material experience is filtered through attribution.** ANES shows that
   financial worry is associated with economic judgment and institutional
   meaning, but party identity and income are also distributed across worry
   groups. The same hardship can therefore be read as government failure,
   firm failure, personal misfortune, structural inequality, or evidence for a
   prior political identity.
2. **Trust is plural, not one score.** Confidence that government will do what
   is right, belief that it serves a few interests, judgment that it wastes
   money, trust in a news source, and confidence that a firm will remedy a
   problem are different objects. They should not be collapsed into generic
   “trust” or “distrust.”
3. **Action is segmented and constrained.** Consumers may complain, switch,
   accept a loss, or stop using a service; citizens may vote, volunteer,
   contact officials, discuss, share news, organize, or withdraw. Pew’s
   Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders show that civic action is
   multidimensional. The ability to act depends on time, money, information,
   social ties, rights, and the cost of exit.

The broad working interpretation is therefore:

> Social and political trends are produced when material conditions and
> institutional encounters are interpreted through unequal resources, identity,
> information, and perceived alternatives, then expressed through many forms
> of consumer and collective action.

This is a provisional synthesis across source populations, not a measured
causal model.

## Keep these outcomes separate

| Outcome | Proper question |
|---|---|
| Material condition | What changed in money, time, health, food, housing, access, or security? |
| Attribution | Whom or what does the person or organization hold responsible? |
| Trust or legitimacy | Does the actor appear competent, fair, honest, representative, or worth relying on? |
| Identity and meaning | What does the experience say about belonging, status, dignity, deservingness, or the social order? |
| Consumer response | Did the person buy, substitute, complain, appeal, accept, switch, or abandon? |
| Civic response | Did the person discuss, share, volunteer, contact, organize, vote, or withdraw? |
| Institutional response | Did the firm, agency, platform, or state remedy, deny, adapt, regulate, surveil, or shift the burden? |

An answer, a complaint, a vote, a purchase, and continued use are not
interchangeable evidence of satisfaction or trust. Staying may reflect
dependence; silence may reflect effort or futility; voting may express identity
as much as material evaluation.

## What is observed, compared, inferred, and open

| Link | Current status | Safe statement |
|---|---|---|
| Financial worry ↔ national economic judgment | Observed association in ANES | More-worried respondents more often judged the national economy worse. |
| Financial worry ↔ institutional interpretation | Observed association in ANES | Worry groups differed in government trust, perceived concentrated interests, and waste judgments. |
| Source environment ↔ participation style | Compared population patterns in Pew | Civic involvement appears in distinct combinations of news attention, voting, volunteering, contacting, and discussion. |
| Service rule → first interaction or visible information | Reported/compared in consumer findings | Interface and rule design can change speed, visibility, sharing, and customer behavior. |
| First response → remedy or restored trust | Open | Faster contact or a published response does not prove the underlying problem was solved. |
| Material pressure → attribution → political action | Inferred bridge, not established end to end | This is the central societal mechanism to test, not a conclusion already demonstrated. |
| Firm/public-system decision → redistributed power or risk | Compared across institutional layers | Outcomes must be followed to the affected consumer, worker, community, or state. |

## The next end-to-end test

Use a same-respondent or same-case longitudinal design where possible:

```text
measured exposure or encounter
  -> immediate material and time cost
  -> attribution and perceived fairness
  -> source environment, identity, and trust
  -> consumer, civic, or political action
  -> actor response and later security, exit, or legitimacy
```

Minimum fields should include the exposure date, actor, price or burden,
alternative options, information source, prior identity and trust, attribution,
action taken, remedy or abandonment, and outcome at a later follow-up. Stratify
by income, race, age, disability, family structure, geography, language,
digital access, and time constraints where measured and appropriate.

The strongest currently available design is to keep the source layers separate
but align them by a common measurement schema: exposure, mechanism, actor,
choice, outcome, distribution, and uncertainty. A valid individual link may be
added only when the same respondent or case is actually observed across steps.

## What would change the picture

The working interpretation would weaken if longitudinal evidence showed that
material and institutional experiences rarely altered attribution, trust, or
action after identity and information were measured; if source environments
did not correspond to different forms of participation; if complaint and
service reforms reliably produced remedies and equal exit options; or if firm,
public-system, and infrastructure decisions did not redistribute meaningful
cost, control, or dependence.

## Relationship to the broad program

This layer sits across the 14-theme map. It joins the material, consumer,
platform, work, care, housing, inequality, public-system, political, firm,
infrastructure, and geopolitical themes at the level of questions and arrows,
not by falsely joining unrelated datasets. It is the current bridge from “what
changed?” to “what did it come to mean, and what did people or institutions do
next?”

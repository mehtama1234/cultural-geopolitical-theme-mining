# Pew source environment and civic-action crosswalk v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12
**Unit:** US adult respondent and public news-influencer account; separate Pew studies
**Status:** reported population/content comparison; not a media-effects or political-causation estimate

## The question

How do people move from an information environment into public action? The
available Pew evidence measures several stages, but not all in the same
respondent and not as a causal sequence.

```text
source environment
  -> attention and familiarity
  -> trust, interpretation, and discussion
  -> sharing, volunteering, contacting, voting, or withdrawal
```

## Crosswalk of measured stages

| Stage | Evidence currently available | What it supports | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source environment | March 2025 survey of 9,482 adults; partisan differences in use and trust of 30 news sources | News-source ecosystems are socially and politically differentiated | Whether exposure changed a belief or action |
| Platform-mediated attention | 2025 influencer study; 21% of adults regularly get news from social-media influencers, including 37% under 30 | Platform-based news is a meaningful route into public information, especially for younger adults | Which content was actually encountered and how long it was used |
| Perceived usefulness/difference | Among influencer-news users, 65% said influencers helped them understand current events and civic issues; 70% said the news was at least somewhat different from elsewhere | Users report an interpretive role for influencers and a differentiated information supply | Accuracy, persuasion, prior belief, and later behavior |
| Civic style | Late-2025 survey clusters adults as Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders using 19 activity/attention measures | Participation is multidimensional rather than one engagement scale | Whether these are stable types or responses to changing conditions |
| Direct civic action | Mobilizers: about 9%; 87% voted in 2024, 65% contacted an official, 77% volunteered. Connectors: about 28%; 87% voted, 13% contacted, 42% volunteered. Outsiders: 41% voted, 3% contacted, 13% volunteered | Voting, contact, and volunteering are distinct forms of public action | Why a person enters a style and whether source exposure caused it |
| Political identity | Mobilizers were 57% Democratic/Democratic-leaning and 41% Republican/Republican-leaning; Connectors were 43% and 51% respectively | Participation style is not reducible to one party identity | Prior identity, material exposure, offline ties, and source use must be modeled together |

## What this adds to the societal map

1. **Attention is a social resource.** People enter public life through
   different combinations of news use, trust, religion, discussion, voting,
   volunteering, and direct contact.
2. **Civic action is not one ladder.** A person can vote without contacting an
   official, discuss without volunteering, or follow news without acting.
3. **Source trust and political identity are entangled.** A partisan source
   pattern is evidence of a differentiated information environment, not proof
   that a source persuaded its audience.
4. **Platform participation can be meaningful without being autonomous.**
   Users may find influencer news useful while the study still does not show
   what they believed, shared, bought, voted for, or could refuse.
5. **Material pressure needs a measured middle.** SHED, ANES, and CPS provide
   separate material, trust, judgment, and turnout layers; this crosswalk adds
   source environment and civic-style structure without falsely joining them.

## Required end-to-end comparison

For the same respondent and issue, record:

1. material or institutional exposure and date;
2. source encountered, platform, frequency, and prior trust;
3. interpretation, blamed actor, fairness, identity, and confidence;
4. discussion, sharing, complaint, contact, volunteering, voting, or
   withdrawal;
5. later institutional response, trust, continued use, or exit.

Compare people with similar exposure but different source environments and
offline ties. Include people who encounter the same source without changing
their judgment, and people whose judgment or action changes without the
measured exposure. Those are the necessary counterexamples to a platform-effect
story.

## Boundaries

The engagement groups are a one-time cluster analysis, not permanent social
identities. The news-source survey and influencer study use different samples
and measures. The reported percentages are population or content descriptions;
they do not establish source accuracy, persuasion, polarization, turnout
causation, or withdrawal. The studies also do not provide a same-respondent
link from material condition through source encounter, meaning, trust, and
action.

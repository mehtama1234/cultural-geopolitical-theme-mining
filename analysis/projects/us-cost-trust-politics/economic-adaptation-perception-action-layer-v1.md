# Economic adaptation, perception, and public action layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-16
**Status:** cross-source population synthesis; not a same-person join or causal estimate

This layer addresses a central question in the broad program: how can
consumer behavior, economic perception, cultural meaning, and political action
be related without collapsing them into one measure of “public mood”?

## The measured sequence

```text
price or financial pressure
  -> buying, saving, borrowing, work, or use changes
  -> personal and national economic judgments
  -> trust, blame, identity, and perceived fairness
  -> discussion, sharing, contacting, volunteering, voting, or withdrawal
```

The source families measure different arrows and different populations. The
sequence is therefore a research model, not a completed causal chain.

## Three distinct population layers

| Layer | Source and unit | What is measured | Result currently available | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer adaptation | Federal Reserve 2025 SHED, US adults; 2024–2025 recontacted respondent panel | Responses to higher prices and financial condition, including persistence and re-entry by later income band and condition path | 77% of adults reported at least one response. In the 4,419-person panel layer, worsening paths generally had more persistence and re-entry into cheaper purchases, reduced use, saving cuts, borrowing, and delayed purchases than improving paths, but the cells were not a universal income gradient. | Which price caused which response, or whether the response changed trust or politics. Later income band is not baseline wealth or liquid cash. |
| Economic perception and institutional meaning | ANES 2024, eligible-voter respondent | Financial worry, national economic judgment, government trust, perceived concentrated interests/waste, party identity, and reported vote | 72.6% of the extremely worried said the national economy got worse, versus 44.5% of those not at all worried. Among the same groups, 67.7% versus 51.3% said they trusted the federal government only some of the time or never. | That a particular bill or price caused the judgment, trust response, or vote; party identity and information are alternative explanations. |
| Civic and information action | Pew 2025, US adult respondent and public account analysis | Voting, volunteering, contacting officials, discussion, news attention, sharing, and source use | Pew identifies Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders. About 9% were Mobilizers, 28% Connectors, and 41% of Outsiders said they voted in 2024. | Permanent social types, platform causation, persuasion, polarization, or a universal participation decline. |

## The key separation

The same broad economic climate can produce several different responses:

- **Consumer response:** substitute, reduce use, delay, borrow, save less, or
  work more.
- **Perceptual response:** judge the national economy as worse or better.
- **Institutional response:** trust government, perceive capture, or judge
  waste and competence.
- **Cultural response:** interpret the condition through identity, fairness,
  dignity, deservingness, blame, or belonging.
- **Public action:** discuss, share, contact, volunteer, vote, organize, or
  withdraw.

Buying cheaper goods is not evidence of a political opinion. Voting is not
proof of a specific price experience. Nonparticipation is not proof of apathy:
it may reflect time, information, access, distrust, dependence, or the cost of
acting. These distinctions are necessary for identifying societal trends.

## What the comparison supports

1. **Adaptation is widespread but not uniform.** SHED shows that price pressure
   enters everyday life through a menu of consumer, financial, and labor
   adjustments, not one hardship score. The 2024–2025 panel also shows that
   persistence and re-entry vary jointly with reported income band and changing
   financial condition; borrowing, emergency-fund use, reduced use, and work
   responses do not form one universal income gradient.
2. **Personal experience and national judgment can diverge.** Federal Reserve
   SHED reports that 73% of adults said they were doing okay or living
   comfortably, while the national economic view was much less favorable. ANES
   likewise shows a gradient between personal financial worry and national
   judgment, but does not identify the cause.
3. **Economic perception is filtered through institutional meaning.** ANES
   shows different trust, capture, and waste judgments across worry and party
   groups. The interpretation is not simply a direct readout of income.
4. **Action is plural.** Pew's engagement groups show that voting, contacting,
   volunteering, news use, discussion, and sharing combine in different ways.
   Political action must therefore be measured beside consumer and civic
   actions, not substituted for them.

## Status of the arrows

| Arrow | Current status | Safe conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Price pressure → consumer adaptation | Reported population evidence | Many adults report changing purchases, use, saving, borrowing, or work. |
| Financial worry ↔ national judgment | Observed ANES association | Worry groups differ in national economic judgments. |
| Worry ↔ trust, capture, and waste judgments | Observed ANES association | Institutional meanings vary with worry, but party and other composition differences matter. |
| Source environment ↔ civic participation style | Compared Pew population patterns | People participate through distinct combinations of attention and action. |
| Adaptation → interpretation → public action | Open cross-source bridge | The exact responsibility, information, identity, and action path remains unmeasured end to end. |

## Strongest next test

Use the SHED 2024–2025 panel where its panel weights support a valid within-
respondent comparison. Measure whether a reported adaptation persists or
reverses, then separately record national judgment, trust, attribution, source
use, and civic or political action. The ideal instrument records:

1. dated price, bill, job, or benefit exposure;
2. exact adjustment and what need it displaced;
3. who the respondent thinks is responsible;
4. prior identity, trust, information source, and available alternatives;
5. consumer, civic, and political action separately;
6. remedy, later security, and whether the interpretation persisted.

If the panel cannot identify the needed middle steps, preserve this as a
cross-source perception/action pattern rather than claiming economic voting,
polarization, populism, or declining trust.

## Counterevidence

The working interpretation would weaken if consumer adaptations were unrelated
to measured financial change after accounting for other shocks; if national
judgment and trust did not vary with financial worry once identity and
information were controlled; if people encountering different source
environments showed the same action patterns; or if longitudinal data showed
that material and institutional encounters rarely changed attribution or
action.

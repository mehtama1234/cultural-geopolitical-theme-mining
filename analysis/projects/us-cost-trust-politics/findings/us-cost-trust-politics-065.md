# Finding 065: Wanting political change is not the same as believing change can happen

**Status:** bounded reform-demand and political-feasibility finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

Pew's July 2026 analysis brings two ideas together. About 77% of Americans in a
2025 survey said the political system needs major changes or complete reform, but
only 27% were confident that such changes could happen. Pew describes roughly
half of Americans as “pessimistic reformers”: people who want major change but
do not expect it to be achievable.

Support for particular reforms is also often broad. Pew reports 87% support for
congressional term limits, 79% for maximum ages for federal elected officials,
72% for limits on campaign spending, and 63% for choosing the president by
national popular vote. These figures come from different surveys and should not
be treated as one unified reform program.

```text
see a political problem
  -> want major change
  -> doubt change is achievable
  -> reform, adaptation, withdrawal, or symbolic support [open]
```

## Why it matters

This sharpens the democracy theme. A public can be dissatisfied and still want
repair, while also believing that the route to repair is blocked. That is a
different condition from simple apathy. It may produce pressure for change,
support for outsider candidates, local workarounds, disengagement, or repeated
symbolic approval without sustained coordination—but this survey does not tell
us which path occurs.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The measures combine a 2025 public-opinion result with separate 2023 and 2024
  reform questions.
- Support for an abstract reform does not show intensity, shared implementation
  details, coalition-building, or willingness to bear costs.
- Perceived constitutional difficulty and partisan conflict may lower
  feasibility confidence without reducing democratic commitment.
- “Pessimistic reformer” is a descriptive survey category, not a stable identity
  or a forecast of voting, protest, or withdrawal.
- Congress.gov amendment proposals show formal activity, not enactment or public
  coordination.

## Next test

Pair reform attitudes with a dated institutional episode: identify the concrete
problem, the reform route people support, the information or organizing effort,
the response from institutions, and whether participants continue, switch
strategy, or exit. Preserve people who want change but remain active alongside
people who want change but do not act.

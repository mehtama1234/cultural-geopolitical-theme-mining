# Finding 052: People use different information systems for different parts of life

**Status:** bounded U.S. information and institutional-meaning finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The bounded finding

In a March 2026 Pew survey, 37% of U.S. adults named news media as their main
source for healthcare-system information, compared with 18% government sources,
13% social media, and 13% friends or family. For foreign policy, news media
rose to 51%. For Social Security, news media and government sources were tied
at 31% each. For federal regulation of technology and media companies, social
media was the most prominent non-news route at 19%, while 44% named news media
and 10% government sources.

## Why it matters

There is no single “information environment” in the practical sense. People
appear to use different combinations of media, government, social networks,
school, and personal contacts depending on the topic and the kind of decision
they face.

```text
topic or need
  -> available information route
  -> source interpretation and trust
  -> understanding, choice, or political meaning
  -> action, compliance, consumer behavior, or legitimacy [open]
```

This matters for public systems and consumers: a government notice may compete
with news and social media; a health decision may travel through family and
platforms; and a technology-regulation debate may be encountered on the very
platforms being regulated. Source choice is therefore part of institutional
access, not just a media preference.

## Evidence boundary

The survey measures the main reported source, not accuracy, depth, trust,
exposure frequency, algorithmic ranking, persuasion, or action. Respondents can
use several sources, and “main source” does not mean exclusive reliance or
agreement.

## Next test

Follow a dated policy, health, consumer, or geopolitical information episode
from source exposure through understanding, confidence, action, correction,
and later trust. Keep source choice, source credibility, actual knowledge,
behavior, and institutional response as separate stages.

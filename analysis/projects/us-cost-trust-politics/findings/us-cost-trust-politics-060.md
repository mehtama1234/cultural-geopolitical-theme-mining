# Finding 060: Trust in public information can weaken while checking political and AI claims remains difficult

**Status:** bounded information-legitimacy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-21

## The bounded finding

AP-NORC's September 2026 *State of the Facts* survey reports declining trust
in federal government information across major issues compared with 2024. For
example, trust in federal information about inflation and the cost of living
fell from 26% to 18%, health care from 26% to 16%, and elections and politics
from 22% to 13%.

The same survey reports difficulty checking political information: 37% said it
was difficult to know whether information they encounter was true, 30% said it
was difficult to find factual information about candidates and their positions,
and 43% said it was difficult to determine whether candidates' statements were
true.

AI adds another layer. Forty-seven percent were more concerned than excited
about AI's effect on how they get information, only 9% were extremely or very
confident that AI, chatbot, or search-result information was reliable and
factual, and only 20% said it was easy to verify AI-generated information.
The public therefore sees possible help with complex information alongside a
harder verification problem.

```text
public information environment
  -> trust in official information and ability to check claims
  -> political judgment and candidate evaluation
  -> AI-assisted search, confidence, correction, or action [open]
```

## Why it matters

This adds a public-information mechanism to the atlas. People may not merely
disagree about facts; they may also lack confidence that official information
is reliable or that candidate and AI claims can be checked efficiently. That
can increase the time and attention cost of political judgment. It does not
show that misinformation caused any election or that AI has displaced human
verification.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The survey is cross-sectional; it does not follow a person from exposure to
  search, correction, persuasion, voting, or withdrawal.
- Trust in government information is not the same as factual accuracy or
  government performance.
- Difficulty checking a claim is self-reported, not a timed or audited task.
- Concern about AI can coexist with use, and low confidence can coexist with
  practical dependence on search or chat tools.
- The 2024 comparison may reflect changing issue salience, political context,
  question conditions, or actual information experience; the source does not
  isolate the cause.

## Next test

Follow a lawful respondent or information episode from a concrete government,
candidate, news, or AI claim through source choice, verification effort,
correction, confidence, and later political action. Preserve people who do not
seek more information and those who act despite low confidence.

# Finding 069: Business value judgments can converge while DEI judgments remain divided

**Status:** bounded firm-culture and political-perception finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The Bentley University-Gallup Business in Society survey reports that 71% of
Americans say businesses with diverse workforces are more profitable and 75%
say they make more innovative products. Those shares rose from 61% and 64%
respectively in 2025.

Seventy-two percent say promoting diversity, equity, and inclusion is an
important business priority, but only 34% rate businesses good or excellent at
doing it. The gap is a public judgment that businesses should do more; it is
not a measured productivity or workplace-outcome gap.

The partisan pattern is mixed. The perceived profitability benefit is 92%
among Democrats, 70% among independents, and 41% among Republicans. Gallup
also reports that Republicans remain more skeptical about the innovation and
profitability claims, even as perceptions became more positive across all
three groups.

The route is:

```text
workplace experience, ideology, information, or firm messaging
  -> belief about diversity and business performance
  -> priority and performance judgment
  -> employer practice, worker experience, trust, or political meaning [open]
```

The safe interpretation is:

> People can increasingly agree that diversity may help business while still
> disagreeing about DEI as a priority, what firms are doing, and what should
> count as success.

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a firm-side cultural layer. It separates perceived business value from
institutional legitimacy and actual workplace conditions. It also provides a
counterexample to a simple polarization story: views can move toward agreement
on an instrumental business claim while remaining divided on the political
meaning and implementation of DEI.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The source measures public perceptions, not firm profit, innovation, hiring,
  retention, pay, promotion, or worker voice.
- The release does not identify respondents' workplaces or direct exposure to
  diversity practices.
- A person may support diverse teams in principle while opposing a particular
  program or distrusting a company's implementation.
- Party differences do not establish that party identity caused the judgment.
- A belief that diversity improves performance does not show that a firm can
  implement it fairly or that workers experience better conditions.

## Next decisive test

The next useful artifact is a linked worker-firm route joining workforce
composition, stated business rationale, hiring and promotion, pay, task
control, employee experience, product or productivity measures, and later
worker trust or voice. Until then, the safe claim is about changing public
judgments, not actual firm performance or workplace equality.

# Finding 071: Neighborhood social infrastructure is unevenly available and connected to belonging

**Status:** bounded neighborhood social-infrastructure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

The 2025 American Neighbor Survey reports that 40% of adults talked with
neighbors at least a few times a week and 40% worked with neighbors during the
prior year to fix a problem or improve their community. Trust was higher in the
aggregate (72%), but varied by education: 64% among adults with a high school
degree or less versus 81% among college graduates.

The source also reports a place-resource gradient. Twenty-seven percent of
people reporting no listed neighborhood amenity within a 20-minute walk talked
with neighbors weekly, compared with 43% among people reporting six or more
such places. Parents without a college degree were less likely than college-
educated parents to say they were comfortable asking a neighbor to watch a
child in an emergency (39% versus 56%).

The safe interpretation is:

> Neighborhood connection is a practical social resource, but its availability,
> trust, and usable mutual-aid capacity are unevenly distributed by education,
> housing, life stage, and local amenities.

## Evidence chain

```text
housing, education, mobility, time, and nearby social infrastructure
  -> opportunities for contact and neighbor trust
  -> stated cooperation and willingness to ask for help
  -> [open] actual aid, burden-sharing, recovery, and collective action
```

The survey does not show whether amenities cause interaction. It also does not
show whether stated willingness becomes help when a household faces a crisis.

## Why it matters to the broader atlas

This adds a middle layer between place conditions and political/cultural
meaning. A neighborhood can contain social capacity without that capacity
being equally reachable. It also gives the atlas a counterweight to purely
institutional trust measures: local trust may coexist with low federal trust,
while practical support depends on reciprocity, time, proximity, and comfort
asking for help.

Keep these states separate:

```text
amenity access       != causal social infrastructure
neighbor trust       != actual assistance
willingness to help  != received or adequate help
interaction          != collective problem-solving success
belonging            != political action
```

## Next decisive test

Follow a named place or household through a dated care need, service outage,
heat event, migration, or housing shock: contact, help requested, help
delivered, time and money transferred, formal assistance, recovery, and later
trust or collective action. Preserve renters, apartment residents, young
adults, noncollege parents, and places with few third spaces as explicit
counterexamples.

# Finding 040: Income, poverty, and insurance measures move on different household-security clocks

**Status:** provisional national household-security baseline · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

## The bounded finding

The Census 2025 CPS ASEC release reports real median household income of
$87,460, described as the highest in the series beginning in 1967. It also
reports an official poverty rate of 10.2%—34.5 million people—including a
13.4% child official-poverty rate, a Supplemental Poverty Measure rate of
13.1%, and 7.9% of people uninsured for the entire year, or 26.7 million.

These measures answer different questions. Official poverty uses pretax money
income and thresholds; SPM incorporates taxes, noncash benefits, work and
medical expenses, and geographic housing costs; full-year uninsured status
measures coverage duration, not access or affordability.

The safe interpretation is:

> A high or rising national median can coexist with poverty, geographically
> adjusted hardship, medical expense pressure, and insurance gaps. National
> levels provide context, not a household episode, causal explanation, or proof
> of usable security.

## Evidence chain

```text
income, taxes, transfers, prices, housing, medical costs, and coverage
  -> median/resource and poverty/insurance measure
  -> household adaptation, care/use, and material security
  -> [open] trust, political attribution, action, and recovery
```

## What the public record supplies

| Measure | 2025 result | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---:|---|
| Real median household income | $87,460 | Median is not disposable income, liquid room, consumption, wealth, or equal gain |
| Official poverty | 10.2%; 34.5 million people | Pretax money income and thresholds exclude noncash benefits and tax effects |
| Child official poverty | 13.4% | Child rate does not identify household care, food, housing, or health pathway |
| Supplemental Poverty Measure | 13.1% | Incorporates taxes, benefits, expenses, and housing costs but is not a dated household event |
| Full-year uninsured | 7.9%; 26.7 million people | Coverage status does not establish underinsurance, network access, affordability, care delay, or medical debt |

The release summary does not supply a common respondent denominator for all
measures or a linked trajectory through income, poverty, insurance, and later
outcomes. The metrics must remain separate.

## Why it matters to the broader atlas

This is a control baseline against overly simple national narratives. A median
income record can look favorable while a different poverty concept, child
subgroup, medical-cost measure, or coverage clock shows continuing exposure.
Conversely, a lower poverty rate does not demonstrate recovery for a particular
household. The next useful unit is a same-household or person-month sequence
that observes the bill, benefit, coverage, adaptation, and later outcome.

Keep these states separate:

```text
median income   != household liquidity
official poverty != SPM poverty
coverage        != care access
national rate   != household episode
lower rate      != causal recovery
```

## Next decisive test

Follow the same household or person through earnings, taxes/transfers,
housing/medical costs, insurance start/stop, care use or delay, food and energy
adaptation, debt, health, trust, attribution, political action, and later
recovery. Preserve households above the median with hardship and households
below a threshold that use assistance or maintain protected outcomes.

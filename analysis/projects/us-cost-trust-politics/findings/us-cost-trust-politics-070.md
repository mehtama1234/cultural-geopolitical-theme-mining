# Finding 070: Americans disagree about what U.S. power is for

**Status:** bounded cultural-geopolitical public-attitude finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

Pew's 2026 political typology shows that foreign-policy views cut across the
usual party labels. Across 10,357 U.S. adults, 71% said sole military
superpower status was at least somewhat important, while 62% said the U.S.
should consider allies' interests even when compromise is required.

The groups combine those beliefs differently. Leftward Progressives largely
rejected the importance of sole-superpower status but strongly supported
considering allies. No Apologies Right and Faith First Conservatives strongly
valued military dominance but generally preferred following U.S. interests
when allies disagreed. Other Republican-oriented groups were more favorable
toward NATO.

The safe interpretation is:

> Americans may agree that the U.S. should matter in the world while
> disagreeing about whether legitimate leadership means dominance,
> partnership, or freedom from compromise.

## What remains open

These are stated survey judgments. They do not show who would support a
budget, accept a military risk, share a burden, permit a base, follow an
alliance decision, or change behavior after an event. The source also does
not show foreign governments' responses.

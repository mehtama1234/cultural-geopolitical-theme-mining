# Finding 038: Institutional confidence remains near historic lows while technology and small-business legitimacy diverge

**Status:** provisional institutional-legitimacy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-17

## The bounded finding

Gallup's June 2026 Confidence in Institutions poll reports average confidence
of 27% across 14 tracked institutions, one point above the 2023 record low,
with five consecutive years below 30%. The average conceals large institution-
specific differences: 67% express a great deal or quite a lot of confidence in
small business, compared with 20% for large technology companies—a 47-point
contrast.

Average confidence is also partisan: Republicans report 13 points more average
confidence than Democrats across the long-running institution list, while the
presidency shows a 70-point Republican-over-Democratic contrast in the 2026
release.

The safe interpretation is:

> Public legitimacy is institution-specific and politically conditioned. Low
> confidence in large technology companies can coexist with high use and
> dependence, while confidence in small business does not establish universal
> access, fairness, or local performance. The survey measures judgment, not
> institutional behavior or consequence.

## Evidence chain

institutional encounter, performance signal, identity, or political control

## What the public record supplies

| Surface | Reported result | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Long-run level | 27% average confidence across 14 institutions; five consecutive years below 30% | Institution-specific performance, behavior, and generalized withdrawal |
| Firm-sector contrast | 67% confidence in small business versus 20% in large technology companies | Local experience, prices, employment, innovation, data practices, power, and switching |
| Partisan conditioning | Republicans average 13 points higher confidence than Democrats; presidency gap is 70 points | Whether party control causes the gap, follows it, or shares another cause |
| Meaning and action | Institution judgments are observed as survey responses | Compliance, use, complaints, organizing, votes, switching, and reform |

The 14-institution average is an arithmetic aggregate across unlike public and
private institutions. It is not a respondent-level trust index and should not
replace institution-specific analysis.

## Why it matters to the broader atlas

This layer sharpens the difference between legitimacy and capability. A firm or
government can remain used, powerful, or operational while public confidence is
low. Conversely, favorable confidence in small businesses does not prove that
every local business is accessible, fair, or trusted by every group. The useful
next unit is an institution-specific encounter and response, not another pooled
confidence average.

Keep these states separate:

```text
confidence != performance
confidence != legitimacy proven by behavior
low trust  != withdrawal
high trust != equal access
party gap  != causal polarization
use/dependence != endorsement
```

## Next decisive test

Follow the same respondent or customer through a named institution-specific
encounter, performance or burden, attribution, confidence, complaint or
compliance, use/switching, and later civic or political action. Preserve
non-users, dependent users with low confidence, and people who report high
confidence without a verified positive outcome.

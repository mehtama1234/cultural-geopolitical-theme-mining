# Source search: arts access, participation, and social connection

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; arts participation is socially patterned and associated with connection, but causation remains open

## Working question

Do arts activities operate as a shared cultural resource, and who can reach
them?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-NEA-HPS-ARTS-2024 | [Arts Attendance, Art-Making, and Social Connectedness](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/HPS-Brief-final.pdf) | NEA analyzes four 2024 Census Household Pulse cycles on arts attendance, art-making, neighborhood access, loneliness, support, and social contact. | Official NEA brief using Census HPS | It is a repeated cross-sectional association, not a randomized arts intervention, longitudinal cultural panel, or measure of artistic quality or community repair. |

## First pattern

```text
arts access and household room
  -> attendance or art-making
  -> social contact and reported support [associated]
  -> [open] belonging, organizational continuity, civic capacity, and action
```

Across April 2–July 22, 2024, 25.2% of adults attended a live performance or
art exhibit in the previous month, 16.3% created or practiced art, and 62.2%
agreed that their neighborhood or community had plenty of arts opportunities.
Attendance was higher among adults with more education and income; for example,
16.2% of adults earning under $25,000 attended an arts event compared with
55.2% of adults earning $200,000 or more.

Arts attendees also reported needed social and emotional support more often
than non-attendees in the same survey, but this is an association. Access,
money, education, health, transportation, age, place, and prior social networks
may shape both participation and connection.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

The source measures the previous month and combines four HPS cycles. A person
may attend because they already have more money, time, health, transport, or
social ties. The data do not show whether arts participation created support,
whether support enabled attendance, or whether either produced collective action.

## Decision rule

Keep arts access, attendance, creation, cost, place, social support, loneliness,
belonging, organizational capacity, and political action as separate stages.
Reopen this lane only with repeated or quasi-experimental evidence that adds
timing and a later social or civic outcome.

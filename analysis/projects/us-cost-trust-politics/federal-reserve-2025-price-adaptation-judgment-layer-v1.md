# Federal Reserve 2025 price adaptation and economic judgment layer v1

The [Federal Reserve 2025 income and expenses report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-income-and-expenses.htm)
adds a current household layer to the broad material-to-meaning path.

In 2025, 58% of adults said changes in the prices they paid made their
financial situation worse, 77% took at least one action in response to higher
prices, 16% did not pay all bills in the prior month, and 8% said family
members sometimes or often did not have enough to eat. The [executive
summary](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-executive-summary.htm)
reports that 73% were doing okay or living comfortably, while about one-fourth
rated the national economy good or excellent.

The contrast is the important result: personal financial status and national
economic judgment are different measures. Adaptation can be widespread while
the public story about the economy is substantially more negative. This does
not establish that a particular price caused a particular action, judgment,
trust response, or vote.

The next test is a same-respondent panel or event design that records price or
income exposure, action, displaced need, attribution, trust, civic action, and
recovery in sequence.

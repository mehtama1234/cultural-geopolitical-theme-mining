# Pew 2026 foreign-policy typology cross-currents layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-23  
**Source:** Pew Research Center, *How foreign policy values and issues cut across the political typology groups*  
**Status:** bounded public-attitude finding; not an action or alliance-behavior estimate

## What this adds

The existing typology record shows that Americans can value both U.S. power and
alliance consideration. This source adds the within-coalition detail: foreign
policy is not one clean left-right line.

In the survey of 10,357 U.S. adults, 71% said it was at least somewhat
important for the U.S. to be the world's only military superpower, while 62%
said the U.S. should consider allies' interests even when compromise is
required. But the nine groups combine those views differently. Leftward
Progressives were the only group in which a majority rejected the importance of
sole-superpower status (only 14% supported it), yet they strongly favored
considering allies' interests. No Apologies Right and Faith First Conservatives
placed especially high importance on military dominance but generally favored
following U.S. interests when allies disagreed. Other Republican-oriented
groups were more favorable toward NATO.

The simple pattern is:

```text
domestic values and identity
  -> interpretation of U.S. power
  -> interpretation of alliance obligation
  -> issue-specific judgment
  -> [open] political action, policy, cost-bearing, or external response
```

## Broader cultural-geopolitical insight

Americans can want a powerful United States without agreeing about what power
is for. For some groups, power means dominance and freedom from allied
constraints; for others, leadership is legitimate only when it considers
partners; still others may favor alliances while rejecting the goal of sole
military primacy.

That is a more useful cultural subtheme than simply calling the public
“isolationist” or “internationalist.” The same formal alliance can carry
different meanings: protection, status, obligation, constraint, or proof of
respect. A high NATO favorability number therefore cannot by itself tell us
whether people would accept spending, casualties, compromise, basing, or a
particular operation.

## Limits

- This is a cross-sectional survey of stated attitudes, not a panel following
  the same respondent through an event.
- Typology groups are cluster-derived summaries, not permanent identities or
  causal categories.
- The article does not measure defense budgets, military service, burden
  sharing, alliance compliance, government decisions, partner consent, or
  foreign reaction.
- Support for allies and support for U.S. dominance are not mutually exclusive
  at the individual level.

## Next test

The next useful artifact is a repeated respondent or event-linked study that
joins typology or value profile to a named foreign-policy decision, perceived
cost, information exposure, political action, and later judgment. Until then,
the safe claim is that U.S. foreign-policy legitimacy is culturally
cross-pressured, not that any group will produce a particular policy.

# Material status, civic capacity, and institutional judgment diverge

**Status:** bounded same-panel cross-route synthesis · **Checked:** 2026-09-19

## The finding

The retained 2010–2012–2014 CCES panel contains two different material-status
routes: reported job loss and reported lack of health insurance. Both are
followed by lower later political contact or local political action, but neither
produces a matching movement in Congress approval.

| Earlier status | Later outcome | Earlier-status group | Comparison group |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Job loss | Any congressional contact | 37.36% | 39.14% |
| Job loss | Any local political action | 23.93% | 31.57% |
| Job loss | Favorable Congress approval | 13.86% | 10.06% |
| No health insurance | Any congressional contact | 31.75% | 40.29% |
| No health insurance | Any local political action | 21.81% | 32.48% |
| No health insurance | Favorable Congress approval | 11.51% | 10.23% |

The repeated pattern is not “material pressure makes people uniformly anti-
government.” It is closer to a capacity/meaning split:

```text
material insecurity
  -> less observed contact or local action
  -> no single predictable approval response
```

One plausible interpretation is that material pressure can reduce the time,
money, confidence, or institutional access needed for action, while approval is
also shaped by prior party identity, attribution, ideology, and the specific
institution being judged. That interpretation is a hypothesis, not an
identified mechanism in these screens.

## What the panel actually closes

- The same retained respondents are observed at earlier and later waves.
- Job-loss and insurance-status groups can be placed before later approval,
  contact, and action measures.
- Action, contact, and approval are separate downstream currencies rather than
  one political-engagement index.

## What remains open

The job-loss item covers the prior two years and does not identify separation
date, employer, involuntary status, income loss, alternatives, remedy, or
recovery. Insurance status is not a dated coverage-loss or medical-bill event.
Neither route measures prior-to-later trust change, attribution, fairness,
institutional response, receipt of a remedy, household recovery, switching, or
exit. The retained panel and supplied weight do not establish a causal,
attrition-adjusted transition estimate, and no replicate-weight uncertainty is
claimed here.

## Why this matters to the broad atlas

This is a useful bridge between the material and cultural layers without
pretending that hardship automatically produces a political attitude. It
suggests that the next decisive same-unit design should measure both:

1. whether a person can act—contact, organize, vote, switch, or withdraw; and
2. what the person thinks the institution did—blame, fairness, legitimacy,
   trust, or approval.

Observing only approval can miss reduced civic capacity. Observing only action
can mistake constraint or exhaustion for political agreement. The end-to-end
chain therefore needs the material event, available alternatives, attribution,
action capacity, institutional response, remedy, and later recovery together.

# HTOPS July 2026: social capacity, material pressure, and specialized authority

**Checked:** 2026-09-25  
**Status:** bounded external-data synthesis  
**Source:** U.S. Census Bureau Household Trends and Outlook Pulse Survey, July
15–August 3, 2026

## Question

Do social support, organized participation, material pressure, and practical
information authority form one common civic-capacity gradient, or do they
separate across groups and domains?

## What the official tables show

The July HTOPS tables represent an estimated 261.5 million adults. I calculated
shares from the published population-count columns, using the same subgroup row
within each table. The release is experimental; Census warns that subgroup
standard errors may be large.

| Group | Always/usually obtain social support | Attend clubs 12+ times/year | Trust doctor a great deal/quite a lot | Trust AI a great deal/quite a lot | Somewhat/very difficult expenses | Sometimes/often not enough food |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Age 18–34 | 47.8% | 9.6% | 63.8% | 16.0% | 28.5% | 22.0% |
| Age 35–49 | 59.5% | 13.0% | 62.4% | 15.0% | 22.1% | 11.0% |
| Age 50–64 | 66.4% | 13.5% | 64.8% | 17.1% | 18.4% | 10.3% |
| Age 65+ | 76.1% | 20.1% | 72.1% | 12.5% | 9.2% | 6.4% |
| Hispanic or Latino | 48.4% | 8.5% | 65.4% | 21.0% | 23.5% | 17.7% |
| White, not Hispanic | 68.7% | 16.7% | 68.4% | 11.4% | 17.1% | 8.7% |
| Black, not Hispanic | 53.7% | 9.4% | 47.7% | 17.1% | 31.0% | 30.4% |
| Asian, not Hispanic | 52.3% | 12.9% | 74.5% | 27.7% | 14.0% | 6.7% |

These comparisons do not identify one latent “civic capacity” score. Older
adults report more regular support and club participation, but lower AI trust;
younger adults report greater material and food pressure. Black respondents
show higher material and food strain alongside lower doctor trust than the
other listed racial/ethnic groups, while Asian respondents show high doctor and
AI trust with relatively low reported material and food strain. Hispanic
respondents show comparatively high AI trust despite lower support and club
participation than the White non-Hispanic group.

## Same-respondent July PUF bridge

The July public-use file permits a stronger, but still same-round, test because
the expense, social, food, and trust variables are present for the same
respondent record. Among 12,088 respondents with valid expense-difficulty and
replicate-weight records:

| Outcome | Not difficult | A little difficult | Somewhat difficult | Very difficult |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lonely always/usually | 1.70% (0.16) | 11.39% (1.19) | 22.52% (3.09) | 22.83% (2.11) |
| Support always/usually | 75.12% (0.73) | 60.69% (1.09) | 43.12% (2.20) | 39.48% (2.05) |
| Club meetings 12+ times | 19.31% (0.51) | 12.12% (0.59) | 5.89% (0.53) | 9.28% (1.12) |
| Sometimes/often not enough food | 1.62% (0.54) | 4.64% (0.57) | 20.81% (3.27) | 33.04% (2.27) |
| Doctor trust: great deal/quite a lot | 74.05% (0.74) | 69.86% (1.46) | 60.23% (2.90) | 54.79% (2.60) |
| AI trust: great deal/quite a lot | 16.07% (0.63) | 16.23% (1.12) | 17.02% (1.65) | 13.75% (1.28) |
| Trust federal statistics | 53.91% (0.68) | 44.73% (1.32) | 36.65% (2.49) | 29.59% (1.69) |
| Congress confidence: great deal/quite a lot | 19.33% (0.62) | 13.11% (0.64) | 12.81% (1.43) | 9.21% (0.91) |

Values in parentheses are replicate-weight standard errors in percentage
points. This is the clearest current evidence for a material-pressure to
social/authority bridge in the pass: expense difficulty is associated with
less reported support, more loneliness and food insufficiency, lower doctor
trust, and lower confidence in federal statistics and Congress. AI trust is
the counterexample: it is broadly flat across the first three expense groups
and lower in the very-difficult group. Club participation is also non-linear,
with the very-difficult group above the somewhat-difficult group but below the
no-difficulty group.

The result is an association within a single cross-sectional respondent file,
not a causal or longitudinal transition. The household condition and
respondent outcomes share a record but not necessarily a single-person
mechanism; the survey does not identify the bill, actor, timing, support
received, information used, remedy, or later action.

## Age-conditioned same-record contrasts

The material-pressure gradient is not the same across age groups. The table
shows point estimates with replicate standard errors in parentheses; the
unweighted cell count follows each estimate.

| Age group | Lonely: no → very difficult | Support: no → very difficult | Doctor trust: no → very difficult | AI trust: no → very difficult |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18–34 | 2.59 (0.36), n=551 → 41.53 (4.39), n=95 | 63.64 (2.75) → 38.68 (4.00) | 75.25 (2.36) → 60.19 (4.97) | 15.06 (1.71) → 11.66 (2.94) |
| 35–49 | 2.13 (0.35), n=1,225 → 10.92 (1.77), n=173 | 72.36 (1.46) → 32.00 (2.91) | 69.37 (1.63) → 48.86 (3.60) | 18.85 (1.70) → 12.88 (2.36) |
| 50–64 | 1.69 (0.31), n=2,001 → 12.99 (2.09), n=148 | 75.93 (1.03) → 45.29 (4.09) | 72.98 (1.21) → 53.22 (3.97) | 18.74 (1.20) → 21.46 (2.77) |
| 65+ | 0.93 (0.12), n=3,759 → 15.97 (2.76), n=111 | 82.66 (0.64) → 62.21 (4.44) | 77.14 (0.98) → 60.66 (4.42) | 12.91 (0.55) → 10.01 (2.24) |

The younger groups show the steepest loneliness gradient, while older adults
retain more reported support at every expense level. AI trust does not follow
the doctor-trust pattern: it rises slightly in the 50–64 very-difficult cell
and falls in the other age groups. This is a stronger reason to treat
“authority” as domain-specific rather than as one generalized trust response.

Race/ethnicity-conditioned estimates point in different directions too, but
the very-difficult cells are small for White non-Hispanic (n=102), Black
non-Hispanic (n=29), and Asian non-Hispanic (n=33) respondents. Those cells
are retained as exploratory diagnostics, not promoted as stable subgroup
effects.

The pattern is therefore better described as **specialized and unequal
authority under unequal material room**. A group can have one authority route
available or credible without having high support, formal participation, low
material strain, or broad institutional trust.

## What this adds to the end-to-end program

This pass supplies several measured stages:

```text
group and material position
  -> reported expense/food pressure
  -> reported social support and organized participation
  -> domain-specific authority choice or credibility
```

It strengthens the fragmented-civic-authority subtheme and the unequal-
optionality lens. It also supplies counterexamples to a single direction:

- lower material pressure does not automatically imply higher AI trust;
- higher social support does not imply generalized trust in every authority;
- high doctor trust and high AI trust can coexist in the same subgroup;
- club participation, support, and cultural or information authority have
  different subgroup gradients.

## Limits and next test

These are separate published tables from one cross-sectional release. The
subgroup rows are not a respondent-level cross-tab linking expense difficulty
to support, participation, or trust. The calculations do not establish age,
race, income, or material-pressure effects; they do not observe whom a person
actually consulted, whether information was accurate, whether support was
received, whether participation produced political action, or whether a
household recovered from hardship. The estimates are not adjusted for age,
income, geography, health, survey response, or other composition differences.

The first authorized-by-public-release July PUF screen is now recorded in the
[machine-readable same-respondent record](data/htops-2026-july-same-respondent-material-social-authority-screen-v1.json).
The next valid test is subgroup-conditioned PUF estimates or a repeated
release comparison. It should
test whether material difficulty predicts a domain-specific authority route
within respondents and whether that route is associated with help-seeking,
community action, non-use, or exit. Until then, this memo remains a
same-release comparative surface, not a causal or longitudinal chain.

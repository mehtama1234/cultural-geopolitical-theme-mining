# Claims ledger: US hidden fees and price salience

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US shopping, posted prices, fees, taxes, credit terms, and price comparison

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Showing the full price can change whether people buy and what quality they choose. | NBER field experiment | Making the final price visible reduced quantity and quality purchased in a large ticket-market experiment. | The setting is online tickets, not every fee or household purchase. |
| C-002 | A fee shown late makes comparison harder and can increase spending. | NBER clickstream evidence | Users facing back-end fees explored and compared differently and spent more than users seeing the full price early. | The study does not measure the later household budget effect. |
| C-003 | Taxes and fees can affect choice differently depending on when they appear. | NBER grocery experiment | Tax-inclusive shelf prices reduced demand relative to prices where tax appeared at checkout. | The result is a specific tax and store setting. |
| C-004 | Firms can target customers with less ability to notice or avoid later costs. | NBER card-market study | Card offers aimed at less-educated customers used more back-loaded fees; fee structure also varied with customer risk. | The study does not show each customer's total welfare. |
| C-005 | Price-transparency rules can require earlier disclosure without banning a fee. | FTC rule record | The FTC ticket and lodging rule requires advertised prices to include mandatory fees and preserves some business flexibility. | It covers defined sectors; enforcement and effects need later checks. |
| C-006 | The household burden is not proven by a changed click or purchase alone. | Synthesis | A choice response can mean lower spending, lower quality, avoidance, or a valued purchase made with better information. | Need final price, quality, income, and later budget records. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
headline price or late fee
  -> attention and comparison
  -> purchase, quality, search, or exit choice
  -> final household cost and repeat behavior
  -> trust, firm strategy, and price rules
```

## Next test

Capture the first price, final price, timing of disclosure, product quality, income, and later repeat or cancellation behavior. Test whether full-price display protects low-liquidity households differently.

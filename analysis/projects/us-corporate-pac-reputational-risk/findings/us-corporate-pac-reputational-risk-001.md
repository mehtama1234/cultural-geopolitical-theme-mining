# Finding 001: Firms may hide a political tie temporarily without giving up the tie

**Status:** bounded firm-politics and reputational-risk finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The finding in simple words

Corporate PACs cut visible political giving when a public shock made their
political ties risky—but the cuts were selective and temporary.

An NBER study of corporate PAC contributions from 2000 to 2024 uses fatal
school shootings and mass shootings as shocks that raise public attention to
gun violence. After fatal school shootings, contributions to Republican House
candidates fell 70.3% in competitive districts and 60.9% in safe Democratic
districts. There was no similar reported decline in safe Republican districts,
no shift toward Democratic candidates, and contributions returned to baseline
within about nine months.

The effect was stronger for firms with greater consumer exposure.

The route is:

```text
public shock raises issue salience
  -> visible political tie becomes risky
  -> firm reduces PAC giving in selected races
  -> public attention fades
  -> giving returns toward baseline
  -> [open] lobbying, policy influence, consumer response, legitimacy
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This is a firm-side version of political legitimacy. Public pressure can change
what a company is willing to show, especially when customers can see and react
to the tie. But a visible contribution is only one channel of political power.

The temporary return to baseline matters: reputational pressure may change the
timing and visibility of corporate politics without changing the underlying
financial incentives or long-run policy strategy.

## What it does not show

- PAC giving is not all corporate political activity.
- The study does not measure lobbying, trade associations, informal access, or
  policy outcomes.
- A contribution cut is not proof that the firm changed its beliefs or public
  legitimacy.
- A consumer-exposed firm may manage reputation without changing products,
  operations, or political goals.
- The result does not show how voters or customers responded to the firms.

## Next decisive test

Join PAC changes to lobbying, trade-group activity, firm communications,
consumer response, and later policy outcomes. The key test is whether visible
divestment is merely reputational timing or a durable change in political
influence.

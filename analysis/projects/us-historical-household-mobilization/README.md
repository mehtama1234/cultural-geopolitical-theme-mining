# Project: Household burden, women's work, and political mobilization

## Question

Can a household's economic responsibility create a route into collective
political action for people who were not already formally represented?

## Decision rule

Keep disability exposure, household burden, women's work, information,
community proximity, political participation, and movement success separate. A
labor-market change can enable action without causing a movement to win.

## Current bounded finding

The [NBER historical finding](findings/us-nber-civil-war-womens-work-temperance-mobilization-2026-001.md)
reports that wives and daughters of disabled Union veterans were more likely to
participate in the labor force, and that towns with more women's labor
participation and disabled-veteran exposure had more Temperance Crusade activity.

The route is:

```text
veteran disability and household burden
  -> women's labor-market participation
  -> information and nearby protest networks
  -> collective mobilization
  -> [open] policy success, durable representation, and household recovery
```

This is historical evidence for a mechanism, not a direct model of present-day
politics.

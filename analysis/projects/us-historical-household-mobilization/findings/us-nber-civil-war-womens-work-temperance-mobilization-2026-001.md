# Finding 001: Household economic responsibility can open a route into political mobilization

**Status:** bounded historical working-paper finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

An NBER working paper links Union Army enlistment records to the 1860 and 1870
Censuses to study wives and daughters of disabled Civil War veterans. It reports
that these women were significantly more likely to participate in the labor
force than women in families of non-disabled veterans.

The paper then uses town-level evidence from 1873–1874. Towns with greater
women's labor-force participation and higher shares of disabled veterans had
more Temperance Crusade activity. Newspaper information and proximity to other
protest towns amplified the relationship.

The route is:

```text
war injury and household burden
  -> women's paid work
  -> information and nearby protest networks
  -> Temperance Crusade activity
  -> [open] policy success and durable political representation
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This gives a historically grounded version of an important cultural-political
mechanism:

> Political participation is not only an attitude. It can depend on whether a
> household change gives someone money, contacts, information, and a reason to
> enter public life.

The study complicates a simple story about work either empowering or exhausting
people. In this setting, work appears partly as a response to household burden
and partly as a channel into collective action. The same person may be carrying
more responsibility while gaining new public connections.

It also shows why information and place matter. Labor-force participation alone
did not constitute a movement; newspapers and proximity to other protest towns
helped convert household-level change into visible collective activity.

## Limits and next test

- The evidence is historical and centered on families of Union veterans; it does
  not estimate a modern effect of disability, caregiving, or employment.
- The study's causal interpretation depends on the instrumental-variable design
  and its assumptions about unit-level disability rates.
- Town-level Temperance activity is not the same as individual participation,
  persuasion, policy success, or durable representation.
- Disability may affect families through health, mortality, wealth, migration,
  community status, and other routes besides women's work.
- The historical movement and gender norms are not interchangeable with current
  labor markets, social media, or political institutions.

The next useful comparison is a modern setting where a household work or care
change can be linked to information access, group contact, organizing, and a
later political or institutional outcome. Until then, the safe claim is that
household economic responsibility can be one route into collective action under
particular historical conditions.

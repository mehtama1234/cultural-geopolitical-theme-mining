# Source search: disaster contracting and local vendors

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** Hurricane Helene, Maui wildfires, and Hurricane Ian; FEMA and
Army Corps disaster contracting  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; contract and data-legibility gaps are
visible, while local economic and household recovery remain open

## Working question

Can disaster contracting be a local economic-recovery tool if agencies cannot
reliably monitor which vendors receive the work or whether local capacity grows?

## Opening source

| ID | Source | What it tells us | Status | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US-GAO-DISASTER-LOCAL-VENDORS-2026 | [GAO-26-108426, Disaster Contracting: FEMA and the Corps of Engineers Have Opportunities to Improve Local Vendor Use](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-108426/index.html) | Reviews local-vendor policies and selected contracts for three disasters; finds weak monitoring, local-area identification problems, and missing documentation. | Official oversight report | The contract sample is nongeneralizable and does not measure local jobs, wages, business survival, repair quality, or household recovery. |

## First pattern

```text
disaster response need
  -> federal contract and local-vendor preference
  -> vendor award and work performance
  -> local business revenue, jobs, and recovery capacity [open]
  -> household repair, income, housing, and security [open]
```

FEMA and the Army Corps had policies to promote local vendors, but GAO found
that neither monitored the associated data well enough to know the extent of
local-vendor use or its effect on local economies. For the three selected
disasters, FEMA's data identified about 3%, or $85 million of $2.9 billion in
contract obligations, as awarded to local vendors.

GAO reviewed a nongeneralizable sample of 40 contracts: 25 FEMA contracts (12
local and 13 nonlocal) and 15 Corps contracts (7 local and 8 nonlocal). Four of
13 contracting officers who awarded contracts to local vendors incorrectly
identified the local disaster area, and all selected Corps contracts lacked
documents related to local-vendor use.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Nonlocal vendors can be necessary when local firms lack capacity, supplies, or
speed, and rapid response can save lives. The local preference applies only to
the extent feasible and practicable. A low recorded local share is therefore a
signal to investigate, not a causal measure of agency failure or local benefit.

## Decision rule

Use this source for the gap between recovery spending and place-level economic
feedback. Do not turn the selected-contract percentages into a national local
vendor rate or into proof of local job creation or household recovery.

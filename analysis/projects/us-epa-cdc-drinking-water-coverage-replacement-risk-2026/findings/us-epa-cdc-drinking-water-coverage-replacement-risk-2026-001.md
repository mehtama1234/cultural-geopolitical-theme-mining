# Finding 001: Broad water coverage coexists with uneven household responsibility and unresolved replacement risk

**Status:** bounded U.S. infrastructure and household-responsibility finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

EPA’s 7th Drinking Water Infrastructure Needs Survey estimates $625 billion in
public drinking-water infrastructure needs over the next 20 years, including
$422.9 billion for distribution and transmission. EPA’s updated service-line
materials estimate approximately four million lead service lines, combining
reported and predicted components.

CDC’s national coverage description shows why those numbers do not translate
into one household experience. About nine in ten Americans receive water from
a community system subject to EPA and state monitoring requirements, while
about one in eight use private wells. Private wells are not generally regulated
federally and owners are responsible for safety; CDC reports that about one in
five sampled private wells had at least one chemical at a potentially
health-affecting level.

The safe interpretation is:

> Public infrastructure investment and broad coverage can coexist with a
> household-level responsibility gap: some people are reached through public
> systems, while others must identify, finance, and remediate risk themselves.

## Evidence chain

```text
aging water infrastructure and possible contamination
  -> public-system or private-well exposure route
  -> monitoring, replacement, testing, or household responsibility
  -> [open] safe water, cost, notice, remediation, health, trust, and action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds a domestic-capability and responsibility-allocation layer to the
infrastructure lane. It distinguishes national need and public coverage from
realized household protection, and public-system governance from private-well
owner burden. It also provides a concrete place where state capacity and
household room can diverge without assuming either universal harm or universal
security.

## What the source does not show

- The four-million service-line figure is an estimate, not completed
  replacement or household exposure.
- The private-well contamination figure applies to sampled wells and does not
  establish individual illness or all-well prevalence.
- The sources do not show testing cost, replacement receipt, notice, bill,
  remediation time, or household ability to move or absorb risk.
- No causal health, trust, political-action, or local-acceptance effect is
  identified.

## Next decisive test

Match service-line and private-well indicators to household notices, testing,
replacement or treatment receipt, costs, time to remediation, water use,
health, and mobility. Preserve public-system and private-well units separately
and record who controls and finances each corrective step.

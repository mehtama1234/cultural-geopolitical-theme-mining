# Official-source extract: drinking-water infrastructure and coverage

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the

## Official materials

- EPA, [7th Drinking Water Infrastructure Needs Survey and
  Assessment](https://www.epa.gov/dwsrf/epas-7th-drinking-water-infrastructure-needs-survey-and-assessment),
  updated May 20, 2026.
- CDC Environmental Health Tracking, [Drinking Water](https://www.cdc.gov/environmental-health-tracking/php/data-research/drinking-water.html),
  July 23, 2026.

## Extracted claims

- EPA estimates $625 billion in drinking-water-system infrastructure needs over
  20 years, including $422.9 billion for distribution and transmission, $107
  billion for treatment, $56.1 billion for storage, and $25.2 billion for
  source needs.
- EPA’s updated estimate is approximately four million lead service lines,
  with reported and predicted components kept distinct in the agency’s
  materials.
- CDC reports that about nine in ten Americans receive water from a community
  water system, while about one in eight use private wells. Community systems
  are regulated through EPA and state requirements; private wells are not
  generally regulated federally and owners are responsible for safety.
- CDC reports that about one in five sampled private wells had at least one
  chemical at a potentially health-affecting level, while cautioning that the
  sample does not measure every private well or individual exposure.

## Boundary preserved

National infrastructure need and coverage categories do not identify a named
household’s water quality, service-line replacement, notice, bill, remediation,
or health outcome. Private-well sample contamination is not a universal risk
rate, and service-line projections are not completed replacement counts.

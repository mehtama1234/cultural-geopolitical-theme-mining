# Official-source extract: HOPE VI and neighborhood opportunity

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked Census paper.

## Official source

- Raj Chetty, Rebecca Diamond, Thomas B. Foster, Lawrence Katz, Sonya R.
  Porter, Matthew Staiger, and Laura Tach, “Creating High-Opportunity
  Neighborhoods: Evidence from the HOPE VI Program,” Census Bureau CES Working
  Paper CES-26-02, January 2026, [official Census page](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/CES-WP-26-02.html),
  [official PDF](https://www2.census.gov/library/working-papers/2026/adrm/ces/CES-WP-26-02.pdf).

## Extracted claims

- HOPE VI invested $17 billion to revitalize 262 distressed public-housing
  developments.
- The study uses matched difference-in-differences with anonymized tax records,
  plus a movers-exposure design and sibling comparisons.
- Revitalization reduced neighborhood poverty partly by attracting higher-income
  families, but the authors report no causal earnings impact for adults living
  in public-housing units.
- Children raised in revitalized public-housing units earned more as adults,
  were more likely to attend college, and were less likely to be incarcerated.
- The authors report increased interaction between public-housing residents and
  peers in surrounding neighborhoods, with larger earnings gains for subgroups
  exposed to higher-income peers.

## Boundary preserved

The public abstract does not supply a resident-level displacement, housing
quality, service-access, safety, rent, or wellbeing account. Child outcomes do
not establish that every original resident benefited, that adult security was
restored, or that social interaction was the only mechanism.

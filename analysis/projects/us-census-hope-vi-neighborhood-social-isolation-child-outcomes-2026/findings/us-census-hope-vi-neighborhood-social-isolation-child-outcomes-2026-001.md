# Finding 001: Redevelopment can improve children's opportunity without improving current adult earnings

**Status:** bounded U.S. housing and intergenerational-mobility finding ·
**Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

The Census CES HOPE VI study examines $17 billion of revitalization across 262
distressed public-housing developments using matched difference-in-differences,
anonymized tax records, movers exposure, and sibling comparisons. The study
reports lower neighborhood poverty after redevelopment, partly because
higher-income families moved into revitalized neighborhoods.

The adult and child results diverge. The study finds no causal earnings impact
for adults living in public-housing units, while children raised in revitalized
units earn more as adults, are more likely to attend college, and are less
likely to be incarcerated. Reported mechanism evidence points to increased
interaction between public-housing residents and surrounding peers, with larger
earnings gains among groups exposed to higher-income peers.

The safe interpretation is:

> Place-based redevelopment can alter long-run opportunity through social
> connection even when it does not raise current adult earnings; a better
> neighborhood aggregate is not the same thing as complete resident recovery
> or control.

## Evidence chain

```text
distressed public-housing neighborhood
  -> HOPE VI redevelopment and changed surrounding social mix
  -> increased cross-neighborhood interaction
  -> child education, earnings, and incarceration outcomes years later
  -> [open] adult security, displacement/retention, housing quality, resident control, trust, and action
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This provides one of the stronger place-to-long-run-outcome bridges in the
current atlas. It adds a protected-versus-sacrificed pattern: children’s later
opportunity can improve while adult earnings remain unchanged. It also makes
social infrastructure and peer exposure a measurable mechanism rather than
equating lower neighborhood poverty with universal household improvement.

## What the source does not show

- The result does not establish that every original resident stayed or
  benefited; attracting higher-income families can change composition.
- It does not measure adult wellbeing, housing quality, rents, safety, service
  access, displacement, or resident decision power in the abstracted record.
- Social interaction is reported as a mechanism, not proof that no other
  redevelopment or selection channel mattered.
- Higher child earnings, college attendance, or lower incarceration do not
  establish complete household security, belonging, or political action.

## Next decisive test

Link redevelopment exposure to resident-level tenure, moves, rent and housing
quality, service access, school and peer exposure, adult work and health, and
child outcomes. Preserve stayers, movers, entrants, and displaced households
as separate units and measure resident voice and later trust or collective
action.

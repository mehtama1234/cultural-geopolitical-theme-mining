# Finding: formal international commitments can outrun usable congressional and public visibility

**Status:** bounded geopolitical accountability finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

GAO reviewed how the State Department reports and publishes international
agreements and qualifying nonbinding instruments under the Case-Zablocki Act.
Between October 2023 and March 2025, State reported 311 items to Congress. It
reported 221 on time and 90 late; the late items averaged 2.3 reporting cycles
late, and one example reached Congress about five months after conclusion.

Public access had a second delay. GAO estimated that text and legal-authority
information was published for about 86% of items subject to publication rules,
but only about 53% met the 120-day deadline. The estimate was higher for
nonbinding instruments (78%) than agreements (38%). GAO also found that users
often had to search separate pages, know dates or special identifiers, and
manually match text to legal-authority information.

The broader geopolitical insight is:

> A commitment can be legally real before it is timely, complete, and usable as
> public or congressional knowledge.

That matters because oversight, partner interpretation, public consent, and
later legitimacy depend on more than the existence of a document. The report
does not say that every delay was intentional; translation, classification,
diplomatic notes, legal review, and administrative errors were among the
reported causes.

## Limits and next test

- The timeliness count excludes classified items and uses State's reported
  universe.
- Publication estimates have sampling uncertainty and statutory exemptions.
- Delayed or hard-to-find reporting does not prove concealment or invalidity.
- The audit does not follow any agreement through implementation, partner use,
  burden sharing, public judgment, or changed foreign behavior.

The next decisive artifact is an agreement-level ledger joining conclusion,
legal authority, transmission, publication, searchability, congressional
review, partner notification, implementation, burden sharing, and later public
or partner legitimacy.

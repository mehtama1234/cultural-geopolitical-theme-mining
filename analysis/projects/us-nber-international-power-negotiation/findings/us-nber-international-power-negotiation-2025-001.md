# Finding 001: Asymmetric import dependence can change bargaining behavior before open conflict

**Status:** bounded comparative geopolitical bargaining finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The bounded finding

NBER Working Paper 34006 develops a model in which asymmetric bilateral import
dependence—weighted by sectoral trade elasticities—creates international power
because one country can make a threatened trade disruption more costly for the
other. The authors construct a country-pair measure over roughly two decades
and combine it with bilateral engagement events and a high-frequency measure of
geopolitical relationships.

The paper reports two relationships. Increases in measured international power
are associated with more bilateral engagement and negotiations. Worsening
geopolitical relationships are associated with countries building greater
international power through trade adjustment, in anticipation of future
disputes.

The safe interpretation is:

> Dependence can operate as a bargaining condition before it appears as a
> sanction, blockade, or tariff episode: governments may negotiate more while
> simultaneously changing trade relationships to improve future leverage.

## Evidence chain

```text
asymmetric import dependence and low substitutability
  -> model-implied international power
  -> engagement and negotiation
  -> anticipatory trade adjustment
  -> [open] threat, concession, substitution, retaliation, and strategic result
```

## What it adds to the broader atlas

This adds a negotiation and anticipation stage between dependence and open
coercion. It complements the conflict-risk record, which estimates how
sanctions-context hostility and retaliation relate to trade, but does not
replace an episode-level record showing a demand, target, response, and later
policy change.

It also gives the infrastructure/dependency lane a sharper control question:
access to a supplier is not the same as power. Power depends on direction,
substitutability, sectoral elasticity, anticipated disputes, and the other
side’s response.

## What the source does not show

- The international-power index is model-implied, not a direct observation of
  coercive capability or willingness.
- More engagement or negotiation does not prove that a threat succeeded or that
  a concession was obtained.
- Trade adjustment may be preparation, diversification, efficiency, or a
  response to unrelated shocks.
- The study is global and does not provide U.S.-household, worker, or firm
  incidence.
- It does not close retaliation, public legitimacy, alliance behavior, or
  durable strategic change.

## Next decisive test

Select one U.S.-linked country-pair and identify the dependence measure,
substitutability, named demand or dispute, negotiation record, trade or
supplier adjustment, firm and public incidence, retaliation, and attributed
policy result. Preserve pairs with rising dependence but no observed
negotiation or changed behavior as counterexamples.

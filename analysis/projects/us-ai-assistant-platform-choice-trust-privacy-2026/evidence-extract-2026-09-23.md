# Source extract: consumer AI platform choice, trust, and privacy

**Captured:** 2026-09-23  
**Purpose:** preserve the smallest source-grounded extract used by the
machine-readable record. This is not a substitute for the linked preprint.

## Source

- Jennifer Zou, “Platform Choice, Trust, and Privacy in the Consumer AI
  Assistant Market,” arXiv:2607.15134, submitted July 16, 2026, [official
  arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15134), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15134).

## Extracted claims

- The study uses a June 2026 sample of 1,999 U.S. adult AI-assistant users and
  weights estimates to the AI-user population using external adoption
  benchmarks.
- Reported primary-assistant shares were ChatGPT 58.2%, Gemini 25.4%, Claude
  7.0%, and Copilot 4.1%. Claude had a much larger coding-task share than its
  overall share, and about 43% of respondents reported intentionally routing
  different tasks to multiple platforms.
- In paired trust comparisons, Claude ranked above ChatGPT and Gemini among
  respondents ranking both. The study reports a large difference between
  Claude users' and aware non-users' trust ratings, consistent with trust being
  learned through use rather than reputation alone.
- More than 80% reported concern about conversation-data use, roughly 60% did
  not know whether their assistant trains on conversations, and 18% had paid
  for a better-privacy plan. The study's regression associates policy knowledge
  with more protective tools than a one-point increase in concern, but is not
  causal.
- In a hypothetical choice experiment, respondents were willing to pay more to
  avoid human review than to avoid model training or sponsored answers; the
  willingness-to-pay estimate for avoiding human review was $11.20 per month
  and rose with task sensitivity.

## Boundary preserved

The study is cross-sectional and relies on self-report, hypothetical choices,
and population weighting. It does not observe provider telemetry, actual
privacy settings, durable switching, data exposure, productivity, wellbeing, or
causal protective action.

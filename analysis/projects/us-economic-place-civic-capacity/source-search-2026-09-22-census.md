# Source search: economic place conditions and civic capacity

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States rural and urban places; CPS Volunteering Supplement respondents 2002-2015  
**Status:** bounded pass complete; material place conditions and civic participation move together without proving one-person causality

## Working question

How did local economic disadvantage, inequality, growth, and the Great Recession
relate to volunteering across rural and urban places?

## First pattern

```text
local economic conditions and national shock
  -> time, resources, need, and organizational environment
  -> formal volunteering or withdrawal
  -> informal help, belonging, political action, and institutional trust [open]
```

Using more than 990,000 CPS Volunteering Supplement observations from 2002-2015,
the paper reports that people in places with greater economic disadvantage and
inequality were less likely to volunteer. The Great Recession dampened
volunteering overall; the earlier rural advantage over urban places disappeared
after the recession. The paper also finds that effects vary by individual and
place characteristics.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Economic strain can reduce time and resources for volunteering, but volunteering
may also be shaped by local organizations, religiosity, social networks,
selection into places, and changing survey or civic norms. A decline in formal
volunteering does not prove a decline in all helping, belonging, protest,
political participation, or mutual aid.

## Decision rule

Use this source for a place-level material constraint on civic capacity. Do not
turn the study into a causal claim that poverty caused one person to withdraw,
or treat volunteering as a complete measure of social cohesion or political
legitimacy without a linked follow-up.

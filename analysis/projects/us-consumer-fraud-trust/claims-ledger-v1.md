# Claims ledger: US consumer fraud, recovery, and trust

**Check date:** 2026-09-11  
**Scope:** US scams, payment routes, unrecovered loss, complaints, and institutional response

| ID | Claim | Evidence type | What supports it | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-001 | Fraud exposure reaches a large share of US adults. | Federal Reserve household survey | 21% of adults reported financial fraud or scams in 2024. | Self-reported and does not measure every attempted scam. |
| C-002 | Loss and recovery differ by payment route. | Federal Reserve and FTC data | Credit-card losses are often not borne directly by consumers; among non-credit-card fraud victims, 63% lost money and 32% said some was not recovered. | Payment protections and recovery depend on the product and facts of the case. |
| C-003 | Reported losses are larger than complaint counts alone suggest. | FTC complaint data | Consumers reported $12.5 billion lost to fraud in 2024, while the Fed estimated $63 billion in net non-credit-card losses using household responses. | The figures use different methods, populations, and reporting behavior. |
| C-004 | The response can become a second burden after the loss. | CFPB complaint records | Consumers report delays, account restrictions, failed transfers, unclear responsibility, and difficulty getting refunds or recovery. | Complaints are not unique people or proof of a legal violation. |
| C-005 | Fraud can test trust in a payment institution. | Synthesis and inference | A loss followed by clear recovery differs from a loss followed by denial, delay, or account lock. | The current sources do not directly measure later trust or service use. |
| C-006 | Reporting behavior shapes what institutions can see. | FTC and survey methods | FTC data depend on people reporting; household estimates capture losses that may never enter a complaint database. | Underreporting and recall error remain. |

## Mechanism under test

```text
trusted name, account, platform, or payment route
  -> scam or unauthorized transaction
  -> money loss, recovery attempt, time cost, or account lock
  -> trust, usage, credit, or household budget change
  -> firm response, regulator action, or political demand
```

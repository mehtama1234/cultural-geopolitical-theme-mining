# FTC Consumer Sentinel fraud and loss layer v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-13  
**Unit:** unverified Consumer Sentinel reports  
**Window:** reports dated 2023–2024, with 2024 age and category detail  
**Status:** administrative report trend; not a prevalence or recovery estimate

## What this adds

The FTC Consumer Sentinel Network extends the consumer-power lane from
complaint routing to reported loss and fraud exposure. In 2024 it received
6,471,708 total reports, including 2,600,678 fraud reports and 1,135,291
identity-theft reports. Fraud reports were nearly flat against 2023, but the
share indicating a loss rose from 27% to 38%, and reported fraud losses reached
$12,537,194,708.

| Measure | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total Sentinel reports | 6,136,404 | 6,471,708 |
| Fraud reports | 2,621,649 | 2,600,678 |
| Fraud reports indicating loss | 27% | 38% |
| Reported fraud losses | — | $12.54 billion |
| Median reported fraud loss | — | $497 |

The age pattern is not one simple vulnerability story: 44% of fraud reports
from adults age 20–29 indicated a loss, compared with 24% among ages 70–79,
but the median loss among people age 70–79 was $1,650 versus $417 for ages
20–29. Investment-related scams had the largest reported category loss at
$5.697 billion; bank transfers and cryptocurrency were the largest aggregate
payment-method loss channels.

## Institutional and cultural boundary

Sentinel reports help the FTC and law-enforcement partners identify trends and
targets. The FTC does not intervene in individual disputes; people who report
losses receive recovery next-step information, not a verified recovery. The
data are unverified reports, not a population survey, and include reports from
multiple contributors. Report totals and category definitions can change as
late reports and new contributors enter the system.

The useful arrow is therefore:

```text
contact/payment/platform exposure -> reported fraud or identity problem
  -> reported loss and recovery request -> enforcement or institutional response
```

The missing same-case fields remain payment dispute, reversal, unrecovered
loss, repeat effort, trust, and exit. Age differences describe the report
frame; they do not identify digital skill, platform causation, or actual risk
per person.

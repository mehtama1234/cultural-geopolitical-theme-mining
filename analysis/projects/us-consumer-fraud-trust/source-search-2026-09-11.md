# Source search: US consumer fraud, recovery, and trust

**Search date:** 2026-09-11  
**Geography:** United States  
**Status:** short discovery pass; no settled finding

## Working question

When a payment or account is used in a scam, what does the household lose and what response does the institution provide?

## Opening sources

## First pattern to test

```text
trusted name, account, platform, or payment route
  -> scam or unauthorized transaction
  -> money loss, recovery attempt, time cost, or account lock
  -> trust, usage, credit, or household budget change
  -> firm response, regulator action, or political demand
```

The Fed measures household exposure and direct losses. FTC measures reported market patterns. CFPB records the search for an institutional response. The missing link is what happened to the same person after the loss and response.

## Counterpoint to keep visible

Credit-card rules can limit direct consumer losses, and many reports do not involve unrecovered money. Fraud reports also reflect reporting behavior, not only incidence. Do not equate a complaint, a scam attempt and a net loss.

## Main gaps

- recovery, dispute outcome and time spent for the same consumer;
- differences between cards, bank transfers, crypto, checks and investment accounts;
- age, disability, language, income and digital-access patterns;
- firm reimbursement, account freezes and complaint resolution;
- whether losses change later trust, use of financial services or political views.

## Decision rule

Pair a loss measure with a recovery or response measure for the same product and period. If the data only show reported scams, describe exposure without claiming a trust change.

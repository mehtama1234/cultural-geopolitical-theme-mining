# SHED fraud and recovery subgroup layer v1

The 2024 SHED public-use file and codebook support a more specific household
fraud path than the published headline tables. Among adults reporting another
type of financial fraud or scam (the non-credit-card path), the weighted share
who lost money, reported some or all money unrecovered, and spent at least ten
hours on recovery can be examined by age and household-income category. The
same path can be split by whether a checking/savings-account incident involved
a peer-to-peer payment service.

Selected results:

- Other-fraud exposure was 6.85% among ages 18–29, 6.93% among 30–44, 10.25%
  among 45–59, and 9.31% among adults 60+.
- Among other-fraud reporters, some or all money was unrecovered for 40.39%
  of respondents under $10,000 income, 53.78% in the $10,000–$24,999 group,
  and 22.57% in the $100,000–$149,999 group. The income pattern is not
  monotonic.
- Among other-fraud cases involving checking, savings, or money-market
  accounts, P2P cases had 46.13% reporting some or all money unrecovered and
  43.76% reporting ten or more recovery/consequence hours, versus 28.83% and
  30.93% for non-P2P cases.

```text
fraud exposure
  -> direct loss
  -> recovery failure and time cost
  -> firm/payment-route response and later trust or exit (open)
```

These are weighted descriptive survey results. The P2P comparison is a small,
selected subset of account-involved fraud cases; the public file does not
provide verified account records or firm-level outcomes. Home-language fields
are available only in the Hispanic module/oversample and are too limited here
to serve as a general language comparison. The public file lacks a clean
all-adult digital-access measure and a general disability measure for this
fraud path; a conditional work-limitation item must not be relabeled as one.

The layer therefore strengthens exposure → loss → recovery burden, but does
not close the route from institutional response to verified remedy, later
financial behavior, trust, switching, or exit.

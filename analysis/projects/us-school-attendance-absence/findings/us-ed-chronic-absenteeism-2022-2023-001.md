# Finding 001: School access can be interrupted at population scale without a single explanation

**Status:** bounded school-participation and unequal-exposure finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

The U.S. Department of Education's 2022–23 chronic-absenteeism file defines
chronic absence as being absent for at least 10% of enrolled school days. In
the national state-level file, 13,406,900 of 48,268,200 students were counted
as chronically absent: 27.8%.

The state-level percentages varied widely. Examples include 22.6% in Alabama,
27.7% in California, 41.2% in Arizona, 43.1% in New Mexico, and 46.7% in the
District of Columbia. The spread is a descriptive difference in the reported
measure, not an explanation of state causes or a ranking of school quality.

The route visible in this data is:

```text
enrollment and school days
  -> absence measurement
  -> chronic-absence classification
  -> school contact or support [not observed in this file]
  -> return, learning, safety, belonging, or later outcome [not observed]
```

## What this adds to the broad atlas

This adds a social-participation version of the atlas's practical-access theme:

> Being enrolled is not the same as being present enough to use the institution.

The rate is large enough to make attendance a population-level cultural and
institutional signal, but it does not tell us whether absence reflects illness,
care work, housing instability, transportation, safety, disengagement, school
climate, family choice, or recording differences. Different mechanisms can
produce the same administrative category.

## Limits and next test

- The national and state figures are aggregate counts, not a same-student
  longitudinal outcome file.
- The file measures absence, not the reason for absence, the school's response,
  actual learning, or later re-engagement.
- State comparisons can reflect differences in reporting, enrollment, calendar,
  policy, and student composition as well as lived conditions.
- Chronic absence does not mean that every absent student permanently left
  school or failed academically.

The next decisive artifact is a privacy-protected student or school episode
joining absence dates, reason or barrier, outreach, services, transportation or
housing context, learning and safety measures, and later attendance or exit.

# Finding 001: Chronic-absence measurement and federal monitoring can fail together when school funding depends on the data

**Status:** bounded education oversight, measurement, and public-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

GAO reports that at least one-quarter of K–12 students were chronically absent
in recent years, with chronic absence defined as missing at least 10% of school
days. All states receiving Title I funding must report chronic absence, and 36
states include it in their statewide accountability systems.

In 2026, the Department of Education discontinued its most comprehensive
monitoring of ESEA programmatic and fiscal requirements. GAO says this limits
the ability to monitor chronic absenteeism and raises undetected fraud, waste,
or abuse risk for a large share of nearly $27 billion in FY2025 ESEA funding.

GAO also found a measurement problem: Education can compare a school-year count
of chronically absent students with enrollment from a single date. More than
30% of low-performing and shared-time schools had implausible rates, compared
with under 2% of other schools; some calculated rates exceeded 100%.

The route is:

```text
student absence and federal funding requirement
  -> state report and accountability metric
  -> federal monitoring, intervention, and resource legitimacy
  -> school response and student support
  -> [open] family cause, intervention receipt, learning, wellbeing, and persistence
```

## What this adds to the atlas

This is a public-capacity finding about the measurement layer itself. A visible
problem can be widespread while the metric used to allocate attention is
unreliable for the schools where enrollment changes, partial-day attendance,
and program structure make the denominator difficult.

It also shows why funding oversight and lived service cannot be separated too
quickly: the same monitoring withdrawal affects both compliance visibility and
the ability to judge whether chronic-absence interventions work.

## Limits and next test

- The report does not identify why individual students were absent or whether
  absence reflected illness, care, transportation, work, housing, safety, or
  school conditions.
- Implausible rate construction does not mean every state or school attendance
  record is false.
- GAO does not estimate the causal effect of monitoring suspension on attendance,
  learning, fraud, or intervention effectiveness.
- The finding does not follow students through a specific support route.

The next decisive artifact is a protected school-year student/school ledger
linking enrollment denominator construction, absence episode, reason,
transportation and care context, intervention, funding, school response,
learning, wellbeing, and later attendance. Separate shared-time and
low-performing-school measurement rules before comparing trends.

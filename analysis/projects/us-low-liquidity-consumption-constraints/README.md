# Project: US low-liquidity consumption constraints

## Question

When people buy less or more than their income seems to allow, is that a preference, a cash limit, or a cost of changing course?

## Short first pass

Separate income, liquid cash, observed spending, expected spending, borrowing limits, present focus, and the cost of changing a purchase pattern.

## Possible connection

The same spending number can hide different lives. A person may buy less because cash is tight, or buy more because waiting or changing the plan is costly. That matters when reading consumer demand as a sign of confidence or choice.

## Decision rule

Move on after one transaction measure, one household expectation measure, and one observed constraint. Do not label lower spending as preference until cash and borrowing limits have been checked.

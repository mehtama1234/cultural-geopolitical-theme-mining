# Finding 001: Defense cooperation can be valuable on paper while its review and coordination system slows the route to joint capability

**Status:** bounded geopolitical, defense-industrial, and institutional-capacity finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-22

## The bounded finding

GAO reports that the Department of Defense established 303 acquisition-related
international arrangements worth $64 billion from fiscal years 2021 through
2025. Both the number and dollar value declined during that period. DOD
officials cited personnel shortages and lengthy review and approval timelines
as contributing factors.

The arrangements are managed through a distributed route: military departments
develop and negotiate them, while Under Secretary-level offices review and
approve them. GAO found that communication about some changes was not timely,
including changes to cost-sharing requirements, and that DOD did not use a
systematic, proactive approach to manage challenges in establishing the
arrangements.

The practical route is:

```text
U.S. security or industrial objective
  -> foreign-partner arrangement
  -> distributed review, approval, cost sharing, and communication
  -> implementation, production, access, or readiness [open]
  -> partner use, deterrence, legitimacy, or strategic leverage [open]
```

The broader geopolitical insight is that alliance or defense cooperation is
also an administrative system. A country can have political intent and a large
nominal portfolio while the internal route for making agreements usable is
slow, fragmented, or hard to manage. That is evidence of institutional
friction, not proof that cooperation failed.

## What this adds to the atlas

This adds a state-capacity stage between international commitment and military
or industrial realization. Existing records show arrangements rehearsed,
contracts signed, systems delivered, or operations conducted. This record
explains why a portfolio can shrink or slow before those later stages are even
available for testing.

## Limits and next test

- The 303 arrangements and $64 billion are DOD selected-arrangement data and
  do not establish a complete measure of all international defense cooperation.
- Declining counts and value are descriptive; they do not identify a single
  cause or show that fewer arrangements reduced capability.
- GAO's process findings do not measure partner consent, implementation,
  delivery, production, readiness, interoperability, deterrence, or external
  behavior.
- Communication and review problems may be offset by selectivity, consolidation,
  later correction, or arrangements not covered by the review.
- The source does not show who bore the cost or whether partner relationships
  became more or less legitimate.

The next decisive evidence would follow a named arrangement from approval
through cost-sharing, implementation, delivery or joint use, partner response,
and later capability or deterrence—while preserving the arrangement's type and
security limits.

# Source search: international defense arrangements as an institutional route

**Search date:** 2026-09-22  
**Geography:** United States and foreign defense partners; DOD arrangements  
**Status:** bounded pass complete

## Working question

Can the United States turn international defense cooperation into usable joint
capability when arrangements must move through distributed review, approval,
cost-sharing, and communication systems?

## Observed pattern

From fiscal years 2021 through 2025, DOD established 303 acquisition-related
international arrangements associated with $64 billion. Both the number and
dollar value declined over the period. Officials cited personnel shortages and
lengthy review and approval timelines as contributing factors.

Coordination was distributed: military departments developed and negotiated
arrangements while Under Secretary-level offices reviewed and approved them.
GAO found that communication about some policy changes was not timely, including
changes to cost-sharing requirements, and that DOD lacked a systematic,
proactive way to manage challenges.

## Working interpretation

```text
U.S. security, industrial, or access objective
  -> foreign-partner arrangement and distributed review
  -> approval, cost sharing, communication, and implementation
  -> joint production, access, readiness, or capability [open]
  -> partner behavior, deterrence, legitimacy, or strategic leverage [open]
```

## Counterpoint and stopping rule

Fewer arrangements or slower review does not by itself mean weaker alliances or
failed cooperation. Some arrangements may be unnecessary, consolidated, or
more selective, and some delays may protect legal, financial, or security
interests. This lane stops at the administrative-capacity boundary unless a
source links a named arrangement to implementation, delivery, partner use, or
external response.

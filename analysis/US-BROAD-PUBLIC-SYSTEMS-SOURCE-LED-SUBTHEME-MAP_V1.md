# Public systems and policy feedback: source-led subtheme map v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** 516 current registry records carry the
`public_systems_feedback` program-theme label. This map distinguishes rules,
eligibility, intake, processing, correction, payment, continuity, and later
security. It does not treat administrative scale as proof that a person
received usable protection.

## What the source material supports

Across public systems, a repeated distinction is:

> a program can expand eligibility, improve a control, or distribute money
> while its route remains difficult to reach, understand, correct, or convert
> into durable security.

## 1. SNAP route design changes receipt before it proves security

The [SNAP administrative-access record](records/us-snap-administrative-access-2026.json)
contains several distinct designs. A Los Angeles field experiment with about
65,000 applicants found flexible interviews raised approvals by six percentage
points and longer-term participation by more than two points. Separate
administrative studies found work requirements reduced participation without
an employment increase, office closures reduced tract participation 7%–9% over
two years, and process-related denials in Los Angeles and San Francisco
increased debt and delinquency while reducing credit scores.

These results use different places, populations, and designs; they do not form
one national causal chain or establish food security, health, trust, or
political action.

## 1a. State SNAP participation is not a substitute for application, recertification, or direct-access performance

The [SNAP state-route record](records/usda-snap-state-route-performance-2026.json)
joins official FY2025 participation, application-processing timeliness (APT),
and recertification-processing timeliness (RPT) tables for all 50 states and
D.C. State participation ranged from 4.7% to 21.9% of population (median
11.3%); APT ranged from 61.24% to 97.22% (median 87.12%) and RPT from 25.23%
to 99.87% (median 93.75%). The unweighted state-level correlations of
participation with APT and RPT were only 0.033 and 0.211.

The named rows show why a single league table would mislead: Georgia had 15.6%
participation with 61.24% APT and 89.35% RPT, Wisconsin 11.6% with 97.22% and
98.64%, Alaska 8.8% with 64.26% and 25.23%, and Idaho 6.6% with 96.30% and
99.87%. The 2023 Program Access Index uses a different, below-125%-FPL
denominator and can exceed one, so it is not a strict take-up rate.

**Supported subtheme:** population participation, initial processing,
recertification continuity, and low-income access indicators are separate
state-system surfaces. A high or low value on one does not identify the others.

**Not established:** eligible nonparticipation, household application effort,
benefit receipt/adequacy, food security, causes of state differences, agency
performance, trust, or political response. These are ecological descriptive
comparisons with distinct quality-control universes, not household-level
causal estimates.

## 1b. A benefit-purchase restriction can change what is bought while creating substitution and stigma routes

The [SNAP sugary-drink restriction record](records/us-nber-snap-sugary-drink-restrictions-consumption-stigma-2026.json)
uses the first 10 states implementing restrictions in early 2026, nationwide
grocery-purchase data, recipient surveys, and a welfare model. SNAP household
purchases of excluded drinks fell 12.4% (SE 1.0). In the paper's partial-
restriction comparison, substitution toward non-excluded sugary drinks and
fruit juices offset up to 39% of the excluded-calorie reduction. Recipient
perceptions of stigma increased 0.10–0.17 standard deviations across three
measures. The reported $1.1 billion annual national benefit is a modeled
counterfactual, about 70% attributed to modeled health-care cost reduction—not
an observed household or health outcome.

**Supported subtheme:** public-benefit design can jointly affect transaction
eligibility, retail purchase, substitution, recipient experience, and modeled
welfare. A purchase reduction is not identical to lower consumption, improved
health, household food security, or respectful program access.

**Not established:** total intake, long-run weight/diabetes/medical spending,
household budget, durable stigma, non-SNAP substitution, individual welfare,
or political response. The preliminary paper does not disclose complete public
sample counts or state-by-state effects.

## 1c. A food-price wedge can leave a health endpoint unchanged when the purchase and implementation route is unobserved

The [Healthy Diné Nation Act record](records/us-nber-navajo-healthy-diet-fiscal-policy-health-null-2026.json)
evaluates Navajo Nation policy that exempted healthier foods and beverages from
sales tax and raised the tax on unhealthy foods and beverages, creating a 7% to
8% price wedge. Its difference-in-differences and event-study analysis finds
no evidence of improvement in studied diet- and weight-related maternal or
newborn outcomes; for gestational diabetes, the estimates are precise enough
to rule out improvements as large as those reported for U.S. city sugary-drink
taxes. The public abstract does not disclose complete sample counts, retailer
implementation, price pass-through, purchases, cross-border shopping, or
household budget changes.

**Supported subtheme:** a fiscal nutrition rule, a price signal, a purchase
route, and a health outcome are separate stages. A null maternal/newborn
endpoint is evidence against that measured health pathway in this setting; it
does not tell us whether the price wedge existed at every retailer or whether
some households changed purchases.
**Not established:** no community benefit, no purchase or revenue effect,
acceptability or cultural response, a universal failure of food fiscal policy,
long-run health, or household food security. Incomplete retailer
implementation and cross-border shopping are reported possible explanations,
not isolated causal estimates.

## 1d. Outreach can raise SNAP enrollment while changing who reaches the program

The [SNAP take-up targeting record](records/us-snap-takeup-targeting-2018.json)
is a randomized Pennsylvania experiment among 30,000 likely-eligible adults
age 60 and older who were not enrolled at baseline. Nine months later,
enrollment was 6% under status quo, 11% with eligibility information, and 18%
with information plus application assistance. Rough program cost was about $20
per additional enrollee in the information arm versus about $60 in the
information-plus-assistance arm. Thirty percent in each intervention arm called
the program, and 75% of applicants across arms succeeded. The marginal
enrollees had lower benefits and less sickness than average control-group
enrollees.

**Supported subtheme:** eligibility information, contact, application support,
successful application, enrollment, benefit profile, and need profile are
separate public-system stages. Improving the intake route can alter both reach
and the composition of people who enroll.
**Not established:** current national take-up, food security, benefit adequacy,
household adaptation, why the marginal profile differs, trust, political
action, or recovery. This is a historical Pennsylvania experiment for likely-
eligible older non-enrollees, not every SNAP population or outreach design.

## 1e. SNAP entry, exit, and continuation are not next-month material-security endpoints

The [SIPP SNAP-transition/following-hardship record](records/us-sipp-snap-transition-following-hardship-2024.json)
uses weighted adjacent person-month pairs from the 2024 reference year. Among
people entering SNAP, 16.40% reported rent or mortgage hardship and 20.80%
utility hardship in the following month; among people exiting, the shares were
11.53% and 20.83%. Continued recipients reported 11.98% and 18.61%, compared
with 3.86% and 5.86% among people with nonreceipt in both months. Entry and
exit cells are small (437 and 387 valid pairs) but retain Fay-BRR intervals.

**Supported subtheme:** observed SNAP state changes and following material
security are distinct stages. Enrollment, continued receipt, and exit do not
themselves establish that rent, mortgage, or utility pressure has ended.  
**Not established:** a SNAP effect; application, notice, recertification,
benefit amount, food security, reason for exit, administrative error, remedy,
or later recovery. Transition groups are selected and hardship can itself be
part of the condition that produces entry or continued receipt.

## 1f. Energy assistance and utility difficulty are not following-month food-security endpoints

The [SIPP energy-assistance/food-follow-up record](records/us-sipp-energy-assistance-food-following-2024.json)
keeps month-t utility and energy-assistance screens separate from month-t+1
food-security reports. Following food insecurity was 51.13% after reported
utility-payment difficulty versus 8.40% after no reported difficulty. It was
32.76% among person-months with energy assistance versus 16.93% among those
without; in the joint utility-difficulty-and-assistance cell, following food
insecurity was 61.73%, with 36.89% classified very low food security. The
record retains Fay-BRR uncertainty, but each cell is a selected conditional
screen rather than a program-effect comparison.

**Supported subtheme:** a bill-pressure screen, energy-assistance status, and
later food-security surface are adjacent but distinct stages. Receipt can
coexist with severe ongoing material pressure, so it cannot be used as proof
of budget stabilization or food recovery.  
**Not established:** that energy assistance caused any food-security outcome;
a dated utility bill, benefit amount, application or payment timing, household
eligibility, need severity, work/care substitution, food quantity or quality,
recurrence, trust, or recovery. Utility and food fields may be conditional
household measures repeated on person-month rows.

## 1g. A work rule can causally increase procedural denials without establishing a work or security outcome

The [SNAP procedural-denial record](records/us-nber-snap-work-requirements-procedural-denials-2026.json)
uses administrative data, age-based exemptions for people age 50 and older,
and waiver-period variation in difference-in-differences designs for the
study's ABAWD populations and jurisdictions. It finds that work requirements
significantly increased procedural and overall denials; the authors interpret
the result as participation deterrence through imposed administrative burden.
The public abstract does not disclose the complete denominator, selected
jurisdictions, or effect sizes, so this is not a national rate estimate.

**Supported subtheme:** a rule → administrative process → procedural-denial
route can change participation before any question of food, health, work, or
security is answered. A procedural denial is a consequential public-system
outcome but is not automatically a final eligibility finding or a durable loss
of support.
**Not established:** each claimant's notice, documentation effort, exemption,
appeal, restored benefits, food security, health, work quality, trust, or
political action. Concurrent administrative or labor-market change could
account for part of the estimated effect if the study's identifying assumptions
do not hold.

## 2. Security controls can add a new usability and recovery route

The [SNAP EBT modernization record](records/us-usda-snap-ebt-modernization-control-2026.json)
documents chip-and-tap migration, PIN requirements, retailer compatibility,
and magnetic-stripe fallback. A separate proposed replacement-card rule
requires notice at a household's fourth replacement request within 12 months
and permits withholding and explanation requirements in some circumstances.

**Supported subtheme:** account security and food-access security are separate
stages; a control can reduce misuse while adding replacement, notice, contact,
and false-positive risk.  
**Not established:** theft reduction, successful replacement timing, completed
purchases, or restored household food access.

## 3. Eligibility verification can become a housing-security decision point

The [HUD verification record](records/us-hud-housing-eligibility-verification-2026.json)
preserves a formal route of verification, secondary review, appeal, proration,
and possible termination for assisted households with status or identity
issues. The source does not provide match accuracy, notice comprehension,
appeal outcomes, continued occupancy, displacement, or later housing
stability.

**Supported subtheme:** being legible to an administrative system is itself a
condition of receiving housing protection.  
**Not established:** whether the verification system correctly identifies
households or improves housing-program integrity without unequal loss.

## 4. Error correction is not the same as learning which corrections work

The [Medicaid eligibility-correction record](records/us-gao-medicaid-eligibility-error-corrective-actions-2026.json)
reviews reporting across all 50 states and D.C. and corrective-action plans in
seven states. States used training, guidance, and eligibility-system changes,
but GAO found CMS feedback did not consistently ensure required plan elements
and did not consistently analyze errors and fixes across states and years for
effectiveness or transferability.

The record observes a public-system learning gap, not patient-level restoration
of coverage, care continuity, health, time, or trust.

## 4a. Outreach can improve renewal and reduce procedural denials, while reach remains a separate delivery problem

The [Medicaid navigator-outreach record](records/us-nber-medicaid-renewal-outreach-administrative-burden-2026.json)
is a Wisconsin field experiment among 92,957 fee-for-service beneficiaries in
80,016 households facing 2023–24 renewal deadlines. In the randomized
prerecorded-call arm, renewal rose 1.0 percentage point from a 67.8% baseline
and procedural denials fell 1.1 points from a 22.3% baseline. Reported effects
were larger for Tribal members, children, people with chronic conditions,
lower-income participants, and adults ages 50–64. The delivery system is part
of the result: the study estimates that at most about 61% of prerecorded calls
reached the intended person, and contact-information searches could confirm
only about half of phone numbers or addresses as likely correct.

**Supported subtheme:** a small administrative-support intervention can move a
measured renewal and procedural-denial outcome, while accurate contact is a
separate capacity constraint rather than an implementation detail.  
**Not established:** a national Medicaid retention rate, continuous coverage,
care use, medical bills, health, trust, or later household security. Assignment
to a call and successful receipt are different estimands; the call arm ended
early, and some nonrenewal may be appropriate after a change in eligibility or
circumstances.

## 5. Eligibility expansion and application completion are independent surfaces

The [Pell/FAFSA record](records/us-gao-pell-fafsa-eligibility-2026.json)
reports 9.9 million Pell-eligible students in 2024–25, about 570,000 more than
the prior year; maximum-award eligibility rose about 31%, or 1.9 million
students. Yet fewer students completed the FAFSA. The aggregate comparison
does not follow the same student to award receipt, enrollment, persistence,
graduation, or debt.

**Supported subtheme:** a broader rule can improve formal eligibility while
the application route still loses potential beneficiaries.  
**Not established:** the net educational or household effect.

## 5a. One program's eligibility expansion can change other-program take-up without proving household security

The [Medicaid-expansion spillover record](records/us-medicaid-expansion-safety-net-spillovers-2014-2019.json)
uses expansion/non-expansion border-county comparisons and restricted ACS data
to test whether ACA Medicaid expansion altered other safety-net participation.
A typical expansion was associated with about a 2.2-percentage-point reduction
in uninsurance and an estimated increase in SNAP participation of about 0.6
participants per 100 people (0.49 percentage points in the restricted ACS),
with larger TANF participation effects among single parents. The preferred EITC
estimate was small and not statistically distinguishable from zero.

**Supported subtheme:** a program expansion can change information,
transaction-cost, or institutional-navigation conditions beyond the program
itself. Cross-program take-up is therefore a measurable public-system effect,
not merely an individual preference or a count of newly eligible people.

**Not established:** the household-level route responsible for the spillover,
benefit amount or adequacy, food security, care access, debt, trust, political
action, or durable recovery. The repeated cross-section does not follow one
household through Medicaid enrollment and later outcomes.

## 6. Disaster assistance totals do not show access, adequacy, or recovery

The [FEMA housing-reach record](records/us-gao-fema-assistance-housing-reach-2026.json)
reports over $3 billion provided to 1.2 million people across selected recent
disasters. GAO also found long helpline waits, confusing requests for more
information, persistent post-disaster housing constraints, and delivery-model
changes that could matter for older, disabled, rural, and phone/internet-limited
survivors.

Agency totals and selected institutional interviews do not identify a named
survivor through eligibility, application, award, repair, move, safe occupancy,
or durable housing stability.

The [EDA disaster-recovery record](records/us-gao-eda-disaster-economic-recovery-outcomes-2014-2024.json)
adds the local economic-recovery stage. It records $2.1 billion in FY2014–24
disaster-recovery grants and 45 federal coordination missions. For the 292
construction/revolving-loan grants with three-year outcome reporting, 55%
reported neither jobs created/retained nor private investment; GAO found that
the measures could not assess intended recovery outcomes and that validation
of grantee-reported jobs had been suspended. This is neither a failure rate for
all grants nor a community-level recovery estimate.

The [state/local disaster-capacity record](records/us-gao-disaster-state-local-response-capability-2025.json)
locates an earlier institutional condition. Across preparedness assessments for
10 selected states and interviews around recent 2024--25 disasters, GAO found
wide variation in state and local response capability; rural or less-resourced
jurisdictions could face constraints even within a well-resourced state.
Officials described substantial dependence on federal grants, training,
technical assistance, and response support, and emphasized clear guidance,
transition time, and continuing coordination when roles change. Even the
best-equipped states were described as needing some federal support in a
catastrophic event.

**Supported subtheme:** disaster recovery needs a distinct evidence chain from
intergovernmental capacity, appropriation, and award through project completion,
usable service, local economic function, and household/community recovery.
Funding and coordination can be real even where the final outcome measure is
inadequate.
**Not established:** a national response-capability rate; response speed;
survivor receipt; a grant's causal effect on jobs, firms, wages, housing,
services, household security, trust, or durable local recovery.

## 7. Short emergency assistance can have a later labor-market effect without proving housing recovery

The [emergency-assistance record](records/us-nber-emergency-financial-assistance-work-outcomes-2026.json)
links Chicago homelessness-prevention hotline callers to federal tax records and
uses variation in fund availability. Referred callers had an estimated
59-percentage-point higher receipt rate (71% versus 12%) for roughly
$900–$1,000 one-time rent/utility assistance. Follow-up up to four years found
employment and earnings gains, particularly for lowest earners, with no
overall labor-supply reduction; address-change evidence was suggestive but not
robust.

**Supported subtheme:** a small, time-sensitive payment can connect to a later
administrative labor outcome, making it stronger evidence than an approval
count alone.
**Not established:** prevention of the housing crisis, job quality, assistance
adequacy, stable housing, debt/health, recipient experience, or transferability
beyond the studied hotline/funding route.

## 8. Emergency rental aid can improve payment and fear while not changing average housing stability

The [rental-assistance record](records/us-nber-emergency-rental-assistance-rent-payment-stability-health-2024.json)
evaluates five lottery-assigned programs in four cities that distributed more
than $200 million during the pandemic. Aid increased rent payment and reduced
eviction concern, with suggestive mental/physical-health gains, but had little
average effect on housing stability or financial distress. Moratoria, expanded
safety-net supports, and a softer rental market were part of the setting.

**Supported subtheme:** payment, perceived threat, health, financial distress,
and physical housing stability are separate outcome clocks. A positive payment
result is neither proof nor disproof of a broader recovery.
**Not established:** effects outside the pandemic setting, an individual
application/landlord/court path, later affordability, debt, or durable security.

## 9. A counted ballot is the end of an administrative route, not merely an expression of intent

The [election-administration record](records/us-eac-election-administration-voting-2024.json)
reports more than 158 million counted ballots in 2024 and 1,736,209 reported
provisional ballots. Of reported provisional ballots, 73.8% were counted and
25.1% rejected; over 98% of jurisdictions had paper or auditable records.
Rules and denominators vary across state and local administrations.

**Supported subtheme:** political participation has a distinct public-system
conversion path—from attempted ballot through provisional status, cure/rule
application, and counting—that should not be inferred from registration or
political interest alone.
**Not established:** equal access, improper exclusion, who experienced a
rejection, persuasion, trust, or later political action.

## 10. A formal public-comment route is not evidence of represented or learned-from input

The [tax-rulemaking record](records/us-gao-tax-rulemaking-public-engagement-2026.json)
reviews 236 proposed and 231 finalized tax regulations from 2017–2026. In a
random sample of ten proposals, all used notice-and-comment and the median
comment period was 61.5 days, yet volume ranged from five or fewer to 11,050;
hearings relied on dial-in rather than broadly available video and were not
always documented. GAO found no developed policy for identifying/documenting
mass or AI-assisted comments.

**Supported subtheme:** participation capacity is shaped by interface,
documentation, scale, and agency-learning practices, not only whether a
comment box exists.
**Not established:** who was represented, substantive comment influence, a
better rule, taxpayer incidence, trust, or legitimacy.

## 11. Public purchasing can yield treatment and fiscal outcomes while leaving equal reach unresolved

The [Medicaid subscription record](records/us-nber-hepatitis-c-subscription-outcomes-2026.json)
studies Louisiana's hepatitis-C subscription model. Relative to comparison
trends, treatment volume tripled and new diagnoses fell 23%; the study reports
$37 million in added antiviral spending and $266 million in estimated
five-year savings. These are program-level results, not one person's full
care path.

**Supported subtheme:** a public payment design can change clinical activity
and fiscal projections, so policy feedback can be observed beyond enrollment
or spending. Treatment offer, receipt, completion/cure, reinfection, equal
reach, and household cost remain separate.
**Not established:** individual cure completion, access barriers by group,
long-run eradication, or national transferability.

## 12. Decentralized delivery can expand reach while dispersing fraud visibility

The [state-administered fraud-visibility record](records/us-gao-state-administered-program-fraud-visibility-2025-2026.json)
reviews 20 large federal programs delivered through states and other entities,
covering about $1.1 trillion in FY2025 obligations. Funds can pass through
states, subrecipients, contractors, Tribes, territories, and local governments;
only five of the 20 programs documented evidence consistent with identifying
and assessing fraud risk to prioritize action, and GAO had 22 open related
recommendations. The government-wide $233–$521 billion annual fraud estimate
is not a loss estimate for these programs, and neither obligations nor a risk
assessment identify beneficiary outcomes.

**Supported subtheme:** flexible multi-layer delivery creates an oversight and
visibility problem alongside any local-reach advantage; integrity control and
beneficiary protection are not the same outcome.  
**Not established:** program-specific fraud prevalence, a recipient loss or
denial, remedy, service continuity, or public-trust effect.

## 13. Recertification can interrupt support through several overlapping time-and-contact failures

The [SNAP recertification-interruption record](records/us-urban-wbns-snap-recertification-interruption-2024.json)
reports that 24% of surveyed working-age adults in SNAP families described an
involuntary stop or interruption in the prior year; 13% said they could not
recertify on time. Among the 182 people reporting that route, 40% cited too
little time after notice, 32% not receiving notice, 22% difficult paperwork,
16% an unavailable interview, and 15% office-lost paperwork—overlapping
self-reported reasons. Interruptions were higher among workers (32%) than
nonworkers (18%) and families with children (29%) than without (19%).

**Supported subtheme:** public benefit continuity depends on a combined notice,
deadline, paperwork, interview, and agency-record route; employment and family
care can sit inside administrative burden rather than outside it.  
**Not established:** agency fault, benefit-gap duration or amount, food
security, appeal/correction, return to SNAP, or causal subgroup mechanisms.

## 13a. Eligibility for a reentry route does not ensure timely movement or usable return

The [federal reentry-timing record](records/us-gao-bop-reentry-timing-2026.json)
keeps individual eligibility, placement capacity, provider finance, and
post-release outcomes distinct. In GAO's review of 29,934 people, 21,190 had
earned time that BOP had not fully applied toward residential reentry-center
(RRC) or home-confinement placement. BOP could not readily identify complete
eligibility dates or the full scale of delayed transfers. Roughly 150 RRCs were
contracted nationally, and 70% of RRC payments in FY2023–24 were late; the
audit reports 65,000 late contractor payments through March 2025 and $12.5
million in interest penalties. Court orders and capacity constraints are among
the stated reasons for timing differences.

**Supported subtheme:** statutory earned time → eligibility calculation →
placement/capacity and provider-payment route → actual transfer timing. A
formal reentry entitlement cannot be read as timely access to a transitional
setting, and late contractor payment is not evidence of provider failure or a
person's unsuccessful return.
**Not established:** an individual transfer date, service receipt, housing,
employment, health, family reconnection, later justice contact, trust, or
durable inclusion. The audit does not link the same person across eligibility,
transfer, and post-release outcomes.

## 14. Eligibility data can be abundant while cross-system matching remains too weak to prove an individual case

The [eligibility-data interoperability record](records/us-gao-federal-eligibility-data-interoperability-2026.json)
finds agencies may use more than 100 federal data sources and 28 were in or
designated for Do Not Pay as of September 2025. All nine selected sources had
missing, invalid, duplicate, or other quality issues, and seven had
cross-source inconsistencies; 2,074 USAspending awards could be matched to
SAM Exclusion records by unique entity identifier, but the match did not prove
an improper award. No government-wide interoperability authority was designated
as of July 2026.

**Supported subtheme:** data availability, matching, eligibility accuracy, and
fair correction are distinct institutional stages; a flagged linkage is not a
finding about a person or payment.  
**Not established:** improper-payment prevalence, a benefit denial, correction,
recipient harm, or whether central interoperability would improve accuracy.

## 15. Measuring who can reach a benefit is itself part of public-system capacity

The [WIC access-measurement record](records/us-census-wic-access-measurement-2026.json)
links restricted WIC administrative records and ACS data to estimate who is
eligible, participating, and eligible but not accessing WIC at state and county
levels, with demographic and socioeconomic disaggregation. It also states that
Census and USDA/FNS estimates differ in method and scope. The public abstract
does not supply numeric local rates, application events, benefit adequacy, or
nutrition outcomes.

**Supported subtheme:** program reach cannot be inferred from enrollment alone;
the eligibility denominator, linkage, place, and measurement design determine
who becomes visible as unserved.  
**Not established:** a causal barrier, an application failure, food security,
child health, or an intervention effect.

## Cross-subtheme finding and next test

The [OFR institutional-capacity record](records/us-ofr-institutional-finance-capacity-2022-2025.json)
adds a financial-governance version of the same distinction: new data
collection, cloud/AI tooling, and institutional visibility are not evidence of
consumer protection, corrected market failure, or financial stability.

```text
rule, eligibility, control, appropriation, public purchasing, or data-governance design
  -> notice, application, verification, matching, comment, ballot, or processing
  -> receipt, denial, correction, payment, treatment, counting, or interruption
  -> usable service, health, housing, work, and household continuity
  -> trust, feedback, and later demand for public action
```

The corpus measures several early stages strongly. The next decisive records
should keep the same person or case through notice, effort, outcome, appeal or
correction, receipt, adequacy, continuity, and later judgment, while making
clear whether no action was an informed choice or a blocked route.

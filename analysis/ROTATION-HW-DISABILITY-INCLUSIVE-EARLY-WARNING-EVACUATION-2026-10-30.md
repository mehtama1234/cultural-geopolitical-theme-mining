# Rotation HW — Disability-inclusive early warning, evacuation and recovery

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When early-warning systems, evacuation plans, shelters and recovery programmes claim universal coverage, do people with disabilities receive understandable warnings, trusted assistance, accessible transport and safe continuity—or remain counted in plans but excluded at the point of action?

## Why this packet matters

Disaster resilience is often measured through a warning system, a risk map, a plan, a shelter or a response operation. For a disabled person, the practical chain is longer and more relational:

```text
hazard / forecast
  -> warning received in an accessible form
  -> meaning understood and trusted
  -> support, transport, medication and assistive-device plan
  -> accessible evacuation / shelter / water / sanitation
  -> family, care, school and income continuity
  -> recovery voice, replacement and durable safety
```

The theme is geographical because terrain, distance, communications and infrastructure determine reach; cultural because trust, language, household care and disabled people’s organizations shape response; and geopolitical because conflict, displacement, humanitarian coordination and uneven state capacity alter who can obtain protection.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| HW1 | UNDRR’s 2025 global status report says more than 60% of countries report multi-hazard early-warning systems and that mortality is nearly six times lower in countries with more comprehensive systems than in those with limited capabilities. | Global system presence and an association with mortality. | Disabled-person receipt, comprehension, evacuation, causal attribution or subgroup equity. |
| HW2 | UNDRR’s inclusive early-warning checklist requires governance, risk knowledge, detection/forecasting, dissemination and preparedness to respond to be gender-responsive and disability-inclusive. | Normative architecture and the full warning-chain design. | Implementation, accessible formats, local trust or completed protective action. |
| HW3 | UNDRR’s 2025 annual report describes Baguio City, Philippines, work enabling persons with disabilities and their organizations to lead disaster-risk governance and early-warning design. | Named participation and governance mechanism. | Warning performance, evacuation, shelter access, survival, recovery or repeat influence. |
| HW4 | UNDRR’s diverse-context case study reports inclusivity work in Mali, Sudan, Cambodia and Latin America/Caribbean; Cambodia’s EWS1294 assessment from 2022–2025 led to national guidance on gender equality and social inclusion. | Multi-place system assessment and policy response. | Individual receipt, interpretation, action, disability subgroup performance or outcome. |
| HW5 | UNICEF states children with disabilities are among the most marginalized in emergencies and can be cut off from information, learning, health care and safe spaces; it calls for participation in preparedness, response and recovery and disability-disaggregated data. | Vulnerability, rights and measurement requirements. | Named child-level warning, movement, service continuity or recovery. |
| HW6 | UNICEF’s India case reports support to the National Disaster Management Authority to revise emergency handbooks with disability inclusion, gender-responsive principles and child protection. | National guidance revision and institutional route. | Accessible dissemination, local implementation, family support or child safety. |
| HW7 | UNICEF’s Indonesia landscape analysis reports that a survey of 769 teachers found none of the DRR education programmes in use were accessible for children with disabilities. | A named counterexample showing formal DRR education can exclude disabled children. | Hazard exposure, later warning, household adaptation or school continuity outcome. |
| HW8 | A World Bank review of EU national DRR policies finds disability inclusion uneven: among 34 countries, 32% scored zero and 44% scored one on its policy-inclusion scale, while accessible information and services are identified as preparedness requirements. | Cross-country policy implementation gap. | Real-time warning access, evacuation, shelter conditions, recovery and lived safety. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

“Accessible” is not one format. It can mean captioning, sign language, easy language, screen-reader compatibility, tactile or visual alerts, trusted intermediaries, medication continuity, transport, personal assistance, accessible toilets and safe spaces. A household may rely on informal care or community organizations that are not visible in official data. In conflict or displacement, documents, assistive devices, family separation and changing control of territory create additional failure points.

## Counterexample and source boundary

Indonesia’s teacher survey is a direct counterexample to treating a DRR curriculum as inclusive because it exists. The global MHEWS mortality association is important but cannot establish that disabled people received or acted on warnings. Public evidence closes system architecture, policy, governance participation and selected implementation examples, but not a comparable disabled-person chain from warning receipt through evacuation, accessible shelter, service continuity and recovery voice.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** An early-warning system, risk map, emergency handbook, shelter, drill, policy score or response operation is not by itself accessible warning, understood risk, safe evacuation, accessible care, continuity or recovery agency for disabled people.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **inclusive-warning-to-disabled-person-protection-and-recovery**. The strongest next acquisition is a named hazard/place/cohort chain linking warning format and receipt, interpretation, support and transport, evacuation, shelter accessibility, medication/assistive-device continuity, family/school/work outcome, complaint and recovery influence.

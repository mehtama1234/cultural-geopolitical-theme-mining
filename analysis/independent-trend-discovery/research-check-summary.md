# Independent trend checks — first wave

All three candidates were formed from public discussion before the research
files below were opened. The result is **qualified**, not confirmed, for all
three.

## 1. Selective exit is more defensible than total disconnection

Research supports reversible non-use, social pressure, substitution, and a
U.S. deactivation experiment. The public pattern is therefore useful as a
question about selective exit and private or professional channels. It does
not show that a broad population is leaving social media or that exits last.

## 2. AI companion continuity is a real concern, but its scale is open

The U.S. survey records emotional connection, disclosure, perceived loss, and
support among people who use AI socially. Farewell experiments record short-run
engagement after users try to leave. Together they support a concern about
continuity and exit friction. They do not show durable dependence, widespread
replacement of human relationships, or what happens after a product shuts down.

## 3. AI job-cut discussion reaches beyond employment

Research supports uneven adoption, weaker early-career hiring and entry
earnings in highly exposed groups, and a gap between task productivity and
worker control. It does not prove that all reported layoffs were caused by AI,
that firms are deliberately mislabeling ordinary restructuring, or that a
demand collapse is underway.

## Decision

Keep all three as qualified candidates. Do not promote them to broad trends in
the atlas. The next test is a second social wave with a fixed sampling rule,
followed by research that measures duration, prevalence, and outcomes rather
than only discussion or short-run reaction.

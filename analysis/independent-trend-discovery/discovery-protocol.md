# Independent discovery protocol

## 1. Capture the raw signal

Save the public URL, platform, account, date, short description, and the exact
phrase or concern that attracted attention. Do not interpret it yet.

## 2. Freeze a candidate

Group only items that share a clear idea. Give the candidate a plain-language
name and define:

- the observation window;
- the people, place, or community involved when known;
- the platforms checked;
- the repetition rule;
- a supporting signal;
- a counter-signal;
- what would count as a different trend instead.

## 3. Test the public pattern

Check whether the idea appears on at least two platforms, across more than one
account, and at more than one point in the window. Note search ranking,
moderation, language, access, and platform bias. If only one prominent account
drives the pattern, label it an influencer signal rather than a social trend.

## 4. Search outside social media

Only after the candidate is frozen, search for papers, surveys, administrative
records, company filings, market data, and public records. The research check
may confirm the candidate, qualify it, reject it, or find no matching evidence.

## 5. Publish the result

Use one of four outcomes:

- **confirmed:** research and public discussion point to a compatible pattern;
- **qualified:** research supports only part of the public interpretation;
- **rejected:** better evidence contradicts the candidate;
- **open signal:** the discussion is repeated but no suitable independent test
  is available yet.

Never turn an open signal into a fact merely because it has high engagement.

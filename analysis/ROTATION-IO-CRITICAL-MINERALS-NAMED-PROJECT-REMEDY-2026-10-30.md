# Rotation IO — Critical-minerals named projects, grievance and remedy

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** In named mineral projects, do disclosed safeguards, consultation, monitoring and grievance systems produce consent, water security, safe work, livelihood restoration and remedy—or only show that procedures and complaints exist?

## Why this packet matters

The previous IN rotation established the strategic and normative frame. This packet moves one level closer to operation by treating project disclosures, rights findings and complaint systems as evidence objects. A procedure is not an outcome; a complaint closure is not automatically a remedy; and monitoring points are not the same as safe water or restored livelihood.

```text
project / concession / exploration decision
  -> impact assessment, disclosure, consent and land process
  -> water, resettlement, worker, contractor and community operation
  -> monitoring, complaint, investigation, correction and remedy
  -> livelihood, income, health, culture, safety and ecological condition
  -> durable legitimacy, benefit sharing and post-closure accountability
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IO1 | IFC’s current Oyu Tolgoi disclosure records downstream herder water dependence, dust/noise complaints, participatory environmental monitoring, 176 monitoring points, resettlement of 10 physically displaced and 89 economically displaced herder households, and eleven community complaints in September 2023–October 2024. | Named project exposure, displacement, monitoring and grievance operation. | Whether water quantity/quality remained safe, livelihoods were restored, complaints were fairly resolved or consent was valid. |
| IO2 | The same IFC disclosure reports 207 employee grievances from August 2023–July 2024, anonymous channels, a worker support mechanism, and an environmental/social action plan requiring labor assessment and worker-accommodation planning. | Named worker grievance channels and unresolved operational risk areas. | Contract quality, retaliation absence, injury rates, wage/voice outcomes, closure quality or worker power. |
| IO3 | Oyu Tolgoi’s earlier IFC project materials specify Mongolian/English disclosure, local information sheets, a six-step community grievance procedure, mediation/community reporting and consultation around water supply. | Design of disclosure, language, grievance and water-consultation mechanisms. | Comprehension, representative participation, consent, water allocation, remedy receipt or cultural continuity. |
| IO4 | UN treaty-body findings concerning mineral exploration on Sami traditional territory state that remedy requires revising the project after a child-rights impact assessment and conducting an adequate process aimed at free, prior and informed consent of the affected siida. | Rights-based remedy and consent standard in a named exploration case. | Whether the revised process occurred, consent was obtained, harms ceased or compensation was adequate. |
| IO5 | The World Bank’s 2025 African Mining Accountability Platform case describes community training and grassroots reporting intended to connect mining complaints to formal grievance redress and reduce access burdens. | Community-led complaint and accountability pathway. | Complaint representativeness, institutional response time, remedy, retaliation protection or improved mine conditions. |
| IO6 | The World Bank’s grievance-redress service states that individuals and communities may submit complaints about adverse effects from supported projects and reports an average of 302 complaints processed over the prior three fiscal years. | External escalation and accountability infrastructure. | Mining-specific causal correction, complainant safety, outcome quality, recurrence or local legitimacy. |
| IO7 | The EBRD/MDB 2026 critical-minerals-to-manufacturing framework identifies coordinated due diligence and monitoring needs covering stakeholder engagement, grievance, Indigenous people, biodiversity, water/groundwater, tailings, pollution, labor, community safety, land/resettlement and mine closure. | Cross-institutional risk taxonomy and lifecycle governance architecture. | A project-level result, financing decision, enforcement, benefit distribution or post-closure condition. |
| IO8 | World Bank/IFC Oyu Tolgoi materials identify local supplier development, finance, information sharing, water governance, herder representation and complaint-resolution agreements as active mechanisms. | Named local capability and participatory governance mechanisms. | Supplier capture by local firms, fair water outcomes, herder decision power, income, ecological recovery or durable trust. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

The evidence shows why “community engagement” must be disaggregated. A herder, worker, contractor, municipal authority, Indigenous institution, women’s group and investor do not have the same exposure or power. A mining grievance channel may improve visibility while still routing disputes through project-controlled systems. Bilingual disclosure can increase access without proving that technical information was understandable or that a community could say no.

The Oyu Tolgoi case is especially useful because it exposes several clocks at once: project finance and production, herder displacement, water dependence, worker grievance, procurement, environmental monitoring and post-settlement livelihood questions. The Sami case shows a different geography and a sharper rights threshold: participation and information do not collapse into consent. The Africa reporting case shows that community-led data can reduce the distance between lived complaint and formal accountability, but the public source does not establish that remedy followed.

## Counterexample and source boundary

Monitoring points are not safe water; complaint counts are not harm rates; closed grievances are not necessarily fair remedy; worker channels are not proof of non-retaliation; local supplier programs are not proof of broad local value; and an MDB risk taxonomy is not project compliance. The evidence closes named mechanisms and documented exposure, but no source here closes a comparable project chain from consent through actual water, worker safety, income, cultural continuity, ecological condition, grievance outcome and post-closure accountability.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A mine’s ESIA, bilingual disclosure, consultation meeting, monitoring network, grievance mechanism, complaint count, resettlement plan, local supplier program or lender action plan is not by itself valid consent, safe water, restored livelihood, decent work, fair benefit, ecological recovery or remedy.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **named-mineral-project-to-consented-safe-water-livelihood-worker-and-remedy-outcomes**. The strongest next acquisition is a project-level longitudinal record linking affected person/community, land and water baseline, consent/representation, displacement, worker contract and safety, procurement/revenue, monitoring result, complaint, investigation, correction, payment or restoration, and post-closure condition.

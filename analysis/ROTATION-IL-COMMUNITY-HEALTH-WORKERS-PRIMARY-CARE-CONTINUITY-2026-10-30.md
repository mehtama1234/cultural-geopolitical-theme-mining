# Rotation IL — Community health workers, primary-care continuity and worker support

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When community health workers are trained, formalized and equipped with digital tools, do households receive reliable, supplied, culturally trusted primary care and referral—or do programmes count workers and visits while leaving supervision, pay, commodities, data burden and continuity unresolved?

## Why this packet matters

Community health workers are a last-mile institutional interface:

```text
household need / prevention / symptom
  -> trusted local contact
  -> screening, advice and data capture
  -> supplies, treatment or referral
  -> facility feedback and follow-up
  -> worker pay, supervision, safety and learning
  -> household health continuity and trust
```

A CHW can be geographically close but unable to diagnose, refer, obtain commodities, use a device offline, or sustain unpaid work. Digital records can reduce paperwork or transfer administrative burdens to workers. Community trust is valuable but cannot substitute for clinical supervision, supply chains or fair employment.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IL1 | World Bank reports Mozambique more than doubled trained and active CHWs from 3,380 in 2018 to 8,300 in 2022; roles were formalized with salaries and career pathways, alongside training and digital data collection. | Workforce scale, formalization and retention mechanism. | Household care quality, supplies, referral completion, pay receipt, workload or outcomes. |
| IL2 | World Bank describes Mozambique’s CHW network supporting home care and plans to strengthen supply chains through digital tracking and fixed import prices for essential commodities. | Household access and supply-chain response. | Commodity availability at household, treatment accuracy, referral, worker burden or health result. |
| IL3 | World Bank reports Eswatini’s offline electronic Community-Based Health Information System replaced paper reporting, integrated with national records and covered NCDs, antenatal care, child nutrition, malaria and HIV. | Digital data/continuity mechanism for rural workers. | Time saved, worker control, data quality, clinical response, privacy, referral or patient outcome. |
| IL4 | World Bank research describes Diabetes Compass supporting CHWs, facility clinicians and managers in Malawi, Sri Lanka and Tanzania through screening, guideline-aligned assessment, referrals and loss-to-follow-up reduction. | Digital team-based chronic-care workflow. | Patient adherence, medicine stock, referral completion, control of disease, equity or sustainability. |
| IL5 | UNICEF Rwanda reports CHWs replacing paper registers with Community Electronic Medical Records to strengthen digital services for mothers and children. | Named digital transition and maternal/child use case. | Device access, offline reliability, training, workload, treatment, referral or outcomes. |

| IL7 | UNICEF Somalia reports a 2025–2029 strategy centered on a professionalized, government-employed community health workforce led by female health workers trained and certified to deliver essential household services. | National professionalization and gendered workforce design. | Recruitment, salary receipt, safety, referral, community acceptance, continuity or outcomes. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

CHWs translate formal medicine into local language, trust and household practice. Female workers may access women and children but face gendered safety, unpaid work and mobility constraints. Conflict, distance, connectivity and supply interruptions change what “coverage” means. Digital tools and donor-funded cadres can improve continuity or create parallel systems, data extraction and short-term employment.

## Counterexample and source boundary

More CHWs do not prove more care. A formal salary pathway can coexist with commodity shortages; an offline system can reduce reporting friction without changing clinical action; a training session can occur without supervision or retention. Public evidence closes workforce scale, formalization, supply-chain intention, digital systems, chronic-care workflow, training and strategy stages, but not a comparable worker–household chain through supplies, referral, treatment, pay, workload and health outcomes.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A CHW headcount, training, certification, digital record, home visit, strategy, formalization or supply-chain plan is not by itself delivered quality care, stocked treatment, completed referral, fair work, worker retention, household continuity or health outcome.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **community-health-worker-system-to-supplied-trusted-primary-care-continuity**. The strongest next acquisition is a named worker/household/cohort chain linking need, contact, task, language, data, supplies, treatment, referral, facility feedback, supervision, pay, safety, workload, follow-up and health/trust outcome.

# Recovery currencies move on different clocks v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** bounded cross-domain synthesis; no pooled recovery index

## The central finding

The atlas repeatedly finds that a positive movement in one part of life does
not erase a loss in another. Health can improve while savings and borrowing
remain strained. A person can report fraud recovery while still being unable
to pay an energy bill. A benefit can begin while housing or utility hardship
continues. A worker can receive partial money while refusing to return because
trust has broken.

The end-to-end chain therefore needs separate currencies:

```text
money | time | access | status | health | control | security | trust | choice
```

No one currency is a reliable proxy for the others.

## Evidence already in the atlas

| Route | Positive or repaired currency | Currency that remains strained or open |
|---|---|---|
| SHED health-direction panel | Health improved for one path | Among prior adopters, reduced-savings persistence was still 56.9% on the improved-health path and borrowing persisted for 56.4%; financial reset did not follow health movement |
| HTOPS fraud/loss follow-up | A small linked subgroup reported loss, reporting, and recovery | In the same 11-person subgroup, 78.8% later could not pay an energy bill and 50.8% searched for jobs; reported recovery was not restored household security |
| SIPP SNAP transitions | Receipt state changed for the same person and next-month resources were observed | Entry and exit could coexist with rent/mortgage plus utility hardship; participation was not food, housing, or financial security |
| SIPP SNAP/child-care bridge | Same-person SNAP status was compared with paid-care use, child-care assistance, and work-prevention constraints | Assistance and participation can coexist with a constrained work/care option set; care timing, adequacy, child outcome, and later security remain open |
| SIPP capacity-conditioned hardship | Same-person status was compared with work-limiting condition, children present, and following rent/utility hardship | Stable receipt did not erase unequal household exposure; capacity and responsibility condition practical protection, while benefit amount, adequacy, trust, and recovery remain open |
| Mudiyanselage worker case | A $4,000 partial payment and later compensation order were documented | Historical wages remained unpaid during the proceeding, the original job was lost, and broken trust explained refusal to return |
| Meadley worker enforcement case | An official institutional account reports compensation payment and further court-ordered monetary relief | Reinstatement failed; the original job and later employment, household recovery, trust, recurrence, and exit remain unknown |
| Malaysia GrabCar Tribunal first hearing | Statutory worker rights, a formal Tribunal, and a named first hearing show institutional use has begun | A hearing is not an award: correction, payment, restored platform access, durability, trust, and exit remain open; route sequencing and eligibility/access burdens still matter |
| Australian Kyei merits case | The merits record accepted a substantive safety reason even after identifying a process defect | The deactivation was not reversed; payment, later platform work, practical alternative, trust, and exit remain open |
| CFPB administered redress | FY2024 reports 69%, 86%, and 88% of distributed funds cashed across three named matters | Aggregate cashing is a stronger endpoint than an offer, but it does not identify recipient receipt, account correction, adequacy, or restored security |
| CFPB Civil Penalty Fund | The CFPB reports $3.6 billion in payments to 7.7 million people since 2010 | Program scale and a reported recipient count do not show each person's amount, timing, uncompensated loss, adequacy, account correction, or durable security |
| Cartisano worker remedy case | Back pay and service continuity were reported, followed by an unconditional order and a reported return | The later court record reports materially altered duties and cessation while still employed; final livelihood and recovery remain open |
| Aitken worker return case | Union-supported return, three months of back pay, and prior-service recognition were reported | Continued work was followed by later dismissal after a safety-policy conflict; post-dismissal livelihood and recovery remain unknown |
| DOL Workers Owed Wages route | Agency recovery, locate/notice efforts, claim/search, and payment-channel support are documented | A particular worker's receipt, amount, timing, adequacy, household relief, continued work, trust, and exit remain unknown; unreachable wages can eventually leave the worker route |
| Innovative Associates cashback case | The Fair Work Ombudsman reports full back-pay of $40,164.49 after a temporary-visa worker was required to transfer money back to the employer | The record does not show payment timing, adequacy, safety, later work, household recovery, trust, recurrence, or exit |
| Southern Cross Care payroll remediation | A named provider self-audit led to reported back-pay of $10.135 million to 3,603 of 5,500 staff, with a replacement time system, audit, training, Board reporting, and worker-voice channels | Unlocated workers' money could move to government after 60 days; individual receipt, adequacy, household recovery, later compliance, trust, and exit remain open |
| Sauna360 product-remedy case | A free repair and then a second professional repair route were offered | The later recall said the first repair was ineffective; owner notice, completion, safe use, and durable protection remain unknown |
| FEMA wildfire route | Registration, referral, eligibility, and aggregate assistance stages were visible | Individual payment, adequate housing, repair completion, and stable recovery were not observed |
| ATUS eldercare layer | Care network and time allocation were measured | One-day time differences do not establish dated care onset, work displacement, health effect, or later recovery |
| Family-care and assisted-living system | Kinship continuity, public financing, and program coverage were visible | A suitable slot, reduced family housing/transport/coordination burden, completed service, safety, and later stability remained unknown |
| Ohio primary-care shortage/site route | A designated shortage organization was linked to an active named site, listed hours, services, and organization-level affordability/new-patient policies | Site-specific appointment, wait, insurance handling, travel/call/portal burden, completed care, continuity, health, and household recovery remained unknown; nominal capacity was not usable care |
| SNAP data-governance route | Data-access authority, database controls, court response, and privacy comments were visible | Applicant notice, benefit accuracy, payment continuity, correction, chilling/non-use, trust, and household recovery remained unknown |

These are different units and designs. They are compared for mechanism, not
pooled into a rate.

The family-care row is grounded in the [family-care capacity
synthesis](projects/us-aging-care-strain/findings/us-aging-care-strain-004.md),
and the data-governance row in the [SNAP data-control
finding](findings/us-snap-data-governance-accountability-001.md). Neither is a
same-family or same-applicant recovery estimate.

The [CFPB Civil Penalty Fund summary](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/payments-harmed-consumers/civil-penalty-fund/)
provides the scale figure in the table: $3.6 billion in payments to 7.7
million people since 2010. The Bureau's aggregate program count is evidence of
institutional reach, not a person-level adequacy or durable-recovery measure.

## Why this matters for societal and consumer interpretation

### Formal completion is its own currency

The newest consumer records make the distinction especially sharp:

| Case | What is now visible | What is still not visible |
|---|---|---|
| CFPB Wells Fargo | The Bureau terminated the 2016 order after reporting that the bank fulfilled its obligations, including remediation to specified consumers and correction/redress work | Which individual borrowers received what, when they received it, whether it was adequate, and whether financial security or trust recovered |
| CFPB Cash App / Block | The order requires a large redress program; the public case page says consumers do not need to take action now and provides a contact route | Individual payment, receipt, cashing, account correction, adequacy, durable fraud protection, and later use or exit |
| FWO Atkins | The official worker record reports that all ten drivers received back-pay | Whether the amount was adequate, whether later work remained available, and whether trust, household security, or recurrence changed |
| FTC repeat-refund routes | Later checks can be tied to people who accepted or cashed an earlier payment | The people who disappeared before that earlier route step, plus later security and account correction |
| Sauna360 | A repair route existed, but a later official notice found the earlier repair ineffective | Whether owners received the second route and whether safe, durable use was restored |

These are not contradictory records. They are different currencies of
completion. An agency can close an order, an administrator can issue a
payment, and a person can still have an open recovery problem. The Cash App
“no action needed” language is a reachability instruction, not evidence that a
consumer was paid. The Wells Fargo termination is institutional completion,
not a borrower-level recovery observation. The Atkins record is stronger on
receipt, but still does not establish adequacy or durability.

This distinction is central to the end-to-end atlas because it prevents a
formal endpoint from swallowing the lived middle of the chain.

Institutions often report the currency they control: money issued, access
restored, a case closed, a facility energized, or a benefit approved. People
live across currencies. A refund may restore cash but not time spent pursuing
it. A reinstatement order may restore formal access but not safety or trust. A
new job may restore income while worsening commute, status, or control. A
health improvement may arrive after debt and depleted savings have persisted.

The Meadley case adds a sharper same-episode counterexample: the official
record reports that compensation was paid, but reinstatement still failed and
the worker had to pursue court enforcement. Monetary remedy and restored
livelihood therefore diverged in one case; the payment does not establish
later employment, household security, or trust.

The Malaysia GrabCar Tribunal record supplies the earlier institutional stage
of the same chain. A statutory right, a functioning Tribunal, and a first
hearing demonstrate that a worker can reach a remedy institution before the
record shows an award, payment, restored access, or durable trust. The
Australian Kyei merits decision supplies a different boundary: a process
defect can coexist with a valid substantive safety reason, so procedural
imperfection does not automatically produce reversal or restored work. These
cases keep “institution used,” “process complied with,” “money received,” and
“livelihood restored” as separate currencies rather than treating any one as
the endpoint.

Durability is a separate currency from immediate implementation. [Cartisano](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-cartisano-backpay-no-return-risk-assessment-event-ledger-v1.json)
shows payment and continuity followed by actual return but materially altered
duties and a later cessation dispute. [Aitken](projects/ai-work-control/data/fwc-aitken-return-backpay-later-dismissal-event-ledger-v1.json)
shows a real return that lasted for a period before a later rupture.
[Sauna360](projects/us-marketplace-product-safety/data/sauna360-repeat-remedy-event-ledger-v1.json)
shows a product-level analogue: a repair route can exist and still be followed
by an official finding that the repair was ineffective. These cases do not
establish one common failure rate, but together they show why a remedy needs a
later durability check rather than a one-time “implemented” label.

The DOL Workers Owed Wages route adds a reachability version of the same split:
the agency can recover and hold money while the worker still must be located,
notified, and able to claim or receive it. This is not evidence that the
worker failed to pursue the remedy; address change, migration, notice
comprehension, payment-channel friction, and administrative burden are all
possible explanations that the public route cannot distinguish. The recovery
currency is therefore not only dollars recovered, but dollars made usable by
the intended person.

The Innovative Associates case adds a worker-side version: the official record
reports full back-pay, but the original harm was the loss of usable wages while
the worker performed the job and was required to transfer money back through a
cashback scheme. “Back-paid in full” therefore closes a formal monetary stage,
not the questions of when the money arrived, whether it was adequate, whether
the worker was safe from recurrence, or whether later work and trust recovered.

This explains several otherwise confusing behavioral patterns:

- continued use can reflect dependence rather than trust;
- switching can reflect constrained escape rather than preference;
- non-return can protect dignity while increasing financial risk;
- searching can show effort without a usable alternative;
- political confidence can remain stable while material participation shrinks;
- a public project can add capacity while shifting local cost or control.

The cultural meaning of an event is therefore not determined by whether an
institution marked its response as positive. Meaning forms through the full
conversion: what the person lost, what was protected, what effort was required,
what was actually restored, and whether a real alternative remained.

The care and data-governance cases extend this beyond ordinary remedy records.
Family continuity can protect a child or care recipient while transferring
time, housing, transport, and emotional work to relatives. A controlled data
system can improve administrative visibility while leaving applicants unsure
about disclosure, correction, or continued eligibility. In both cases, formal
capacity and lived security are different currencies.

The Ohio care-access route adds the provider-interface version: an active site,
listed weekly hours, and an organization-level affordability policy can coexist
with an unresolved appointment, wait, phone/portal, insurance, and completed-use
stage. Capacity is therefore not the same currency as reachable care, and
reachable care is not the same currency as health recovery.

### Capability recovery has its own vector

The same divergence appears above the household and firm level. A domestic
manufacturing route can improve production geography while leaving qualification,
yield, upstream material independence, affordability, and replaceability open.
A data-center project can create local tax revenue while leaving household
rates, reliability, interruption risk, and community consent unresolved. A
defense agreement can strengthen a state's planned capability while shipment,
acceptance, maintenance, operational usefulness, public legitimacy, and an
adversary's changed behavior remain separate. In these cases, “recovery” or
“resilience” must be decomposed into capacity, service, distribution,
replaceability, legitimacy, and leverage rather than treated as one strategic
score.

This is the state-scale counterpart to the household lesson: a stronger
capability can coexist with continued dependence or shifted burden. Domestic
production is not automatically autonomy; fiscal revenue is not automatically
local welfare; and a formal alliance response is not automatically deterrence.

## Measurement rule

Every promoted episode should report, separately:

1. what changed in money, time, access, status, health, control, or security;
2. what was protected and what was sacrificed;
3. what the institution controlled and what it actually restored;
4. whether the person received and could use the remedy; and
5. what later happened to trust, choice, switching, non-use, action, or exit.

An improvement in one row must not be used to impute improvement in another.
The atlas should prefer a vector of outcomes over a composite recovery score
until a source directly measures a validated composite construct.

## Boundary and next test

The current records support asynchronous recovery as a recurring cross-domain
mechanism, not a universal law or causal estimate. The decisive next evidence
is a same-unit event record that observes at least two downstream currencies
over time, plus the institutional response and later choice/meaning endpoint.
The authorized UAS/PSID routes remain the strongest candidate for adding that
within-person structure; public episode records remain useful for remedy and
reachability detail.

# Rotation ET — Tax morale, visible public value and voluntary compliance

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction; Tier 2 fiscal-contract conversion question  
**Research lane:** domestic revenue mobilisation / tax administration / tax morale / public services / fairness / informality / gender / trust / remedy

## Why this rotation

The atlas already covers tax transparency, illicit financial flows, domestic
resource mobilisation, public spending and fiscal federalism. This deepening
rotation isolates the social-contract leg: whether a tax system is experienced
as a fair exchange rather than merely a successful collection machine.

Tax-to-GDP, registration, filing, audits, e-filing, arrears recovery and
revenue growth are important fiscal signals. They do not by themselves show
that households and firms understood the liability, could pay without harmful
trade-offs, saw revenue converted into usable public value, received fair
service and appeal, or became more willing to comply voluntarily. The unit is
a named taxpayer, firm, household, locality, public service and time-bounded
tax encounter, not a national revenue ratio alone.

## Research question

When a government expands tax capacity through a new register, digital filing,
enforcement, VAT/property-tax reform, anti-evasion effort, tax expenditure
review or revenue-sharing arrangement, under what conditions does it produce
fair burden allocation, usable public value, respectful administration,
correction and remedy, and durable voluntary compliance across formal and
informal, urban and rural, large and small, and women-owned and male-owned
taxpayers?

## Conversion chain

```text
tax rule / reform / enforcement / register / service / public-finance constraint
  -> liability, information, filing, payment, audit, refund, appeal and allocation
  -> taxpayer or firm encounter with administration and public service
  -> burden paid or avoided, error corrected, service received, dispute resolved
  -> household security, firm survival, investment, locality capability and equity
  -> trust, legitimacy, cooperation, switching, informality or resistance
```

The minimum unit must preserve taxpayer or firm, tax instrument, liability,
date, geography, income/sector/formality, payment or arrears, administrative
friction, enforcement contact, service received, complaint, remedy and later
compliance. Collection volume, digital adoption or an audit count is an
intermediate signal—not proof of a fair fiscal contract.

## Evidence snapshot

### 1. Tax morale is not the same as legal compliance

OECD defines tax morale as the intrinsic motivation to pay tax and identifies
trust in government, perceived fairness, tax administration quality, taxpayer
awareness and public-service quality as drivers of long-term voluntary
compliance. This makes culture and legitimacy part of tax capacity, not a
decorative communications layer.

Source: [OECD tax morale](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/tax-morale.html).

### 2. Public revenue can be collected without being seen as public value

The OECD/IFAC/ACCA Public Trust in Tax 2024 survey found that only 33% agreed
tax revenue was spent for the public good and only 32% agreed public services
and infrastructure were a fair return for taxes paid; 50% disagreed with the
latter. Most respondents could navigate filing, but refunds and payment were
less easy. This separates administrative compliance from perceived reciprocity
and from actual service return.

Source: [OECD Public Trust in Tax 2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2024/12/public-trust-in-tax-2024-latin-america-and-beyond_84e1bf05.html).

### 3. Trust, transparency and technology are complementary, not substitutes

The World Bank’s domestic-resource-mobilisation roadmap frames technology,
transparency and trust as mutually reinforcing. Technology can improve
administration and enforcement; transparency can limit avoidance and evasion;
trust can strengthen voluntary compliance. The implication for research is
that a new platform or register must be tested alongside burden, explanation,
service, correction and public allocation—not credited with trust merely
because transactions became digital.

Source: [World Bank, Raising Revenue Right](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/raising-revenue-right).

### 4. Revenue capacity is shaped by fairness and the tax base

The World Bank notes that weak tax systems are narrowed by exemptions and
loopholes, while heavy reliance on consumption taxes and preferential treatment
of top incomes can limit inclusive growth. Simplification and better-targeted
exemptions may improve efficiency and fairness together, but a revenue increase
still needs an incidence test: who pays more, who gains from services, and who
can contest an error.

Source: [World Bank, Raising Revenue Right](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/raising-revenue-right).

### 5. Fiscal space can be absorbed before citizens see service improvement

UN Trade and Development reports that between 2018 and 2024 rising interest
payments reduced the share of government revenue available for development
spending in 99 developing countries. Revenue mobilisation can therefore coexist
with shrinking room for schools, health, infrastructure and climate action.
The chain must distinguish money collected, money available after debt service,
budget allocation, execution and the named service experienced locally.

Source: [UNCTAD, financing development and external flows](https://unctad.org/publication/financing-development-external-flows-financial-capital-developing-countries-and-their).

### 6. Place and informality change what “compliance” costs

UNCTAD’s Least Developed Countries Report links widespread informality and
weak job quality to low fiscal capacity, while noting that revenue shortfalls
undermine investment in housing, transport, sanitation and public utilities.
Formalisation can widen protection and public contribution, but registration
can also impose documentation, travel, payment, inspection and visibility
costs on low-margin firms and workers. The test must compare formal and
informal routes, including gendered time and care burdens.

Source: [UNCTAD, The Least Developed Countries Report 2025](https://unctad.org/system/files/official-document/ldc2025_en.pdf).

## Bounded proposition 151

**A tax-capacity reform, revenue increase, taxpayer register, e-filing system,
audit programme, VAT or property-tax change, anti-evasion drive, tax amnesty,
public-spending dashboard or fiscal-transfer rule is not by itself fair burden
sharing, visible public value, usable public service, voluntary compliance,
trust, household security, firm viability or fiscal legitimacy.** Tax-to-GDP,
registrations, filings, audits, payments, arrears recovery, digital adoption,
revenue identified or budgeted do not establish that named taxpayers and firms
could understand and pay without harmful trade-offs, obtain fair treatment and
remedy, see collected resources converted into an accessible local service, or
change later compliance because the fiscal exchange became credible.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

1. **Tax is an everyday relationship with the state.** The relevant cultural
   question is whether payment is interpreted as contribution, extraction,
   protection or arbitrary coercion.
2. **Visibility matters.** A taxpayer may see a notice, fee or audit directly
   while experiencing public value only through an indirect and delayed service;
   this asymmetry shapes trust.
3. **The same rule has different local prices.** Distance to offices, language,
   connectivity, informal intermediaries, documentation and lost work change the
   burden of compliance.
4. **Formality is not a single good.** Registration can unlock contracts,
   credit and protection, but can also expose precarious firms to fixed cost,
   penalties or harassment without services in return.
5. **The household behind the taxpayer matters.** Consumption taxes, property
   taxes, unpaid administrative work and care time can distribute burdens by
   gender, age, disability, migration status and household structure.
6. **Fiscal legitimacy is repeated.** One visible project or one easy filing
   experience cannot establish durable trust; the test is repeated payment,
   service, correction and remedy over time.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- Revenue can rise while debt service, leakage or budget non-execution prevents
  local service improvement.
- Digital filing can reduce friction for formal firms while excluding people
  without connectivity, documents, literacy or reliable devices.
- A tax amnesty can collect arrears quickly while weakening perceived fairness
  for compliant taxpayers.
- A property register can improve local capacity while creating displacement or
  insecure tenure if recognition and appeal are absent.
- Enforcement of high-risk evasion can improve fairness, but coercive or
  corrupt encounters can reduce tax morale even when collections increase.
- A national tax ratio can improve while women-owned microfirms, rural workers,
  informal traders or low-income households bear higher effective costs.
- A taxpayer survey can report trust while actual refunds, appeals, services or
  later compliance remain unobserved.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote to **Tier 1 bounded distinction** for the proposition. OECD, World
Bank and UNCTAD evidence supports the separate tax-morale, fairness,
administration, informality, fiscal-space and public-value mechanisms. Keep the
integrated fiscal-contract outcome at **Tier 2** because the public evidence
rarely follows the same taxpayer or firm from liability through payment,
service, remedy and later voluntary compliance.

## Next test

Follow one household, one woman-owned microfirm, one larger formal firm and one
informal worker across a tax encounter. Record liability, information, payment,
time, travel, intermediary, audit/refund/appeal, public service use, complaint,
remedy and subsequent compliance. Compare urban/rural, formal/informal,
high/low-income and localities with different service execution and debt-service
pressure.

| Stage | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| Signal | tax rule, register, filing, audit, revenue, transfer, service or trust event |
| Mechanism | liability, exemption, price, information, time, travel, connectivity, enforcement, allocation and debt service |
| Operation | named taxpayer/firm/household/locality/office/service/appeal and dates |
| Material consequence | payment, arrears, refund, income, firm survival, service use, household trade-off and budget execution |
| Meaning | fairness, contribution, extraction, dignity, trust, resentment, belonging and legitimacy |
| Power | appeal, bargaining, refusal, informality, exit, compliance, corruption reporting and remedy |

## Source boundary

The packet closes the tax-morale, public-return, technology/transparency/trust,
fairness, fiscal-space and informality mechanisms. It does not close named
taxpayer burden, local service receipt, budget execution, refund or appeal
fairness, household/firm consequences, gendered burden, later voluntary
compliance or durable legitimacy. Those remain acquisition targets.

The fourth and sixth stages—material consequence and later power—are still
unobserved in most cross-country tax sources. That is the next conversion
opportunity, not a reason to pool perception and outcome data.

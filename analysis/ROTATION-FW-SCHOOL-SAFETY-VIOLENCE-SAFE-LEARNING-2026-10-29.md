# Rotation FW — school safety, violence prevention and safe learning

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 conversion question
**Research lane:** social, cultural, geographical, public-health, education and conflict systems

## Why this rotation exists

School safety is often reduced to attendance, infrastructure, disciplinary
rules or a school-shooting count. A safe learning environment also includes
bullying, cyberbullying, gender-based violence, corporal punishment, sexual
harassment, discrimination, teacher and student safety, conflict attacks,
reporting, safeguarding and psychosocial response. The meaningful outcome is
not merely that a school exists, but that named learners can attend, learn,
belong and seek help without violence or retaliation.

## Research question

When a ministry, school, community, platform, donor or security actor adopts a
safe-schools policy, safeguarding system, teacher training, anti-bullying
program, reporting channel or conflict-protection measure, under what conditions
does it produce safer daily learning, equitable participation, wellbeing,
teacher agency and durable school continuity?

## Conversion chain

```text
policy / infrastructure / training / reporting / prevention / protection
  -> student, teacher, family or school encounter
  -> exposure, disclosure, response, support, discipline or interruption
  -> safety, attendance, learning, health, belonging or continuity outcome
  -> trust, agency, inclusion, legitimacy or community resilience
```

## Evidence snapshot

1. UNESCO's 2026 education monitoring reports that more than half of lower-
secondary students reported bullying in 70 of 83 countries with data, and that
3,909 attacks on education were recorded in 69 countries in 2024. These are
different phenomena—peer violence and conflict attacks—and must not be merged.
2. UNESCO's safe-learning material says one in three learners face bullying and
   links violence to learning, health and dropout; it also identifies gender,
   sexual orientation, socioeconomic status and other identities as sources of
   unequal exposure. Global prevalence is not a school-level causal estimate.
3. WHO/Europe's 2024 HBSC analysis across 44 countries and regions reports that
   cyberbullying increased from 2018, with about 15% of adolescents reporting
   being cyberbullied. Digital violence can continue beyond school gates and
   requires a platform, family and school response chain.
4. UNESCO reports that only 32 states, 16%, had a comprehensive legal framework
   to combat violence in schools in the evidence it reviewed. A legal framework
   is a governance input, not proof that reporting is safe, cases are resolved
   or violence falls.
5. UNESCO's Senegal case shows a practical model involving teacher and
   pedagogical-supervisor training, classroom discussion, trusted-adult
   reporting and community participation. The case illustrates a pathway but
   does not establish population-wide effect or long-term recurrence reduction.
6. UNICEF reports that conflict-related school closures in West and Central
   Africa have nearly doubled since 2019, with millions of children affected in
   2025. Closure, attack, displacement and trauma create a different safety and
   continuity pathway from routine peer violence.

## Bounded proposition

**A safe-schools policy, school building, security measure, anti-bullying
campaign, teacher training, safeguarding code, reporting app, counselling
service, legal framework or conflict-protection commitment is not by itself a
safe and inclusive learning environment, lower violence, attendance, learning,
wellbeing, belonging, teacher agency or durable school continuity.** A policy,
training completion, reported case, closure count, security presence or reopened
school does not establish that named learners were safe, could disclose without
retaliation, received effective support, learned without fear or experienced
non-recurrence.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

- **Safety is relational:** students judge safety through peers, teachers,
  families, transport routes, online spaces and whether adults respond fairly.
- **Identity changes exposure:** gender, disability, sexuality, caste, ethnicity,
  migration, poverty and language can affect targeting, reporting and access to
  protection.
- **School boundaries are porous:** cyberbullying, violence on the route to
  school, sexual exploitation and community conflict cross the campus boundary.
- **Reporting is power:** a channel may exist while fear, stigma, dependency or
  mistrust prevents disclosure; a rise in reports can mean improved access or
  worsening exposure.
- **Conflict geography:** an attacked school, a closed school and an unsafe
  school operating under threat require different protection, continuity and
  recovery measures.
- **Cultural legitimacy:** restorative practice, discipline and safeguarding
  must fit local language and authority while preserving children’s rights and
  preventing normalization of violence.

## Counterexamples and cautions

1. More reports can indicate better trust and access rather than more incidents;
   fewer reports can indicate silence, fear or missing data.
2. Security hardware can reduce one threat while increasing surveillance,
   criminalization, exclusion or fear for particular students.
3. A school can reopen after an attack while teachers, learners and families
   remain displaced, traumatized or unable to travel safely.
4. Anti-bullying lessons may improve knowledge without changing peer behavior,
   teacher response, online exposure or institutional accountability.
5. Counselling can support a child while leaving the perpetrator, environment or
   structural discrimination unchanged.
6. National prevalence estimates may not represent remote, informal, refugee,
   conflict-affected or minority-language schools.
7. Attendance or test-score improvement does not prove that safety caused the
   change or that vulnerable students gained equal protection.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote the Tier 1 distinction that safe learning requires prevention, exposure,
disclosure, response, continuity and non-recurrence, differentiated by violence
type and geography. Keep the following as Tier 2:
**school-safety-to-safe-learning-and-belonging conversion**. The next evidence
must name the learner, teacher, school, exposure, report, response, support,
attendance/learning effect, recurrence and remedy.

## Next test

Build a school-cluster case ledger covering routine peer violence, cyberbullying
and one conflict or closure context. Record exposure, identity, location/time,
reporting route, response time, confidentiality, support, discipline,
attendance, learning, wellbeing, teacher conditions, family voice and recurrence
over 12 months. Include non-reporting and harmful-response cases.

## Six-stage closure table

| Stage | What is currently supported | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | bullying, cyberbullying, attacks, legal frameworks and program commitments | local exposure, silence and cultural meaning |
| Mechanism | prevention, adult response, reporting, safeguarding, support and conflict protection | stigma, identity, retaliation and institutional power |
| Operation | learner, teacher, school, incident, route, platform and case can be named | repeated encounter, response and continuity pathway |
| Material consequence | safety, attendance, learning, health, belonging, closure and recovery are relevant | named learner outcome, non-recurrence and equal protection |
| Meaning | dignity, trust, identity, inclusion and legitimacy are central | participant attribution, cultural fit and contestation |
| Power | reporting, remedy, discipline, exit, voice and community authority alter leverage | durable accountability, prevention and institutional learning |

## Source boundary

The packet closes discovery and framing for school safety and violence prevention.
Public sources support prevalence signals, legal and program mechanisms,
cyberbullying, conflict attacks and measurement limitations. They do not by
themselves establish a named learner’s safe daily experience, effective remedy,
learning outcome or durable non-recurrence.

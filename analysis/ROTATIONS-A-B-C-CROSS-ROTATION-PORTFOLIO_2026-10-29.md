# Rotations A–C cross-rotation trend portfolio

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** discovery synthesis; not yet promoted to end-to-end findings  
**Inputs:** Rotation A (platform exit, infrastructure incidence, geopolitical leverage), Rotation B (time, household optionality, administrative legibility, belonging), and Rotation C (AI judgment, dependency, social infrastructure)

## Executive readout

The first three rotations do not justify one pooled “master trend.” They do,
however, produce a coherent portfolio of recurring mechanisms:

1. **Formal capacity is separating from usable capacity.** A right, standard,
   connection, procurement, alliance commitment or AI deployment becomes
   meaningful only when a person, worker, customer, community, operator or
   partner can use, contest, correct, substitute or sustain it.
2. **Access is increasingly converted through effort.** Time, liquidity,
   information, identity verification, administrative navigation and practical
   entry routes determine who can turn nominal access into participation or
   protection.
3. **Contestability is becoming part of infrastructure.** Removal, deletion,
   algorithmic work decisions, public-service AI, large-load operation and
   alliance capability all create a question of who can observe, challenge and
   correct the system after deployment.
4. **Operational behavior is moving closer to the point of dependence.** The
   important unit is shifting from the announcement or asset to the facility,
   account, case, task, component, service or event where consequences occur.
5. **Resilience is qualified and temporal, not merely owned.** A substitute,
   domestic source, backup generator, AI tool or alliance commitment counts as
   resilience only if it works at the relevant quality, cost, speed and scale,
   and remains maintainable.
6. **Cultural and geopolitical meaning is produced through attribution.** A
   burden or capability becomes legitimacy, distrust, leverage, consent or
   backlash when affected actors can connect it to an identifiable decision,
   institution, operator or partner.

These are cross-source hypotheses. They are useful for discovery and
prioritization, not pooled estimates or causal conclusions.

## Theme cards

### P1. Formal capacity versus usable protection

**Core proposition.** The existence of a formal right, rule, standard or
technical capability is only the first stage. Usability requires a route that is
discoverable, reachable, timely, intelligible and correctable.

**Observed signals.** The FTC describes timed removal and known-copy handling
under the Take It Down Act; its GM/OnStar order describes consumer data-copy and
deletion controls. USDA describes a 30-day SNAP notice/benefit route. The ILO
platform-work standard frames disclosure and information access around automated
work decisions. The World Bank emphasizes monitoring, maintenance, testing,
portability, interoperability, auditability and ownership for public AI.

**Distinct subthemes.**

- Procedural exit: a right becomes meaningful through identity, timing, action,
  residual copies/data, recurrence and later account state.
- Administrative legibility: a deadline is not usable access unless the person
  can apply, understand the notice, receive the benefit and correct an error.
- Algorithmic contestability: disclosure is not a remedy unless workers can
  challenge and correct a decision without losing access to work.
- Public-AI maintainability: procurement is not institutional capacity unless
  the owner can monitor, repair, audit and fund the system after launch.

**Counterevidence and caution.** Rules can improve access without producing a
measurable trust or welfare gain. Some users may prefer informal workarounds;
some systems may correct errors without a formal appeal. Do not treat a policy
page, convention or procurement principle as implementation evidence.

**Next acquisition.** Prioritize implementation/enforcement records with a
stable case, account, worker or service identifier; request date; action;
correction or appeal; residual state; and later use, receipt or trust signal.

### P1. Unequal optionality and access-through-effort

**Core proposition.** People do not experience the same service, price, rule or
opportunity equally because the usable alternative depends on time, cash,
schedule control, information, care obligations and social entry points.

**Observed signals.** ATUS separates work, care, household and civic time.
Federal Reserve SHED separates headline income from income variability and
financial room. Gallup identifies work, responsibilities, not being asked and
not knowing how as barriers to participation. Pew identifies distinct patterns
of civic, news, political and religious engagement.

**Distinct subthemes.**

- Time is an access price: participation may be formally available but
  practically expensive in schedule and care time.
- Liquidity is optionality: income level does not reveal the ability to absorb a
  shock, substitute, delay payment or preserve care/participation.
- Administrative effort is a hidden co-payment: forms, waiting, proof,
  navigation and correction consume scarce time and attention.
- Practical entry produces cultural legitimacy: shared values do not equal a
  credible invitation, participation route or institutional voice.

**Counterevidence and caution.** More time or cash does not always increase
  participation; people may choose nonparticipation for identity or preference.
  Survey-reported financial comfort, belonging and engagement are not direct
  measures of welfare, agency or institutional influence.

**Next acquisition.** Use downloadable SHED and ATUS microdata where possible,
then seek a longitudinal or administrative bridge that observes the constraint,
the alternative chosen and a later work, care, debt, health, participation or
security outcome.

### P1. Operational accountability at the point of dependence

**Core proposition.** Governance is moving from high-level promises to the
operating behavior of the account, facility, case, task, component or service
on which others depend.

**Observed signals.** PJM reports a large-load event involving approximately
3,800 MW of computational load and later emphasizes operator information,
incident logging and ride-through. FTC materials make execution of removal and
deletion operational. NBER evidence points from AI adoption to task and
organizational allocation. World Bank guidance makes post-procurement operation
part of AI capacity.

**Distinct subthemes.**

- Compute as grid-governance actor: large loads have system behavior, backup,
  cost and local-burden consequences.
- Execution over declaration: removal, deletion, benefit delivery and AI
  correction need event-level evidence.
- Task-level AI: organizational adoption can coexist with heterogeneous or
  modest realized productivity and labor reallocation.
- Service-level accountability: public AI should be evaluated by continuity,
  error correction and user access, not launch or model quality alone.

**Counterevidence and caution.** Operational visibility may increase because
  regulators are paying attention, not because the underlying systems are more
  risky. A single grid event or AI survey cannot establish a general trend.

**Next acquisition.** Build named-unit event ledgers: facility/interconnection,
 account/request, firm/task, or agency/service. Preserve dates, operating state,
 affected users, payment/cost allocation, correction and subsequent response.

### P1. Capability is qualified, integrated and maintainable

**Core proposition.** Resilience and leverage depend on an integrated operating
system—not ownership, procurement, adoption or announcement alone.

**Observed signals.** NATO defines capability across doctrine, organisation,
 training, materiel, leadership, personnel, facilities and interoperability;
 its Ankara declaration connects procurement to manufacturing, deployability,
 sustainment and interoperability. GAO distinguishes mineral ownership and
 disclosure from processing, substitution, recycling and time-to-effect. NBER
 separates AI adoption and expectations from measured productivity. ILO and
 World Bank materials emphasize dialogue, monitoring, maintenance and ownership.

**Distinct subthemes.**

- Integrated geopolitical capability: fielded readiness and partner
  coordination matter more than procurement announcements.
- Qualified substitution: an alternative counts only when it meets technical,
  commercial, environmental and timing requirements.
- Realized AI productivity: adoption must be connected to task redesign,
  measured output and worker/customer distribution.
- Social infrastructure: skills, representation, audit, correction, funding and
  maintenance are part of the capability.

**Counterevidence and caution.** A capability can deter or create leverage
  before an observable event response. Substitution may have option value even
  before commercial qualification. Avoid demanding a single metric where the
  strategic effect is intentionally ambiguous; instead record the exact claim
  and its observable proxy.

**Next acquisition.** Select one capability/component and trace investment,
 qualification, operation, partner/user response, maintenance and reversal or
 durability.

### P2. Correction as a legitimacy technology

This is the most promising cultural bridge across digital, administrative,
workplace and public systems. Affected people are more likely to treat a system
as legitimate when they can identify what happened, obtain a response, correct
the record and see the correction persist. This should not be reduced to a
generic trust score.

**Testable chain:**

```text
decision or harm -> notice/attribution -> accessible challenge
  -> correction/remedy -> persistence/recurrence -> continued use, voice or exit
```

The relevant evidence is not only whether an appeal exists. It is the response
time, success rate, residual error, repeat exposure, cost to the claimant and
whether the person remains able to participate.

### P2. Time and attention as hidden infrastructure

The rotations repeatedly identify time as the scarce input that makes other
resources usable. Care, application, verification, monitoring, training,
representation and maintenance all require labor. A system may appear cheap or
accessible because the cost is shifted to households, workers, local operators
or public administrators.

**Required distinction:** record who supplies the time, whether it is paid,
whether it displaces care or participation, and whether the burden is
recoverable. Do not infer burden from a formal process alone.

### P2. Dependence becomes political through burden allocation

Infrastructure, supply chains, connected vehicles, platform systems and public
AI all create dependency. Dependency becomes political or geopolitical when
actors can attribute the burden or benefit and mobilize around it.

**Testable chain:**

```text
dependency -> operational burden/benefit -> identifiable payer/recipient
  -> local, worker, consumer or partner response -> policy durability/reversal
```

This is the bridge from technical incidence to cultural legitimacy and
geopolitical leverage.

### P3. Belonging and legitimacy require a practical route

The cultural evidence suggests that shared values and reported belonging are
important signals but weak endpoints. The decisive question is whether a person
is invited, knows how to enter, has time to participate, can influence a
decision, and sees a response. The same logic can be tested for community
infrastructure, workplace voice, public-service design and alliance partner
acceptance.

    reskilling, bargaining or remedy.

    training, wages/retention and customer/product outcome.

## Promotion gate

Do not promote a theme to a headline finding unless the packet records:

- a stable unit or explicitly bounded system;
- a dated starting condition and a later observation;
- the mechanism connecting them;
- who gained, lost, paid, waited, adapted or gained voice;
- at least one plausible counterexample or alternative explanation;
- enough source detail for another analyst to reproduce the claim;
- a clear cultural, institutional or geopolitical meaning that is evidenced,
  not merely rhetorically inferred.

Until then, label the item **source-supported hypothesis** or **mechanism
candidate**, retain the missing conversion, and keep the lanes separate.

## Decision

The next work package is not another broad scan. It is a **conversion packet
program**: acquire a small number of named-unit records that test whether formal
capacity becomes usable protection, whether access burdens are unequally
distributed, whether correction produces legitimacy, and whether operational
capability creates durable resilience or leverage.

# Rotation HX — Shock-responsive social protection, digital cash and household protection

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When states and humanitarian actors expand social registries, electronic payment systems and shock-responsive cash transfers, do households receive timely, adequate and usable protection through crisis—or does the chain stop at eligibility, enrollment, payment issuance or nominal coverage?

## Why this packet matters

Cash is often treated as an endpoint. For a household facing conflict, displacement, drought, inflation or illness, cash is an intermediate capability whose value depends on timing, amount, access, prices, safety, control within the household and the services available around it.

```text
shock / poverty / displacement
  -> identification and eligibility
  -> registration, consent and safe contact
  -> approval and payment route
  -> receipt, cash-out and control
  -> food, shelter, health, education or debt decision
  -> reduced harm, recovery and future resilience
```

Digital delivery can increase speed and scale, but it can also create phone, identity, language, literacy, connectivity, fee, privacy and gender-control barriers. A payment ledger is therefore not the same as household protection.

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| HX1 | UNICEF’s 2025 global results report says cash transfers supported more than 137 million children in 70 million households across 87 reporting countries, including US$559 million in fragile and humanitarian settings. | Global scale, systems strengthening and cash-delivery reach. | Same-household receipt, adequacy, spending, intrahousehold control or later recovery. |
| HX2 | UNICEF’s HOPE annual report describes expansion of a digital public good for humanitarian cash transfers, with onboarding in Senegal, Sierra Leone, Zimbabwe and Mozambique. | Digital delivery infrastructure and country expansion. | Connectivity, identity, privacy, payment receipt, cash-out, fees or household outcomes. |
| HX3 | Armenia launched needs-based electronic cash assistance for displaced and host-community families in January 2025 through the Idram application, alongside social-service capacity building. | Named displacement response, electronic route and service-system design. | Registration exclusion, device/access barriers, receipt, adequacy, safety, spending or integration. |
| HX4 | World Bank reports Egypt’s Takaful and Karama programme reached 4.67 million vulnerable households by December 2023, with a grievance mechanism and adaptive social registry, and expanded during COVID-19, currency devaluation and the Ukraine-war shock. | Large-scale registry, grievance architecture and shock expansion. | Claimant-level correction, payment adequacy, consumption, service use, women’s control or durable exit from poverty. |
| HX5 | World Bank reports Somalia’s Baxnaano safety-net programme expanded emergency support during recurrent shocks and reached more than 487,000 households; Pakistan reduced response time from about two months during COVID-19 to ten days during the 2022 floods. | Response-time and reach signals in fragile/shock contexts. | Payment receipt, purchasing power, household decisions, exclusion, protection and recovery. |
| HX6 | UNICEF reports that in South Sudan cash-transfer expansion is being considered for longer-term goals, while community-level implementation and humanitarian-to-development links remain part of the design challenge. | Transition and system-convergence problem. | Timely receipt, safety, adequacy, household resilience or lasting institutional capacity. |
| HX7 | UNICEF’s Myanmar 2025 report says cash and services reached 30,240 households between January and July, prioritizing pregnant/lactating women, children with disabilities and earthquake-affected families. | Targeted crisis delivery and inclusion priorities. | Individual receipt, disability accessibility, safe cash-out, spending, protection or recovery. |
| HX8 | UNICEF’s Haiti reporting records 4,338 households receiving cash across 16 displacement sites through mobile money. | Named site-level mobile-money operation. | Phone ownership, fees, identity, safety, intrahousehold control, consumption and displacement recovery. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

The same amount of cash has different practical value across currencies, markets, conflict zones and household structures. Women may receive a transfer but lack control over spending; displaced people may lack documents or phones; people with disabilities may need assisted access; and digital systems may rely on private platforms, donor finance and cross-border data flows. Local merchants, inflation, insecurity, social norms and language shape whether a transfer protects or merely records a transaction.

## Counterexample and source boundary

The packet treats faster response and larger reach as important but incomplete. A ten-day response time does not establish that the right household received an adequate amount, could safely cash out, or avoided hunger, debt, displacement or school interruption. Electronic payment expansion can also leave people without devices, connectivity, identity or privacy behind.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A social registry, eligibility decision, electronic payment, cash-transfer count, response-time improvement or grievance mechanism is not by itself received, adequate, safe, controlled or outcome-producing household protection.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **shock-responsive-cash-to-received-household-protection-and-recovery**. The strongest next acquisition is a named household or cohort chain linking shock, eligibility, contact, payment, cash-out, fees, price environment, spending/control, service access, harm avoided and recovery, disaggregated by gender, disability, displacement and digital access.

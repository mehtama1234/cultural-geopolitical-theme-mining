# Rotation IU — Sacred sites, conflict, access and living heritage

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When conflict, occupation, securitization or displacement affects sacred and religious sites, do legal protection, monitoring and dialogue preserve safe worship, community authority and living heritage—or can site designation coexist with restricted access, identity erasure and disrupted transmission?

## Why this packet matters

Sacred sites are not only buildings. They are routes, rituals, custodians, calendars, community institutions, burial places, landscapes and relationships. Conflict can damage a site directly, restrict access, target custodians, remove artifacts or displace the people who make the site meaningful.

```text
conflict / occupation / security threat / displacement
  -> site damage, closure, checkpoint, seizure or custodial disruption
  -> legal protection, monitoring, dialogue, humanitarian access
  -> safe worship, ritual, pilgrimage, care and community presence
  -> identity, memory, belonging, transmission and local authority
  -> durable protection, accountability and peace
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IU1 | UNESCO’s 2026 humanitarian guide states that conflict and forced displacement disrupt communities’ ability to practice, express, enjoy and transmit living heritage, and that sacred spaces, landscapes, resources and heritage-based livelihoods may be targeted or lost. | Conflict/displacement risk taxonomy for living heritage. | Site-level protection, safe access, community authority, recovery or long-term practice. |
| IU2 | The same UNESCO guide warns that displaced communities may lose social, political, economic and security conditions for practice, while host communities may face pressure over limited resources and cultural spaces. | Displacement and host-community mechanism. | Actual host access, social protection, cultural negotiation, safety or durable integration. |
| IU3 | The UN Commission of Inquiry’s 2025 report on the Occupied Palestinian Territory and Israel states that it investigated attacks on educational, religious and cultural sites and reports restrictions on Palestinian worshippers’ access to Haram al-Sharif/Temple Mount alongside attacks and harassment affecting Christian leaders and sites. | Named conflict case linking site access, security practice and religious/cultural rights, attributed to the Commission’s findings. | Generalization to all actors or conflicts, final adjudication, durable access, protection or local reconciliation. |
| IU4 | The Commission reports that it treated investigated religious and cultural sites in Gaza as civilian objects and cultural properties under international law and links attacks and restrictions to cultural and territorial claims. | Legal-protection and identity/power frame in a named conflict. | Independent causal consensus, full damage record, accountability outcome, reconstruction or community-led return. |
| IU5 | UNESCO states that the 1954 Hague Convention and related protection systems seek to safeguard cultural heritage during war and support recovery, while special protection requires conditions including non-military use. | International legal and protection architecture. | Enforcement, deterrence, safe access, custodial legitimacy or protection of living ritual. |
| IU6 | UNESCO’s special-protection mechanism allows designated cultural property and, for non-parties to the Second Protocol, a request to the Director-General; protection is tied to recognition and non-military use. | Designation and non-military-use mechanism. | Whether designation changes battlefield behavior, access, community voice or post-conflict continuity. |
| IU7 | UNESCO’s 2025 enhanced-protection list includes provisional protection for Nahlé temple in Lebanon during hostilities, based on exceptional importance and a non-military-use declaration. | Named emergency designation and state commitment. | Physical safety, worship access, local custodianship, enforcement, damage avoidance or recovery. |
| IU8 | UN and UNESCO materials on safeguarding religious sites and living heritage emphasize interreligious dialogue, community-level prevention, monitoring and the protection of worship and cultural practice. | Prevention, dialogue and monitoring architecture. | Power equality, representation, safe dissent, site access, accountability or durable peace. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Sacred-site protection is inseparable from movement and authority. A site can be legally recognized while worshippers face checkpoints, identity screening or changing custodial rules. Conversely, a security restriction may be presented as neutral while its uneven application changes who can enter, pray, maintain or narrate the site. A monument can survive physically while the community, ritual calendar, language and livelihood that animate it are displaced.

The packet keeps the Palestine/Israel evidence explicitly attributed to a UN Commission rather than presenting contested findings as a universal causal model. The broader UNESCO framework supplies comparative mechanisms: community-centered safeguarding, protection of living heritage in displacement, humanitarian access, non-military-use commitments and interreligious dialogue. The next evidence task is to identify comparable site-level longitudinal records in multiple geographies.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A World Heritage inscription, Hague-Convention status, special-protection listing, non-military-use declaration, checkpoint, security operation, damage estimate, interfaith meeting or monitoring report is not by itself safe worship, equal access, community authority, cultural continuity, accountability or peace. Legal designation can coexist with restricted access; physical preservation can coexist with ritual loss; and dialogue can coexist with unequal power.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A protected sacred site, legal designation, security protocol, access permit, monitoring report, dialogue initiative or reconstruction plan is not by itself safe worship, equal access, living ritual, community authority, cultural continuity or durable peace.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **conflict-pressure-to-safe-sacred-access-living-heritage-and-community-authority**. The strongest next acquisition is a named site/community/custodian/worshipper chain linking hazard, access rule, identity, route, security encounter, ritual, custodial decision, damage, humanitarian support, complaint, accountability, return and repeated practice.

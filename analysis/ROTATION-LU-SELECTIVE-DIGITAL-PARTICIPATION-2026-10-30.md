# Rotation LU — Selective digital participation and practical control

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Lane:** culture / social behavior / platform governance / digital geography  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded participation finding plus Tier 2 control-to-wellbeing question

## Research question

Are people leaving online life, or are they remaining connected while changing
the form of participation—posting less, using short-lived or private channels,
deleting apps, limiting exposure and relying on platform controls? What converts
that selective behavior into greater agency, safety and connection rather than
silent exclusion or loss of voice?

## Conversion chain

```text
online dependence, social pressure or harmful exposure
  -> selective posting, private/ephemeral channel, app deletion or content control
  -> altered visibility, reach, exposure and social effort
  -> maintained connection, safer encounter, reduced burden or lost opportunity
  -> repeat behavior, wellbeing, trust, voice and platform exit/switching
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ofcom Adults' Media Use and Attitudes 2026 | Social-media use remained widespread, but active posting/sharing/commenting fell from 61% in 2024 to 49%; qualitative participants described more selective posting, preference for limited-lifespan posts, app deletion and leaving smartphones behind. | Population signal plus practical-control strategies | Whether selective behavior improves wellbeing, safety, relationships or voice; who can afford to withdraw |
| Ofcom Online Nation 2025 | Adults spent about 4.5 hours online daily; 65% said online benefits outweighed risks, down from 71% two years earlier; children continued using social platforms for connection and wellbeing while some described doomscrolling and “brain rot.” | Continued dependence and ambivalent meaning | Whether controls or reduced exposure change durable wellbeing and social participation |
| Ofcom content-control RCT, 2025 | In a nationally representative trial of more than 3,650 UK adults, granular content-control choices led more than seven in ten participants to use controls to reduce sensitive-content exposure; wording alone had no impact and visual cues sometimes reduced trust. | Choice architecture can change protective behavior in an experimental setting | Real-platform persistence, exposure reduction, subgroup effects, social cost and later trust |
| Ofcom protective-defaults RCT, 2026 | Safe-by-design defaults received strong support and high retention across age groups; adult acceptance differed by default configuration. | Default settings can alter control uptake | Whether default protection creates false reassurance, reduces participation or persists outside the experiment |
| Pew Research Center, U.S. teens 2025 | 44% of teens reported cutting back on social-media use, and the same share reported cutting back on smartphone use. | Self-reported reduction signal | Cause, duration, platform substitution, social cost, mental-health and school/relationship outcomes |
| Pew Research Center, U.S. adults 2025 | Platform use remained broad: YouTube 84%, Facebook 71%, Instagram 50%, TikTok 37%, WhatsApp 32%, Reddit 26% and X 21% reported use. | Counter-signal against generalized exit | Whether use is active, passive, private, coerced, beneficial or harmful for different groups |

## Bounded finding

> **Selective participation is not the same as digital exit or restored agency.**
> Lower posting, ephemeral sharing, app deletion, safety-control uptake or
> reduced screen time can change exposure and visibility while leaving people
> dependent on the same platforms for relationships, work, news, services or
> civic voice. The direction of the effect is not uniform.

## Cultural and social interpretation

The meaningful shift may be from a binary “online/offline” model to a portfolio
of participation modes. A person may keep messaging while stopping public posts,
keep a professional profile while leaving an entertainment platform, or use a
story that expires instead of a permanent post. That is culturally meaningful:
it changes the boundary between public and private self, the cost of future
interpretation, and the amount of social labor required to remain visible.

But selective non-use is unequally available. A worker, migrant, student,
caregiver, activist, small business, disabled person or isolated young person
may need the same platform for income, support, identity, services or safety.
Quiet withdrawal can therefore be protective for one person and exclusionary
for another. A platform can also move the burden of moderation and exposure
management onto users through defaults, controls, blocking and self-censorship.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Ofcom and Pew show continued widespread use and meaningful social benefits;
  the evidence does not establish a mass exit from online life.
- Self-reported reduction may reflect temporary experimentation, age-specific
  behavior, platform substitution or recall rather than durable non-use.
- A content-control choice in a mock or experimental environment does not prove
  lower exposure or better wellbeing on a live platform.
- Ephemeral or private posting can reduce future visibility while also reducing
  discoverability, income, civic reach or evidence preservation.
- Lower public posting may reflect fear, harassment, employment risk or platform
  fatigue; it should not be treated automatically as greater autonomy.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: participation mode, platform presence,
public visibility and practical agency are separate stages. Keep the broad
selective-exit, wellbeing, voice and inequality claims in Tier 2 until the same
user or cohort is followed across baseline use, a control or exit action,
exposure/connection, opportunity cost and later wellbeing or participation.

## Next acquisition

Build a longitudinal, multi-platform ledger that distinguishes public posting,
private messaging, passive consumption, safety-control use, app deletion,
account deactivation and complete connectivity loss. For a named cohort, record
reason, platform dependence, work/education/service use, relationship continuity,
harm exposure, control setting, substitution, lost opportunity, wellbeing,
voice and re-entry or durable exit. Preserve an active high-benefit user and a
protective or forced non-user comparator.

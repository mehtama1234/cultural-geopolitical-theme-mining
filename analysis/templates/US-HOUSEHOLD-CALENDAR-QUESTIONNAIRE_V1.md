# Household calendar questionnaire v1

This is a draft research instrument for a voluntary US household panel. It is not a public opinion poll and it does not ask for identifying information. Participants may skip any question.

## Baseline: household and place

1. How many people share money, food, housing, or regular care in this household?
2. Is the home rented, owned with a mortgage, owned without a mortgage, a manufactured home, or another arrangement?
3. What county or broad area is the home in? Do not record a street address.
4. Which people in the household need regular childcare, aging care, disability support, or medical equipment?
5. How often does the household rely on a car for work, school, food, care, or medical visits?
6. Is there a relative, friend, or community group that could help with money, food, housing, care, or rides during a short emergency? Choose available, limited, unavailable, or prefer not to say.
7. How predictable are household earnings and pay dates? Choose stable, variable, seasonal, or unknown.

## Monthly money and time

Record ranges when exact amounts are uncomfortable. The study needs timing and direction as well as dollars.

1. What income arrived this month, on what dates, and from what sources?
2. Which required costs were due this month? Record amount and due date for rent or mortgage, utilities, insurance, transport, care, food, medicine, debt, fees, and other essentials.
3. At the start of the month, what liquid savings or available credit could the household use without asking another person?
4. How many hours did household members spend on unpaid care, paperwork, travel to services, repairs, or resolving a complaint?
5. Did work hours, pay, schedule, commute, or job status change?
6. Did the household delay, reduce, replace, or go without food, medicine, care, transport, energy, rest, housing, or communication?
7. Did anyone provide money, housing, food, care, or rides? Was it a gift, loan, expected exchange, or unclear?
8. Was any service, account, benefit, insurance, or housing arrangement denied, interrupted, restored, or renewed?
9. What was the household’s financial room at the end of the month: more, about the same, less, or unknown?
10. Did the household trust the firm, agency, landlord, utility, lender, employer, or other institution involved more, less, or about the same? Why?

## Event questions

Ask these whenever a dated shock occurs.

1. What happened, and on what date?
2. When did you first know it might affect the household?
3. What amount, hours, deadline, or waiting period was involved?
4. Which need was most at risk?
5. What did the household do first: pay, delay, borrow, switch, appeal, ask for help, work more, work less, or go without?
6. What did that choice protect?
7. What did it make harder or postpone?
8. Who could change the situation: the household, employer, landlord, firm, lender, utility, agency, court, family member, or nobody the household could reach?
9. What remedy was offered? Was it affordable, timely, understandable, and usable?
10. What happened by the next month: debt, fee, missed work, health change, repeat bill, weaker option, recovery, or unknown?
11. What part of this account is directly documented, what part is your report, and what part is our interpretation?

## Optional public-judgment module

Ask only with separate consent and never make participation depend on answering.

1. Which recent cost, service failure, work change, or policy decision most affected your view of the economy?
2. Which person, firm, agency, party, or government did you think could have changed it?
3. What action did you take, if any: changed a purchase, complained, switched, joined a group, contacted an official, voted, did not vote, or none?
4. Did the event change your trust in the relevant institution? What evidence would change your view?
5. Do you want to report turnout or vote choice? Yes / no / skip.

## Interviewer and data rules

- Do not suggest that one event caused a later belief or vote.
- Ask for the date and the choice before asking for an explanation.
- Record “unknown” when the participant cannot tell or the record is unavailable.
- Do not turn a skipped answer into zero, no harm, or no support.
- Separate the receiver of help from the person who supplied it.
- Ask whether a lower payment reflected lower price, lower quantity, lower quality, delay, or help from someone else.
- Ask whether a service was restored with a cleared balance or with new debt.
- Keep direct identifiers, consent records, and research responses in separate systems.

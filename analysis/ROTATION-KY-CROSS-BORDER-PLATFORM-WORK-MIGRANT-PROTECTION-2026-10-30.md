# Rotation KY — Cross-border platform work, migrant protection and portable labour justice

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** Do digital labour and recruitment platforms give migrant workers safer access to income across borders, or do they relocate algorithmic control, informality, fraud and weak remedy into a jurisdictional gap?

## Why this packet matters

Digital platform work sits between migration, labour, technology and geography.
It can let a worker earn from a place where a conventional work permit or local
employer relationship is unavailable, but the same distance can make identity,
classification, social protection, payment, inspection and remedy difficult. The
ILO's 2026 platform-economy work is especially important because the International
Labour Conference adopted Convention No. 193, while the evidence still shows
measurement gaps, migrant over-representation, cross-border service provision and
weak protection.

```text
recruitment / platform onboarding / ID and location verification
  -> algorithmic matching, rating, task allocation and payment
  -> cross-border work, income and client/employer relationship
  -> classification, social protection, safety, voice, complaint and remedy
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| KY1 | ILO's 2026 platform-work portal records adoption of the Decent Work in the Platform Economy Convention, 2026 (No. 193), promoting decent work, formalization and worker protection. | International norm and formalization direction. | Ratification, domestic implementation, enforcement, worker awareness or remedy. |
| KY2 | ILO's 2026 article identifies algorithmic management, worker classification and cross-border service provision as active policy questions, with gaps in legal frameworks, employment status, labour protection and social dialogue. | Governance and jurisdictional mechanisms. | A platform's actual classification, algorithmic decision, worker voice or outcome. |
| KY3 | ILO survey evidence from 1,153 web-based platform workers across 21 Latin American and Caribbean countries finds 52% use platform work as secondary income, 53% work for recruiters outside the region and around 40% lack health/social-security coverage. | Regional cross-border reach, income dependence and protection gap. | Causal effect, platform-specific conditions, migrant subgroup outcome or durable income. |
| KY4 | The same survey reports 8% of workers are migrants, mainly Venezuelan, while ILO research across 14 European countries finds migrants about 20% more likely to undertake platform work, with vulnerability and traditional labour-market disadvantage shaping participation. | Migrant over-representation and selection mechanism. | Uniform migrant experience, legal status, discrimination, earnings, safety or agency. |
| KY5 | ILO states platform work can offer opportunity for young people, women with caregiving responsibilities and remote workers, while also producing informality, insecurity and weak protection. | Countervailing opportunity and distributional mechanism. | Whether flexibility is voluntary, compatible with care, safe, adequately paid or transferable to social protection. |
| KY6 | ILO's 2026 ASEAN forum calls for governance of online recruitment, clearer responsibility for platforms and social media, identity verification, digital literacy, safe complaints, wage recovery, compensation and cross-border claims. | Recruitment-fraud and remedy architecture. | Implementation, worker uptake, retaliation protection, recovered wages or cross-border enforceability. |
| KY7 | ILO's platform-work statistics brief highlights growth alongside persistent measurement gaps and the need for internationally agreed statistical standards. | Comparability and denominator uncertainty. | Number of workers, hours, income, platform power, migrant exposure or trend magnitude in every country. |
| KY8 | OECD's 2026 social-protection analysis documents legal responses including the EU presumption-of-employment framework and Mexico's 2025 social-protection measures, while noting different coverage thresholds and national designs. | Policy comparator and portability problem. | Actual enrollment, contribution continuity, coverage after crossing borders, enforcement or benefit receipt. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Platform work can function as a virtual labour corridor. A worker may live in
one city, be recruited by an intermediary in another country, serve a client in
a third, receive payment through a fourth jurisdiction and appeal to no clearly
responsible authority. The platform can therefore make geography look irrelevant
while making legal geography more consequential.

Migrants and refugees may value the ability to work without entering a local
labour market, but onboarding can require documents, bank access, language
fluency, stable connectivity and a location that the platform accepts. Ratings,
automated fraud controls and identity checks can reproduce exclusion even when
the interface appears formally open. Digital recruitment can also connect workers
to genuine jobs while enabling deceptive advertisements, illegal fees,
trafficking and impersonation.

The cultural layer is not only language. Platform rules may assume a standard
worker, household, address, workday and dispute process. A migrant worker's
family, remittance obligations, religious schedule, disability, care role or
temporary status can be treated as a productivity risk rather than a legitimate
social condition. Worker voice must therefore include migrants, returnees,
refugees, women, disabled workers and informal intermediaries—not only platform
companies and regulators.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A convention, legal presumption, platform count, worker survey, migrant share,
recruitment app, identity check, flexible-work claim or complaint mechanism does
not prove decent work. Online work can provide income and autonomy for some
workers while deepening insecurity for others. Migrants may be over-represented
because of selection into platform work, not because platforms cause migration or
poverty. Cross-country survey estimates are not interchangeable, and a worker's
legal remedy may depend on where the platform, client, payment provider and worker
are located.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Cross-border platform work is a growing labour and
migration governance mechanism with a documented opportunity/protection tension,
especially around algorithmic management, migrant selection, recruitment fraud,
social protection and remedy.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **cross-border-platform-work-to-portable-decent-work-social-protection-and-labour-justice**. The next acquisition should follow one worker from recruitment and onboarding through task allocation, rating, payment, classification, social-protection enrollment, incident, complaint, voice, exit and post-return remedy, with a locally employed or non-platform comparator.

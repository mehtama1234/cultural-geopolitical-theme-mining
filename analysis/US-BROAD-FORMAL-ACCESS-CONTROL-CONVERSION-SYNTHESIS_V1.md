# US broad atlas: formal access is not usable control

**Checked:** 2026-09-19  
**Purpose:** consolidate a cross-domain mechanism now visible in the retained
public records. This is a synthesis of bounded routes, not a pooled estimate.

## The mechanism in plain language

A person may technically have a benefit, coverage, refund, or remedy while
still lacking control over the thing that matters. The system can say “issued,”
“approved,” “covered,” “reversed,” or “reimbursed” while the person still has to
find the problem, prove it, wait, appeal, monitor a channel, or risk losing
access.

```text
formal entitlement or institutional response
  -> interface, gate, or delivery channel
  -> person must notice, document, appeal, monitor, or wait
  -> usable control may be gained, delayed, or lost
  -> money/time/access/health/security changes
  -> trust, switching, voice, political action, or protective non-use
```

The cross-domain claim is narrow: **formal availability and practical control
are distinct states**. The records do not justify saying that every formal
response fails, that every delay causes lasting harm, or that people respond in
one predictable cultural or political way.

## The evidence ladder

The atlas should keep these stages separate:

```text
rule or offer
  -> institutional implementation
  -> reported recipient receipt
  -> independently verified receipt
  -> adequacy and remaining loss
  -> durable protection or recovery
  -> trust, meaning, switching, voice, or exit
```

The Fair Work Ombudsman’s Atkins follow-up is a useful positive comparator:
the agency reports that all ten drivers received back-payments after funds were
released from court-held trust. That is stronger than an order or payment
schedule, but it remains institutionally reported rather than independently
audited, and it says nothing about adequacy or later livelihood. The distinction
prevents the atlas from treating every “paid” label as either failure or full
recovery.

## Five evidence routes

| Route | Formal state visible | Control conversion problem | Still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| SNAP EBT | Benefits issued; more than $320m in stolen benefits replaced for nearly 679,000 households during the covered statutory period | Theft can leave an active benefit balance unusable; prevention often requires recipient action such as locking a card, changing a PIN, or filing a claim | Replacement timing, interim food access, next-month stability, trust, reporting, and exit |
| Medicare Advantage SNF authorization | 12% of requests denied; 18% of denials appealed; 95% of appealed denials overturned | The person/provider must detect the denial, assemble an appeal, and wait after a hospital stay; the appeal gate may filter who reaches reversal | Days delayed, substitute care, admission, functional recovery, caregiver burden, cost, and plan switching |
| Wildfire insurance | Claims and partial payments, regulator enforcement, and new smoke-restoration standards are visible | A partial payment or legal standard does not itself make a home repairable, safe, affordable to insure, or livable | Property-level adequacy, completed repair, safe return, renewal, move/stay, and security |
| Consumer redress | CFPB or a firm reports a refund, reimbursement, or distribution route | Payment can depend on notice, claim submission, cashing, account correction, or an active reissue request | Person-level receipt, adequacy, residual loss, repeat failure, trust, and switching |
| Wage recovery | DOL records recovered wages, requires electronic-payment updating and identity-backed claim submission in WOW, or receives employer proof of payment through PAID | The worker may still need stable contact information, documents, login access, and claim effort; PAID verifies an employer payment step but not the worker's later use | Worker completion, receipt/cashing, adequacy, remaining loss, household security, continued work, trust, and exit |
| Privacy control | Walmart reports access, deletion, and sale-opt-out requests separately, including completed, denied, and median-time stages | Identity or authorization verification and legal exemptions can stop a request; a company-level “completed” label does not expose the final data state to the person | Independently verified deletion, residual exposure, re-collection, explanation quality, trust, switching, and behavior |
| Platform safety | Users report direct satisfaction with blocking and unfollowing, but much lower satisfaction with reporting that requires platform action | Users can do safety work themselves while depending on opaque moderation for collective protection | Verified harm reduction, recurrence, institutional response, time cost, continued use, and exit |

## Why this matters culturally and socially

The conversion work is not neutral. It consumes different currencies:

- **time:** waiting, calling, appealing, and documenting;
- **money:** travel, interim care, overdrafts, food substitution, or missed work;
- **knowledge:** knowing that a claim, lock, appeal, or correction route exists;
- **status and confidence:** feeling safe enough to challenge a provider, agency,
  insurer, or employer;
- **control:** the ability to stop a transaction, preserve an account, remain
  housed, or choose another provider;
- **future room:** whether the person can keep searching, paying, caring, or
  participating after the immediate problem.

This helps explain why the same administrative response can produce different
meanings. One person may experience an appeal as effective voice; another may
experience it as a costly barrier and stop trying. Continued use may show
dependence rather than satisfaction. Non-use may protect privacy or dignity
while sacrificing care, food, benefits, or future recourse.

## What the atlas can now say

1. **The first gate selects the visible recovery population.** People who do
   not know, cannot afford, or cannot safely complete the route disappear from
   later “successful” counts.
2. **The institutional clock is earlier than the lived clock.** Issuance,
   denial reversal, partial payment, and distribution are observable before
   usable access, adequate protection, durable recovery, or trust.
3. **Control is an outcome in its own right.** A benefit that can be stolen, a
   service that requires an appeal, or a payment that cannot be cashed is not
   equivalent to a usable benefit, treatment, or remedy.
4. **The same mechanism crosses domains without making the domains identical.**
   EBT theft, prior authorization, wildfire claims, consumer redress, and wage
   recovery have different causes and institutions; they share a measurable
   conversion problem, not one common causal effect. Wage recovery adds a
   useful internal contrast: one route makes the worker perform the final
   reachability and digital-payment work, while another requires employer proof
   of payment, yet neither public route observes durable worker recovery.
   Digital control adds the same distinction in a different form: a privacy
   request can be administratively completed while the person cannot
   independently see whether all relevant data was removed; a safety tool can
   block one account while a report leaves the user waiting on platform
   judgment.

## What would close the middle

The next decisive evidence is not another aggregate count. It is a lawful,
de-identified episode record that preserves both route failures and route
successes:

1. event or denial date and responsible actor;
2. notice, knowledge, effort, and usable alternative;
3. money/time cost and interim substitution;
4. claim, appeal, correction, or remedy decision;
5. actual receipt, admission, repair, access, or account restoration;
6. adequacy and durability at a later date;
7. trust, switching, voice, political action, or exit only when directly
   measured.

Until then, the atlas should report formal access and lived control separately.

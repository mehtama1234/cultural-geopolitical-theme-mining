# Rotation JN — Carbon dioxide removal, permanence, MRV and climate justice

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When carbon dioxide removal (CDR) is presented as necessary for net zero, hard-to-abate sectors and climate stabilization, does it deliver additional, durable and verifiable atmospheric removal with fair land, water, energy and community outcomes—or mainly produce future pathways, pilot capacity, carbon credits and technology narratives that may not survive measurement, reversal, scale or local consent?

## Why this packet matters

CDR is not one technology. It includes land-based biological methods, bioenergy with carbon capture and storage, direct air capture with storage, enhanced weathering, mineralization and ocean-based approaches. They differ radically in maturity, permanence, energy use, land and water demand, ecological effects, cost and governance. Treating one tonne of “removal” as interchangeable across methods can hide reversal risk, leakage, additionality, local burdens and the possibility that removal claims delay direct emissions reduction.

```text
residual-emissions claim / removal method / project site
  -> feedstock, energy, land/water, capture, transport, storage and MRV
  -> atmospheric removal, permanence, leakage/reversal and local effects
  -> credit or policy recognition, payment, community outcome and accountability
  -> durable net climate benefit, adaptation capacity and legitimate transition
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

CDR creates a politics of temporal distance. A company or government can claim a future tonne while a local community experiences land conversion, water use, biomass demand, pipeline construction or energy demand now. The people bearing present costs may not be the people receiving future climate benefits, and the actor purchasing a credit may be far from the site where removal, reversal or environmental harm occurs.

The geography of CDR is uneven. DAC may cluster near low-carbon power, transport and geological storage; BECCS and afforestation depend on land, biomass, labor and tenure; enhanced weathering depends on minerals, crushing and transport; ocean methods implicate shared waters and uncertain ecological feedbacks. A global removal portfolio can therefore reproduce extractive patterns unless it tracks who owns land, who controls water, who receives payment, who carries reversal liability and whose knowledge counts in monitoring.

CDR also changes the geopolitical meaning of net zero. Wealthy emitters may seek removal capacity abroad to preserve high-carbon production or purchase cheap credits from jurisdictions with weaker monitoring. Conversely, well-designed public procurement can create technical capability, rural income and ecosystem restoration. The decisive question is not whether CDR appears in a scenario, but whether every claimed tonne is additional, measured, durable, attributable and governed alongside rapid emissions reduction.

## Counterexample and source boundary

An IPCC pathway, technology roadmap, pilot, announced capacity, carbon-removal credit, offtake agreement, registry, MRV protocol, restored hectare, biomass supply contract, DAC plant or geological storage permit is not by itself additional atmospheric removal, permanence, climate benefit, affordability, environmental justice, consent or a substitute for emissions reduction. A tonne can be counted while reversal liability is unclear; a project can improve soil while failing additionality; and a durable-storage claim can ignore upstream energy, land, water or community burdens.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** CDR scenarios, pilots, credits, offtake agreements, registries and MRV plans are not by themselves additional durable atmospheric removal, equitable climate benefit, environmental protection, community legitimacy or evidence that direct emissions cuts are not being delayed.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **carbon-dioxide-removal-to-verifiable-permanent-climate-benefit-and-just-local-transition**. The strongest next acquisition is a named project/method/community chain linking residual-emissions rationale, baseline, energy/land/water inputs, measured removal, storage permanence, leakage/reversal, lifecycle emissions, credit issuance, buyer use, local consent, benefit receipt, liability and later verification.

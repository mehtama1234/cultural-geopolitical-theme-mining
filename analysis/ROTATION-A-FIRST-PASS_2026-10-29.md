# Rotation A first pass: platform exit, infrastructure incidence, and geopolitical leverage

**Checked:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** provisional source-led analysis; not a pooled estimate and not a
claim that any single source follows the full chain.

## Why these three lanes

The existing atlas marks platforms/data/attention, infrastructure/technology/
dependency, and geopolitical/state consequences as thin-middle lanes. Their
common problem is that formal capacity is visible before the dependent actor's
actual experience, response, trust, switching, acceptance or leverage. This
pass therefore adds one distinct primary/official source pair per lane and
stops at the next unobserved conversion.

## Preliminary trend candidates

### 1. Exit is becoming a procedural control surface

The FTC's Take It Down guidance describes a concrete platform duty: covered
platforms must create a notice-and-removal process and remove valid
nonconsensual intimate images and known identical copies within 48 hours. The
FTC's GM/OnStar order separately requires a way for U.S. consumers to request
their data and seek deletion. Together, these sources suggest a provisional
subtheme: **practical exit is shifting from a vague privacy promise toward a
timed, procedural and auditable control surface**.

This is not evidence that removal or deletion succeeds for a particular user.
The missing chain is:

```text
request -> identity/eligibility -> action -> known-copy or re-collection state
       -> harm/correction -> continued use, switching or exit
```

The next useful source must preserve a request identifier or stable case/account
unit. A policy page, request count or enforcement order cannot close the later
state.

### 2. Compute is becoming a grid-governance actor

PJM reports that a July 22 event caused approximately 3,800 MW of computational
large load to disconnect and transfer to backup generation. Its later FERC
technical-conference account emphasizes operator information, incident logs,
ride-through requirements, interconnection timing, cybersecurity and
accountability for facility performance. This supports a provisional
subtheme: **AI/data-centre growth is changing the governance object from “how
much capacity is needed?” to “how a large computational load behaves during
system stress, and who bears the cost?”**

The evidence is operational and system-level. It does not show which facility
transferred, how long backup operated, who paid, what emissions occurred, what
customers experienced, or whether local communities accepted the bargain. The
next qualifying route should join a named facility or interconnection docket to
metered behavior, payment/cost allocation, service incidence and local response.

### 3. Capability is an integrated alliance system, not a purchased object

NATO's capability-development page defines capability as a combined system of
doctrine, organisation, training, materiel, leadership, personnel, facilities
and interoperability. The Ankara declaration then connects procurement,
industrial capacity, deployability, sustainment, interoperability and Ukraine
support. This supports a provisional subtheme: **geopolitical leverage is more
likely to depend on integrated readiness and partner coordination than on a
single platform or announcement**.

The source pair does not establish deterrence, adversary adaptation, partner
acceptance, public consent or durable alignment. The next test is an
event-country-party ledger that follows one accepted/operated capability to an
attributed external response, cooperation/refusal, maintenance or reversal.

## Cross-lane synthesis

The three lanes show the same structural movement at different levels:

| Lane | Formal capacity | Newly visible operating implication | Still unobserved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Platform | Removal/deletion right | Timed process, identity, copy handling and accountability become part of the service | Actual user correction, recurrence, trust and exit |
| Infrastructure | Grid connection and compute capacity | Large-load behavior, ride-through, incident logging and cost responsibility become governance objects | Facility-level payment, customer/local burden and acceptance |
| Geopolitics | Procurement and alliance commitment | Doctrine, people, training, facilities and interoperability determine usable capability | Counterpart response, leverage, consent and durability |

The common trend candidate is **operational accountability is moving closer to
the point of dependence**. A platform must account for removal execution, a
large load for system behavior, and an alliance for integrated readiness. This
is a cross-source interpretation, not a single causal finding.

## What this pass changes in the discovery queue

1. Promote **procedural exit and recurrence** as the first platform subtheme;
   search for implementation and account-level evidence rather than more
   policy statements.
2. Promote **large-load operational accountability** as the first
   infrastructure subtheme; search for named facilities, event logs, payment
   allocation and local service/burden records.
3. Promote **integrated capability-to-response** as the first geopolitical
   subtheme; search for one accepted operation, one attributed counterpart
   response and one later durability signal.
4. Keep the three lanes separate. Do not convert the shared pattern into a
   universal index or pooled trend rate.

## Decision boundary

This first pass adds source-led hypotheses and sharper acquisition targets. It
does not promote any lane to an end-to-end finding. The next pass should either
produce a stable unit with a later consequence or record the missing identifier,
payment, acceptance, response, trust, or exit field and rotate onward.

# Rotation B first pass: time, household optionality, administrative legibility, and belonging

**Checked:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** provisional source-led analysis; source families remain separate.

## Why this rotation

The broad atlas repeatedly finds that a nominal offer is not the same as a
usable option. Rotation B tests that proposition through four different
measurement surfaces: time allocation, financial room, administrative process,
and cultural participation. It deliberately does not combine survey percentages
into one social condition.

## Preliminary trend candidates

### 1. Time is an access price, not just a background variable

The 2025 American Time Use Survey separates work, household activity, care,
civic/religious activity and socializing, with demographic and work-status
breakdowns. The Federal Reserve's 2025 SHED adds a different pressure surface:
annual income can hide month-to-month income variation, and 11% of adults
reported struggling to pay bills because income varied. Together these sources
support a provisional subtheme: **participation and care are filtered through
time and income timing, not only through stated preference or available
services**.

The sources do not follow one person from a care need through a schedule choice
to later work, wellbeing or participation. The next test needs a longitudinal,
administrative or matched design that observes the alternative and the later
consequence.

### 2. Household optionality depends on liquidity, not headline income

The Federal Reserve reports that 73% of adults were doing okay financially or
living comfortably in 2025, while lower-income, younger and Black adults
reported meaningful declines. It also reports price increases as the most
common concern. This supports a bounded subtheme: **households may experience
the same price or income shock differently because cash timing, emergency
capacity, debt and credible substitutes differ**.

This is not a claim that the survey measures welfare or actual choice. The next
pass should use the downloadable SHED data to distinguish income level,
income variability, emergency capacity, spending changes, debt and care/work
adaptation.

### 3. Administrative access is a timed route with a hidden burden

USDA's SNAP rights guidance gives a clear formal deadline: applicants should
receive benefits or a notice of ineligibility within 30 days after applying.
That makes the administrative pathway observable as a rule. The missing middle
is whether the person can successfully apply, receives a comprehensible notice,
appeals when needed, receives benefits, and experiences adequate protection.

The defensible subtheme is therefore **administrative legibility is a practical
resource**. Eligibility language and a deadline are not the same as usable
access. The next test should join processing/appeal records to a case or panel
with receipt, adequacy and correction fields.

### 4. Belonging and agency require an entry path, not only shared values

Gallup's 2026 American Identity Survey associates community connection with
reported agency and belonging. It also reports practical barriers: personal
responsibilities, work, not being asked and not knowing how to get involved.
Gallup's separate shared-values study finds that many Americans can identify at
least one shared value and that a large majority say respecting different values
matters more than sharing the same values. Pew's civic-life research adds that
people occupy different combinations of news, politics, community and religious
engagement.

Together these sources support a provisional cultural subtheme: **legitimacy is
not produced by value agreement alone; it depends on whether people have a
credible, practical route into participation and voice**. The sources do not
establish causality or durable institutional influence. The next test needs a
place or organization that records an invitation/entry route and a later
participation or response.

## Cross-lane synthesis

| Lane | What is newly visible | What remains unobserved |
|---|---|---|
| Time/care | Work, care and civic participation compete inside measurable daily time | Which alternative was chosen, who absorbed the burden and whether continuity improved |
| Household optionality | Income timing, prices and emergency capacity shape usable room | Actual substitution, debt, protected meaning and later security for the same unit |
| Administrative legibility | Rules create deadlines and notice routes | Successful application, comprehensible notice, receipt, adequacy, appeal and correction |
| Cultural legitimacy | Belonging, agency and shared values are associated with participation routes | Whether entry causes action, institutional voice, acceptance or durable trust |

The shared hypothesis is **access is converted through effort**. A care service,
financial resource, benefit, invitation or civic value matters only when people
can reach it within their time, information, cash, identity and institutional
constraints. This is a cross-source interpretation, not a pooled estimate.

## What this pass changes in the discovery queue

1. Prioritize time-and-care records that include schedule control or a later
   work/participation outcome, not another time-use average.
2. Use SHED's downloadable data to identify subgroup differences in emergency
   capacity, income variability and spending adaptation before searching for a
   new headline survey.
3. Treat administrative deadlines as the start of the route; acquire notice,
   appeal, receipt and adequacy fields before calling a program accessible.
4. Treat belonging and shared values as cultural signals; search for an actual
   invitation, participation event, institutional response or refusal.

## Decision boundary

This rotation adds source-led subtheme hypotheses and specific acquisition
targets. It does not claim that time pressure causes withdrawal, that liquidity
determines welfare, that a 30-day rule produces usable access, or that belonging
causes civic action. Those require unit-level or stronger comparative evidence.

# Held candidate: care and longevity as access-time-work capacity

## Plain-language claim

Care and longevity demand may be becoming an operating-capacity problem. People
experience it through waiting, unpaid care, reduced work room, household tradeoffs,
and uneven access to services—not only through purchases of health or wellness
products.

## Evidence lanes

The candidate combines seven existing external records covering NHS waiting and
legitimacy, time-use and disability care, unpaid-care employment tradeoffs, carer
time and social capacity, demographic transformation, family caregiving burden,
and gendered employment effects. The new Inc. 5000 observation adds a company-side
health/longevity signal: 964 source-linked companies out of 1,627 flagged
companies (59.3%).

That company-side figure is a coverage measure, not population prevalence or proof
that care demand caused growth. The records also span different countries,
populations, periods, and definitions.

## Current status

`held_candidate` — not a confirmed structural trend.

## Counter-signal

The company examples include discretionary pet-wellness and other consumer offers.
The public-system records may describe distinct access, labor, demographic, and
institutional mechanisms rather than one common market.

## Promotion test

Construct a dated, comparable panel covering care demand, access/waiting time,
unpaid-care hours, employment effect, service capacity, and spending across at
least two independent source families and geographies. Reconcile populations,
units, periods, and outcome direction before promotion.

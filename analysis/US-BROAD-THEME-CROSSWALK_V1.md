# US broad theme crosswalk v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-19  
**Purpose:** explain the relationship between the five reader umbrellas and
the full 14-theme research program.

The five reader umbrellas are navigation devices for the connected atlas. They
are not a replacement for the 14-theme program. The relationship is many-to-
many: one broad theme can appear in several umbrellas, and one umbrella can
contain household, cultural, political, firm, infrastructure, or geopolitical
questions.

## The five reader umbrellas

| Reader umbrella | Plain meaning | Main question |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | What people and organizations give up to pay, protect, or continue | Who absorbs the money, risk, and security cost? |
| **Time** | Waiting, care, travel, attention, and unpaid coordination | Who gets time back, and who loses usable time? |
| **Voice** | Information, trust, recourse, identity, participation, and contestability | Who can understand, challenge, influence, or leave? |
| **Work** | Production, jobs, firms, bargaining, ownership, and control | Who controls the work and receives the gain? |
| **Energy** | Physical systems, infrastructure, capacity, dependency, and resilience | What systems make life possible, and who controls their failure or replacement? |

## Why the crosswalk matters

The reader path may begin with a cost, time, voice, work, or energy question,
but the answer often crosses several social scales:

```text
household or user experience
  -> consumer, worker, family, or community adaptation
  -> firm, platform, agency, market, or local-system response
  -> cultural meaning and political legitimacy
  -> sector capacity, state capability, or geopolitical consequence
```

For example:

- a payment fee is a **cost** issue, but also a consumer-power, financial-
  access, trust, and firm-margin issue;
- unpaid eldercare is a **time** issue, but also a health, gender, work,
  housing, and intergenerational-support issue;
- AI adoption is a **work** issue, but also a privacy, attention, status,
  infrastructure, and geopolitical-dependence issue;
- insurance withdrawal is an **energy/place** issue, but also a household-
  balance-sheet, mobility, public-backstop, and political-legitimacy issue;
- migration is a **place** issue, but also a labor, consumer-demand, service-
  capacity, identity, belonging, and electoral-conversion issue.

The crosswalk prevents the connected reader from becoming the definition of the
research program. It also prevents the 14-theme inventory from becoming a list
of unrelated topics: the umbrellas are the recurring mechanisms through which
themes connect.

## Reading rule

Use the five umbrellas to navigate. Use the 14 themes to define scope. Use the
[theme/subtheme map](US-BROAD-THEME-SUBTHEME-MAP_V1.md) to choose questions and
the [current theme pulse](US-BROAD-CURRENT-THEME-PULSE_V1.md) to see the
strongest recurring patterns. Use the evidence and event ledgers to determine
how far any particular connection can honestly travel.

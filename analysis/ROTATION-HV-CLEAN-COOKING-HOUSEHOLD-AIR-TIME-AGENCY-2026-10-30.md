# Rotation HV — Clean cooking, household air and daily agency

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When households receive clean fuels, stoves, electricity, insulation or awareness support, does that become affordable, reliable, culturally usable daily cooking with lower exposure and more time/agency—or remain equipment access without primary use, fuel security, health protection or household control?

## Why this packet matters

Clean cooking is often measured as an access or technology problem. The lived unit is more demanding: a household must be able and willing to use the technology for ordinary meals, afford fuel or electricity, keep it working, adapt it to local food and cooking practices, and avoid reverting during price shocks, outages, cold weather or supply disruption. The chain is:

```text
fuel / stove / grid / finance offer
  -> acquisition and installation
  -> affordable reliable fuel or electricity
  -> culturally compatible daily cooking
  -> lower indoor exposure and safer household work
  -> time, health, income and care agency
  -> durable use through shocks and local legitimacy
```

This lane connects public health, energy, gendered labor, climate, poverty, geography and culture. It also requires a strict distinction between device ownership, primary-use status, exposure reduction and health outcome.

| HV1 | WHO reports around 2.1 billion people still cook with polluting fuels or inefficient stoves; household air pollution caused an estimated 2.9 million deaths in 2021, including over 309,000 children under five. | Global exposure and health burden. | Household-level fuel switching, exposure reduction, affordability or causal health change. |

| HV5 | UNICEF’s Mongolia case documents the CHIP intervention for children and pregnant women in gers and explicitly notes limits of household interventions amid severe ambient pollution, requiring sustained government and multisectoral action. | Named household intervention and an important interaction/counterexample. | Indoor exposure reduction attributable to the intervention, continued use, outdoor exposure, health or household agency. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Cooking technologies are judged through taste, speed, pot size, meal sequence, fuel collection, household roles, religious and regional food practices, safety, landlord constraints and trust. LPG supply, electricity reliability, import dependence, carbon finance and local manufacturing make clean cooking geopolitical as well as domestic. A stove can be technically clean yet unusable for staple foods, unaffordable during a price spike, or abandoned when service and repair fail.

## Counterexample and source boundary

The Mongolia case prevents a simple success story: a household intervention can be appropriate for indoor exposure while ambient pollution remains severe. The IEA definition of clean-cooking access also stresses primary use, which prevents counting a device as success when households retain it but continue cooking primarily with polluting fuels. Public evidence here closes global burden, policy, technology, country progress and intervention design, but not a comparable household chain through daily use, exposure, health, time and resilience.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** Clean-cooking access, stove distribution, electrification, fuel availability, policy support or awareness is not by itself primary use, affordability, lower exposure, health protection, time agency or durable household resilience.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **clean-cooking-access-to-reliable-safe-household-agency**. The strongest next acquisition is a named household or community chain linking technology and fuel choice, price, reliability, meal compatibility, primary use, indoor exposure, cooking time, care/work allocation, health and reversion during shocks.

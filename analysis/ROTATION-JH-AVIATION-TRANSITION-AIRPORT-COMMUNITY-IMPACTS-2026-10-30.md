# Rotation JH — Aviation transition, airport communities and equitable connectivity

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When aviation pursues net-zero targets through sustainable aviation fuel, aircraft technology, operational improvements and airport engagement, do these measures produce lower climate and local pollution, safer and quieter neighborhoods, decent work and affordable connectivity—or mainly produce fuel certificates, aggregate emissions claims and consultation without a named community-level benefit?

## Why this packet matters

Aviation is a global network experienced in specific places. Fuel pathways and international targets are decided far from airport neighborhoods, while noise, ultrafine particles, land use, jobs, fares and access are concentrated around routes and facilities. A fuel can reduce lifecycle emissions while leaving combustion emissions, noise and local air quality largely unchanged; a quieter flight path can shift exposure to another community; and a net-zero target can raise costs or concentrate supply-chain land and labor burdens.

```text
air route / aircraft / airport / fuel supply chain
  -> fuel, engine, route, landing, ground operation and community engagement
  -> CO2, non-CO2 effects, noise, local air, jobs, fares, land and access
  -> mitigation, rerouting, monitoring, compensation and worker/community voice
  -> health, mobility, affordability, livelihood and place legitimacy
  -> credible aviation transition with equitable local benefit
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Air connectivity can be a lifeline for islands, remote regions, migrants, patients, students and culturally connected diasporas, but access is not evenly distributed. A route can bring tourism and jobs while increasing noise, land pressure and emissions; an airport can be a gateway for regional identity while surrounding residents bear risks without equivalent mobility benefits. The relevant unit is therefore not “aviation” or “the passenger” alone, but the route, airport, neighborhood, worker, traveler and supply-chain community.

SAF also relocates geography. Feedstocks, renewable power, refining, certification, airports and airlines may sit in different countries. A transition can reduce fossil dependence while competing with food, water, land or other sectors for biomass and energy. Global standards and accounting rules can improve comparability, but they do not automatically give source communities bargaining power or prevent green claims from outrunning actual production.

Noise and local air-quality governance reveal a legitimacy problem. Operational improvements can reduce total exposure while shifting flight paths; a consultation can hear residents without changing schedules; and a city may celebrate connectivity while a low-income neighborhood bears concentrated exposure. Effective governance needs transparent spatial data, night-time and worker measures, route-level remedies and an account of who gains access and who pays.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A net-zero target, SAF certificate, announced plant, blend mandate, emissions model, quieter-aircraft scenario, route redesign, airport consultation or jobs estimate is not by itself lower neighborhood exposure, affordable connectivity, safe work, equitable supply-chain benefit, health protection or durable place legitimacy. Lifecycle carbon reduction can coexist with local exhaust and noise; a noise reduction can move exposure; and aggregate passenger growth can conceal exclusion or fare burden.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** Aviation decarbonization, SAF deployment, operational efficiency, noise modeling and airport engagement are not by themselves lower local exposure, affordable connectivity, safe work, equitable transition benefit or legitimate airport-community relations.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **aviation-transition-to-lower-local-exposure-affordable-connectivity-and-airport-community-legitimacy**. The strongest next acquisition is a named route/airport/neighborhood/worker chain linking aircraft, fuel pathway, lifecycle and local emissions, noise, monitoring, route change, health, jobs, fares, access, supply-chain land/labor, consultation, complaint, remedy and durable agreement.

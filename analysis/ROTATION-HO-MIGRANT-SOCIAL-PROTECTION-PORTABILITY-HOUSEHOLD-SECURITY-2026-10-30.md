# Rotation HO: Migrant social-protection portability and household security

**Date:** 2026-10-30
**Lane:** migration / social protection / labour / household security / digital public administration / geopolitics
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 portability-to-real-household-security question

## Research question

When workers and families move across borders, do bilateral agreements,
eligibility rules, digital certificates and portable benefits convert into actual
health coverage, pension continuity, injury protection and household security—or
do formal rights remain unusable because of fragmented systems, status,
documentation, language, fees or delayed claims?

## Conversion chain

```text
migration or cross-border work event
  -> eligibility, contributions and entitlement record
  -> agreement, digital proof and agency coordination
  -> claim, health access, pension aggregation or family payment
  -> timely receipt and continuity through movement/return
  -> reduced poverty, risk and insecurity across the household life course
```

Portability is not merely legal eligibility. The unit is the person or family,
origin/destination, employment status, contribution history, benefit type,
document, claim, waiting time, payment, health event and later return or move.

## Stage audit

| Observed signal | Safe interpretation | Prohibited leap |
|---|---|---|
| Bilateral or regional agreement | States have established a coordination framework | Every migrant is covered for every benefit and can claim it |
| Contribution record or portable document | An entitlement or proof may be recorded | A claim was completed, paid or adequate |
| Digital exchange case volume | Administrations exchanged information | Individuals experienced faster, accessible, error-free service |
| Eligibility rule | A formal route exists | Informal, undocumented, domestic, seasonal or platform workers can use it |
| Pension/health portability policy | A benefit can potentially cross a border | Household security, health continuity or dignified return resulted |

## Bounded finding

> Social-protection portability is a necessary bridge for mobile households, but
> current evidence supports promotion only of the distinction between formal
> coordination/digital proof and actual benefit receipt, continuity and security.
> The mechanism remains Tier 2 until a named worker or household links movement,
> contributions, eligibility, claim, delay, payment, health/pension event and
> later financial or care outcome.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Benefits are embedded in family roles, language, gender, age, disability,
documentation, migration status and trust in public agencies. A digital-only
claim can be efficient for a worker with records and connectivity while
excluding a seasonal worker, older returnee, dependent spouse or person whose
name and identity records differ across countries.

Portability also shapes whether people return, stay, circularly migrate or send
remittances. A pension right that cannot be claimed may prolong unwanted stay;
a health card without cross-border provider access may fail during illness; and a
home-country welfare fund may protect a family while leaving the worker without
host-country injury protection.

## Counterexamples and limits

- EESSI/ESSPASS technical feasibility and case volume do not prove individual
  benefit receipt or absence of errors.
- An agreement may cover pensions but exclude healthcare, unemployment, injury or
  family benefits.
- Legal inclusion does not establish effective access for undocumented,
  informal, domestic or seasonal workers.
- A welfare fund or subsidy can reduce cost while remaining inadequate for
  disability, old age, injury or household shock.
- Portability rules vary by jurisdiction and benefit; this is evidence analysis,
  not benefits or immigration advice.

## Promotion decision

Promote the formal-portability-versus-real-household-security distinction to
Tier 1. Keep the cross-border-entitlement-to-received-protection mechanism in
Tier 2.

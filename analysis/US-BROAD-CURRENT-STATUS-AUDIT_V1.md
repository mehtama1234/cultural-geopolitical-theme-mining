# US broad program current-status audit v1

The newest water-infrastructure depth artifact is the [water infrastructure,
funding access, household service, and health audit](projects/cross-domain/water-infrastructure-funding-household-service-audit-v1.md).
It joins NBER water-quality and mortality evidence, GAO rural and Tribal funding
reviews, UN SDG6 global coverage and financing, Brazil water-security planning,
Northern Ireland household routines, and bounded infrastructure discussion. The
finding is a funding-to-service gap: capital, coverage, or construction does not
automatically become continuous, affordable, maintained household service.

The newest displacement-and-belonging depth artifact is the [displacement,
host capacity, livelihood conversion, and belonging audit](projects/cross-domain/displacement-host-capacity-livelihood-belonging-audit-v1.md).
It joins UNHCR global stocks and funding, World Bank/UNHCR/CEGA Kenya panel
evidence, UNHCR Uganda operational outputs, IOM migration-development analysis,
Ipsos/UNHCR attitudes, and bounded discussion. The finding is a protection-to-
capacity gap: status, aid, training, land, and return counts do not automatically
become durable livelihoods, host legitimacy, or genuine future choice.

The newest firm-and-place depth artifact is the [local firm formation, survival,
place capacity, and practical access audit](projects/cross-domain/local-firm-formation-survival-place-capacity-audit-v1.md).
It joins NBER business-formation stages, small-firm support crowd-out, disaster
loan causal evidence, Gallup local-business sentiment, New York Fed firm
capacity, Census nonemployer service/cultural scale, World Bank size-stratified
establishment capacity, Pew place-retention evidence, and bounded discussion.
The finding is a formation-to-capacity gap: applications and firm survival do
not automatically become durable local services, good jobs, household choice,
or belonging; nonemployer operators also represent a distinct local-capacity
route rather than simply failed scale.

The newest education-and-AI depth artifact is the [AI in education, learning,
teacher agency, and equity audit](projects/cross-domain/ai-education-learning-agency-equity-audit-v1.md).
It joins NBER K–12 school-capacity evidence, OECD task-performance and learning
research, Gallup teacher-control data, Chicago randomized outreach trials,
Google teen-use evidence, UNESCO governance, and bounded discussion. The
finding is a performance-to-learning gap: access and polished output can grow
faster than independent learning, teacher control, and equitable institutional
capacity.

The newest credit-and-household-room depth artifact is the [credit cost, score
feedback, household room, and exit audit](projects/cross-domain/credit-cost-score-household-exit-audit-v1.md).
It joins Philadelphia Fed borrowing-cost evidence, NBER randomized credit
information and score-feedback studies, linked Federal Reserve credit records,
CFPB market data, New York Fed expectations, and bounded finance discussion.
The finding is a conversion gap: information can lower a loan price while
credit access, repayment strain, score feedback, and safe household room move
in different directions.

The newest AI-service depth artifact is the [AI customer service, context,
human fallback, and remedy audit](projects/cross-domain/ai-customer-service-context-remedy-audit-v1.md).
It joins an HBS/Management Science randomized field experiment with FTC refund
administration, EU platform review, UK Ombudsman routing, CFPB Ombudsman
redress-friction evidence, public platform-worker remedy records, and bounded
discussion. The finding is a context-to-remedy gap: faster or more pleasant
replies do not by themselves prove restored money, access, income, recurrence
prevention, or voluntary exit; even formal U.S. redress can leave recipient-side
reissue and administrator friction unobserved.

The newest geoeconomic depth artifact is the [geoeconomic coercion, firm
spillovers, household costs, and attribution audit](projects/cross-domain/geoeconomic-coercion-firm-household-attribution-audit-v1.md).
It joins NBER coercion theory and bilateral trade-risk evidence with Census
firm-level export-control spillovers, a named BIS Applied Materials settlement,
Hygon reported adaptation and growth, CRS retaliation chronology, NBER tariff
pass-through, Ipsos household attribution, and bounded public discussion. The
finding is a translation gap: pressure can alter trade, payroll, supply chains,
prices, and enforcement while strategic capability, household incidence, and
legitimacy remain separate outcomes.

The newest AI-health depth artifact is the [AI health navigation, trust, control,
and continuity audit](projects/cross-domain/ai-health-navigation-trust-control-audit-v1.md).
It joins Pew health-chatbot and health-information evidence with GAO wearables,
Ipsos task-specific diagnostic trust, WHO lifecycle governance, PwC continuous
health tracking, and bounded LinkedIn discussion. The finding is a control gap:
speed and low-cost access can expand first-step agency while disclosure,
verification, human fallback, care continuity, privacy, and remedy remain
separate and mostly unobserved.

The newest material-to-meaning/action depth artifact is the [repeated-respondent
material pressure, trust, and action rotation audit](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/material-pressure-meaning-action-rotation-audit-v1.md).
It compares HTOPS, CCES, CES, ANES, and Pew longitudinal surfaces and shows
that persistence, institutional judgment, policy demand, turnout, contact,
protest, and generalized social trust are distinct endpoints. The evidence
supports identity- and attribution-conditioned translation, not a universal
hardship-to-action path; a dated event, responsible actor, usable alternative,
remedy, and later recovery remain open.

The newest payment-and-remedy depth artifact is the [digital payment adoption,
fraud recovery, and practical-exit audit](projects/us-financial-intermediation/digital-payment-fraud-recovery-practical-exit-audit-v1.md).
It compares NBER outage research with OECD authority reports, Federal Reserve
household fraud data, UK open-banking activity, Brazil's Pix payment diaries,
and ASEAN scam-recovery evidence. The route shows that payment scale and
preference can coexist with unresolved loss, weak fallback, and incomplete
recourse; actual reimbursement, restored financial room, and switching remain
unobserved.

The newest workplace-control depth artifact is the [AI implementation,
worker-control, and security audit](projects/us-worker-control-remedy/ai-implementation-worker-control-security-audit-v1.md).
It separates task activity, shipped output, firm expectations, worker
adaptation, training returns, organizational design, autonomy, and outside
options. The current evidence supports a conversion gap: measurable AI use or
productivity can arrive before worker discretion, bargaining power, durable
earnings, or household security are observed.

The newest housing-risk depth artifact is the [property shock, repair,

It compares wildfire destruction, FEMA housing assistance, eviction orders,
rental-assistance lotteries, insurance pricing, mortgage lock-in, and housing

intervention can improve one stage while safe occupancy, return, mobility,
household security, and durable place attachment remain unknown.

The newest service-legitimacy depth artifact is the [delivery, attribution,

**Checked:** 2026-09-30
**Scope:** the full 14-theme societal, cultural, consumer, institutional,
financial, political, firm, infrastructure, and geopolitical program

**Program orientation:** this is a broad cultural-social-consumer-political
atlas, not a single health-cost, consumer-remedy, or AI-work study. The
empirical core is U.S.-anchored, while comparative and global evidence is used
to test capability, inequality, institutional capacity, dependence, state
power, and geopolitical consequences. Those layers are kept separate: a
global comparison is context or a mechanism test, not a U.S. household result;
an observed domestic capability is not automatically external leverage. The
program's central task is to map how conditions, rules, prices, technologies,
and shocks move through money, time, access, control, status, culture,
institutions, political action, firm strategy, infrastructure, and state
power—and to mark the missing links rather than collapse them into one story.

## Coverage finding

All 14 themes in the [broad theme inventory](US-BROAD-THEME-INVENTORY_V1.md)
have a current evidence anchor, an identified measurement level, and a named
missing arrow in the [coverage matrix](US-BROAD-THEME-COVERAGE-MATRIX_V1.md).
The matrix currently contains 14 theme rows and 108 local or external evidence
links; the local-link audit found no missing targets on 2026-09-17.

Coverage means “there is a source and a bounded question.” It does not mean
that a theme has been demonstrated as a single trend or that its arrows have
been causally joined.

## The shared end-to-end model

```text
condition / price / rule / technology / shock
  -> money, time, access, control, status, health, or security changes
  -> people, consumers, workers, firms, agencies, and places adapt
  -> the next actor responds and redistributes cost, risk, data, or power
  -> trust, identity, culture, public demand, political action, firm exposure,
     sector capacity, or geopolitical leverage changes
```

For every arrow, record the unit, date, geography, source, weight, method,
uncertainty, and status: observed, reported, estimated, compared, inferred, or
open. A cross-source pattern is useful context; it is not a same-person or
same-firm join unless the identifiers and design support that claim.

## What we should pursue next

1. Execute the material/time/care linkage pass: acquire or identify a same-unit
   design with a dated bill, care need, or work change, alternatives and
   schedule control, paid/unpaid time response, and follow-up outcomes.
2. Follow one dated consumer or public-program case from first contact through
   effort, response, remedy, abandonment, and later trust or exit.
3. Use SHED panel variables that genuinely repeat to test whether adaptation
   reverses after financial improvement, and preserve the nonresponse limits.
4. Add a matched place or firm comparison for ownership, local distribution,
   service quality, and practical replaceability.
5. Build a longitudinal political instrument that measures exposure,
   attribution, source, identity, trust, consumer action, civic action, and
   vote separately.
6. Seek counterexamples: households that recover without the predicted loss of
   trust, agencies that switch providers, firms whose gains diffuse locally,
   and places where infrastructure adds capability without dependence.

## Stop conditions

- Do not treat one household or one current dataset as the program.
- Do not call a complaint response a remedy without a verified outcome.
- Do not call consumer adaptation political action without measuring meaning
  and action.
- Do not infer culture, polarization, or distrust from hardship alone.
- Do not call planned infrastructure operational or sovereign.
- Do not join separate surveys by matching geography and call them the same
  respondents.

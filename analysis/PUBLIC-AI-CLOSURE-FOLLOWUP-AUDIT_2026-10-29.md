# Public AI closure follow-up audit: SSA

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Purpose:** test whether the public record closes the chain from SSA AI capability and workflow use to claimant exposure, human contestability, correction, benefit/service receipt and durable protection  
**Status:** agency use cases and oversight are observable; claimant-level remedy and outcome remain unverified

## Executive result

The current official record supports a bounded finding, not a claim about whether
SSA AI is beneficial or harmful to claimants:

> A public agency can name AI use cases, workflow targets and an oversight review
> before the public record shows which claimant encounters were AI-mediated, how a
> person could contest or correct the result, and whether the correction restored
> a benefit or service.

SSA's Office of Inspector General lists **The Social Security Administration's Use
of Artificial Intelligence (142321)** as an ongoing audit that began March 30,
2026 and is planned for FY2027 Q2. The listing is evidence of an oversight
process, not evidence of audit findings or claimant outcomes. The agency budget
and performance materials describe modernization, automation and AI-related
capabilities, but do not, in the records reviewed here, provide a public
case-level bridge from a named AI-assisted action to exposure, appeal,
correction, receipt or later continuity.

This is a documented source boundary, not evidence that SSA failed to protect
claimants or that a particular AI system made a particular decision.

## Stage audit

| Stage | Publicly observable | Not publicly closed in the reviewed sources |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | AI named in agency planning, modernization or oversight material | Which public-facing claimant problem is being solved and for whom |
| Mechanism | Broad workflow/use-case and management descriptions | Model version, input/output trace, human role, vendor/system boundary and error mode |
| Operation | OIG review is active; agency has formal program and disability-process routes | Which named claimant encounter used AI and when |
| Contestability | SSA has established notices, reconsideration/hearing/appeal structures in its formal processes | Whether an affected person was told AI was involved, could challenge the output, or received an understandable explanation |
| Correction | Formal correction and appeal routes exist in the program architecture | AI-specific correction rate, time to correction, rollback, remediation and claimant notification |
| Material consequence | Benefits and services are the relevant institutional endpoint | Individual receipt date, amount/service delivered, interruption duration, adequacy and later continuity linked to AI exposure |
| Meaning and power | Agency accountability and oversight are visible institutionally | Claimant trust, participation, repeat contact, exit, advocacy or political response attributable to AI-mediated experience |

## What this changes in the theme portfolio

The public-AI theme should be narrowed from **AI adoption** to **public-AI
readiness-to-remedy**. Readiness has at least four separable components:

1. **operational readiness:** the agency can deploy or integrate a tool;
2. **epistemic readiness:** the agency can identify what the tool did and why;
3. **procedural readiness:** a person can contest, correct or escalate the result;
4. **material readiness:** the correction restores the benefit or service quickly
   enough to protect the person.

The public record currently supports the first component and some institutional
capacity for the second and third. It does not close the fourth for a named AI
encounter.

## Cultural and institutional meaning

For a claimant, an AI-enabled public encounter is not experienced as a model,
budget line or audit number. It is experienced as a notice, delay, request for
evidence, changed status, call, appointment, payment or missed payment. The
legitimacy question is therefore whether the person can identify what happened,
reach a responsible institution, make a correction travel through the system,
and see the material result before the harm compounds.

For the institution, an ongoing audit creates an accountability promise. That
promise becomes meaningful only when the eventual record connects system use to
errors, human review, correction speed and actual claimant protection. Until
then, agency readiness and claimant remedy should remain separate variables.

## Counterexamples and boundaries

- An ongoing audit is not a finding of failure, bias or harm.
- A formal human-review or appeal route does not prove that a claimant can
  identify AI involvement or obtain a timely correction.
- An agency performance measure is not a claimant-level outcome unless its
  denominator, unit, time window and endpoint are explicit.
- A corrected record or decision is not the same as payment, service receipt,
  adequacy or durable continuity.
- The absence of a public case linkage does not establish that SSA lacks such
  records; it establishes that the reviewed public sources do not expose them.

## Closure request

The decisive fields for a future named-unit conversion are:

```text
claim/case key | program | AI system/task | model/version or rule boundary
| human reviewer role | claimant notice | adverse/changed action
| contest route | correction request date | correction decision/date
| benefit/service receipt date | interruption days | amount/adequacy
| repeat contact | later continuity | claimant trust/action/exit
```

If the OIG final report, SSA disclosures, litigation records or public records
requests cannot supply these fields, the correct result is a source-boundary
finding. Do not substitute an AI inventory, modernization target, audit start
date or agency-wide processing metric for claimant remedy.

## Promotion decision

Promote the narrower Tier 2 mechanism:

**Public-AI readiness can become observable before public-AI remedy is
verifiable.**

Keep the SSA materials as institutional-capacity and oversight evidence. Do not
promote them to evidence of claimant harm, claimant benefit, fairness, trust or
productivity until a named-use-case outcome chain is available.

# Rotation BC — Carbon capture, storage, MRV and community legitimacy

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** carbon management / industrial technology / climate governance / infrastructure / place  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 capture-to-permanent-reduction question

## Research question

When carbon capture, utilization and storage projects receive tax credits or
demonstration funding, what converts capture equipment and subsurface storage
into verified, durable emissions reduction—and how are pipeline, pore-space,
land, safety, monitoring and community burdens governed?

## Conversion chain

```text
emissions source or atmospheric carbon
  -> capture and conditioning
  -> transport and injection/use
  -> monitoring, reporting and verification
  -> permanent storage or genuinely lower-emission product
  -> measured emissions reduction, local safety and durable public legitimacy
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| GAO-26-107711 carbon-capture tax credit | 45Q incentivizes capture and storage/use; 33 U.S. facilities existed as of March 2026 and claims more than tripled from 2019 to 2023. | policy incentive and facility/claim trend | verified additionality, permanent storage, tax administration and net reduction |
| GAO 45Q MRV analysis | Geological storage requires monitoring, reporting and verification plans; rules distinguish storage and utilization pathways. | formal MRV and credit procedure | measurement quality, leakage, lifecycle emissions and enforcement |
| GAO-22-105274 CCUS assessment | Wider deployment requires capture, transport, utilization and storage infrastructure; cost, land access, proximity and community engagement are challenges. | system components and deployment constraints | connected operation, cost, safety and host acceptance |
| GAO-22-105111 DOE demonstration management | DOE CCS demonstrations require management and oversight; recommendations remained open in the cited review period. | governance and management-risk signal | completed demonstrations, lessons learned and operating performance |
| GAO-24-106489 DOE carbon-capture project risks | DOE funded substantial R&D and demonstration activity while project-selection and risk-management processes required improvement. | funding scale and management challenge | construction, capture rate, storage integrity, cost and public benefit |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** capture and storage are framed as climate, industrial and energy
  strategies supported by tax credits and public funding.
- **Mechanism:** capture energy penalty, transport network, injection geology,
  pore-space rights, monitoring, reporting, verification, permits, finance and
  community consent determine whether a claim is credible.
- **Operation:** CO₂ is captured, compressed, transported, injected or used,
  monitored and reported over the relevant storage and credit period.
- **Material consequence:** atmospheric or facility emissions decline durably,
  while nearby communities experience jobs, traffic, land use, safety and
  possible environmental exposure.
- **Meaning:** a project can signal industrial renewal and climate leadership,
  but residents may interpret it through previous extraction histories, pipeline
  risk, property rights, unequal benefits and confidence in monitoring.
- **Power:** emitters, pipeline operators, storage owners, regulators, tax
  authorities and project financiers control the technical and legal chain;
  communities often bear local risk without controlling the subsurface asset.

## Bounded finding

> **Carbon capture is verified permanent reduction, not captured volume or a tax
> credit.** A capture facility, 45Q claim, MRV plan, pipeline, injection permit
> or demonstration award does not establish additional, durable net emissions
> reduction, safe operation, fair compensation or durable community legitimacy.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

CCUS makes the hidden geography of climate policy visible. Capture is attached to
an industrial source; pipelines cross multiple jurisdictions; storage depends on
subsurface geology and property rights; monitoring continues across time. The
benefit may be global or corporate while the risks are local and cumulative.

MRV is therefore not only a technical requirement. It is a public relationship:
who can see the baseline, challenge the measurement, receive warning, claim
compensation, and verify that the carbon remains stored? Without those routes,
“permanent” is a promise made by the operator rather than a shared public fact.

## Counterexamples and limits

- MRV plans and tax-credit rules can be meaningful safeguards; this packet does
  not treat them as empty formalities.
- Utilization can have value in some products, but product life, displacement and
  lifecycle emissions differ by application.
- A project can reduce emissions at a source without proving net system-wide
  decarbonization.
- Local opposition is not proof that storage is technically unsafe, and a
  technically valid storage site is not proof of local legitimacy.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: capture, transport, storage/use, MRV,
permanent reduction and community legitimacy are separate stages. Keep the net-
reduction and public-benefit claims in Tier 2 until a named project ledger closes
the measured operating chain.

## Next test

Track one 45Q or DOE-supported project from baseline emissions through capture,
transport, injection/use, MRV, credit claim, leakage/monitoring, local exposure,
jobs, compensation and community response. Include a utilization pathway and a
geological-storage pathway where possible.

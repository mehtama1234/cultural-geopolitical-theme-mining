# Rotation GR — open science, usable knowledge and local agency

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 conversion question  
**Research lane:** open science, research infrastructure, citizen participation, knowledge sovereignty and local decision-making

## Why this rotation exists

Open publication, open data, open-source software, shared infrastructure and
citizen science are often treated as equivalent to access and social benefit.
They are not. A resource can be technically public while remaining difficult to
find, understand, translate, verify, afford, reuse or govern. The same opening
can also expose sensitive information, extract Indigenous or local knowledge,
create uncompensated participation, or concentrate value in institutions with
better compute, language capacity and legal support.

## Research question

When publicly funded research, datasets, software, infrastructure or citizen
science are opened, under what conditions do named people and communities gain
usable, trusted, locally relevant knowledge and decision influence rather than
mere technical availability or extractive visibility?

## Conversion chain

```text
funded research / paper / dataset / code / infrastructure / citizen invitation
  -> person or community can discover, understand, contribute, verify and reuse safely
  -> local decision, education, health, environmental practice or livelihood improves
  -> knowledge is trusted, recognized and usable across language and place
  -> communities retain consent, authority, benefit and control over sensitive data
```

## Evidence snapshot

1. UNESCO's Open Science Recommendation defines open science broadly: open
   scientific knowledge, infrastructure, engagement with societal actors and
   dialogue with other knowledge systems. It explicitly frames openness as a
   change across the research cycle, not merely free publication. This is a
   normative architecture, not proof that every open object reaches a user or
   changes a decision.
2. UNESCO's implementation page records a global commitment to accessibility,
   inclusiveness and equity, while the 2025 consultation and reporting cycle
   shows that implementation itself requires monitoring and institutional work.
   Adoption of a recommendation therefore closes policy intent, not local use.
3. UNESCO's Open Science Outlook identifies digital, technological and
   knowledge gaps and the need for training, infrastructure, capacity-building
   and international cooperation. Geography, language, connectivity and skills
   are conversion conditions, not background variables.
4. OECD guidance on publicly funded research data makes reusability operational:
   human-readable and machine-actionable metadata, provenance, licensing,
   curation, reproducibility and maintained software are required. It also
   identifies unclear ownership, weak provenance, certification gaps and rights
   issues as barriers. A downloadable file is therefore not automatically a
   reusable dataset.
5. UNESCO's Recommendation requires respect for knowledge sovereignty and
   governance, including Indigenous and local communities' authority over data
   about traditional knowledge, lands and resources. Open access may be the
   wrong endpoint for sacred, sensitive or collectively governed knowledge;
   responsible access can mean governed, tiered or closed access.
6. UNESCO-supported citizen-science water platforms provide a concrete positive
   mechanism: communities collect and analyse environmental data, and projects
   can connect participation to local water governance. The same guidance calls
   for feedback, participant benefit, data quality, attribution and evaluation
   of wider policy impact. Participation or an open dashboard alone does not
   prove that a local authority acted or that contributors retained agency.

## Bounded proposition

**A paper, open-access link, dataset, code repository, research infrastructure,
citizen-science invitation, public consultation, knowledge platform or
open-science policy is not by itself usable knowledge, equitable participation,
local decision influence, reproducibility, trusted evidence, livelihood,
health/environmental protection or knowledge sovereignty.** A file published,
dataset released, citizen enrolled or finding cited does not establish that
named people could discover it, understand it in their language, contribute
safely, verify it, use it in a decision, receive recognition or retain control
over community data and interpretation.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

The relevant unit is the person or community trying to move from discovery to
safe use. That route varies by language, literacy, bandwidth, device, disability,
institutional capacity, research incentives, legal status, local epistemology
and trust in the source. Rural and lower-resourced institutions may receive an
open resource without the storage, compute, training or staff needed to reuse
it. Indigenous and local knowledge may be meaningful only within place,
relationship, practice and customary authority; extracting it into a universal
repository can destroy context or transfer value away from its holders.

The geographic question is therefore not “where is the dataset hosted?” but
“who can interpret it, contest it, act on it and govern its next use?” A strong
open-science pathway combines multilingual communication, local intermediaries,
usable metadata, feedback to contributors, attribution, benefit sharing,
privacy and a clearly governed route from evidence to decision.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- Open access can reduce paywall barriers, accelerate collaboration and make
  publicly funded research more inspectable; the packet does not argue against
  openness.
- Citizen science can generate useful local observations and learning, but
  sampling bias, uneven participation, weak feedback or unpaid extraction can
  limit both scientific and civic value.
- Sensitive health, location, biodiversity and cultural data may require
  controlled access. “Open as possible, closed as necessary” is a governance
  distinction, not a failure of openness.
- A citation, download, portal visit or policy adoption is an upstream signal.
  It should not be promoted to household, community, health, environmental or
  geopolitical impact without a named actor, place, time and later-stage record.

## Promotion decision

Promote as **Tier 1 bounded distinction**: open science expands the possible
availability and participation surface, and official frameworks identify
reusability, capacity, inclusion, consent and monitoring as necessary design
conditions. Promote as **Tier 2 conversion question**: open-science-to-usable-
knowledge-and-local-agency, requiring named evidence from access through
interpretation, contribution, decision use, recognition, benefit and governance.

## Six-stage closure table

| Stage | What to observe | Current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | policy, publication, repository, dataset, code, platform or invitation | Official sources clearly document the expansion of open-science intent and infrastructure |
| Mechanism | discoverability, language, metadata, rights, training, connectivity, consent and feedback | Frameworks name the conditions; comparative local incidence remains incomplete |
| Operation | person/community finds, understands, contributes, verifies and safely reuses the resource | Cases exist, but a cross-place named-user operating ledger is not yet assembled |
| Material consequence | changed service, health, environmental practice, education, livelihood or local decision | Public sources rarely join open-science objects to same-unit later outcomes |
| Meaning | trust, recognition, epistemic respect, attribution and knowledge legitimacy | Principles are explicit; durable community perception is not established at portfolio scale |
| Power | control, benefit sharing, correction, refusal, revocation and agenda influence | Indigenous/local governance principles are documented; realized power by case needs further evidence |

## Next test

Select three place-bound cases with different access regimes: an open public
research-data project, a citizen-science environmental project and a
community-governed Indigenous/local knowledge project. For each, trace one
named contributor or user from discovery to interpretation, action, feedback,
benefit and governance. Record language, device/connectivity, intermediary,
data rights, attribution, decision recipient, observed outcome and any refusal
or correction. Do not infer local agency from repository or publication metrics.

## Source boundary

The official record is strong for the definition, policy intent, infrastructure
requirements, ethical safeguards and examples of participatory science. It is
weaker for comparable, same-unit evidence that an open resource produced a
durable household, community or local-government outcome across geographies.
The conversion remains a research opportunity, not a promoted causal finding.

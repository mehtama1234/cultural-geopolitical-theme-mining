# Rotation AK: Eviction prevention, legal access and durable tenancy

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** housing / courts / legal access / household stability / place  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 tenancy-retention question

## Research question

When a household faces rent arrears or an eviction filing, what converts notice,
legal assistance, rental aid or a court outcome into safe, affordable and
durable tenancy rather than displacement, doubling-up or recurring crisis?

## Conversion chain

```text
rent pressure or lease dispute
  -> notice/filing
  -> information, legal representation and rental assistance
  -> hearing, payment, agreement or judgment
  -> possession/eviction or preserved tenancy
  -> six/twelve-month housing stability, health, school and work continuity
```

An eviction filing is not the same as a completed eviction, and a payment or
court resolution is not the same as durable tenancy. The household may still
face arrears, rent burden, landlord screening, unsafe conditions, relocation or
repeat filing.

## Stage audit

| Observed signal | Safe interpretation | Prohibited leap |
|---|---|---|
| Eviction notice or filing | A formal housing-court or landlord process began | The household lost possession or became homeless |
| Legal-aid availability | A representation or advice route exists | The household found, qualified for and completed representation |
| Rental-assistance award/payment | Funds were approved or disbursed | Landlord accepted payment, case ended or rent became affordable |
| Court judgment or agreement | A formal resolution was recorded | The household remained safely housed for six or twelve months |
| Housing-risk survey response | A household reports risk at a point in time | Cause, legal pathway, actual displacement or later recovery is known |

## Bounded finding

> Eviction prevention is a completed legal-and-housing route, not a notice count,
> legal-aid offer, rental-assistance payment or court disposition alone. Durable
> tenancy requires preserving possession, affordability, safety and household
> continuity after the formal case or payment event.

This finding does not treat every filing as an eviction or every assistance
program as ineffective. It specifies the stages needed to distinguish crisis
intervention from durable housing stability.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Housing court is an institutional encounter where legal language, time, money,
transport, childcare, fear, documentation and landlord power converge. A tenant
may understand the debt but not the procedural deadline, may receive advice but
not representation, or may win a payment agreement that still leaves the rent
structurally unaffordable.

Place changes the route through court accessibility, legal-aid supply, landlord
market power, rent levels, informal family networks, shelter capacity, transit,
school continuity and local stigma. Displacement can sever cultural and social
ties even when a household avoids a formal judgment by moving, doubling up or
using emergency arrangements.

## Counterexamples and limits

- A filing can be dismissed, cured or resolved without displacement; filing is
  not possession loss.
- Rental assistance can prevent an eviction and preserve a family’s home;
  delays, targeting and incomplete oversight do not prove failure in every case.
- Legal representation may improve procedural fairness without making rent
  affordable or guaranteeing long-term retention.
- Linked Census studies provide unusually strong outcome evidence but focus on
  studied places/populations and do not identify every intervention pathway.
- Survey housing risk and court records have different units, timing and
  coverage; they should not be pooled as one eviction rate.

## Promotion decision

Promote the filing/legal-aid/payment/court-disposition-versus-durable-tenancy
distinction to Tier 1. Keep the mechanism in Tier 2 until a named jurisdiction
links the same household’s filing, representation, payment, possession,
relocation and six/twelve-month housing, school, work and health outcomes.

## Next test

Build a jurisdiction-level eviction ledger with notice, filing, counsel,
hearing, payment, judgment, possession, move/doubling-up, rent burden, repair,

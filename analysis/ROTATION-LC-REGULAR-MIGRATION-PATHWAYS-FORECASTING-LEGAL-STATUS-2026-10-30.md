# Rotation LC — Regular migration pathways, forecasting and the geography of legal status

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** Do regular migration pathways make movement safer, more rights-bearing and more predictable, and can forecasting help governments prepare without turning people into aggregate flows or using risk models to narrow access?

## Why this packet matters

“Regular migration pathway” sounds like a settled category, but it is a lived
sequence of information, recruitment, visa, border, status, work, family, health,
renewal and return decisions. IOM's 2026 evidence shows pathways are selective
and uneven across regions, groups and geopolitical relationships. OECD's migration
forecasting handbook adds a second boundary: forecasts must be category-specific,
continuously updated and embedded in governance, but they cannot reliably capture
sudden political shocks or individual aspirations.

```text
information / recruiter / sponsor / visa / proof of funds
  -> border and status decision
  -> work, study, family, health, housing and social protection
  -> renewal, change of employer/status, return, regularization or exclusion
  -> safety, rights, contribution, remedy and future pathway
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| LC1 | IOM's World Migration Report 2026 challenges the idea that regular pathways are uniformly open or that closing them solves irregular migration; it finds pathways increasingly selective and uneven across regions, countries and groups. | Definition, selectivity and policy myth boundary. | A person's access, fairness, safety, cost, rights or actual route choice. |
| LC2 | IOM links regular status to access to rights, health, labour markets, remittances and investment, while restrictive policies can displace rather than reduce mobility into riskier irregular journeys. | Status-to-rights and restriction-to-risk mechanism. | Causal attribution, route substitution, protection, exploitation, family outcome or remedy. |
| LC3 | OECD's 2026 handbook says migration forecasting must be category-specific because forced, labour, student and family flows have different drivers, volatility and data structures. | Forecast-design and category distinction. | Forecast accuracy for a person, policy shock, hidden movement or local service need. |
| LC4 | OECD reports aggregate net-migration forecasts are too coarse for operational policy; integration services, visa quotas and labour programmes require knowing who is moving and why. | Named-user/service planning requirement. | Individual consent, non-discrimination, local knowledge, service quality or humane decision-making. |
| LC5 | OECD identifies data trade-offs among accuracy, timeliness, coverage, granularity and continuity; real-time digital traces can help short-term forecasting but may be unreliable. | Data and uncertainty boundary. | Privacy, representation, surveillance risk, model fairness, public legitimacy or benefit. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Legal status is experienced through ordinary encounters: a recruiter, a visa
portal, a border officer, an employer, a landlord, a clinic, a school or a
police checkpoint. A pathway can be formally open while being culturally or
geographically inaccessible because information is not in the right language,
documents require costly travel, digital access is weak, or the sponsor controls
the worker's ability to change jobs.

Regularity is also relational. A pathway may be regular in the destination but
produce debt or exploitation in the origin country; a visa may authorize entry
but not family reunification, health coverage or a safe job; a worker may be
regular at arrival and irregular after an employer breach or missed renewal.
Forecasting that sees only admissions can miss these status transitions.

Geopolitically, pathway design expresses who is considered desirable, risky,
temporary, assimilable or strategically useful. Selective pathways can deepen
inequality between countries and groups while restrictive policies shift movement
to smugglers and informal intermediaries. Forecasts can improve housing,
language-service and reception capacity, but they can also be used to justify
caps without measuring rights, family unity or the alternatives available to
people.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A visa category, pathway count, forecast, labour-shortage list, regular-status
grant, online portal, employer sponsor, border statistic, pre-departure guide or
anti-fraud recommendation does not prove safe migration. A restrictive policy
may reduce recorded entries while increasing irregularity; an open pathway may be
unusable for low-income applicants; and a forecast can be statistically good at
aggregate level while missing a sudden crisis or a particular language, family or
occupation. Aggregate flows must not replace named rights and service outcomes.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Regular migration pathways are selective legal,
economic and geopolitical infrastructures. Their effect depends on information,
cost, recruitment, status transitions, rights, family, services and remedy—not
only admission volume.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **regular-pathway-to-safe-status-rights-service-access-and-adaptive-preparedness**. The next acquisition should follow one applicant/worker/student/family from information and recruitment through documents, fee, visa, border, status, employer or institution, housing, health, family, renewal/change, complaint and return/settlement, alongside the forecast and service capacity assumptions used by authorities.

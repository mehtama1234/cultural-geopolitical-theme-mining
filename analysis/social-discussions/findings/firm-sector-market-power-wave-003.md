# Firm, sector, and market power — social wave 003

Window: 2025-10-17 through 2026-10-17

Records: three public items: one LinkedIn business experience, one Reddit
policy discussion, and one X antitrust summary. No reproducible, clearly
relevant YouTube item was retained in the restricted search.

## What the discussion adds

The LinkedIn account makes switching friction concrete: a large renewal
increase can be absorbed for a while, but migration costs do not make exit
impossible when the price shock is large enough. The Reddit discussion turns
concentration into a public argument about prices, work, investment,
innovation, and whether antitrust action is legitimate. The X summary adds a
governance layer: competition concerns can arise from board and capital ties,
not only from a visible price increase.

The bounded interpretation is that market power is experienced through a chain:
structure and dependence -> pricing, access, or risk allocation -> switching,
survival, local availability, or public remedy. The items do not establish
market-wide concentration, consumer welfare, causal harm, or a final legal
finding.

## Bridge to the atlas

The social items attach to the open route:

firm scale, ownership, or dependence
  -> price, contract, labor, investment, access, or risk-transfer condition
  -> switching, entry, survival, local service, worker power, or public remedy

They add ordinary language about being trapped, making exit worthwhile,
private governance ties, and the competing efficiency and concentration frames.
They do not replace the atlas's market-share, administrative, firm-panel,
quasi-experimental, or sector-access evidence.

## Counter-signal and next test

Large firms can finance investment, spread fixed costs, and provide continuity;
small firms can also face financing, scale, and reliability constraints. A
customer switching successfully is not the same as low switching friction for
everyone. The next test should compare these claims with concentration,
contract renewal, entry and exit, prices, wages, local access, ownership ties,
and sector-specific remedies. A YouTube gap remains documented.

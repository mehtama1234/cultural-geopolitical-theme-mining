# Political judgment and collective action — social wave 003

Window: 2025-10-17 through 2026-10-17

Records: three public items: one LinkedIn movement post, one Reddit local
organizing thread, and one X trending summary. No reproducible, clearly
relevant YouTube item was retained in the restricted search.

## What the discussion adds

The discussion shows several stages between concern and institutional response.
The LinkedIn item describes fast, decentralized youth activism but warns that
political openings can be captured after mobilization. The Reddit item makes
the sequence local and practical: protest review, a site tour, a local-law
campaign, phone banking, and election work appear in one organizing meeting.
The X summary adds the platform layer, where hashtags and voter-registration
appeals can create visibility and coordination while leaving actual turnout and
representation unresolved.

The bounded interpretation is that collective action is a chain, not a single
engagement score: interpretation -> coordination -> participation -> pressure
-> institutional response -> possible capture, persistence, or withdrawal.
These items do not establish causality, turnout, policy success, or durable
political change.

## Bridge to the atlas

The social items attach to the open route:

material condition or perceived injustice
  -> attribution, identity, efficacy, and coordination
  -> protest, organizing, voting, lobbying, or withdrawal
  -> institutional response, capture, legitimacy, or exit

They add ordinary language about decentralized organizing, local action chains,
voter mobilization, and the risk that visibility does not become power. They do
not replace the atlas's survey, panel, election, civic-network, or institutional
evidence.

## Counter-signal and next test

Organizing can widen participation, but some participants may prefer local,
nonpartisan, or issue-specific action over party alignment; others may withdraw
because of risk, time, distrust, or low expected efficacy. The next test should
compare social action claims with attendance, voter registration and turnout,
coalition composition, policy response, retaliation, and later persistence. A
YouTube gap remains documented rather than filled with unrelated content.

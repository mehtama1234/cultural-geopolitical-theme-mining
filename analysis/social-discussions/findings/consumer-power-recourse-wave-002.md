# Consumer power and recourse — social wave 002

Window: 2025-10-17 through 2026-10-17

Records: four public items: one YouTube community item, one Reddit thread,
one LinkedIn post, and one X support reply.

## What the discussion adds

The four items point to a practical distinction in the consumer-recourse
research: a route can exist on paper while still feeling unusable in practice.
The Reddit and YouTube items describe refund uncertainty, automated or buried
support, and difficulty reaching a person. The LinkedIn item describes public
posting as an escalation route when ordinary support fails. The X item is a
counter-signal: a public support account describes a case-specific reversal
and adjustment, suggesting that public escalation can sometimes produce a
visible response.

The strongest bounded interpretation is therefore support friction and public
escalation are recurring discussion signals, not proof that most consumers
cannot obtain remedies or that public posting causes resolution.

## Bridge to the atlas

The social items attach to the open arrow:

formal complaint or remedy
  -> usable response or continued friction
  -> trust, continued use, switching, or practical exit

They add language about time, uncertainty, human access, and public reputation.
They do not measure complaint prevalence, resolution rates, monetary recovery,
or whether people actually switched providers.

## Counter-signal and next test

The X support reply and several Reddit replies describe routes that can still
reach a human or produce a refund. The next pass should compare public
escalation with formal complaint records and verified outcomes: time to remedy,
amount recovered, repeat contact, and whether the customer stayed, switched,
or stopped complaining silently.

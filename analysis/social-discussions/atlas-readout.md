# Social-discussion readout — first wave

The current AI/work-control extension adds a LinkedIn discussion from Harvard's
Project on Workforce about the NBER pro-worker AI framework. The discussion
turns the research taxonomy into an implementation question: can firms name
new tasks, preserve worker judgment, and make collaborative AI viable rather
than treating productivity as cost reduction? It is a public interpretation
and selected reaction layer, not worker prevalence or an implementation result.

The first wave adds a missing interpretive layer to the atlas: public
discussion often makes the hidden trade visible before it can measure it.

- **AI and work:** public talk moves between career survival, worker control,
  subgroup inequality, and professional research. It amplifies the atlas's
  open question about whether task-level time savings become worker-level
  security or autonomy.
- **Platform exit:** people describe non-use as selective, reversible, and
  socially costly. It complements the atlas's exit research by showing that
  professional identity, relationships, and definitions of “social media” shape
  the feasible alternative.
- **AI companionship:** discussion combines support, loneliness, disclosure,
  continuity, and governance. It complements usage measures by showing why
  control, memory, and the ability to leave matter alongside perceived empathy.

This readout is an interpretation of a small, searchable public sample. It is
not a population sentiment estimate, a causal result, or a substitute for the
underlying research records.

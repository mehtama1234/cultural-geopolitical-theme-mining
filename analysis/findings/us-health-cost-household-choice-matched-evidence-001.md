# A medical cost can change care, work, and debt in different ways

## Short answer

Medical cost is not one burden. A person may delay care, borrow, miss work, lose income, or carry a collection record. These paths can happen together, but they do not have the same cause or result. Current US household survey evidence shows many adults skipping care because of cost. NBER studies show that health shocks can damage work and credit, while coverage can reduce collections. A separate randomized study shows that debt relief can improve some financial measures without improving health or care use.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Care choice | The Federal Reserve reports that 26% of adults skipped some care because of cost in 2025. | Which care was skipped and what happened later. |
| Health shock | Hospital admissions can affect unpaid bills, earnings, credit, and borrowing. | How much came from the bill versus lost ability to work. |
| Coverage | Medicaid studies find lower medical collections and lower out-of-pocket costs. | Whether coverage protected the same family across work and health changes. |
| Debt record | Medical debt relief can change payments or credit access. | Whether health, care use, or financial security improved. |
| Work rule | Employer-linked coverage can keep a worker in a job after a shock. | Whether the job remained good, safe, or freely chosen. |

Sources: [Federal Reserve household survey](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-economic-hardships.htm), [NBER hospital-admission study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22288), [NBER Medicaid and financial health study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24002), [NBER medical-debt relief experiments](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32315), [NBER medical-debt reporting study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33644), and [NBER employer coverage and work study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17223).

## The connection

```text
medical need + price or coverage gap → care delayed, changed, or paid for
                                    → debt, lost work, or lost income
                                    → housing, saving, family, and credit choice
                                    → trust and public response
```

The evidence supports several parts of this path, but not one complete story for the same household. A collection record can be removed without the illness being treated. A job can be kept because it carries insurance, even when the worker would prefer to leave. Coverage can reduce debt while increasing care use, which means lower debt is not the only measure of success.

## The important split

There are at least three different things people call “medical debt.” First is the bill that arrives. Second is the payment problem that can force a family to delay care or use savings. Third is the record that can follow the person into credit decisions. A policy can fix one layer and leave the others in place.

The NBER medical-debt relief experiments are a useful counterweight. Relief changed some financial outcomes, but did not improve average health, health-care use, or financial wellness in the reported study. That does not mean relief is useless. It means the result must name the layer that changed.

## Four views

**Household:** Record the care delayed, bill paid, work missed, savings used, and family help requested.

**Customer:** Separate the provider price, insurer share, deductible, denial, collection, and relief.

**Money and finance:** Track out-of-pocket cost, unpaid balance, credit record, earnings, and coverage separately.

**Public power:** Ask whether the rule changed care, debt, work freedom, or only the record seen by a lender.

## What would change the finding

- A linked record of care need, price, coverage, payment, work, debt, and later health.
- Results split by income, race, age, disability, family structure, state, and insurer.
- A comparison of bill relief, coverage expansion, credit-record deletion, and direct care access.
- Evidence on whether employer coverage keeps people in jobs they would otherwise leave.
- Household measures of rent, food, saving, caregiving, and family support after a medical event.

## Reading rule

Do not say “medical debt relief worked” until you name whether it changed the bill, the credit record, care use, health, work, or household security.

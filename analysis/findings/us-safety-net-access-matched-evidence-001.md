# A benefit can be lost without the need going away

## Short answer

US safety-net access depends on more than eligibility. Work rules can remove people from SNAP without increasing their employment, and closing an enrollment office can reduce participation even where need remains.

The deeper finding is that help has a price in time, travel, paperwork, and proof. A person leaving the program may have found work, lost the benefit because the process was too hard, or both. The current evidence does not let us treat exit as progress.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Part of the path | What the record shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Work rule | NBER finds a 23-point increase in SNAP exits among incumbent participants after 18 months and no employment effect. | Whether a particular person found better work. |
| Parents | NBER finds parents did not increase work but were much less likely to receive SNAP. | The food, debt, or care result for each family. |
| Administrative burden | The parent study identifies the work of proving compliance as the main disenrollment mechanism. | Which form, deadline, office, or notice caused the loss. |
| Place | NBER finds participation fell 7–9% over two years after an enrollment office closed in one state. | Whether the lost participation meant lost food security. |
| Benefit value | USDA summarizes research that SNAP improves food security and helps smooth food access across the month. | Whether every participant receives enough or can use it easily. |

Sources: [NBER 28877](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28877), [NBER 32441](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32441), [NBER 34529](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34529), [USDA SNAP research](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-nutrition-assistance/supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap/key-statistics-and-research).

## The connection

```text
food or income need
  -> eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task
  -> benefit kept, lost, or never reached
  -> food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome
  -> policy change, complaint, or political response
```

The key separation is between need, access, participation, and outcome. Each can move in a different direction.

## Four views

**Household:** Count food, cash, work hours, care, travel, internet, paperwork, and the cost of waiting.

**Customer and worker:** A benefit office is a public service point; its distance can change how a person manages food and work.

**Money and finance:** Measure the benefit, missed payment, borrowing, fees, and food bought after access changes.

**Public power:** Judge a work rule by work, food, health, and household results—not only by a smaller caseload.

## What would change the finding

- The same household's application, notice, office trip, renewal, benefit, work, earnings, food security, debt, and health.
- Online access, language, disability, childcare, travel time, and office quality.
- Results by parent status, race, age, place, housing, and work schedule.
- Whether benefit loss reflects higher income, administrative failure, or both.
- Evidence that a work rule improves lasting earnings or food security after its paperwork cost.

## Next test

Follow households before and after a rule or office change. Measure who leaves, who works more, who eats better, who borrows, and who returns to the program.

## Reading rule

Do not call program exit a work success. Ask whether the household became safer, or simply lost the route to help.

# Privacy protection can open the door to more sharing

## The argument

A privacy rule can make people feel safer and share more. That may mean a clearer, fairer exchange. It may also make it easier for a firm to collect a richer record of a customer’s life. More sharing is not proof that privacy improved, and less sharing is not proof that a rule failed.

The real questions are plain: what did the person understand, what did they agree to, what could they refuse, and what did the firm do afterward? A customer may trade receipts, visits, and purchase history for a useful reward. The customer may not know that the same record can later shape offers, prices, ranking, credit, or access.

The deeper finding is that trust and power can move in opposite directions. A customer can feel safer, share more, and become more dependent on the firm at the same time. The result depends on the use that follows consent and whether saying no carries a real penalty.

## Follow one permission

A shopper sees a privacy notice and joins a reward app. The notice feels safer after a new state rule. The shopper shares more receipts and store visits. The firm now knows more about the shopper, while refusal may mean losing the reward or service.

The permission may be legally valid and still be a weak bargain if the customer cannot understand later uses, withdraw easily, or get the same service without sharing. The first moment—clicking agree—does not settle the whole exchange.

```text
privacy rule + clearer permission
  -> customer feels safer
  -> more data shared
  -> firm learns more
  -> offers, prices, ranking, service, or access may change
  -> trust and customer power may move in opposite directions
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Evidence | Meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| HBS reports more information, receipts, store visits, and retail categories shared after California and Virginia rules in one app. | A rule can change sharing behavior. | One app and selected states do not establish a general result. |
| HBS identifies reassurance as one reason people shared more. | Feeling safer can change consent. | More comfort does not prove better later outcomes. |
| FTC guidance says people should see why data is collected and shared and have meaningful control. | Permission needs a usable “no.” | Guidance does not show how often control works in practice. |
| More receipts and visits give a firm a richer customer record. | Sharing can increase firm knowledge. | The later use and customer benefit remain unknown. |

Sources: [HBS More Trust, More Data](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/more-trust-more-data-the-upside-of-privacy-laws-for-companies), [FTC privacy guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2024/04/when-companies-share-your-personal-information-without-your-permission), and [FTC consent-design research](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/linfarronatofradkin.pdf).

## The deeper finding

The useful measure is not the amount of data collected. It is the full exchange: what the customer understood, what benefit they received, what later uses were allowed, whether they could withdraw, and whether they could challenge a bad result.

This changes the meaning of trust. Trust is valuable when it helps people make an informed choice. It becomes a risk when it lowers scrutiny while the firm’s knowledge and ability to sort customers increase. A customer who shares more may receive better offers, but may also reveal a pattern that changes eligibility or price without a clear explanation.

## What remains unknown

The HBS result comes from one app and selected states. The sources do not follow a customer from permission to later price, offer, ranking, denial, service quality, deletion, withdrawal, or ability to use the service without extra sharing.

## Next test

Track what customers were shown, what they shared, what they could refuse, and what happened later to prices, offers, service, eligibility, deletion, withdrawal, and complaints. Compare people by income, age, language, disability, and digital access.

## Reading rule

Do not call a privacy rule successful because sharing rose. Show what the customer understood, what the firm did next, and whether the customer could still say no.

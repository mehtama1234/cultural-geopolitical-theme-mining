# When a business idea leaves home

## Short answer

A town can have more business applications without gaining more lasting local jobs, services or owners who live there. The deeper change may be that starting a business is becoming less tied to the place where the founder grew up.

The careful finding is that local business counts and local belonging are different things. Current evidence shows a decline in the hometown advantage and gives better county data for testing business formation. It does not yet show that a place lost a service, a job or a shared identity because owners moved away.

## What the sources show

### 1. Founders are less tied to their birthplace

The HBS account of Census-based research reports that, by 2018, self-employed US-born White men were only 2 percentage points more likely than wage workers to remain in their birthplace state. The difference was 8 points in 1970. For the full sample, the local advantage fell below zero in the latest census used.

This is a change in where entrepreneurs live, not proof that entrepreneurship itself declined or that towns became less useful.

[HBS: Charting the Decline of the Hometown Entrepreneur](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/charting-the-decline-of-the-hometown-entrepreneur)

### 2. The kind of business matters

The reported research finds that self-employment in high-capital sectors such as agriculture, retail and utilities made up a smaller share in the later period. Lower-capital fields such as personal services, construction and real estate grew for both locals and movers.

One possible reading is that financing and customers may be less bound to local ties in some sectors. That is a mechanism to test, not a settled reason for the whole change.

### 3. An application is only the first step

Census county data now cover business applications from 2005 through 2025. The agency distinguishes applications, projected business formations and employer firms. The 2026 release also warns that privacy protection adds a different kind of noise to the county file.

The practical rule is simple: do not call an application a new employer, a lasting business or a new service.

[Census county Business Formation Statistics](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/data/county.html)

### 4. Small firms are large in the US economy, but national counts hide place

The SBA's 2025 US profile reports 36.2 million small businesses and 62.3 million small-business employees. Those totals show why the topic matters. They do not show which county gained a stable employer, which service closed, or whether the owner lived nearby.

[SBA 2025 US small-business profile](https://advocacy.sba.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/United_States_2025-State-Profile.pdf)

## The connection

```text
housing, credit, labor, or customer conditions
  -> founder stays, moves, opens, closes, or changes sector
  -> lasting firm, job, service, price, or tax-base change
  -> residents' access to work and daily needs
  -> local sense of control, belonging, or political demand
```

The founder-location and application steps are measured. The local service and political steps remain open.

## What is fact, inference, and unknown

**Fact:** The hometown advantage in entrepreneurship declined in the reported Census analysis. County applications are available, but they are not the same as employer firms.

**Inference:** A place may lose some local control over business decisions even while business activity continues. The person who opens a firm, the person who owns it later and the people who work there may live in different places.

**Unknown:** We do not know whether owner movement changed local prices, jobs, services, tax revenue or belonging. We also do not know whether remote work and online customers made local residence less important.

## What would change the finding

- County-level links between applications, employer starts, closures, jobs and owner residence.
- Measures of essential services and prices before and after local business turnover.
- Housing, rent, credit and wage conditions matched to the founder's move.
- Results by race, age, gender, sector, rural or urban place and business size.
- A case where owner movement changed little because local workers, customers or institutions kept control.

## Next test

Choose one county pair and join Census applications with employer-firm and establishment records. Add local service data and owner location. Treat applications as the beginning of the path, not the outcome.

## Reading rule

A new application is not a new job. A new store is not proof of local control. Count who owns, who works, who serves customers and who can leave.

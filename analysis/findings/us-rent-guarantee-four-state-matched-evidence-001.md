# Four states show four different levels of visibility

## Short answer

The first comparison across California, New York, Texas, and Illinois does not produce one national rule. California gives us public bond terms. New York gives us a regulator's history of lease-bond rates and oversight. Texas gives us a clear tenant rule for insurance bought in place of a security deposit. Illinois gives us a licensing starting point, but not yet a product record.

The practical finding is simple: we can see the risk transfer more clearly than we can see the price or the household result. California and Texas make clear that paying a fee or having an insurer pay a landlord does not automatically erase the tenant's rent duty.

## What the matched evidence shows

| State | What is visible | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| California | The public bond terms cap the bonded lease at 24 months, require an indemnity agreement and paid premium, require landlord mitigation after default, and give the surety collection rights after payment. | Actual renter price, claim rate, repayment experience, and renewal outcome. |
| New York | DFS describes lease rental bonds as bought by tenants to protect landlords and records a past rate-law violation involving a coverage gap and rate filing. | Current provider form, current quote, and tenant outcome. |
| Texas | State law allows a fee in place of a security deposit and says the fee does not change the tenant's duty to pay rent; an insurer may seek limited reimbursement after a valid claim. | Which providers use this structure and how claims are handled in practice. |
| Illinois | State guidance confirms producer licensing matters for insurance sales. | A rent-guarantee policy form, state filing, price, claim record, and tenant rule specific to this product. |

Sources: [California bond terms](https://www.theguarantors.com/terms-and-conditions-california), [New York DFS consent order](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/enforcement_discipline/ea20220628_guarantors), [Texas Property Code Chapter 92](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/DocViewer.aspx?DocKey=PR%2FPR.92&ExactPhrase=False&HighlightType=1&Phrases=can%7Cconstables%7Cserve%7Ccivil%7Cpapers&QueryText=can+constables+serve+civil+papers), and [Illinois licensing guidance](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/banks/cbt/comcl/btfaqins.html).

## The connection

```text
state rule and filing
  -> provider bond or insurance form
  -> landlord offer and tenant fee
  -> default claim
  -> insurer or surety recovery
  -> tenant debt, renewal, credit, and housing
```

State law changes the path after the provider pays. It can also change what the tenant must be told before paying and which records exist to check the claim.

## Four views

**Household:** See the fee today and the possible repayment tomorrow as two different costs.

**Resident:** Check whether the policy creates a right, a duty, a limit, or only a promise to the landlord.

**Money and finance:** Follow premium, claim, recovery, reserve, carrier, and debt separately.

**Public power:** Treat state filing and consumer rules as part of access, not as small print after the decision.

## What would change the finding

- Current policy forms and approved rates in all four states.
- The same quote and lease tested across states.
- Claim and repayment data, including disputes and credit reporting.
- Renewal, move-out, and later rent outcomes.

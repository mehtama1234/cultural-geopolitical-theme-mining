# When four states make rent guarantee a different deal

## The argument

A renter may be told that a rent guarantee replaces a large deposit or a co-signer. That sounds like one trade: pay a smaller fee now and get the apartment. The four-state record shows a more complicated deal. State law changes what may be sold, what paperwork is visible, what the landlord receives after a missed payment, and what the renter may owe afterward.

Two renters with the same income and lease can therefore face different rights and duties. A national website can make the product look uniform while the contract and recovery path remain local. “No deposit” does not mean “no exposure after default.”

## Follow the money after the missed payment

California exposes detailed bond terms. New York exposes regulatory history. Texas exposes a tenant rule for a fee used instead of a deposit. Illinois exposes a licensing gate. These records answer different questions; none alone proves a renter's long-term result.

```text
state rule and filed form → provider offer and renter fee → landlord accepts lower upfront cash
→ renter defaults or causes covered damage → provider pays landlord → provider seeks recovery
→ debt, dispute, renewal, credit, or housing result
```

## What the sources actually establish

| State | What is visible | Practical meaning | Missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| California | Public bond terms cap the bonded lease at 24 months, require an indemnity agreement and premium, require landlord mitigation after default, and give collection rights after payment. | Move-in cash may fall while a contract-based recovery path remains. | Usual price, claim rate, disputes, repayment, and renewal. |
| New York | DFS describes lease rental bonds as landlord protection bought by tenants and records a past rate-law violation involving a coverage gap and rate filing. | Insurance oversight and filed terms can shape the deal. | Current form, quote, claim record, and renter outcome. |
| Texas | Property law allows a fee in place of a security deposit and says the fee does not change the duty to pay rent. | A fee can change move-in payment without changing rent liability. | Providers using the rule, notices, claims, and repayment in practice. |
| Illinois | State guidance confirms licensing matters when insurance is sold. | The seller's legal status must be checked before comparing price. | Product form, filing, price, claim record, and tenant rule. |

Sources: [California bond terms](https://www.theguarantors.com/terms-and-conditions-california), [New York DFS enforcement record](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/enforcement_discipline/ea20220628_guarantors), [Texas Property Code Chapter 92](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/DocViewer.aspx?DocKey=PR%2FPR.92&ExactPhrase=False&HighlightType=1&Phrases=can%7Cconstables%7Cserve%7Ccivil%7Cpapers&QueryText=can+constables+serve+civil+papers), and [Illinois licensing guidance](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/banks/cbt/comcl/btfaqins.html).

## The deeper finding

Legal permission, product availability, quote price, claim behavior, and household result are separate fields. Combining them into “available in the state” hides the part that matters after a claim. A lower move-in cost may help a person get housed today while a repayment demand later makes the next move harder.

## What remains unproven

The four states do not yet show four renter outcomes. They show four levels of public visibility. We lack a common record of fee, covered amount, claim, dispute, repayment, renewal, credit, move-out, and later housing for the same lease and renter profile.

## Next test

Collect the same fields in all four states: provider, carrier, filed form, fee, covered amount, disclosures, claim trigger, notice, repayment, dispute, collection, renewal, credit, and later housing. Compare the guarantee with a deposit, personal guarantor, direct rent aid, and public insurance.

## Reading rule

Do not call a state covered because a provider has a national website. Find the state rule, actual form, quote, and renter duty after payment.

# When a debt promise changes the household budget

## Short answer

A promise of a payment pause or debt relief can change what borrowers do before the promise becomes real. They may pay less on the loan and spend more elsewhere, then face a sharper problem if payments return.

An expectation is not a benefit received. It may still change a household's choices for months or years.

## What the source shows

In a US information experiment linked to credit, employment, and spending records, NBER finds that more optimistic borrowers reduced student-loan payments by about $40 per month and increased nondurable spending by about $80 per month. Borrowers optimistic about payment pauses were 7.5 percentage points more likely to be delinquent after payments resumed.

Another NBER study provides evidence on actual loan cancellation and borrower outcomes. The belief experiment does not show how information formed outside the study, what need received the extra spending, or the full housing and family effect.

- [NBER Working Paper 35319](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35319)
- [NBER Working Paper 33462](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33462)

## The connection

```text
policy promise or legal uncertainty
  -> belief about future payment
  -> loan payment and spending choice
  -> policy settles, pauses, or fails
  -> delinquency, credit, housing, and family choice
```

Using money elsewhere can be sensible when a pause seems likely. Delinquency after payments resume may reflect the end of the pause, not only a mistaken belief.

## The four outside views

**Household:** Lower payments can free money for food, rent, care, or a needed purchase. A return to payments can remove that room quickly.

**Company and sector:** Lenders, employers, schools, landlords, and retailers respond to borrower cash flow. A household's changed spending can support one sector while increasing another bill.

**Money and finance:** Payment, balance, interest, delinquency, credit access, and spending must be tracked together. Lower debt payment is not the same as lower debt.

**Public power:** Announcements, court decisions, servicing, notices, and program rules shape what borrowers believe and whether relief is actually delivered.

## What would change the finding

- Where borrowers got the information and how certain the policy was.
- Income, liquidity, household need, balance, rate, loan type, and payment history.
- Nondurable and durable purchase purpose, rent, home purchase, care, and work.
- Delinquency, credit access, housing, family choices, and later repayment.
- Effects by age, race, income, balance, and borrower type.

## Next test

Follow borrowers from the first announcement through three years after the policy settles. Join notices, beliefs, payments, balances, spending, delinquency, credit, housing, work, family, and actual relief.

## Reading rule

Keep promise, belief, payment, spending, delinquency, and received relief separate. A policy announcement can change behavior without changing the debt balance.

# When a debt promise changes the household budget

## The argument

A promise of a payment pause or debt relief can change what borrowers do before the promise becomes real. A borrower may pay less on the loan and use the money for food, rent, care, or a needed purchase. If payments return, the household can face a sharper bill than it expected.

An expectation is not a benefit received. It can still change a household’s present choices. The question is whether the extra room was a sensible bridge, a mistaken bet, or the only way to meet an immediate need.

## What the source establishes

In a US information experiment linked to credit, employment, and spending records, NBER finds that more optimistic borrowers reduced student-loan payments by about $40 per month and increased nondurable spending by about $80 per month. Borrowers optimistic about payment pauses were 7.5 percentage points more likely to be delinquent after payments resumed.

Another NBER study provides evidence on actual loan cancellation and borrower outcomes. The belief experiment does not show how information formed outside the study, what need received the extra spending, or the full housing and family effect.

Sources: [NBER Working Paper 35319](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35319) and [NBER Working Paper 33462](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33462).

## Follow the promise through the budget

policy promise or legal uncertainty → belief about a future payment → loan payment and spending choice → policy settles, pauses, or fails → delinquency, credit, housing, and family choice

Delinquency after payments resume may reflect the end of the pause, not only a mistaken belief. Lower payments may protect a household in the short run while leaving the balance and future claim unchanged.

## What must stay separate

- Policy announcement and relief received.
- Belief and legal outcome.
- Lower payment and lower debt.
- Extra spending and improved well-being.
- Delinquency after resumption and delinquency before the promise.

## Next test

Follow borrowers from the first announcement through three years after the policy settles. Join notices, beliefs, payments, balances, spending purpose, delinquency, credit, housing, work, family, and actual relief. Split results by income, liquidity, loan type, age, race, and balance.

## Reading rule

Keep promise, belief, payment, spending, delinquency, and received relief separate. An announcement can change behavior without changing the debt balance.

# When a business application is not yet a local job or a local place

## The argument

A county can show many new business applications without gaining a lasting shop, job, service, or meeting place. Starting a business is only the first step. The firm must receive money, survive, hire, serve customers, and remain in the community.

That makes business formation a conversion problem. A permit, loan, grant, tax break, or favorable headline may create interest without creating a durable local option. The same application rate can produce a lively main street in one county and a list of abandoned ideas in another because rent, credit, labor, permits, roads, customers, and public buying differ.

## Follow one local idea

A resident applies to open a repair shop. Rent rises, credit is expensive, permits take time, and a national chain already serves the area. The application is counted. The shop never opens, or opens briefly and closes. The neighborhood has gained a record of intention, not a lasting option. If it does open, the next questions are whether residents use it, whether it hires locally, and whether it stays.

```text
local cost, credit, labor, or customer change → owner applies, starts, closes, or moves
→ lasting firms, jobs, services, prices, and meeting places change → household choices, local identity, trust, and political voice
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Step | Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Place and owner | HBS reports the hometown advantage for US-born White male entrepreneurs fell from 8 percentage points in 1970 to 2 points in 2018. | Entrepreneurs are less tied to birthplace than before. |
| First step | NBER finds business applications measure pursuit of an idea. | An application is not yet a firm. |
| Conversion | NBER finds places differ in the rate at which applications become employer firms. | Local conditions shape whether an idea becomes a job-creating business. |
| County signal | Census publishes annual county applications for 2005–2025 with definition and privacy cautions. | Applications are an early signal, not evidence of local life. |
| Missing result | Public records do not show lasting service, quality, payroll, customer use, or belonging. | The customer and place result remains open. |

Sources: [HBS hometown entrepreneurship](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/charting-the-decline-of-the-hometown-entrepreneur), [NBER business formation](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34881), and [Census county Business Formation Statistics](https://www.census.gov/econ/bfs/data/county.html).

## The deeper finding

Local business policy should be judged by conversion, not excitement. A town becomes more entrepreneurial when ideas become firms that remain open and useful. That requires a path from application to finance, customers, workers, and repeat demand. If one link fails, the application count can rise while local choice falls.

## What remains unproven

The evidence does not show whether an owner served the birthplace community, why an application failed, or whether an opening changed customer prices, quality, jobs, travel time, belonging, or political participation. It also does not show whether mobile or online firms create the same local value as a physical business.

## Next test

Compare counties with similar application rates but different application-to-employer transition rates. Follow firms for two years and measure openings, payroll, survival, services, prices, customer travel, ownership, and local response. Track the failed applications too.

## Reading rule

Do not call a county entrepreneurial because applications rose. Ask how many became lasting firms, who used them, and what changed afterward.

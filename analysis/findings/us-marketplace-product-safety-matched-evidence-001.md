# A visible listing does not prove a safe product

## Short answer

Online marketplaces can put a seller, review, and safety claim in front of a customer without making the product safe. CPSC says counterfeit safety labels can mislead buyers and move hazardous goods through online channels. The INFORM Consumers Act adds seller checks and reporting tools aimed at counterfeit, unsafe, and stolen goods. FTC guidance still tells customers to check the seller, reviews, return terms, payment method, and platform help. The deeper finding is that safety depends on the path after the signal: detection, removal, recall, refund, and recovery.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Listings may show seller identity, reviews, and safety marks. | Whether the signal is true, current, and understood. |
| Risk | CPSC says fake safety labels can mislead buyers and move hazardous products through online channels. | Product-level exposure and injury. |
| Verification | INFORM requires covered marketplaces to collect and verify seller information and provide reporting tools. | Whether verification finds unsafe products or repeat sellers. |
| Response | CPSC and FTC provide reporting, recall, return, refund, and dispute paths. | Removal speed, refund rate, and who carries the loss. |
| Household result | A bad product can create replacement cost, injury, lost time, or distrust. | A joined record from purchase to harm and recovery. |

Sources: [CPSC fake-label crackdown](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2026/US-Consumer-Product-Safety-Commission-Launches-Crackdown-on-Fake-Safety-Labels-Used-to-Push-Dangerous-Foreign-Products-into-US-Homes), [FTC INFORM Consumers Act notice](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/ftc-puts-online-marketplaces-notice-about-their-responsibilities-under-new-inform-consumers-act), [FTC marketplace guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buying-online-marketplace), and [CPSC online sellers' guide](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Online-Sellers-Safety-Guide).

## The connection

```text
seller and safety signal shown
  → customer believes the product is acceptable
  → counterfeit, unsafe, or non-conforming product arrives
  → report, recall, return, refund, or injury
  → household money, health, and trust change
```

The sources support the risk and response systems. They do not yet join a specific US listing to a customer's purchase, injury, refund, and later use.

## The useful surprise

More information can still leave the customer carrying the hardest part. A label or seller name may appear at checkout, while the customer must detect the danger, report it, stop using the product, return it, and wait for money back.

## Four views

**Household:** Count purchase price, replacement, medical cost, time, return shipping, and lost use.

**Customer:** Record seller, listing, safety claim, review, product received, warning, report, recall, and remedy.

**Money and finance:** Separate refund, platform reimbursement, insurance, medical bill, dispute, and unrecovered loss.

**Public power:** Measure detection, listing removal, repeat appearance, recall reach, seller suspension, and appeal.

## What would change the finding

- Product-level listing, seller, certification, purchase, recall, and complaint records.
- Time from report to removal and from return to refund.
- Repeat listings under changed seller or product names.
- Injury, fire, poisoning, or property-damage records linked to purchase channel.
- Results by product type, income, age, language, and payment protection.

## Reading rule

Do not call a marketplace safe because it shows a seller name or safety mark. Follow the product through detection, removal, harm, and recovery.

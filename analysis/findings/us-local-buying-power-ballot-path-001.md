# When local buying power reaches the ballot

## The argument

People may respond to what their pay can buy, not simply to the inflation rate in a national report. If local prices rise faster than local pay, a household can lose room even while another household in the same county gains.

But a county result is not a voter interview. A person may blame prices, a party, immigration, a local employer, or a national leader. They may feel poorer and vote on another issue. The evidence is strongest for a place-level economic pattern and weaker for the path from one household’s bills to one ballot.

## What the sources establish

An NBER study uses county-level family-budget costs, nominal income, and presidential and congressional results from 2021 to 2024. It reports that declining real wages—not higher inflation alone—predicted Republican gains, while inflation remained associated with presidential vote share after accounting for real wages.

Separate NBER survey work finds that people dislike inflation mainly when they believe wages do not keep up with prices, and that this belief relates to reported shopping and saving choices. The county and survey studies describe related pieces, not the same household from prices to a vote.

Sources: [NBER Working Paper 35301](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35301) and [NBER Working Paper 32300](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32300).

## Follow the weaker political link

local prices and wages → real buying power and household room → buying less, saving less, borrowing, or delaying a need → blame, trust, and economic judgment → turnout, vote choice, or support for change

Party identity, immigration, abortion, safety, age, media, and candidates can outweigh economic conditions. County averages also hide large household differences. A real-wage pattern is a lead for research, not a verdict about voters.

## What must stay separate

- Local price pressure and personal loss.
- Real wages and the inflation rate.
- Economic judgment and blame.
- County voting and an individual vote.
- A changed budget and a changed political belief.

## Next test

Join local prices and wages to a household panel for two election cycles. Follow spending, saving, borrowing, media, policy knowledge, trust, turnout, and vote choice. Compare a fall in real pay with inflation that leaves real pay unchanged.

## Reading rule

Do not say voters punish inflation when the evidence may show lost real pay. Do not turn a county pattern into a story about every voter.

# When help disappears before the need does

## The argument

A public benefit can be available on paper and still be hard to keep. A work rule, form, deadline, office closure, renewal task, or long trip can remove food support before a person finds a job or earns more.

That creates a basic distinction: a program count can fall because people no longer need help, or because people lost access while the need remained. The evidence below supports the second possibility in several studied settings. It does not mean every work rule has the same result or every person who exits still needs the benefit.

## What the sources establish

NBER linked SNAP and earnings records and found that work requirements increased exits from SNAP by 23 percentage points among incumbent participants after 18 months, with no employment effect in that setting. A study of parents found lower SNAP receipt without increased work and identified reporting and administrative burden as an important path.

Another NBER study found that closing a SNAP enrollment office reduced participation in nearby census tracts by 7–9% over two years, with suggestive increases after openings. USDA research finds that SNAP improves food security in several studies and that food security worsens before benefits arrive and improves afterward.

GAO estimated that about 59% of potentially eligible food-insecure college students did not report receiving SNAP in 2020. Student rules make this a special group, not a national estimate, but it shows that legal eligibility and actual receipt can diverge.

Sources: [NBER SNAP work requirements](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28877), [NBER parents and SNAP](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32441), [NBER enrollment offices](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34529), [USDA SNAP research](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-nutrition-assistance/supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-snap/key-statistics-and-research), and [GAO college students](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-107074).

## Follow access into the household

food or income need → eligibility, work rule, office distance, renewal task, or documentation → benefit kept, lost, or never reached → food, work, debt, health, or trust outcome

Administrative work is a household cost. It can require time, travel, documents, internet access, language help, and the confidence to challenge an error. The cost may be greatest when food or cash is already short.

## What must stay separate

- Eligibility and receipt.
- Program exit and employment.
- Office closure and household food loss.
- Administrative burden and fraud prevention.
- Participation loss and completed harm.

## Next test

Use county or census-tract participation records with office locations, food security, and local employment data. Compare online renewal, in-person help, and automatic renewal. Treat participation loss as the first outcome; test food, work, debt, and health separately.

## Reading rule

Program exit is not employment, and eligibility is not receipt.

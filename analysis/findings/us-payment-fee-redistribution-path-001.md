# When payment choice changes who carries the price

## Short answer

The payment method can change who pays for ordinary shopping even when everyone sees the same shelf price. Merchant fees may enter common prices, while rewards return more value to some card users.

Cards can also provide security, records, credit, and useful rewards. The right question is the full value and cost of the payment method for each household.

## What the sources show

Using data across US merchants and payment methods, NBER estimates that interchange fees transfer about $30 billion a year from cash and debit users to credit-card users. Consumer sorting and different merchant fees reduce but do not remove the transfer.

Other NBER research finds that card issuers use more back-loaded and hidden fees with some customer groups and market conditions. HBS describes how merchant fees can enter common prices while rewards return more value to premium-card users.

The transfer estimate depends on how fees reach prices. It does not show every shopper's actual price, reward, interest payment, or alternative.

## The connection

```text
shopper chooses cash, debit, or credit
  -> merchant pays a different fee
  -> price, reward, surcharge, or margin responds
  -> households carry different effective costs
  -> payment policy and merchant competition become distribution questions
```

The same shelf price can hide different net costs. A shopper's choice may be limited by income, credit access, merchant location, or safety needs.

## The four outside views

**Household:** Credit can smooth a bill and provide protection. A household without a rewards card may still pay part of the system's cost through common prices.

**Company and sector:** Merchants pay different fees and may change prices, rewards, margins, or which payment methods they accept. Small stores may have less room to absorb fees.

**Money and finance:** Rewards, annual fees, interest, late fees, and overdraft costs determine the household's real result. A reward is not a gain if carrying the balance costs more.

**Public power:** Fee limits, disclosure, surcharges, bank competition, and access to safe low-cost payments decide how much choice shoppers have.

## What would change the finding

- Payment method, merchant fee, shelf price, surcharge, reward, interest, and late fee.
- Household income, credit access, cash use, shopping place, and merchant choice.
- Small-merchant margins, sales, handling costs, and payment acceptance.
- Changes after payment regulation or reward-program changes.
- Whether the household pays its balance in full and can switch merchants or methods.

## Next test

Follow households and merchants for one year through payment method, price, fee, reward, interest, and shopping location. Compare the effective cost by income and place before and after a fee or payment-rule change.

## Reading rule

Keep the fee transfer estimate separate from each household's net cost. A shared price does not mean a shared burden.

# Competition helps only when customers can use it

## Short answer

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment. US research shows that competition can change a firm's reason to serve a customer group, but the result depends on which customers can leave and which cannot. Historical evidence finds nondiscrimination rising in less competitive markets and higher prices at nondiscriminatory firms in the studied setting. Other research finds that uniform retail prices can weigh more heavily on poorer households, while online information and some FinTech lending narrow certain price gaps. The deeper finding is that customer power is the bridge: competition helps only when people can compare, switch, and receive a comparable service.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | NBER's historical study finds nondiscrimination increased in the least competitive markets. | Whether the mechanism appears in current markets. |
| Price | NBER finds higher prices at nondiscriminatory firms in its historical sample. | Whether price differences reflect service, cost, or unequal treatment today. |
| Uniform price | US retail chains often charge similar prices across stores with different customers and competition. | Who bears more cost relative to income. |
| Information | Online search and some FinTech lending can narrow observed customer price gaps. | Who can use those tools and whether access remains equal. |
| Customer power | Time, money, identity, transport, information, and switching shape the value of competition. | A current customer-level measure of the ability to leave. |

Sources: [NBER competition and consumer discrimination](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33547), [NBER uniform retail pricing](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23996), [NBER consumer-lending discrimination](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25943), and [NBER internet car pricing](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8668).

## The connection

```text
customer group + ability to leave → firm incentive
                                 → price, service, treatment, or access
                                 → customer cost and choice
                                 → trust, regulation, and market entry
```

The evidence supports a competition-and-customer-power mechanism, but not one universal result. A firm may serve a group better when it fears that group will leave. A customer who cannot leave may receive less attention even where several firms exist.

## The useful surprise

The historical result warns against a simple story. If discriminatory firms are numerous and compete for a majority group, a firm may stop discriminating only when that group has less ability to defect. In the studied market, nondiscriminatory firms also charged more. That does not make discrimination acceptable or explain every price. It shows that treatment and price can move in different directions.

The information result adds a second condition. Online comparison can reduce a seller's access to personal cues and give a buyer more bargaining power. But a digital path helps only people who can reach it, understand it, and use it without losing other protections.

## Four views

**Household:** Record price, service, time, travel, search, and the cost of leaving.

**Customer:** Compare the same product, terms, quality, and treatment across firms.

**Money and finance:** Measure dollar price, income burden, credit price, fees, and denied access separately.

**Public power:** Ask whether competition, disclosure, antitrust, and civil-rights rules give each group a real way to challenge the firm.

## What would change the finding

- Current matched-customer records across firms, products, prices, and service quality.
- Search and switching data by income, race, age, disability, language, and place.
- Complaints and enforcement linked to the customer and firm response.
- Digital versus in-person comparison with the same customer and product.
- Measures of denied access, repeat treatment, and later household cost.

## Reading rule

Do not say competition protects customers until you show who can compare, who can leave, and who receives the same product and treatment.

# Competition helps only when customers can use it

## The argument

More sellers do not automatically mean fairer treatment. Competition helps a customer only when the customer can compare, switch, and receive a comparable service somewhere else. Time, money, identity, transport, information, and the cost of moving all decide whether “choice” is real.

A firm may treat a group better when it fears that group will leave. A customer who cannot leave may receive less attention even where several firms exist. Online search and new lenders can narrow some price gaps, but only for people who can reach and use those tools.

## Follow one customer across firms

A customer receives a higher price or worse service and considers switching. The other firm is farther away, requires more paperwork, has a weaker network, or uses a different risk screen. The customer stays. The market has several sellers, but the customer has no cheap exit. The firm's incentive comes from the customer's actual alternatives, not the number of logos nearby.

```text
customer group + ability to leave → firm incentive
→ price, service, treatment, or access → customer cost and choice
→ trust, regulation, and market entry
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Step | Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | NBER's historical study finds nondiscrimination increased in the least competitive markets. | Competitive pressure can change treatment. |
| Price | NBER finds higher prices at nondiscriminatory firms in its historical sample. | Treatment and price can move in different directions. |
| Uniform price | NBER finds retail chains often charge similar prices across stores with different customers and competition. | One posted price can weigh differently by income. |
| Information | NBER finds online search and some FinTech lending can narrow observed customer price gaps. | Information can improve bargaining power. |
| Missing power | The records do not measure each customer's time, alternatives, and ability to leave. | Choice remains partly hidden. |

Sources: [NBER competition and discrimination](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33547), [NBER uniform retail pricing](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23996), [NBER consumer lending](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25943), and [NBER internet car pricing](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8668).

## The deeper finding

Customer power is the bridge between competition and fair treatment. A market can have many firms and weak customer exit. A smaller market can still serve people well if switching is easy and treatment is comparable. The useful measure is not seller count; it is the customer's real alternative at the moment of a decision.

## What remains unproven

The studies do not follow a current US customer across firms, products, prices, service quality, complaints, and repeat treatment. They also do not show how digital comparison changes outcomes for people with less time, money, language access, or transport.

## Next test

Match customers across comparable firms. Record search, travel, wait, identity checks, price, quality, denial, complaint, switch, and repeat outcome by income, race, age, disability, language, and place.

## Reading rule

Do not say competition protects customers until you show who can compare, who can leave, and who receives the same product and treatment.

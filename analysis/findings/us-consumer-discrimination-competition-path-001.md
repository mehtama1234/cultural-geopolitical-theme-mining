# When competition protects some customers first

## The argument

Competition may push firms to treat customers fairly when those customers can leave. But the customers with the most time, money, transport, information, and nearby alternatives may be the first to receive better treatment. Another group may pay more or get less choice because leaving is costly. A market can have many sellers and still give a particular customer very little power.

This is a historical US finding, not a claim about every current market. It supplies a mechanism: a firm responds to the customers whose defection would hurt it. That response can improve treatment for one group without removing the unequal cost carried by another. A market can therefore become more competitive and still leave some customers with weak power.

The word “fair” also needs separating. A firm may treat a customer with respect but charge more. It may offer the same price but deny a service. It may stop an openly discriminatory practice while leaving a customer with no nearby alternative. Price, treatment, service quality, and access are related but not interchangeable outcomes.

The deeper question is not whether a firm is competitive in the abstract. It is whether the particular customer can find the alternative, reach it, pay its price, and switch without losing time or service. Competition is a customer result only when the customer can use it.

## Follow one purchase

A Black customer visits one of two local businesses. The second business offers fairer treatment but charges more, and it is farther away. A customer with a car and flexible work hours can switch. A customer without either may remain with the cheaper but discriminatory option, pay the higher price, or go without. The market’s average price does not show this choice set.

The same logic applies online when a platform shows different offers, limits search, or makes leaving hard. A price difference may reflect a real service difference, a higher cost, or unequal treatment. Those possibilities need separate tests.

The customer’s exit route has several costs. It may require a longer trip, a new application, a deposit, a new identity check, a different language service, or the loss of account history. A person can see a better offer and still be unable to use it. If the customer cannot credibly leave, the firm has less reason to change.

This is why complaints alone are not enough. A customer who complains may receive a remedy, may leave, or may stop asking because the effort is too high. A low complaint rate can mean good treatment or low expectation of help. The missing measure is what happened after the customer tried to use an alternative or seek correction.

```text
customer group and ability to leave → firm competition and incentive
→ treatment, price, service, denial, or access
→ customer effort and effective cost
→ switch, complaint, enforcement, trust, or withdrawal
```

## What the source actually establishes

| Step | Evidence | Meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical treatment | Using nationwide data from three industries before the 1964 Civil Rights Act, NBER finds increases in non-discrimination were concentrated in the least competitive markets, where the threat of White customer defection was lowest. | Firms may change treatment when ordinary competitive pressure is weak. | This is historical evidence, not a current estimate for every market. |
| Price | Non-discriminatory firms charged higher prices than discriminatory firms in the same local market, while Black customers were harmed through higher prices in the non-discriminatory market. | A fairer treatment label does not by itself reveal the full customer cost. | Price is not the full value of service, safety, or access. |
| Exit cost | Time, money, mobility, language, credit, disability access, and information affect whether a customer can switch. | Seller count can overstate usable competition. | These costs are not measured in the historical study. |
| Scope | The evidence measures historical treatment and prices. | It does not measure today’s firms, online markets, service quality, or every reason for a price difference. | Present-day mechanisms need their own data and tests. |

Source: [NBER Working Paper 33547](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33547).

## The deeper finding

Customer power is unevenly distributed. Time, money, mobility, language, credit, disability access, and information can determine whether competition is usable. A rule that relies on switching may protect the people who can leave and miss the people who cannot. Public enforcement and clear remedies matter because they do not require every customer to shop their way out of unequal treatment.

The deeper finding is that competition works through a credible threat of exit. The threat is weak when a customer needs one local branch, one accessible route, one network, one language service, or one approval process. The market may look open from above while feeling closed from inside the household.

This also connects to platform data and credit. Search can expose a better offer only if the customer can understand it and switch. A credit denial can make a competing lender unusable. A complaint system can offer a remedy only if the customer can reach someone with authority. In each case, customer power depends on the practical path, not the formal existence of another option.

## What remains unproven

The study does not establish how current firms treat different groups, whether online choice reduces or increases unequal treatment, or how much of a higher price buys a different service. It does not follow a present-day customer from treatment to search, switch, complaint, remedy, and trust. It does not show whether low switching means satisfaction, high exit cost, or both.

## Next test

Follow customers and firms in current markets for one year. Join concentration, switching, treatment, prices, fees, search time, travel, identity checks, service quality, denial, complaints, enforcement, and customer outcomes. Compare people with different ability to leave and different access to alternatives. Record what happened after the customer tried to switch, not only whether a second seller existed.

## Reading rule

Keep competition, customer power, treatment, price, service, access, and discrimination separate. Do not assume competition protects everyone equally.

# The same energy bill means different things in different homes

## Short answer

Energy affordability depends on more than the dollar amount. DOE measures energy burden separately for renters and owners and points to a basic split: renters may pay the bill while landlords control insulation, equipment, and many repairs. EIA's 2024 survey separates owned and rented homes, housing types, and reported energy insecurity. GAO's manufactured-home review found older manufactured homes had higher energy spending per square foot than detached homes in the data it examined. The deeper finding is that the same bill shock can lead to different choices: a homeowner may repair or upgrade, a renter may ask a landlord, and a manufactured-home resident may face a building or equipment problem that a bill payment cannot fix.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Tenure | DOE LEAD separates renter and owner energy burden. | Monthly burden, bill timing, and ability to change the home. |
| Control | DOE identifies the renter split between paying the bill and controlling upgrades. | Landlord response, permission, delay, and completed work. |
| Housing type | EIA 2024 RECS includes housing type and energy-insecurity measures. | Which building and equipment features drive the difference. |
| Manufactured homes | GAO found higher energy spending per square foot for older manufactured homes than detached homes in its reviewed data. | Current costs, repair options, financing, and relocation effects. |
| Household result | EIA counts unsafe temperatures, skipped food or medicine, shutoff notices, and failed equipment. | Whether tenure or housing type changed the recovery path. |

Sources: [DOE LEAD FAQ](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/slsc/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool-frequently-asked-questions), [DOE LEAD tool](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool), [EIA 2024 RECS](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/), [EIA 2024 energy-insecurity data](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/hc/pdf/HC11.1_2024.pdf), and [GAO manufactured-home review](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-12-848r).

## The connection

```text
housing type and tenure
  → bill, equipment, and repair control
  → energy burden and safe-temperature risk
  → upgrade, aid, debt, move, or continued sacrifice
```

The sources establish differences in measurement, control, and reported risk. They do not yet show the same household's bill, repair decision, and recovery over time.

## The useful surprise

A lower energy bill is not equally reachable from every home. A renter may need landlord permission; a manufactured-home resident may need a costly building or equipment fix; an owner may have control but lack cash or access to credit. “Energy burden” without control hides the path to improvement.

## Four views

**Household:** Record who pays, who controls the repair, what equipment works, the bill, the room temperature, and the next available fix.

**Customer and resident:** Make the landlord duty, repair route, upgrade permission, and aid options visible before the bill becomes a crisis.

**Money and finance:** Compare energy cost with rent or mortgage, repair, deposit, debt, financing, and the value of staying or moving.

**Public power:** Target aid and upgrades by housing type, tenure, building age, fuel, climate, and control—not by income alone.

## What would change the finding

- Same-household bills, indoor temperatures, equipment, repair requests, landlord actions, and aid records.
- Current energy burden and shutoff outcomes for renters, owners, manufactured homes, and multifamily buildings.
- Upgrade costs, permission, completion, savings, comfort, and health after the work.
- Whether a household can move, refinance, repair, or change fuel when the home remains costly.
- Results by income, age, disability, race, place, and housing market.

## Reading rule

Do not compare homes only by energy burden. Ask who pays the bill, who controls the fix, and who can leave.

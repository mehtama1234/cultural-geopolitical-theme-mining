# A platform-worker judgment can separate missing-pay correction from restored work control

**Status:** provisional named platform-worker remedy finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-18

The Walmart Spark Driver case follows a more specific work-control route than a
generic platform settlement: alleged base-pay, tip, incentive, and promotion
shortfalls lead to a court-entered monetary judgment, driver allocations,
prospective earnings verification, and a partial payment-status surface. The
public record still does not follow an individual driver into restored work,
alternative work, schedule control, trust, or exit.

```text
platform pay promise or incentive
  -> missing base pay, tip, or promotion payment [alleged case mechanism]
  -> court judgment and driver-relief allocation
  -> payment identification and dispatch [partial status]
  -> [open] receipt, restored work control, alternatives, trust, and exit
```

## Short answer

The FTC case page records a March 3, 2026 court-entered order for a $100 million
judgment. The official FAQ says $79 million is directed to drivers, $10 million
is paid to the FTC for intended driver distribution, and $11 million is paid to
11 states as civil penalties. It also says Walmart began making payments in
mid-2025, before the settlement announcement, and that some drivers should
have payment-date emails while other drivers remain in identification or
payment processing.

This is stronger than a settlement design or an aggregate promise: it observes
allocation, eligibility, payment timing, and a partial implementation status.
It is not an individual recovery rate. The public materials do not disclose
the full eligible-driver denominator, each driver's amount or receipt, or what
happened to work, alternatives, schedule control, trust, or exit afterward.

## Evidence boundary

| Stage | Public evidence | Still open |
|---|---|---|
| Work exposure | FTC complaint and FAQ describe alleged unpaid base pay, tips, incentives, and promotions | Individual offer, completed work, promised amount, driver-level loss, and counterfactual route |
| Institutional response | Court-entered $100M judgment and 10-year earnings-verification program | Actual compliance, recurrence, and driver ability to contest or correct records |
| Remedy allocation | $79M directed to drivers, $10M to FTC for driver distribution, $11M to states | Complete eligible denominator, allocation formula, and group-specific exposure |
| Payment implementation | Payments began in mid-2025; some drivers should have payment-date emails; others remain in identification/payment processing | Individual payment receipt, amount, date, failed or unresolved status |
| Work recovery | None reported at driver level | Continued work, alternative work, schedule control, income stability, switching, trust, and exit |

The central distinction is between correcting a missing payment and restoring
the worker's practical position. A driver can receive compensation after the
work opportunity has changed, can remain dependent on the platform, or can
have moved to another route. The settlement does not identify which path
occurred.

## Next test

The smallest decisive follow-up is a lawful driver-level remedy ledger with a
stable eligibility key, alleged payment type, amount owed, payment date and
receipt, correction or appeal status, continued or alternative work, schedule
control, recurrence, trust, and exit. Preserve drivers who were not eligible,
were not reached, or stopped working before payment.

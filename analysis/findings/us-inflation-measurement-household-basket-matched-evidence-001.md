# The inflation average is not the household basket

## Short answer

The official data show why a broad inflation number cannot stand in for household experience. BLS reports that average consumer units spent 1.8% more in 2024 while prices rose 2.9%, which means average real spending fell 1.1%. The same report says housing took a larger share of spending. BLS also publishes real earnings and separate price categories, but these records are not joined into one person's rent, wage, food basket, debt, and choices.

The deeper finding is about measurement. “People are under pressure” can be true while the size and source of pressure differ by household. The low-income renter facing food and rent increases is not represented by the same basket as a high-income owner spending more on other goods.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Record | What it shows | What it does not show |
|---|---|---|
| Spending | Average nominal spending rose 1.8% in 2024 while prices rose 2.9%; average real spending fell 1.1%. | Whether a particular household bought less, borrowed, or changed quality. |
| Housing share | Housing, personal insurance and pensions, and miscellaneous spending each rose as a share of average spending. | Whether housing crowded out food, care, transport, or debt payment for the same household. |
| Wages | BLS real-earnings releases provide inflation-adjusted hourly and weekly pay. | A household's hours, job changes, benefits, rent, and irregular income. |
| Price categories | CPI tables separate food at home, housing, and other categories. | A household's product mix, package size, quality, promotion, and shopping place. |
| Household meaning | CE, earnings, and CPI can be placed on one research table. | A same-person path from price to substitution, saving, debt, trust, or voting. |

Sources: [BLS Consumer Expenditures in 2024](https://www.bls.gov/opub/reports/consumer-expenditures/2024/home.htm), [BLS real earnings](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/realer.htm), and [BLS CPI category table](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.t01.htm).

## The connection

```text
national price index
  + average wage
  + average spending
  -> broad pressure signal
  -> household basket still unknown
  -> substitution, debt, saving, or reduced quality
  -> possible trust and political response
```

The plus signs matter. These records are useful together, but they are not a single household record. Combining averages does not create a person.

## Four views

**Household:** Build the basket from rent, food, transport, care, debt, and savings rather than from the average alone.

**Customer:** Record product, size, quality, promotion, and substitute—not only the category price.

**Money and finance:** Separate nominal spending, real spending, pay, price level, debt, and cash buffer.

**Public power:** Use averages to see scale, not to claim that every household experienced the same loss or blamed the same institution.

## What would change the finding

- Public-use household spending microdata linked to income and geography.
- Product-level prices matched to what those households bought.
- Rent, debt, hours, benefits, and savings measured in the same period.
- Repeated views of the economy for the same households.

## Next test

Use BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey microdata to build three sample baskets by income and tenure. Compare each basket with CPI categories, wages, rent, and savings, and label every result as an average, a subgroup estimate, or a same-household measure.

## Reading rule

Do not turn three national averages into one family story. Say which record measures the price, the pay, the purchase, and the missing choice.

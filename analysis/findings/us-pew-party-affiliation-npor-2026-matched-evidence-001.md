# Party identity is broad, but the coalition lines remain uneven

## The deeper finding

Pew's 2026 National Public Opinion Reference Survey found that 47% of U.S.
adults identified with or leaned toward the Democratic Party and 43% toward the
Republican Party. Ten percent did not lean toward either party or declined to
answer. Before leaners are assigned, 44% called themselves independent or
something else.

The national gap is small, but the social pattern is not uniform. In the same
survey, men were more likely to be Republican or Republican-leaning (49%),
while women were more likely to be Democratic or Democratic-leaning (52%). The
Democratic side was larger among Black adults (73%) and Hispanic adults (57%).
Adults without a college degree were close to evenly divided but leaned
Republican (45%), while college graduates leaned Democratic (54%).

```text
life experience, group position, and political context
  -> identity or partisan leaning
  -> issue interpretation, coalition belonging, and possible vote
  -> [open] turnout, organizing, policy demand, institutional response,
           and persistence
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

The simple story “America is split in two” hides two different facts. Many
people do not use a party label for themselves, yet most of those who do not
identify with a party still lean toward one. At the same time, the combined
coalitions are built from uneven demographic and generational patterns rather
than one shared social experience.

That makes party identity a social meaning layer, not a complete explanation of
political behavior. The same label can gather people with different material
pressures, media habits, religious ties, places, and views of institutions.
Small shifts in affiliation therefore need to be checked against action and
issue-specific judgment rather than treated as proof of realignment.

## What the source does not establish

- Repeated cross-sections do not show that the same people changed sides.
- Party affiliation or leaning is not the same as a vote, turnout, ideology, or
  policy preference.
- Subgroup gaps do not identify whether age, education, place, race, gender,
  religion, income, cohort, or political events caused them.
- A national 2026 snapshot does not show whether the pattern will persist.

## Next test

Join repeated identity measures to the same respondent or compatible panel,
then follow material change, messenger or institutional trust, issue judgment,
turnout or organizing, and later identity. Compare people who keep a label,
lean without identifying, switch, or move to no lean while preserving subgroup
and place differences.

## Reading rule

Do not call a small affiliation shift a realignment. Separate self-label,
leaning, issue judgment, vote, turnout, and durable coalition membership.

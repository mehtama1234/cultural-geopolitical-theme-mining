# When privacy protection increases the data exchange

## Short answer

A privacy rule can make people more willing to share, not less. If a person feels more protected, the service may receive more receipts, visits, or other details. That can improve an offer, but it can also give a firm more power to predict, sort, or target the customer.

The careful finding is a change in willingness to share after a privacy rule. It is not proof that people understood the deal, that the data were used fairly, or that privacy improved in every meaningful sense.

## What the source shows

An HBS account reports an analysis of nearly 16,000 users of a receipt-and-reward app before and after California and Virginia privacy rules took effect. Users in those states submitted 9% more information, including 1.5 additional receipts per month, 5% more unique store visits, and 4% more retail categories.

The study covers one platform and uses a policy comparison. It does not show whether users read the notice, understood data retention or later uses, received better service, or gained equal bargaining power.

- [HBS: More Trust, More Data](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/more-trust-more-data-the-upside-of-privacy-laws-for-companies)

## The connection

```text
privacy rule and clearer permission
  -> person feels more protected
  -> more receipts, visits, or categories shared
  -> firm gains a richer customer record
  -> offers, sorting, pricing, or exposure may change
  -> trust, choice, and power move together
```

More sharing can be a useful exchange when the purpose is clear and the reward is real. Consent is not the same as equal bargaining power.

## The four outside views

**Household:** A customer may share more because the service feels safer or because the reward matters. The person may not know how long the data stay, who receives them, or whether sharing can be undone.

**Company and sector:** A richer record can improve targeting, fraud checks, product design, or service. It can also support higher prices, exclusion, or a new use the customer did not expect.

**Money and finance:** Data may be payment for a discount or reward. The account must include the value received, time spent, privacy risk, price, and any later cost of being sorted.

**Public power:** Privacy rules set permission, access, deletion, notice, and enforcement. A useful rule should help people make an informed choice without pretending that a long notice creates equal power.

## What would change the finding

- Notice comprehension, consent, deletion, retention, sale, and later use.
- Which data were shared: receipts, location, health, financial, or identity data.
- Offers, prices, service quality, fraud outcomes, and customer complaints.
- Results by age, income, race, disability, language, and digital access.
- Whether sharing stayed higher after a breach, unwanted use, or policy change.
- Which firms gained the data and whether customers could switch or refuse.

## Next test

Follow users for one year before and after a privacy rule. Randomly vary the notice, permission, reward, and deletion controls, then measure what people understand, share, receive, and can stop. Compare the customer's value with the firm's new ability to predict and sort.

## Reading rule

Do not call more sharing proof that privacy improved. Do not call it proof that privacy failed. Ask what the person knew, what the firm did, and whether the customer could say no.

# AI use is not one thing: firms, functions, and workers can move on different clocks

## The deeper finding

A Census CES study using the 2026 Business Trends and Outlook Survey AI
supplement separates three layers that are often collapsed into “AI adoption.”
During November 2025–January 2026, 18% of firms used AI in a business function,
or 32% when weighted by employment. Use reached roughly 50%–60% of very large
firms in Information, Professional Services, and Finance.

Among firms, 23% reported workers using AI in work-related tasks, or 41% on an
employment-weighted basis. Worker task use sometimes occurred without formal
firm-level adoption, and firm-level adoption sometimes occurred without worker
task use. Most users—66%—reported using AI only to augment tasks; AI-related
employment decreases were reported by 2% of firms.

The study also finds that broader functional deployment and operational
investment were positively associated with employment decreases, while worker-
task integration was not significantly linked to headcount reduction after the
other factors were considered.

```text
firm decision
  -> business-function deployment
  -> worker task use or non-use
  -> augmentation, redesign, or headcount change
  -> [open] worker control, pay, job quality, bargaining, household security
```

## What this adds to the broader atlas

This sharpens the AI and firm-culture lanes. AI can be present in a company’s
strategy without being used by workers, or workers can experiment before a
formal company rollout. Employment-weighted adoption can also look much higher
than the share of adopting firms because large firms contain more workers.

The early evidence therefore supports neither “AI is replacing everyone” nor
“AI is only a harmless assistant.” It points to a layered transition in which
the location of control—firm leadership, a business function, or the worker’s
task—matters for the eventual social meaning.

## What the paper does not establish

- This is an early working-paper snapshot based on firm reports, not a worker-
  level causal panel.
- The public abstract does not report the analytic sample size.
- The associations do not prove that AI caused employment decreases or better
  commercial performance.
- The paper does not measure worker pay, monitoring, bargaining, job quality,
  retention, household wellbeing, or political response.
- Employment-weighted adoption is not the experience of a typical small firm.

## Next test

Follow the same firm and workers from AI decision through function deployment,
task assignment, training, monitoring, pay, hours, headcount, internal moves,
worker voice, exit, and later firm and household outcomes. Keep formal adoption,
actual task use, and operational investment as separate fields.

## Reading rule

Do not use one AI-adoption number to stand for firm deployment, worker use, job
loss, productivity, or worker power. Keep those stages separate.

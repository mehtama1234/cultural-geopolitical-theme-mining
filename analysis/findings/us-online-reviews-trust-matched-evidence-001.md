# Reviews can reveal quality and distort choice at the same time

## Short answer

Reviews can help a customer judge a seller when they reflect real experience. They can also become a paid or controlled signal. NBER evidence finds that rewarded feedback increased sales and was linked to higher-quality sellers in one online marketplace. The FTC now prohibits several deceptive review practices, including fake reviews, sentiment-based incentives, undisclosed insider reviews, and certain suppression of negative reviews. The deeper finding is that review design affects both what customers believe and which sellers survive.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Reviews can help customers learn about a seller they cannot inspect directly. | Which review features predict actual quality. |
| Incentive | NBER finds rewarded feedback generated nearly 30% higher sales and was associated with higher-quality sellers in the studied setting. | Whether rewards produce honest feedback or mainly more visible feedback on US platforms. |
| Certificate | An eBay certificate redesign used more direct seller inputs and changed which sellers were certified. | Whether the change improved customer outcomes or only changed the label. |
| Manipulation | FTC prohibits fake reviews, certain paid sentiment, insider reviews, deceptive review sites, and review suppression. | How much manipulation remains and who is most exposed. |
| Customer result | A rating can change what a customer notices, trusts, and buys. | Price, quality, returns, complaints, and repeat purchase after the choice. |

Sources: [NBER review rewards](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22584), [NBER eBay quality certificates](https://www.nber.org/papers/w29674), [FTC final rule](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), and [FTC review guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-evaluate-online-reviews).

## The connection

```text
review rule or incentive
  → different people write and different reviews remain visible
  → customer forms a belief about quality
  → seller changes effort, price, or promotion
  → customer chooses, returns, complains, or comes back
  → platform trust and seller competition change
```

The evidence supports parts of this chain in different markets. It does not follow one US customer from review exposure to later quality and price.

## The useful surprise

More reviews are not always better information. A reward can bring more feedback and higher sales, and in the NBER setting it was linked to higher-quality sellers. But a platform can also reward the act of writing rather than the truth of what was experienced. Volume and trust are different measures.

## Four views

**Household:** Compare the review signal with price, quality, delivery, return cost, and the time needed to check another source.

**Customer:** Record whether the reviewer bought the product, received an incentive, has a history, and can be heard if the review is removed.

**Money and finance:** Track review position, seller ad spending, price, refunds, complaints, and the cost of a bad choice.

**Public power:** Ask whether the platform explains ranking and removal, preserves evidence, and gives customers and sellers a workable appeal path.

## What would change the finding

- Review exposure linked to the same customer's purchase, return, complaint, and repeat choice.
- Verified purchase, incentive, reviewer history, edit, removal, and appeal records.
- Seller quality measured before and after a rating or certificate change.
- Price and advertising changes for sellers with similar products and different review positions.
- Results by income, age, language, disability, and digital access.

## Reading rule

Do not treat review volume or star rating as quality. Check who wrote it, what the platform showed, what the seller did, and what happened to the customer afterward.

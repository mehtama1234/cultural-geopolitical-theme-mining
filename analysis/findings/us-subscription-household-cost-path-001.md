# When a forgotten subscription becomes a household cost

## The argument

Automatic renewal can be useful when a person wants a service to continue. It can also keep a charge running after the service has lost value or the customer has stopped paying attention. The central issue is not renewal by itself. It is whether the customer understood the deal, received a clear reminder, and could leave without an obstacle course.

A subscription becomes a household problem when the charge competes with food, rent, care, debt, or a needed purchase. The dollar amount may be small, but the timing can still matter in a thin budget.

## What the sources establish

NBER payment-card data across ten digital and non-digital products show a sharp rise in cancellations when replacement cards force subscribers to actively renew. The authors estimate that inattention raises seller revenue by 14% to more than 200% compared with immediate cancellation after value falls below price. The range is modeled, and a customer who does not cancel may still value or use the service.

HBS describes recurring revenue as useful when the fee is clear and fits the service’s value, while subscription fatigue can make charges hard to see or stop. In May 2026, the FTC said Shutterstock agreed to pay $35 million to settle allegations that it charged consumers without informed consent and made cancellation difficult. That is one enforcement case, not a rule that every renewal is unlawful.

Sources: [NBER inattention](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31547), [HBS subscription fatigue](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/with-subscription-fatigue-setting-in-companies-need-to-think-hard-about-fees), and [FTC Shutterstock action](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices).

## Follow the recurring charge

subscription starts → payment renews without a fresh choice → use falls or value becomes unclear → notice or a forced card change finally triggers action → cancellation, continued payment, fee, or overdraft → household room and trust change

The customer’s silence can mean several things: the service still matters, the customer forgot, the customer could not afford the attention, or the cancellation path was hard. Those are different findings.

## What must stay separate

- Automatic renewal and unwanted renewal.
- Not cancelling and not valuing.
- A clear choice and a silent default.
- Cancellation effort and service value.
- A settlement and proof of harm for every customer.

## Next test

Choose one recurring service. Follow sign-up, disclosure, first renewal, use, reminder, cancellation attempt, final charge, refund, and the next household payment for 90 days. Split results by income, age, disability, digital access, and essential versus optional service.

## Reading rule

Automatic renewal is not automatically a household tax. A customer needs a clear choice and a real way out.

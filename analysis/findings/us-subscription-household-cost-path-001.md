# When a forgotten subscription becomes a household cost

## The argument

Automatic renewal can be useful when a person wants a service to continue. It can also keep a charge running after the service has lost value or the customer has stopped paying attention. The central issue is not renewal by itself. It is whether the customer understood the deal, received a clear reminder, and could leave without an obstacle course.

A subscription becomes a household problem when the charge competes with food, rent, care, debt, or a needed purchase. The dollar amount may be small, but the timing can still matter in a thin budget. A customer who does not cancel may still value the service, may have forgotten it, or may have judged that cancellation took too much effort. Silence does not tell us which.

The deeper finding is that attention is part of the price. A business can earn from a service that customers use, but it can also earn from the gap between declining value and the moment a customer notices and exits. A fair system makes renewal visible, cancellation real, and the final charge easy to understand.

## Follow one recurring charge

A household signs up for a digital service during a busy month. It uses the service often at first. Months later, use falls, but the card renews automatically. The customer sees the charge only after the account balance is low. She cancels, asks for a refund, or keeps paying because the cancellation path is confusing. A forced card replacement may finally make her choose whether to renew.

```text
subscription starts → payment renews without a fresh choice → use falls or value becomes unclear
→ reminder or forced card change triggers action → cancellation, continued payment, fee, or overdraft
→ household room and trust change
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Step | Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Inattention | NBER payment-card data across ten digital and non-digital products show a sharp rise in cancellations when replacement cards force subscribers to actively renew. | A fresh payment choice can reveal that a service is no longer wanted. |
| Seller revenue | The NBER authors estimate inattention raises seller revenue by 14% to more than 200% compared with immediate cancellation after value falls below price. | Customer inattention can have a measurable business value. |
| Customer meaning | A customer who does not cancel may still value or use the service; the range is modeled. | Continued payment is not proof of unwanted use. |
| Enforcement | The FTC said Shutterstock agreed in May 2026 to pay $35 million to settle allegations of charges without informed consent and difficult cancellation. | One enforcement case shows why consent and exit rules matter, not that every renewal is unlawful. |

Sources: [NBER inattention](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31547), [HBS subscription fatigue](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/with-subscription-fatigue-setting-in-companies-need-to-think-hard-about-fees), and [FTC Shutterstock action](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/05/shutterstock-pay-35-million-settle-ftc-allegations-over-illegal-subscription-cancellation-practices).

## The deeper finding

The customer’s exit path is part of the product. A low monthly price can become costly when the service is hard to monitor, the reminder is weak, or cancellation takes more attention than the customer has. The fair measure is not only whether renewal was technically disclosed; it is whether a person could make a timely, informed choice.

## What remains unproven

The studies do not show the same household’s use, value, reminder, cancellation attempt, final charge, and later budget. They do not show which groups face the greatest attention cost, or whether stronger reminders reduce useful renewals along with unwanted ones.

## Next test

Follow one recurring service for 90 days. Record sign-up, disclosure, first renewal, use, reminder, cancellation attempt, final charge, refund, account balance, and the next household payment. Split results by income, age, disability, digital access, and essential versus optional service.

## Reading rule

Automatic renewal is not automatically a household tax. A customer needs a clear choice and a real way out.

# Finding: A platform's own product can help today's customer while changing tomorrow's choice set

**Status:** bounded platform-competition and consumer-choice finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-20

## The bounded finding

An NBER field experiment hid Amazon-owned brands from shoppers and compared
consumer choice and search behavior. In the tested categories, consumers chose
similar products when the private labels were absent, without more search effort
or more cross-platform substitution. Yet the authors' short-run welfare
estimates indicate that removing Amazon brands reduced consumer surplus by about
5.4% (5.5% in the NBER summary), with heterogeneous effects across categories.
Demoting the brands in search did not increase estimated consumer surplus because
some consumers valued those products and their prominent placement.

**Why it matters:** this is a different platform-power mechanism from an ad fee.
A platform-owned product can be a real convenience or quality fit for some
customers while the same firm controls the shelf, data, fulfillment, and rules
under which rivals reach those customers. “Own product present” is therefore not
equivalent to “consumer harm,” and “consumer benefit today” is not equivalent
to durable competition tomorrow.

**Do not overread:** this is one field experiment in selected Amazon categories,
not a long-run market-power estimate. It does not establish seller entry or
exit, platform data use, preferential treatment, future prices, product quality,
or welfare outside the tested setting.

## The deeper finding

The result separates immediate consumer surplus from future market structure.
An own-brand product can be valuable because of fit, trust, delivery, or price,
while the platform may still hold a private advantage over the shelf and the
data generated by that shelf. The relevant policy and consumer question is
therefore not simply whether the product is shown, but whether capable rivals
can continue to reach customers on workable terms and whether customers retain
usable alternatives over time.

## Reading rule

Do not call platform-owned products harmful because the platform controls the
shelf, or helpful because some customers prefer them. Keep today's product fit,
price, delivery, and search effort separate from seller entry, exit, data use,
future variety, and durable consumer choice.

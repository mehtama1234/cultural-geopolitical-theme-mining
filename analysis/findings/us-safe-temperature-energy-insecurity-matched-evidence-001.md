# A safe temperature can compete with food and medicine

## Short answer

Energy insecurity is not only an unpaid bill. In the preliminary 2024 EIA survey, households reported several different breaks: giving up food or medicine, living at an unhealthy temperature, receiving a shutoff notice, or losing use of heating or cooling equipment. These reports can overlap, but they point to one household choice: which basic need is postponed when the home cannot be kept safe. DOE programs are designed to help through weatherization and safety work. The missing proof is whether that help restores the room, the bill, health, work, and housing stability for the same household.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 17.55 million homes reported leaving home at an unhealthy temperature. | What temperature, season, duration, and health result followed. |
| Essentials | 32.89 million homes reported reducing or forgoing food or medicine to pay energy costs. | Which necessity was cut, by how much, and for how long. |
| Service | 16.19 million homes reported a disconnect or delivery-stop notice. | Whether service was stopped and whether the notice led to debt, relocation, or recovery. |
| Equipment | 6.83 million homes could not use heating equipment and 8.16 million could not use air-conditioning equipment. | Whether repair, replacement, weatherization, or landlord action fixed the problem. |
| Help | DOE weatherization includes energy use and safety checks and can serve renters and owners. | Wait time, completion, comfort, health, work, and household cash after help. |

Sources: [EIA 2024 household energy insecurity table](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/hc/pdf/HC11.1_2024.pdf), [EIA 2024 RECS](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2024/), [DOE weatherization application](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/wap/how-apply-weatherization-assistance), and [DOE LEAD](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/scep/low-income-energy-affordability-data-lead-tool).

## The connection

```text
energy bill or equipment failure
  -> unsafe temperature, skipped food or medicine, or shutoff risk
  -> illness, lost work, care burden, or housing strain
  -> help, repair, debt, relocation, or continued sacrifice
```

The first arrow is measured by EIA as separate reported conditions. It is reasonable to connect them as competing household needs, but the table does not prove a single sequence inside each home. DOE describes the repair and weatherization response; it does not by itself show the later result.

## The useful surprise

The count of “energy-insecure homes” hides different forms of loss. A family can be counted because it skipped medicine, because a broken air conditioner made the home unsafe, or because a shutoff notice arrived. Those cases may need different help. A bill payment program, a repair, and a safer rental home are not interchangeable.

## Four views

**Household:** Record the room temperature, food and medicine tradeoff, equipment status, sleep, illness, care, and work—not just the bill.

**Customer and resident:** Ask who controls the meter, the repair, the lease, and the weatherization decision. A renter may pay the bill but lack the power to fix the home.

**Money and finance:** Compare energy cost with food, medicine, repair, debt, rent, and shutoff timing. A lower bill is not enough if the first payment or waiting period is impossible.

**Public power:** Measure who gets help, how long it takes, what is completed, and whether results differ by tenure, income, age, disability, place, and housing type.

## What would change the finding

- Same-home energy bills, indoor temperatures, equipment records, and shutoff records before and after help.
- Food, medicine, illness, missed-work, care, and housing records linked to the same household.
- Repair and weatherization completion, wait time, landlord permission, and contractor quality.
- Results separated for renters, owners, manufactured homes, older homes, hot places, and cold places.
- A record of whether assistance prevented a sacrifice or only moved it to a later month.

## Reading rule

Do not call energy insecurity a bill problem alone. Ask what the household gave up to keep the home safe, and whether help changed that choice.

# Finding: A childhood neighborhood can shape both skills and access to jobs

**Status:** bounded place, firm, and intergenerational-mobility finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

An official Census working paper links the 2000 Census to employer records to
study parents, children, childhood neighborhoods, and later earnings. It finds
that the relationship between a child's earnings and the earnings of people in
the childhood neighborhood is stronger than the relationship found inside the
same neighborhood. Neighborhood parent earnings explain more than 80% of the
variation in the paper's modeled neighborhood effects.

The authors separate two possible routes:

```text
childhood neighborhood
  -> people, skills, networks, and local conditions
  -> person effects and access to better-paying establishments
  -> adult earnings
  -> [open] security, belonging, health, mobility, and political meaning
```

The paper also reports that neighborhood conditions account for about 30% of
the Black–White adult-earnings gap after conditioning on parent earnings. But
conditional on neighborhood average earnings, children from neighborhoods with
higher Black shares had higher adult earnings. That is a counterexample to
reading neighborhood race composition as a simple deficit measure.

## What this adds to the atlas

The deeper societal insight is that place can transmit two different things at
once: human-capital conditions and access to firms or jobs. A neighborhood is
therefore not only a housing location or a school label. It can shape who a
child meets, which establishments are reachable, and which kinds of work become
available later.

## Limits and counterexamples

- This is a modeled observational study, not randomized neighborhood placement.
- The public abstract does not identify every school, network, discrimination,
  mobility, or firm-access mechanism.
- Earnings are not the same as wellbeing, health, safety, belonging, or civic
  participation.
- The Black–White decomposition does not explain every source of the gap.
- A higher Black-share conditional pattern is not evidence that race itself
  causes higher earnings; it may reflect unmeasured institutions, networks, or
  selection.

## Next decisive test

The strongest follow-up is a protected family-place-firm panel joining childhood
neighborhood, moves, schools, networks, discrimination, firm access, job quality,
earnings, housing, health, belonging, and later civic action. Until then, the
safe claim is that neighborhood and establishment context are associated with
intergenerational earnings pathways, not that relocation alone creates mobility.

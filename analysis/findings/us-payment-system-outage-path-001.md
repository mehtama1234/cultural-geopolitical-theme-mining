# When the power fails, payment becomes access

## Short answer

A household can have money and still be unable to use it when electricity, communications, or a merchant terminal fails. Payment is part of access to food, medicine, fuel, and other basics.

Cash can help as a backup, but it is not a complete answer. It can be lost or stolen, and a long outage can also empty shelves or stop fuel pumps.

## What the source shows

NBER combines outage, weather, transaction, scanner, and survey data. It finds that spending rises before disasters as households stock up, often using credit. After outages, digital payments fall and cash use rises. Nearly half of surveyed consumers could not use their preferred electronic payment during an outage, while greater cash access reduced the decline in completed purchases.

The study does not show which households missed food, medicine, fuel, or work access, or which backup payment design works best in every outage.

[NBER Working Paper 35115](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35115)

## The connection

```text
storm or infrastructure failure
  -> electronic payment unavailable
  -> cash, credit, offline tool, or no transaction
  -> essential purchase completed or missed
  -> household cost, health, work, and recovery change
```

A shift to cash may show a backup working or a preferred payment failing. It is not by itself a sign of hardship.

## The four outside views

**Household:** Cash, another card, or a nearby working store may preserve a purchase. Access to each backup differs by income, age, disability, safety, and place.

**Company and sector:** Banks, payment networks, merchants, utilities, and fuel providers each control one link. A failure at one link can stop a purchase that all the others could have served.

**Money and finance:** Stocking up may use credit before the outage and create repayment later. A missed purchase may also create a larger cost than the original item.

**Public power:** Cash access, outage rules, network repair, emergency benefits, merchant backup systems, and supply delivery decide whether money remains usable.

## What would change the finding

- Outage length, location, cause, network failure, weather, and store supply.
- Cash access, bank access, merchant acceptance, offline tools, and network restoration.
- Food, medicine, fuel, care, work, and other essential purchases completed or missed.
- Credit use, repayment, fees, lost work, health, and later recovery.
- Income, age, disability, race, place, broadband, and transportation.

## Next test

Follow households through a real outage for three months. Join payment attempts, cash access, merchant records, essential purchases, credit, work, care, health, and recovery. Compare households with different backups but similar needs.

## Reading rule

Keep payment availability, purchase completion, essential need, and later loss separate. Having money is not the same as being able to use it.

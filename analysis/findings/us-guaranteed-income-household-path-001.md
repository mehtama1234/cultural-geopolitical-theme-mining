# When cash changes the rest of the household

## Short answer

Cash paid to one person can change what other people in the home do. Another household member may enter training, change work, spend differently, or come into contact with health care.

That spillover can be a family gain. A report of forgone care can also reveal an unmet need rather than show that the payment made health worse.

## What the sources show

In a randomized guaranteed-income experiment, NBER finds that other household members were more likely to take part in education or training. Recipients were also more likely to report that someone in the household had forgone health care. The authors say this may reflect greater engagement with care and the finding of needs that were already present.

Another US experiment with $1,000 or $50 monthly payments found higher short-term consumption and near-zero average change in net worth because debt also rose. HBS reports that money can give people more control over daily problems and reduce stress.

The evidence shows effects beyond the named recipient. It does not settle the health meaning or what remains after payments end.

## The connection

```text
one household member receives regular cash
  -> money, time, and decision power move inside the home
  -> another member trains, works, receives care, or changes spending
  -> the family gains room or finds an unmet need
  -> work, health, debt, and family roles change
```

The person who receives the payment is not the only person affected. The direction of the effect needs to be measured member by member.

## The four outside views

**Household:** Cash can let one person train or another person handle care. It can also change who controls spending and create conflict or dependence.

**Company and sector:** Training can improve a worker's next job, while unpaid care can make a current job harder to keep. Health providers may see needs that money helped a family name.

**Money and finance:** More spending can meet a real need while debt rises too. Count who holds the debt, who controls the payment, and what happens to savings.

**Public power:** Payment rules, health coverage, care access, and the end date decide whether household flexibility becomes lasting room or a short pause.

## What would change the finding

- Who receives the payment, who controls it, and how money is shared.
- Work, training, education, unpaid care, and health care by each member.
- Health need, care use, forgone care, out-of-pocket cost, and later health.
- Spending, saving, debt, housing, conflict, and family support.
- Results during payments and after they stop.

## Next test

Follow every household member before, during, and two years after payments. Join payment, work, training, care, health, spending, debt, housing, and decision-making records. Ask whether a care report reflects new unmet need, better detection, or both.

## Reading rule

Do not treat a household result as only the recipient's result. Do not treat reported forgone care as proof that cash harmed health.

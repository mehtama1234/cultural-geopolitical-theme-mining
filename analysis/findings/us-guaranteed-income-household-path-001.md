# When cash changes the rest of the household

## The argument

Cash paid to one person can change what other people in the home do. Someone may enter training, change work, spend differently, or finally come into contact with health care. That spillover can be a family gain. A report of forgone care can also reveal an unmet need rather than show that the payment made health worse.

The payment therefore has to be followed member by member. Who receives it is not always who benefits first. One person may gain time, another may take on care, and a third may hold the new debt. A household average hides the bargaining and tradeoffs inside the home.

## Follow one payment through a family

A recipient uses regular cash to reduce a bill and take a training course. A partner changes work hours to cover care. A child gets a needed appointment. The family gains a path forward, but it may also spend more, carry debt, or discover a health need that had been delayed. When payments end, each member may face a different return to the old constraint.

```text
one household member receives regular cash → money, time, and decision power move inside the home
→ another member trains, works, receives care, or changes spending → family gains room or finds an unmet need
→ work, health, debt, and family roles change
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Step | Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Spillover | In a randomized guaranteed-income experiment, NBER finds other household members were more likely to take part in education or training. | A payment can affect people beyond the named recipient. |
| Health report | Recipients were more likely to report that someone in the household had forgone health care; the authors say this may reflect greater engagement with care and needs already present. | A new report is not proof that cash caused worse health. |
| Consumption | Another US experiment with $1,000 or $50 monthly payments found higher short-term consumption and near-zero average change in net worth because debt also rose. | Cash can meet a need while debt absorbs part of the gain. |
| Control | HBS reports that money can give people more control over daily transport, food, and care problems and reduce stress. | Cash can buy choice before income changes. |
| Limits | The evidence does not settle the health meaning or what remains after payments end. | Short-term household effects are not lasting security. |

Sources: [NBER Working Paper 35589](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35589), [NBER Working Paper 32784](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32784), and [HBS on money and control](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/more-proof-that-money-can-buy-happiness).

## The deeper finding

Cash changes the household's internal clock. One person can use it to buy time for training, another can handle care, and the family can address a problem before it becomes an emergency. But flexibility is not free. It can change who controls spending, who takes unpaid work, and which need becomes visible first.

## What remains unproven

The studies do not show who controlled the payment, how money was shared, whether training led to better work, or whether the reported care was newly created or newly detected. They do not show the same family's roles, debt, health, and security after payments stop.

## Next test

Follow every household member before, during, and two years after payments. Join payment, work, training, care, health, spending, debt, housing, and decision-making records. Ask whether a care report reflects new unmet need, better detection, or both.

## Reading rule

Do not treat a household result as only the recipient's result. Do not treat reported forgone care as proof that cash harmed health.

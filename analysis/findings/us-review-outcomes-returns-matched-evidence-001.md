# The cost of a bad choice begins after the review

## The argument

Reviews and search reduce the work of choosing. The real customer cost appears after purchase if the product is wrong, late, poor quality, or hard to return. A low price is not a low-cost choice if the buyer pays return shipping, loses work time, waits for a refund, or buys the product again.

This makes trust a two-part question. Did the information help the customer choose? And, when the choice failed, could the customer recover money and time? A platform that measures only the sale sees success where the customer may be carrying the loss.

The deeper finding is that a review system can move risk forward without reducing it. It helps a buyer decide, but the buyer may still carry the cost of bad quality, weak seller support, and a slow remedy. The quality of the return path is part of the product.

## Follow one purchase

A customer sees a high rating and a low price. The item arrives late and does not work. The return window is short, shipping is expensive, and the refund waits until the seller receives the package. The customer has paid in money, time, and attention before the platform records a complaint.

The seller may still receive a good rating because the customer forgets, gives up, or cannot revise the review. Another buyer then sees the same signal. The marketplace has a record of a transaction, but not necessarily a record of the total harm or the effort required to correct it.

```text
review and search
  -> customer belief and purchase
  -> product, price, delivery, and quality
  -> return, complaint, refund, repair, or replacement
  -> time and money recovered or lost
  -> repeat choice, exit, and trust
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Evidence | Meaning | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| NBER finds eBay shoppers searched about 36 times per purchase across three days and estimates a cost near 25 cents per search page. | Comparison takes time and can have value. | The study does not measure the cost after a failed purchase. |
| More search was linked to finding lower prices. | Search can improve the first decision. | A lower price is only one result. |
| NBER finds rewarded feedback changed sales and was linked to higher-quality sellers in another marketplace. | Reviews can move demand and seller behavior. | It does not prove reviews predict every buyer’s outcome. |
| FTC says return windows, shipping cost, restocking fees, refund rules, records, and payment method shape recovery. | The remedy has its own price and timing. | Guidance does not show the result of each dispute. |

Sources: [NBER search study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22302), [NBER review-reward study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22584), and [FTC online-shopping guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/online-shopping).

## The deeper finding

The cheapest choice is not always the lowest-cost choice. A review system that measures sales can reward a signal while leaving the customer to carry bad quality, return work, and refund delay. The meaningful measure is total recovery: what arrived, what failed, what the buyer had to do, and what was finally recovered.

This also links reviews to customer power. A buyer has more power when a bad purchase can be returned cheaply, a complaint reaches someone with authority, and the record can warn the next buyer. A rating without a workable remedy mainly helps the market move the next item.

## What remains unknown

The evidence does not show whether a review predicted a later return, how long refunds took, or whether customers stopped using a platform after a failed remedy. It does not show how outcomes differ by seller size, price, income, language, disability, or digital access.

## Next test

Link review exposure to purchase, delivery, quality, return reason, refund date, complaint, seller advertising, and repeat choice. Count customer time, replacement cost, and abandoned claims alongside dollars recovered.

## Reading rule

Do not call a review useful because it raises sales. Check what arrived, what the customer had to do when it failed, and what was finally recovered.

# Finding: High participation can hide a shortfall in the dollars that reach families

**Status:** bounded public-benefit, household-resource, and administrative-conversion finding · **Checked:** 2026-09-23

## The finding in plain language

The Census and IRS linked survey and tax records to estimate how many eligible
taxpayers received the Child Tax Credit and how much of the eligible money was
actually paid in tax year 2021.

The headline numbers are different:

- 91% of eligible taxpayers participated.
- 85% of eligible credit dollars were paid.
- About 3.1 million eligible taxpayers did not participate.
- The report estimates about $26 billion in eligible dollars went unclaimed or
  was not fully claimed.

So “most eligible taxpayers participated” does not mean “most eligible money
reached families.” The people counted as nonparticipants were not all the same:
82% were in tax units with adjusted gross income below $75,000, and 53% had
filed a return.

## What this adds to the broader theme atlas

This gives the program a clear benefit-conversion pattern:

```text
eligibility on a modeled tax unit
  -> filing, claiming, and payment rules
  -> taxpayer counted as participating
  -> full, partial, or zero eligible dollars received
  -> timing, adequacy, household use, and later security [open]
```

A public program can look broadly successful when measured by people touched
while still leaving a meaningful amount of the intended resource unconverted.
The gap is especially important for a family benefit because a smaller credit,
late payment, or missing payment may affect care, food, housing, debt, and work
even when the family is counted in the participation numerator.

## Limits and counterexamples

- The study estimates participation conditional on modeled eligibility; it does
  not observe every eligible household directly.
- Eligibility, tax units, dependents, and credit amounts are modeled and then
  adjusted with administrative data; those steps can introduce error.
- Survey and tax records refer to different populations and reference dates.
- The 2021 credit was temporarily expanded under the American Rescue Plan Act,
  including advance monthly payments, so this is not a timeless program rate.
- The paper says the 2021 methodology is not directly comparable with its prior
  2020 paper and that unclaimed-dollar estimates may be revised.
- The study does not show why a family did not claim, whether payment was timely,
  how money was used, whether it was adequate, or whether household security
  improved.

The next decisive artifact would follow one protected tax unit from eligibility
through filing, advance payment, reconciliation, correction or appeal, receipt
timing, spending or debt relief, and later household security.

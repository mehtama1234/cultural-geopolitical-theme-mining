# When a free app charges in time

## Short answer

A free app can still have a real price: time, attention, ads, and the other things a person cannot do at the same moment. Time spent is not automatically wasted, because the service may be useful or social.

The right test asks what the user receives, what replaces the activity, and who can leave.

## What the source shows

Using two field experiments on Facebook and Instagram, NBER finds that users respond only a little to ad load, the main user cost in the setting. The share of a person's time devoted to an activity and the cost of that time affect which online or offline activities can replace it. The paper also models a possible separation of the two platforms.

The evidence does not show long-run well-being, work, care, sleep, household effects, or the result of an actual platform separation.

[NBER Working Paper 34743](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34743)

## The connection

```text
free or low-money-price service
  -> time and attention cost
  -> staying, switching, or offline activity
  -> user benefit and platform competition
  -> public rule or firm design
```

People may stay because the service is valuable, familiar, or socially important. More ads may be a tolerable price for a service that remains free.

## The four outside views

**Household:** Time on an app may offer connection, information, or rest. It may also replace sleep, care, work, or another service.

**Company and sector:** Platforms choose ad load, design, ranking, and how hard it is to leave. A higher time cost may not cause exit if alternatives are weak.

**Money and finance:** A free service can create value without a bill, while its data and attention support firm revenue. Count time value and any later purchase or fee.

**Public power:** Privacy, competition, child safety, labor, and platform rules shape whether users have real choice and whether the service's time cost is visible.

## What would change the finding

- Time use by income, age, disability, household role, work, care, and sleep.
- Ad exposure, content value, notifications, design, and user control.
- Staying, switching, offline substitutes, exit cost, and social ties.
- Well-being, work, care, sleep, spending, and consumer value.
- Actual merger, separation, pricing, or design change.

## Next test

Follow users for six months before and after a change in ad load or platform design. Join time use, ads, content, work, care, sleep, well-being, switching, spending, and exit.

## Reading rule

Keep money price, time price, attention, use, well-being, and competition separate. Do not call a free service low-cost without counting time.

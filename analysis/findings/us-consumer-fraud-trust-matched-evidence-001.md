# The loss is only the first part of a fraud victim’s experience

## Short answer

Fraud changes more than a household balance. It creates a question about who will answer, reverse the payment, unlock the account, and carry the loss. The Federal Reserve reports that 21% of adults experienced financial fraud or scams in 2024. Among people facing non-credit-card fraud, 63% lost money and 32% said some of it was not recovered. FTC complaint data show $12.5 billion in reported losses, while CFPB complaints show the time and uncertainty after a payment problem. The trust effect is plausible, but not yet directly measured.

## What the matched evidence shows

| Layer | What the evidence says | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure | 21% of adults reported financial fraud or scams in 2024. | The number of attempts never reported. |
| Payment route | Credit-card losses are often protected; other routes can leave consumers carrying the loss. | The exact rule and facts for each transaction. |
| Household loss | The Fed estimated $63 billion in net non-credit-card losses in 2024. | The effect on each household's budget and future choices. |
| Market signal | FTC received 2.6 million fraud reports and recorded $12.5 billion in reported losses in 2024. | The full amount of fraud and underreporting. |
| Response | CFPB complaints describe delays, restrictions, failed transfers, and refund disputes. | Whether the response restored money, access, or trust. |

Sources: [Federal Reserve household fraud data](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-banking-and-credit.htm), [FTC 2024 fraud data](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/new-ftc-data-show-big-jump-reported-losses-fraud-125-billion-2024), [CFPB 2025 Consumer Response report](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/2025-consumer-response-annual-report/), and [Federal Reserve fraud-protection remarks](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/bowman20260505a.htm).

## The connection

```text
scam or unauthorized payment → loss or account lock
                             → dispute, complaint, and recovery effort
                             → money returned, delayed, or kept lost
                             → trust and future use of the system
```

The first three links have household, complaint, and market evidence. The last link is still a test. A customer may keep using a bank because there is no easy substitute, even while trust falls. Another may move accounts, stop using transfers, or accept more friction to feel safe.

## The recovery gap

Credit-card fraud and a bank-transfer scam are not the same household event. The payment route changes who must notice the loss, who can stop it, who investigates, how quickly money may return, and who is left carrying the shortfall. A complaint can also add time and uncertainty: an account may be restricted while a firm reviews it, and the sending and receiving institutions may point to each other.

This is why the FTC's reported $12.5 billion and the Fed's estimated $63 billion should not be merged. The first comes from complaints; the second estimates net non-credit-card losses from household responses. The gap is not a contradiction. It shows that the system sees only part of the loss through formal reports.

## Four views

**Household:** Record amount lost, amount recovered, time without access, and what bill or asset was affected.

**Customer:** Compare payment route, warning, dispute path, response time, and responsibility rule.

**Money and finance:** Separate gross loss, recovery, fees, credit effect, account lock, and later borrowing.

**Public power:** Ask which institution controls the payment route, who must reimburse, and what evidence the customer must provide.

## What would change the finding

- A transaction-level record linking payment route, scam type, dispute, recovery, and resolution time.
- Results by age, disability, language, income, digital access, and account type.
- Firm-level reimbursement, account-freeze, complaint, and appeal records.
- Later household budgets, credit use, account switching, and trust measures.
- Reporting estimates that compare complaint data with anonymous household surveys.

## Reading rule

Do not equate a scam attempt, a complaint, a gross loss, and an unrecovered loss. Name the payment route and the response before making a trust claim.

# When app convenience leaves money on the trip

## The argument

Two apps can show different prices for the same ride while most riders check only one. That may reflect habit, trust, discounts, wait time, or a better service—not simple carelessness.

The fare gap matters only beside the effort of comparison and the value of arriving on time. A rider carrying a child, starting a shift, or traveling somewhere unsafe may rationally pay more to reduce uncertainty. The customer’s real cost is fare plus time, risk, and the cost of a missed trip.

## What the source establishes

An NBER audit of 2,238 matched New York City trips found an average absolute price gap of about $3.50, roughly 14% of the average fare. Device data show that only about 16% of people who opened one app also opened the other, even though neither app was consistently cheaper.

The study covers one city and a short price window. It does not show each rider’s income, trip purpose, service quality, final cost, or reason for choosing an app.

Source: [NBER Working Paper 34441](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34441).

## Follow the ride choice

two platforms show different prices → checking takes effort and attention → rider chooses an app → fare, wait, reliability, and safety are exchanged → money and time remain

Platforms benefit when one app becomes the default and comparison is costly. But a higher fare may buy a shorter wait, a trusted driver, a discount, or fewer cancellations. Calling every gap a loss would ignore what the rider bought.

## What must stay separate

- Posted fare and final fare.
- Fare and total trip cost.
- Comparison effort and customer preference.
- Price and service quality.
- A single gap and repeated household exposure.

## Next test

Follow riders and matched trips for six months. Join both app prices, comparison time, wait, cancellations, reliability, trip purpose, income, safety, total cost, and switching. Measure what money and time remain after repeated rides.

## Reading rule

A price gap is not automatically a customer loss. Show what the rider paid for besides the fare.

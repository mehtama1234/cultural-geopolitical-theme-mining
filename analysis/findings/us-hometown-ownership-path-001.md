# When business ownership moves away from home

## Short answer

Business owners are less tied to the place where they were born than they were in the past. That may weaken a local link between ownership and place. It does not by itself show that services, jobs, or trust got worse.

The useful question is not whether an owner is local. It is whether a place keeps firms, useful services, good jobs, and a say in what happens there.

## What the sources show

An HBS account of US Census research reports that the extra chance of staying in one's birth state among self-employed White men fell from 8 percentage points in 1970 to 2 points in 2018. The hometown advantage was below zero for all Americans in the latest sample. The measure is about staying near birthplace, not about business survival or service quality.

NBER research finds that home equity can help some people start longer-lived businesses, while much house-price-linked entry is small or tied to construction and real estate. Another NBER study links county business formation to long-run local traits, but that does not show why a person moved or whether residents benefited.

- [HBS: Charting the Decline of the Hometown Entrepreneur](https://www2.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/charting-the-decline-of-the-hometown-entrepreneur)
- [NBER Working Paper 21458](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21458)
- [NBER Working Paper 28606](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28606)

## The connection

```text
capital, skills and local networks
  -> where a would-be owner lives
  -> who owns firms and controls local services
  -> jobs, prices, place identity and local wealth
  -> housing, migration and political demands
```

The evidence supports a weaker tie between birthplace and business location. It does not yet support a claim that local control was lost or that mobile owners serve people worse.

## The four outside views

**Household:** A move may bring better pay, a better market, or a chance to start a firm. People who stay may lose a familiar service or gain a new one.

**Company and sector:** Owners follow workers, customers, credit, housing, and supply chains. A local owner may know the place better; a new owner may bring money or a useful skill.

**Money and finance:** Home equity and credit can decide who can start and who must leave to find room. A business can look local while its profits and decisions leave the place.

**Public power:** Zoning, housing, lending, taxes, schools, and local procurement shape who can own a firm and who gets a voice in the place.

## What would change the finding

- Firm openings, closures, owners, workers, wages, prices, and service access in the same place.
- Owner birthplace, residence, race, gender, age, industry, credit, housing wealth, and reason for moving.
- Whether movers build firms that last longer or employ more people.
- Whether local residents can still start firms, buy homes, and shape public decisions.
- Where business profits, taxes, and major decisions end up.

## Next test

Link owner moves to firm records and local customer outcomes over five years. Compare places where owners leave with similar places where owners stay. Track firm survival, jobs, wages, prices, service gaps, housing costs, and local participation.

## Reading rule

Birthplace is not the same as commitment. A business can be locally useful without a local-born owner, and a local owner can still extract value from a place.

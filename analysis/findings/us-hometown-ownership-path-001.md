# When business ownership moves away from home

## The argument

A business owner does not have to be born in a place to serve it well, and a local-born owner does not automatically protect it. The useful issue is not birthplace. It is whether the place keeps durable firms, useful services, good jobs, local wealth, and a voice in decisions.

An owner may move because customers, workers, credit, housing, or a better market are elsewhere. The move can bring a stronger business to the new place and leave a service gap in the old one. A firm can also look local while its profits, key decisions, and ownership sit elsewhere. Those are different facts and need different records.

## What the sources establish

An HBS account of Census research reports that the extra chance of staying in one’s birth state among self-employed White men fell from 8 percentage points in 1970 to 2 points in 2018; the hometown advantage was below zero for all Americans in the latest sample. This measures birthplace and residence, not business survival or service quality.

NBER research finds that home equity can help some people start longer-lived businesses, while much house-price-linked entry is small or tied to construction and real estate. Another NBER study links county business formation to long-run local traits, but neither study shows whether residents gained from local control.

Sources: [HBS hometown entrepreneur](https://www2.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/charting-the-decline-of-the-hometown-entrepreneur), [NBER Working Paper 21458](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21458), and [NBER Working Paper 28606](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28606).

## Follow ownership into place

capital, skills, housing, and local networks → where a would-be owner can live and start → who owns firms and controls services → jobs, prices, profits, and place identity → housing, migration, and political demands

The evidence supports a weaker tie between birthplace and business location. It does not show that local control was lost or that mobile owners serve people worse. The next question is what happens to the customer and worker when ownership changes.

## What must stay separate

- Local-born ownership and local commitment.
- Firm location and decision location.
- Business opening and business survival.
- Revenue and local wealth.
- A new service and a preserved service.
- Owner movement and customer harm.

## Next test

Link owner moves to firm records and local customer outcomes over five years. Compare places where owners leave with similar places where they stay. Track firm survival, jobs, wages, prices, service gaps, housing costs, profits, taxes, and local participation.

## Reading rule

Birthplace is not the same as commitment. Follow where decisions, profits, jobs, and services end up.

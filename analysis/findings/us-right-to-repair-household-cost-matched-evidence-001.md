# When repair access is part of a product's real price

## The argument

The price of a product includes what happens when it breaks. A phone, car, appliance, or work tool can be cheap to buy and expensive to keep alive. If parts, tools, repair information, software access, or a usable warranty are unavailable, a failure can become a forced replacement or a long period without the product.

Safety and quality are real reasons to control some repairs. The customer still needs a fair, workable choice: an authorized shop, an independent shop, a part, a manual, a warranty claim, or a replacement with a clear price. Without that choice, the purchase price hides a future cost and shifts risk toward people with the least cash to replace the item.

## Follow one broken product

A delivery worker's phone stops charging, or a family's washer fails. The part is unavailable, software blocks a third-party fix, the approved shop is far away, or the warranty process takes weeks. The household borrows a device, misses work, pays for a replacement, or puts the repair on credit. The product failure has become a cash, time, and access shock.

```text
product breaks → parts, tools, software, warranty, and shop choices → price and waiting time
→ repair, replace, borrow, or go without → money, work, care, safety, and waste change
```

## What the sources actually establish

| Step | Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Design barriers | The FTC identifies design, parts, information, software, and contract limits that can make repair harder. | The barrier can be built into the product. |
| Warranty | The FTC says warranties may cover repair, replacement, or refund and must be available before purchase. | Coverage is part of the product offer. |
| Customer choice | The FTC warned that requiring specified parts or service providers may violate warranty law. | A warranty should not quietly remove lawful repair options. |
| Replacement | The FTC says repair restrictions can steer people toward replacement before useful life ends. | A failure can create a second purchase. |
| Public rule | The FTC-Deere settlement advances repair access for farm equipment. | Enforcement can change the information and tools owners receive. |

Sources: [FTC Nixing the Fix](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions/nixing_the_fix_report_final_5521_630pm-508_002.pdf), [FTC warranty warning](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-warns-companies-stop-warranty-practices-harm-consumers-right-repair), [FTC warranty guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties), and [FTC-Deere settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-secure-settlement-deere-company-advancing-farmers-right-repair).

## The deeper finding

Repair access is financial slack. A household that can fix a product may avoid a large payment and keep work, transport, communication, or care moving. A household that cannot may pay for replacement even when the original product could have continued working. The real measure is whether repair is affordable, timely, safe, and available where the customer lives.

## What remains unproven

The FTC record identifies restrictions and legal protections but does not join a product failure to a repair quote, waiting time, replacement debt, work interruption, and later household stability. It also does not show whether independent repair produces the same safety and repeat-failure results across product types.

## Next test

Track product failure, repair options, parts price, labor, warranty claim, waiting time, replacement, borrowing, work or care interruption, and repeat failure. Compare authorized and independent repairs by product, place, income, rural access, and disability.

## Reading rule

Do not call a product affordable from its purchase price alone. Include the cost and availability of keeping it working.

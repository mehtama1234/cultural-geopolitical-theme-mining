# Rotation EF — Indoor environmental quality, housing health and retrofit justice

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction; Tier 2 later-outcome conversion question  
**Research lane:** housing / public health / energy / schools / geography / household agency

## Why this rotation

The atlas covers ambient air, noise, energy insecurity, extreme heat and durable
housing. This rotation isolates the indoor environment where people actually
sleep, learn, work and provide care: dampness, mould, ventilation, thermal
comfort, combustion, radon, building materials and retrofit conditions.

The key distinction is between a building intervention and lived indoor health.
Insulation may reduce heat loss and bills but reduce ventilation if poorly
designed. Heating may improve comfort but be unaffordable. Remediation may remove
mould while leaving leaks, structural defects or landlord retaliation. A school
can install monitors while classrooms remain poorly ventilated. The unit is the
dwelling, classroom, workplace and household pathway—not an energy certificate
or retrofit count.

## Research question

When a landlord, city, school system, utility, developer or retrofit programme
improves insulation, heating, ventilation, mould remediation, indoor monitoring
or building materials, under what conditions does it produce safe air, thermal
comfort, lower energy burden, attendance, health and housing retention without
creating new costs, exposure or displacement?

## Conversion chain

```text
building envelope / moisture / fuel / occupancy / climate / affordability
  -> design, maintenance, retrofit, heating, ventilation and housing rule
  -> dwelling, classroom, workplace and household operation
  -> temperature, humidity, mould, pollutant, energy, cost and time result
  -> respiratory health, sleep, learning, care, work and housing stability
  -> tenant/occupant agency, landlord accountability, remedy and retention
```

The unit must be a named dwelling, classroom, workplace, building, household,
tenant, student or worker, with moisture, temperature, ventilation, pollutant,
energy, health and intervention dates. A retrofit, certificate, CO2 reading,
heat-pump installation or complaint closure is an intermediate signal, not proof
of healthy indoor conditions or household security.

## Evidence snapshot

### 1. Dampness and mould have a direct respiratory-health pathway

WHO’s dampness and mould guidance finds that persistent moisture and microbial
growth are associated with respiratory symptoms, allergies and asthma. WHO
recommends preventing or minimizing dampness, identifying root causes, controlling
moisture, improving ventilation and temperature, and providing prompt remediation.

This closes the health-risk mechanism but not whether a named tenant, school or
worker received effective remediation or whether the cause was fixed rather than
the visible mould covered.

Sources: [WHO dampness and mould guidelines](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789289041683)
and [WHO compendium actions for existing dampness and mould](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/who-compendium-on-health-and-environment/who_compendium_chapter2.pdf?download=true&sfvrsn=14f84896_8).

### 2. Indoor quality depends on linked building systems

WHO housing guidance explains that insulation can reduce temperature extremes and
dampness, but can also reduce ventilation if not designed with adequate air
exchange. Poor ventilation can allow mould, combustion pollutants, biological
contaminants and radon to accumulate. Natural ventilation can improve air and
cooling, but must be adapted to climate, security, outdoor pollution and building
design.

The implication is systems-based: one measure can improve one clock and worsen
another. Retrofit evaluation must track indoor pollutants, temperature, humidity,
energy, health and occupant control together.

Source: [WHO healthy housing and ventilation guidance](https://www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/healthy-urban-environments/housing/strategies?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse-little-text-block).

### 3. Indoor conditions are unequal and often under-measured

The EEA reports that poor indoor air quality is associated with respiratory and
cardiovascular disease and that building quality, furnishings, combustion,
crowding, mould and dampness contribute. It identifies lower-quality housing and
indoor air inequality as key concerns, while school monitoring shows elevated
CO2 in many classrooms as an indicator of inadequate ventilation.

Indoor exposure is private and fragmented. Tenants, children, informal workers,
caregivers and people in overcrowded homes may have the least ability to measure,
relocate, demand repairs or control heating and ventilation.

Source: [EEA indoor environment, mould and dampness](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/beating-chronic-respiratory-disease/indoor-environment-mould-and-dampness).

### 4. Energy poverty and housing quality form one pathway

World Bank housing evidence links dampness and mould with energy poverty and
describes how poor insulation and inefficient heating lead to high costs,
under-consumption and inadequate indoor temperatures. Self-reported measures
capture lived deprivation, but can contain cultural interpretation and exclusion
errors; direct building and household measurements are needed alongside them.

This connects to the energy-insecurity packet without collapsing the themes:
indoor health depends on both the physical building and whether the household can
afford to operate it.

Source: [World Bank housing report and energy-poverty evidence](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099061526053533170/pdf/P515073-7e7018c8-61c6-4112-b663-928e1441f8ec.pdf).

### 5. Remediation requires responsibility and root-cause repair

WHO recommends prompt and adequate remediation, moisture control, mould removal,
root-cause identification and accessible information for vulnerable groups. This
implies a governance chain: who detects, who pays, who temporarily relocates,
who verifies the repair, and who protects the tenant or worker from retaliation?

A complaint closed, a surface cleaned or a tenant advised to ventilate does not
prove that leaks, insulation, heating, ventilation or building defects were
corrected. Occupant behaviour is part of the system but cannot substitute for
owner and institutional responsibility.

### 6. Schools and workplaces are separate exposure systems

The EEA school evidence and WHO housing guidance show that indoor conditions
affect children, teachers, workers and people who spend long periods in buildings.
Schools may face a trade-off between ventilation, outdoor pollution, noise,
temperature, energy cost and infection control. Workplaces may have formal
occupational duties but temporary, seasonal or informal workers may have weaker
voice and remedy.

The test must therefore include attendance, concentration, symptoms, work time,
care burden and building operation—not only compliance or sensor readings.

## Bounded proposition 137

**A building retrofit, insulation project, heat pump, ventilation upgrade,
mould remediation, indoor-air monitor, school HVAC programme, energy certificate,
landlord repair, heating subsidy or healthy-housing standard is not by itself
healthy indoor air, thermal comfort, lower energy burden, better attendance,
respiratory health, secure tenancy or retrofit justice.** Retrofit counts, CO2
readings, temperature compliance, energy savings, complaint closure, mould
removal, equipment installation or heating assistance do not establish that
named households, students, workers and tenants experienced lower dampness and
pollutant exposure, affordable operation, fewer symptoms, improved learning or
work, protected tenure, root-cause repair, equal enforcement or remedy.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

1. **The building is a health institution.** It structures sleep, study, work,
   care, privacy, social connection and exposure every day.
2. **Comfort is culturally and economically situated.** Temperature, ventilation,
   window opening, privacy and energy use are shaped by climate, norms, safety,
   crowding and affordability.
3. **Retrofit can empower or displace.** Better buildings may lower bills and
   illness, but rent increases, renovation eviction or landlord control can
   remove residents from the benefit.
4. **Indoor data changes accountability.** Sensors can support tenant or school
   claims, but data ownership, interpretation and enforcement determine whether
   measurement produces repair.
5. **Maintenance is the long clock.** A high-quality installation can fail when
   filters, leaks, ventilation, heating, mould and controls are not maintained.

## Counterexamples and cautions

- Insulation can reduce bills and heat exposure while trapping pollutants or
  moisture if ventilation is inadequate.
- Ventilation can improve CO2 and infection control while increasing heating,
  cooling, noise or outdoor-pollution exposure.
- Mould cleaning can improve visible conditions while leaving leaks or structural
  moisture unresolved.
- A heat pump can improve comfort and emissions while increasing capital cost,
  maintenance needs or tenant dependence on electricity prices.
- A retrofit can improve a building while increasing rent, causing eviction or
  excluding undocumented and informal occupants from assistance.
- A monitor can make exposure visible without changing ownership, maintenance,
  landlord behaviour, school funding or worker remedy.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote to **Tier 1 bounded distinction** for the proposition. WHO, EEA and
World Bank sources support the dampness, ventilation, retrofit, energy-poverty,
school and remediation mechanisms. Keep the integrated outcome at **Tier 2**
because public sources rarely follow the same building and people through indoor
exposure, health, affordability, attendance, tenancy and repair durability.

## Next test

maintained or energy-poor comparison. Follow dwellings, classrooms and workplaces
across heating and cooling seasons, including tenants, children, older people,
people with asthma, informal workers and households unable to relocate.

## Source boundary

The current packet closes the dampness, ventilation, retrofit, energy-poverty,
school and remediation mechanisms. It does not close named indoor exposure,
health, affordability, attendance, tenancy, maintenance, displacement,
enforcement or remedy outcomes. Those remain acquisition targets.

# Geopolitical leverage closure follow-up audit: Poland/NATO

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Purpose:** test whether the public record closes the chain from Poland/NATO response to attributable foreign-actor adaptation, deterrence, concession or durable legitimacy  
**Status:** response persistence and continuing security work are observed; leverage and deterrence remain unverified

## Executive result

The follow-up closes a later institutional stage but not the external-behavior
stage.

NATO describes Eastern Sentry as continuing within a broader 2026 air-defence
posture, and Polish official sources report continued monitoring, air activity,
border-adjacent threats and attribution analysis. A G7 statement also records an
information-contestation dimension aimed at perceptions of Poland, NATO and
Ukraine support.

These records establish persistence, adaptation of the defensive institution and
an information burden. They do not establish that:

- a specific foreign actor changed behavior because of Eastern Sentry;
- the frequency or severity of comparable incursions declined;
- a later object belongs to the same actor or event class;
- an F-35 or JASSM-ER milestone became mission-ready capability; or
- public reassurance translated into durable local or partner legitimacy.

This is a source-boundary result after repeated acquisition attempts, not a
claim that deterrence failed.

## Acquisition audit

| Acquisition path | Strongest result | Decisive field still missing | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original Poland/NATO incident | National defensive activation, interception and Article 4 consultation are recorded | Independent object-level attribution, intent, complete denominator and civilian outcome | Incident-to-response, not deterrence |
| Eastern Sentry | NATO records additional assets, coordination and integration into the 2026 air-defence posture | Asset-level duration, maintenance, cost, readiness and incident-specific effect | Persistent posture, not causal leverage |
| Later Polish official records | Continued air-security activity and formal analysis of later incidents/objects | Comparable same-actor event series and foreign response | Recurring security environment, not a clean outcome series |
| G7 information-operations record | Perception manipulation and trust-undermining claims are documented | Audience reach, belief change, institutional trust and behavioral response | Information contestation, not measured legitimacy |
| F-35 acceptance/count | Arrival, acceptance and aircraft-count milestones | Trained personnel, serviceability, spares, sorties and mission use | Procurement/acceptance, not readiness |
| JASSM-ER agreement | Authorization, agreement value and delivery plan | Shipment, acceptance, inventory, training and operational use | Contract, not fielded leverage |

## Stage audit

| Stage | Closed? | Safe conclusion | Do not infer |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Incident | Yes, bounded | A security episode triggered national and alliance decisions | Complete attribution or intent |
| Immediate operation | Yes, bounded | Defensive action and interception are more concrete than rhetoric | Reduced future threat |
| Alliance response | Yes | Consultation and Eastern Sentry are institutional responses | Deterrence |
| Posture persistence | Yes, bounded | Defensive integration continued into 2026 | Causal effect on adversary |
| Capability realization | Partly | Some arrival/acceptance milestones are visible | Readiness, sustainment or mission availability |
| External adaptation | No | Later activity and attribution work remain observable | Concession, restraint, repeated-incursion decline or reversal |
| Durable legitimacy | No | Solidarity and information-contestation narratives are visible | Public or partner trust change |

## Bounded finding

> Geopolitical response can persist and become institutionally integrated while
> the public record still cannot demonstrate that an external actor changed
> behavior because of it.

The cultural meaning is a problem of attribution and time. A resident or ally
may experience vigilance, patrols, alerts, fiscal burden and information noise
without being able to see whether the response made the threat less likely. A
government can therefore demonstrate action and solidarity while still facing a
legitimacy question about effectiveness, burden and escalation.

## Promotion decision

Promote the geopolitical lane to a **Tier 1 source-boundary finding** for
incident-to-response and posture persistence. Retain deterrence, leverage,
foreign-actor adaptation and durable legitimacy as Tier 2.

Required future fields remain:

```text
event/object | actor attribution/confidence | comparable baseline
| deployed assets | duration/maintenance | cost/local burden
| later incident frequency/severity | foreign adaptation/concession
| partner/public response | alliance persistence or reversal
```

# Rotation BG — Space weather and critical-infrastructure continuity

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** space weather / power / navigation / aviation / satellite systems / critical infrastructure / geographic resilience  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 forecast-to-continuity question

## Research question

When solar and geomagnetic activity threatens power, communications, navigation,
aviation and spacecraft, what converts observation and warning into an adapted
operation, a protected service and a recoverable outcome for a named place or
user?

## Conversion chain

```text
solar event and near-Earth disturbance
  -> observation, forecast and alert
  -> operator interpretation and protective action
  -> grid/aviation/satellite/navigation adjustment or fallback
  -> service continuity, safe operation and recovery
  -> public trust, economic resilience and geopolitical autonomy
```

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** solar observations, forecasts, watches and alerts make a distant
  physical hazard legible to institutions and infrastructure operators.
- **Mechanism:** lead time, forecast uncertainty, operator authority, technical
  procedures, redundancy, geographic exposure and fallback capacity determine
  whether information can be converted into protection.
- **Operation:** a utility changes configuration, an airline reroutes or alters
  procedures, a satellite enters a protective mode, or a navigation user adopts
  an alternative.
- **Material consequence:** power, communications, positioning, flight safety,
  pipeline integrity or space-system availability is maintained or restored.
- **Meaning:** space weather is planetary in origin but experienced locally;
  resilience can become a story of shared scientific protection or of unequal
  exposure where high-latitude, remote or infrastructure-poor places have fewer
  alternatives.
- **Power:** states, observatories, utilities, satellite operators, airlines and
  navigation providers decide who receives alerts, who can act, and whose
  continuity is treated as critical.

## Bounded finding

> **A space-weather forecast is not critical-infrastructure continuity.** An
> observation, warning, risk scenario, monitoring mission, operator plan or
> protective protocol does not establish that a named utility, aircraft,
> satellite, navigation-dependent service or community acted in time, avoided
> damage, maintained an affordable service or recovered equitably.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Space weather exposes a layered geography of dependence. The physical driver is
global, but vulnerability follows latitude, grid design, satellite orbit,
aviation route, pipeline orientation, communications alternatives and the
ability of local operators to interpret and act on a warning. The same alert can
therefore be useful intelligence for one operator and unusable information for a
community that lacks backup power, alternative navigation or institutional
authority.

The key cultural question is not whether people know that solar storms exist. It
is whether continuity responsibilities are visible, rehearsed and fairly
allocated. A technically successful forecast can still produce weak public
legitimacy if service disruption, repair cost or communication burden is shifted
onto places with the least ability to contest or substitute.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Forecasting can reduce risk even when no outage or damage occurs; absence of
  an observed failure is not proof that the warning was unnecessary.
- A geomagnetic event can produce spectacular aurora while causing little
  material harm; cultural visibility and infrastructure impact are separate
  outcomes.
- A national operator plan may protect bulk infrastructure while leaving local
  customers, aviation users or dependent services without a usable fallback.
- A short lead time may still be operationally valuable, but value depends on
  the action window, authority and reversibility of the protective measure.
- Public sources often describe hazard and capability more clearly than they
  disclose operator-specific incidents, avoided loss or customer continuity.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: observation, forecast, warning, operator
action, service continuity and geographic/public outcome are separate stages.
Keep forecast effectiveness and equitable continuity in Tier 2 until named
event-to-operator ledgers close the warning, action, service and recovery chain.

## Next test

Build one event ledger across a power operator, airline/navigation user and
satellite operator. Record alert time, forecast confidence, recipient, decision
authority, action, fallback, outage/degradation, repair, customer or user

# Rotation IG — Sanitation, fecal sludge and the safe service chain

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When toilets, sanitation plans and fecal-sludge infrastructure are built, do people receive safe, affordable, accessible and continuously managed sanitation—or does the chain stop at construction, open-defecation declarations or household containment while workers, neighborhoods and waterways remain exposed?

## Why this packet matters

Sanitation is a service chain, not a facility. The practical chain is:

```text
need / household / school / clinic / settlement
  -> accessible toilet and containment
  -> water, privacy, use and maintenance
  -> emptying / desludging and worker safety
  -> transport, treatment and safe disposal or reuse
  -> environmental protection and disease prevention
  -> affordability, dignity, resilience and accountable service
```

The chain has cultural and geographical dimensions: privacy, gender, disability, caste/class, informal tenure, water scarcity, flood risk and displacement shape use. It has a labor dimension because sanitation workers bear exposure and equipment gaps. A toilet count can conceal failed emptying, unsafe dumping or unaffordable service.

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Sanitation systems intersect with caste and occupational stigma, gendered privacy, disability access, informal settlement tenure, flood and climate risk, and the politics of urban land. Waste movement can export risk from affluent neighborhoods to peripheral communities. Public health, municipal finance, private operators, informal workers and humanitarian agencies must coordinate across jurisdiction and time.

## Counterexample and source boundary

An open-defecation-free declaration or toilet construction milestone is not proof of safe management. Indonesia’s national gap and GLAAS worker-equipment findings show that governance, containment and worker safety remain separate stages. Crisis desludging can protect a site while leaving final treatment, recurrence and worker exposure unobserved. Public evidence closes coverage, health burden, governance, worker-safety gaps, planning, value-chain design and crisis operations, but not a comparable household/worker/environment chain through treatment, affordability, health and remedy.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A toilet, latrine, open-defecation-free declaration, sanitation plan, desludging operation, treatment plant, coverage statistic or constructed handwashing station is not by itself safely managed sanitation, dignity, worker safety, environmental protection, affordability or disease reduction.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **sanitation-construction-to-safe-affordable-worker-protected-service-chain**. The strongest next acquisition is a named settlement or service area linking toilet/containment, use, water, maintenance, emptying, worker safety, transport, treatment, disposal/reuse, tariff, accessibility, contamination, illness and complaint/remedy.

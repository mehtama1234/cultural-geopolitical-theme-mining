# Rotation E: public-AI contestability and human fallback

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** AI-mediated practical judgment, public services, oversight, feedback and human review  
**Status:** comparative Tier 2 mechanism packet; adoption is broad, contestability infrastructure is uneven

## Why this source matters

The OECD's *Digital Government Outlook 2026* separates AI adoption from the
institutional conditions required for trustworthy public use. Across 36 OECD
countries, AI is used in at least one government area in 35 countries, but only
14 report mandatory pre-deployment risk assessments, 11 report post-deployment
audits, and 8 report citizen feedback or complaint mechanisms for government AI.

The UK Ministry of Justice's AI governance framework adds a concrete design
counterpoint: people must be able to inquire about or appeal AI-influenced
decisions and request human review when they disagree or are uncomfortable.

The conversion is therefore:

```text
AI adoption or service deployment
  -> user encounter and possible output/action
  -> disclosure, traceability and human fallback
  -> complaint, appeal or correction
  -> service/decision outcome and continuity
  -> trust, avoidance, participation or exit
```

The comparative data closes governance-capacity stages, not claimant-level
outcomes. The UK framework closes a design requirement, not evidence that a
person successfully used human review or obtained a corrected result.

## New trend interpretation

The relevant trend is not simply “governments adopt AI.” It is:

> Public institutions are scaling AI faster than they are standardizing the
> social infrastructure that lets people inspect, challenge, reverse and learn
> from AI-mediated action.

This is a comparative mechanism candidate, not a claim that all countries have
the same deficit. The country-level spread matters: a public-AI system with a
visible feedback route, human override and low-tech alternative creates a
different cultural encounter from one that offers only a digital output with no
traceable appeal.

## Cultural and geographical meaning

Human fallback is not only a technical control. It distributes recognition and
voice. A person who can reach a human reviewer can frame their situation in
context; a person limited to an automated channel must translate their life into
the system's categories or absorb the error.

Geography enters through connectivity, language, disability access, local office
availability, broadband cost, administrative capacity and the presence of
trusted intermediaries. A low-tech alternative can reduce exclusion, but it can
also shift time, travel and documentation burdens onto people least able to
carry them.

## Counterexamples and limits

- High adoption with strong safeguards can coexist; the OECD country counts are
  not a causal ranking of service quality.
- A formal complaint route does not prove people know about it, can access it,
  or receive correction.
- A human-review requirement does not prove the reviewer has authority,
  context, time or independence from the automated workflow.
- A post-deployment audit count does not reveal whether audits find or correct
  harm.
- The OECD indicators are country-level and not a linked claimant or case
  dataset.
- The UK framework is a governance specification, not an observed outcome study.

## Promotion decision

Promote the following Tier 2 mechanism:

**AI adoption is becoming an institutional contestability problem: the scarce
resource is not only model capability, but reachable review, traceability,
fallback and correction.**

The next qualifying evidence is a named public service or case series that links
AI exposure to disclosure, human review, correction time, service continuity and
different outcomes by geography, disability, language, age or digital access.

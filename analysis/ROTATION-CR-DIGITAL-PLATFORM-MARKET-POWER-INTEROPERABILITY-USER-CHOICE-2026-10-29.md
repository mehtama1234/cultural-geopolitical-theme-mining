# Rotation CR — Digital platform market power, interoperability and user choice

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Lane:** digital platform competition / gatekeepers / interoperability / data portability / switching / cloud dependence / SMEs / cultural and service diversity / digital sovereignty
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 market-power-to-practical-choice-and-cultural-diversity question

## Research question

When dominant digital platforms control identity, data, devices, search,
messaging, cloud or app distribution, what converts regulatory contestability
and interoperability into practical user choice, viable local businesses,
cultural diversity, privacy and durable digital sovereignty?

## Conversion chain

```text
network effects, data, device, app, cloud or audience dependence
  -> gatekeeper control, default, ranking, access term, fee, API, data silo or switching barrier
  -> interoperability, portability, multi-homing, alternative provider, consent and security
  -> completed switch, viable entrant, fair business access, user control, privacy and service continuity
  -> diverse products, local cultural expression, innovation, affordability, resilience and legitimate digital markets
  -> contestable digital public and commercial sphere
```

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** regulators increasingly describe large platforms as gatekeepers and
  use interoperability, portability, choice screens and ex ante obligations to
  make digital markets more contestable.
- **Mechanism:** network effects, defaults, data accumulation, identity and
  social graphs, cloud migration costs, ranking, API access, fees, security
  controls and loss of history make exit costly or unattractive.
- **Operation:** a user or business requests data, changes a default, installs
  an alternative, connects a third-party device, migrates cloud workloads or
  multi-homes; the gatekeeper approves, limits or delays access; a regulator
  investigates or specifies a remedy.
- **Material consequence:** formal choice may coexist with broken history,
  incompatible features, high migration costs, reduced discoverability, privacy
  risk, degraded service or no viable local alternative.
- **Meaning:** platform dependence shapes how people encounter language,
  culture, commerce and public information. A local creator or small business
  may be technically allowed to enter but culturally invisible if ranking,
  data access or audience portability remain concentrated.
- **Power:** gatekeepers control defaults, infrastructure, data and technical
  specifications; users and SMEs bear switching risk; regulators control
  remedies but must understand fast-moving technical systems and cross-border
  effects.

## Bounded finding

> **Interoperability, portability or regulatory contestability is not practical
> user choice or cultural diversity.** A DMA obligation, API, choice screen,
> data-transfer tool, alternative app store, open specification, regulator
> decision or new entrant does not establish that named users completed a safe
> switch, retained history and relationships, obtained equivalent service,
> exercised meaningful consent, or that local businesses and cultural voices
> gained durable reach.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Choice is not evenly distributed. A high-income user with multiple devices,
stable connectivity and technical support can switch more easily than a rural
household, migrant, disabled user, small merchant or language community whose
contacts, reputation, payments, archives and customers are concentrated in one
ecosystem. A local platform may be legally permitted but unable to reach users
because discovery, payments, identity and data remain controlled elsewhere.
Interoperability can support sovereignty and pluralism, but a poorly designed
interface can export privacy, fraud and security risk to users least able to
absorb it. The relevant geographic outcome is completed, safe and socially
meaningful choice—not the existence of an API.

## Counterexamples and limits

- Interoperability can increase innovation and convenience while creating new
  privacy, security and surveillance risks.
- A choice screen can expose alternatives without changing default behavior,
  awareness, trust, price or quality.
- Data portability may move files but not social graphs, reputation, algorithms,
  interoperability, customer relationships or cultural context.
- More entrants do not automatically mean more competition if they depend on
  the same gatekeeper, cloud, ranking or advertising infrastructure.
- A regulator’s specification decision may define implementation without
  finding non-compliance or proving user benefit.
- Small firms and public-interest services may be unable to bear the cost of
  security, standards, migration and support even where rights exist formally.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: market power, interoperability, portability,
switching, adoption, local business viability, cultural reach and sovereignty
are separate stages. Keep the market-power-to-practical-choice claim in Tier 2
until a named remedy joins user and business exposure to completed migration,
data/history retention, service quality, privacy/security, adoption, market
entry, local cultural reach and durable use.

## Next test

Select one mobile-device interoperability remedy, one cloud/data portability
route and one local or culturally specific digital service. Record user/business
type, language, disability, geography, data and relationship inventory, migration
time/cost, missing history, security incident, service quality, default choice,
new-provider adoption, revenue/reach and continued use. Compare technically
proficient and ordinary users, large and small providers, dominant-language and
minority-language communities, and urban and peripheral places.

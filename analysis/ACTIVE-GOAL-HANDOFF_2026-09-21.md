# Active goal and handoff

**Last updated:** 2026-09-21  
**Status:** active; not complete and not blocked

## The goal

Continue the long-term, U.S.-centered Cultural, Social, and Geopolitical Theme
Mining program. Review diverse data sources and use them to identify and test
recurring societal, cultural, political, institutional, consumer, firm,
infrastructure, and geopolitical subthemes.

The work must preserve:

- the source's actual denominator, unit, date, method, and uncertainty;
- the difference between observed, reported, compared, inferred, and open;
- counterexamples and alternative explanations; and
- the difference between a broad cross-source pattern and a same-person or
  same-household longitudinal result.

The program should advance through bounded research passes. Do not collect
sources forever or turn every new statistic into a stronger claim. A useful
pass adds one of three things: a new recurring pattern, a later stage in an
existing route, or a clearer unresolved boundary.

## Plain-language research question

> What is happening beneath visible events in American life, and how do money,
> time, institutions, firms, technology, culture, politics, infrastructure,
> and international commitments change people's real choices and society's
> ability to act?

Preserve this chain without pretending it is one universal causal model:

```text
condition or shock
  -> exposure and who is affected
  -> money, time, access, health, status, control, or security change
  -> adaptation: delay, substitution, borrowing, giving up, staying, moving,
     switching, reporting, organizing, seeking help, or going without
  -> household, worker, customer, firm, community, or institution outcome
  -> official, market, platform, employer, legal, or political response
  -> redistribution of cost, risk, ownership, information, waiting, or power
  -> trust, meaning, identity, legitimacy, action, dependence, or exit
  -> sector, state, alliance, or geopolitical consequence
```

## What the latest passes established

The broad atlas now has repeated evidence for these distinctions:

- formal help versus help that is actually usable;
- account or system protection versus protection experienced by the recipient;
- visibility, correction, and remedy versus restored household security;
- technical operation or public financing versus household benefit;
- alliance support versus trust in a particular partner;
- AI use or access versus control, local fit, and real exit;
- contact or belonging versus dependable support and political capacity; and
- aggregate capacity or spending versus leverage, legitimacy, or changed
  behavior.

## How to continue

1. Inspect the current queue and identify one unintegrated source or one open
   arrow that can add a later consequence or a meaningful counterexample.
2. Keep the source's unit and limits separate. Do not pool different people,
   households, firms, places, or countries into one implied trajectory.
3. Write the result in the machine-readable record if a new record is needed.
   Otherwise integrate the bounded insight into the main readout and concept
   register.
4. Rebuild the reader pages:

   ```bash
   python3 scripts/build_us_societal_readout_page.py
   python3 scripts/build_us_societal_concept_page.py
   python3 scripts/build_us_finding_pages.py
   ```

5. Run the full publication gate and report any failure accurately.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Do not claim a source follows the same person or household unless it really
  does.
- Do not treat an announcement, approval, contract, dashboard, exercise,
  refund amount, or reported completion as proof of lived recovery.
- Do not call an aggregate amount a success or recovery rate without a common
  denominator.
- Do not infer trust, political action, deterrence, welfare, or legitimacy
  from capacity alone.
- Do not mark the overall program complete merely because the publication gate
  passes. The program remains active while open cross-domain routes and source
  boundaries still require research.

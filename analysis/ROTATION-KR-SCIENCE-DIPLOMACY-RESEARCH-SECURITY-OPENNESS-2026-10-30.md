# Rotation KR — Science diplomacy, research security and reciprocal openness

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Follow-up to:** [GV science diplomacy rotation](ROTATION-GV-SCIENCE-DIPLOMACY-RECIPROCAL-COOPERATION-2026-10-29.md) and [GX research-security rotation](ROTATION-GX-RESEARCH-SECURITY-OPEN-COLLABORATION-ACADEMIC-AUTONOMY-2026-10-29.md)  
**Research question:** Can research-security policy protect people, institutions and sensitive knowledge while preserving academic freedom, reciprocal international collaboration and scientific diplomacy—or does security become a justification for unequal access, talent exclusion and scientific decoupling?

## Why this follow-up matters

The new evidence shows a policy transition from broad principles to institutional machinery: the EU has proposed a science-diplomacy framework, published a Research Security Monitor, considered a Centre of Expertise and a due-diligence platform, and balanced Horizon Europe openness with strategic exclusions. UNESCO warns that geopolitical securitization can fracture collaboration, while OECD evidence emphasizes that overly broad controls can stifle academic freedom. The conversion question is whether safeguards are narrow, evidence-based, reciprocal and contestable in practice.

```text
research partnership / mobility / infrastructure / funding
  -> risk appraisal, data/IP/security controls, partner due diligence and governance
  -> collaboration, publication, training, infrastructure use and knowledge exchange
  -> scientific result, local capability, public problem-solving and diplomatic trust
  -> reciprocity, academic freedom, rights, security and durable cooperation
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Research security is not only a technical compliance issue. It changes who can meet, travel, publish, access instruments, co-own data and define a problem. A risk framework created in a wealthy research system may classify a partner as a security concern while overlooking unequal bargaining power, historical extraction or the partner’s need for reciprocal infrastructure and authorship.

Science diplomacy works through relationships that may survive political conflict: shared water, climate, health, agriculture, space and disaster problems often cannot be solved within one jurisdiction. But the same research can also transfer dual-use capability, sensitive data or strategic advantage. The useful distinction is not open versus closed in the abstract; it is whether a control is targeted, time-bounded, transparent, reviewable and matched by a route to legitimate cooperation.

Geography enters through partner classification and institutional capacity. A large university may afford due diligence, export-control counsel and secure data environments; a regional university or researcher may experience the same system as a barrier to funding and mobility. “Global partnership” can therefore become a hub-and-spoke structure if agenda, infrastructure and IP remain concentrated in the initiating power.

The strongest cultural test is reciprocity: who benefits, whose knowledge counts, who can refuse, who owns the data, who receives credit, and who can challenge a security decision. Inclusion programmes that add regional voices without changing decision rights are participation signals, not shared diplomacy.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A science-diplomacy framework, research-security monitor, due-diligence platform, partner-risk assessment, critical-technology list, funding restriction, international project or inclusion programme is not secure research, academic freedom, reciprocal partnership, local capability, trust or peace. A control can reduce leakage while excluding legitimate collaborators; openness can produce extraction; and a stated global perspective can leave agenda and resource power unchanged.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Research security and science diplomacy are now being institutionalized together. The policy record supports a bounded distinction between risk-management architecture and actual reciprocal, rights-respecting collaboration.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **research-security-to-reciprocal-open-science-diplomacy-local-capability-and-academic-agency**. The next acquisition should join one partnership or funding decision to risk classification, data/IP terms, mobility, security measure, affected researcher/partner, appeal, project output, local capability, authorship/benefit and later relationship outcome, with a restricted or rejected comparator.

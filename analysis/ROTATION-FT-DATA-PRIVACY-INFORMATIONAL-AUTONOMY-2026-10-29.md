# Rotation FT — data privacy, informational self-determination and cross-border control

**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 conversion question
**Research lane:** technological, social, geographical, legal and geopolitical systems

## Why this rotation exists

Privacy is often represented by a law, consent banner, privacy policy or data
localization rule. Those signals do not show whether people understand what is
collected, can refuse or correct it, can obtain services without excessive
surveillance, or can obtain remedy when data travels across institutions and
borders. The practical issue is informational self-determination: who can
observe, infer, reuse, sell, combine, retain or delete information about a
person, household or community.

## Research question

When a government, firm, platform, employer, health system, school or data
broker collects and reuses personal data, under what conditions does that
produce legitimate service value and user agency while preserving privacy,
security, cultural dignity, cross-border rights and meaningful correction or
exit?

## Conversion chain

```text
collection / consent / sharing / inference / localization / transfer / deletion
  -> data-processing encounter for a named person, group or service
  -> access, decision, convenience, exposure, exclusion or dependence
  -> agency, safety, cost, discrimination, trust or remedy outcome
  -> institutional legitimacy, sovereignty, interoperability or geopolitical power
```

## Evidence snapshot

1. The UN General Assembly's 2024 resolution on privacy in the digital age
   reaffirms privacy as a human-rights issue and addresses digital technology,
   surveillance, artificial intelligence, business responsibility and data
   practices. A resolution establishes a normative and political baseline, not
   an observed user-level remedy.
2. The Council of Europe's Convention 108 framework treats fairness, lawfulness,
   transparency, purpose limitation, retention and access/rectification or
   erasure as practical data-protection principles. The existence of a principle
   does not show that a person can exercise it quickly, affordably or safely.
3. Convention 108+ developments in 2025–2026 include work on large-language-
   model data risks, neuroscience, transborder flows and political-campaign
   processing. This shows that privacy governance is expanding into new
   inference and geopolitical domains, not only database security.
4. OECD Digital Government Outlook 2026 finds that data strategies and privacy
   compliance standards are widespread in OECD governments, while data quality,
   interoperability, impact measurement and end-to-end service delivery lag.
   Formal governance can therefore coexist with weak operational control.
5. OECD reports that cross-border data flows create challenges involving privacy,
   national security, cybersecurity, digital protectionism and regulatory reach;
   localization may protect control in one dimension while raising costs or
   reducing interoperability in another.
6. OECD evidence says public trust in government data handling is not guaranteed
   and can take time to rebuild. This makes notice, purpose, access, correction,
   independent oversight and visible remedy part of service performance rather
   than compliance paperwork.

## Bounded proposition

**A privacy law, consent notice, data-protection authority, data-localization
requirement, encryption system, anonymization standard, data-sharing agreement,
privacy-enhancing technology or deletion request is not by itself meaningful
privacy, informational autonomy, equal service access, cultural dignity,
security, trust or remedy.** A policy, compliant workflow, audit, dataset,
consent click or successful deletion does not establish that named people
understood the use, could refuse without penalty, avoided harmful inference,
corrected records, escaped surveillance or received an effective remedy.

## Cultural, geographical and social interpretation

- **Consent is situated:** language, literacy, disability, urgency, dependency
  and unequal bargaining power shape whether consent is informed or coerced.
- **Household and community data:** individual consent may not resolve harms to
  families, neighborhoods, Indigenous groups or small language communities
  whose identities can be inferred collectively.
- **Cross-border geography:** data may be collected in one place, processed in
  another, stored in a third and used to make a decision elsewhere; jurisdiction
  and remedy can become materially distant.
- **Public-service dependency:** refusing data processing may be unrealistic when
  identity, health, education, welfare or employment access depends on it.
- **Inference exceeds collection:** privacy risk includes predictions about
  health, politics, religion, credit, location or relationships—not only fields
  a person knowingly supplied.
- **Trust is operational:** people judge privacy through understandable notice,
  predictable use, correction, non-retaliation, independent review and whether
  the service still works after a complaint.

## Counterexamples and cautions

1. Data localization may improve jurisdictional control while increasing cost,
   fragmentation or dependence on a domestic authority.
2. Anonymization can reduce direct identification but fail against linkage,
   re-identification or group inference.
3. A consent rate can measure interface completion rather than comprehension,
   voluntariness or later control.
4. Strong encryption can protect confidentiality while still leaving abusive
   collection, metadata exposure or unjustified retention untouched.
5. Deletion from one database may not remove copies, derived models, backups or
   records held by partners.
6. Privacy safeguards can conflict with evidence-sharing for safety, research or
   public health; the relevant question is necessity, proportionality, oversight
   and remedy, not maximal secrecy.
7. A data-protection authority or audit can exist while people lack affordable
   access to complaint, correction or compensation.

## Evidence status and promotion decision

Promote the Tier 1 distinction that privacy has legal, operational, inferential,
cross-border and community dimensions, and that formal compliance does not
equal user control. Keep the following as Tier 2:
**data-governance-to-informational-autonomy-and-trust conversion**. The next
evidence must name the data subject, controller, purpose, inference, access or
service decision, refusal/correction route, cross-border transfer and remedy.

## Next test

Build one public-service or platform case ledger across two jurisdictions. Track
notice and language, consent or legal basis, data fields and inferences, sharing
partners, retention, access/correction/deletion request, service effect,
security incident, complaint, independent review and outcome. Include a person
who refused or exited and a group-level or community-level harm case.

## Six-stage closure table

| Stage | What is currently supported | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | privacy resolutions, laws, standards, strategies and cross-border policy debate | user understanding and local salience |
| Mechanism | collection, consent, inference, sharing, retention, localization and oversight | dependency, bargaining power and group harm |
| Operation | controller, data subject, dataset, service, transfer and request can be named | repeated processing and response pathway |
| Material consequence | access, decision, exposure, exclusion, convenience, cost and security are relevant | correction, exit, non-recurrence, service continuity and compensation |
| Meaning | autonomy, dignity, trust, cultural control and legitimacy are central | participant attribution and collective recognition |
| Power | data control, interoperability, jurisdiction and refusal alter leverage | durable user control, substitution, accountability and remedy |

## Source boundary

The packet closes discovery and framing for data privacy and informational
self-determination. Public sources support the rights baseline, governance
principles, cross-border tension, operational gap and emerging technology
issues. They do not by themselves establish a named person’s comprehension,
freedom to refuse, harmful inference, correction, service outcome or effective
remedy.

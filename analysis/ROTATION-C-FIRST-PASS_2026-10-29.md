# Rotation C first pass: AI judgment, firm power, dependency, and social infrastructure

**Checked:** 29 October 2026  
**Status:** provisional source-led analysis; no pooled estimate.

## Why this rotation

Rotation C tests whether new technology and industrial capacity become shared
capability or instead redistribute control, risk and dependence. It joins four
surfaces without treating them as one outcome: firm-level AI use, algorithmic
management, critical-mineral substitution, and public/institutional capacity
to govern AI.

## Preliminary trend candidates

### 1. AI adoption is separating from realized productivity

NBER executive surveys describe broad adoption and expected productivity gains,
while also reporting modest recent measured effects, perceived-versus-measured
gaps and compositional labor reallocation. The defensible subtheme is
**AI adoption is becoming an organizational-allocation question, not merely a
technology-diffusion question**.

The open chain is:

```text
AI adoption and spending -> task redesign and management choice
  -> measured output and worker/customer distribution
  -> training, bargaining, retention and durable productivity
```

Adoption, executive expectation or a benchmark cannot establish shared gains,
worker control or customer benefit. A named firm/task record is needed.

### 2. Algorithmic management is becoming a contestability infrastructure

The ILO's 2026 platform-work Convention addresses disclosure of automated
systems, their effects on working conditions and access to information when
automated systems generate work decisions. This supports a provisional
subtheme: **the central question is shifting from whether algorithms manage
work to whether workers can understand, contest and correct their effects**.

The Convention is a formal standard, not evidence of implementation or worker
outcomes. The next test should compare a country or platform implementation
route through notice, explanation, appeal, correction, representation and social
protection.

### 3. Resilience requires qualified substitution, not domestic ownership alone

GAO's critical-minerals work separates import dependence, ownership visibility,
processing capacity, substitution, recycling and time-to-effect. It reports that
some battery-mineral recycling could reduce reliance in two to three years,
while semiconductor substitution/recycling may not reduce near-term reliance.
The provisional subtheme is **resilience is a qualified-output and timing
problem, not simply a sourcing or ownership problem**.

The next evidence must follow one mineral or component through qualification,
processing, cost, environmental burden, buyer use and disruption response.

### 4. Social infrastructure for AI is procurement, correction and ownership

The World Bank argues that governments cannot scale AI as a one-off technology
purchase: they need ongoing monitoring, maintenance, testing, data portability,
interoperability, auditability and clear ownership/funding. The ILO's digital-
transition brief adds that social dialogue can help manage restructuring and
skills change, but implementation is uneven and often ad hoc. This supports a
provisional subtheme: **institutional AI capacity is a social infrastructure
problem—who can monitor, correct, represent and sustain the system matters as
much as the model**.

The next test needs a named agency or sector, a procurement/deployment route,
an error or appeal record, a support owner, and a measured service outcome.

## Cross-lane synthesis

| Lane | Formal signal | Distributional question | Missing decisive evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI and firms | Adoption, spending and expected productivity | Who receives the productivity gain and who absorbs job churn, training and control loss? | Named firm/task implementation with worker and customer outcomes |
| Algorithmic work | Disclosure and contestability standards | Can a worker understand, challenge and correct an automated decision without losing access to work? | Implementation, appeal, correction and worker-voice records |
| Supply chains | Domestic production, ownership and substitution policy | Does the alternative produce qualified output at acceptable cost and with lower strategic exposure? | Qualification, processing, buyer use, price and disruption evidence |
| Public AI | Procurement, auditability and social dialogue guidance | Can an institution sustain, correct and govern AI after procurement? | Named service, ownership, error correction, access and durable service result |

The shared hypothesis is **capacity is only real when it remains contestable,
qualified and maintainable**. This is a cross-source interpretation, not a
universal law or pooled trend rate.

## What this pass changes in the discovery queue

1. Move from AI adoption counts to named firm/task and worker/customer outcome
   records.
2. Treat algorithmic-management transparency as a route to contestability,
   not as proof that workers can exercise rights.
3. Search critical-mineral routes for qualification and buyer use, not only
   ownership, mine counts or policy announcements.
4. Search public AI deployments for ownership, audit, correction, portability,
   interoperability and service continuity after launch.
5. Keep firm, labor, state and geopolitical sources separate; connect them only
   when their units and clocks are explicitly compatible.

## Decision boundary

This rotation identifies source-supported mechanisms and sharper next tests. It
does not claim that AI raises productivity for a particular firm, that workers
can successfully contest automated decisions, that critical-mineral
substitution is commercially qualified, or that any public AI deployment has
achieved durable service improvement.

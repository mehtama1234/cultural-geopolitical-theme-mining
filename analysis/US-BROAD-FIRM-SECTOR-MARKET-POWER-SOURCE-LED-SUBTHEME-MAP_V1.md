# Firm, sector, and market power: source-led subtheme map v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** 338 registry records carry `firm_sector_market_power`. This map
separates market structure, firm scale, labor dependence, consumer switching,
and access consequences; it does not assume concentration proves harm.

## What the source material supports

> a larger firm is not automatically a dominant firm; an observed wage or
turnout pattern is not a direct coercion record; a market share is not welfare;
> and aggregate capacity can grow while usable local access falls.

## 1. Employer concentration can turn local job dependence into political exposure

The [electoral-monopsony record](records/us-nber-employer-market-power-political-influence-2026.json)
reports that workers at major local employers were more likely to encounter
employer political communication and report influence on voting/career
expectations. Its shift-share design finds greater electoral monopsony power
increased Republican turnout; counterfactual election claims are model based.

**Supported subtheme:** labor-market structure can enter political life through
dependence and expectations, not only wages.  
**Not established:** a linked worker-level chain from message to coercion,
belief change, vote, welfare, or later voice.

## 2. Labor-market power is heterogeneous, and model mechanisms must not be narrated as observed markdowns

The [labor-market-heterogeneity record](records/us-nber-labor-market-power-heterogeneity-2026.json)
models worker sorting and firm wage setting. It attributes 78% of variation in
labor-supply elasticities and 89% of large/small-firm markdown differences to
worker heterogeneity in the model. These are theory/calibration results—not
worker-level wage, harm, or policy-incidence estimates.

**Supported subtheme:** the route from firm size to pay depends on who can move,
which firm they can reach, and the amenities and constraints shaping that
choice.  
**Not established:** a named firm's markdown, wage theft, reduced job quality,
or universal tax-policy implication.

## 3. Consolidation can centralize capacity while thinning local geographic access

The [hospital-consolidation record](records/us-census-hospital-consolidation-geographic-access-rural-south-2026.json)
finds U.S. hospital establishments fell more than 20% from their 1988 peak to
about 5,040 in 2010 while employment rose more than 40% through 2019. In the
decade beginning 2009, 39.2% of rural Southern counties had no study-defined
hospital, compared with 30.1% of rural counties elsewhere. Establishment and
employment data do not observe patient travel time, price, quality, insurance
acceptance, or health outcome.

**Supported subtheme:** aggregate sector capacity and geographically usable
service capacity are distinct.  
**Not established:** that consolidation caused a local access loss, or whether
nearby facilities, transport, telehealth, staffing, and affordability offset it.

## 4. Search-market power includes beliefs and switching friction, not only default shares

The [search-engine experiment record](records/us-nber-search-engine-market-power-field-experiment-2025.json)
randomized about 2,300 U.S. desktop Google users. An active-choice prompt
increased Bing share 1.1 percentage points; paid trial exposure updated quality
beliefs, and 33% preferred to continue. The paper's 15-point market-share and
$6-per-consumer-year counterfactuals are modelled, not observed welfare.

**Supported subtheme:** alternatives may not discipline a platform when people
lack experience, accurate quality beliefs, or low-friction switching.  
**Not established:** durable exit, privacy/security improvement, mobile-user
effects, accessibility, or a general consumer-welfare result.

## 5. Public purchasing rules can reallocate opportunity inside the “small-business” category

The [size-rule record](records/us-small-business-support-size-rule-2026.json)
uses quasi-random timing around expanded program eligibility in administrative
Census data. In the studied setting, eligibility moving toward larger firms
reallocated government contracts to them; affected smaller firms had lower
revenue and wages, more exits, and less patenting—especially younger, more
productive, financially constrained firms.

**Supported subtheme:** a program labeled small-business support can distribute
contracts unevenly within the small-firm population. Eligibility thresholds are
therefore a market-structure decision, not a neutral administrative detail.
**Not established:** contract quality, prices, service quality, worker hours or
security, owner-household wellbeing, or a general result for every procurement
program.

## 5a. A contracting ban can preserve firm survival while narrowing the route into ownership and scale

The [affirmative-action contracting record](records/us-census-affirmative-action-contracting-mwbe-2026.json)
uses restricted Census business data to study states ending affirmative action
in state contracting. The paper reports gradual downsizing among existing
minority- and women-owned business enterprises (MWBEs), with the most
pronounced reductions among Black-owned businesses and larger MWBEs. Existing
MWBEs were no more likely to shut down than other businesses, while new MWBEs
became relatively less common after a state ban. Its productivity and scrap
value interpretation is calibrated-model output, not a directly observed firm
productivity loss.

**Supported subtheme:** policy can change market opportunity through distinct
entry, scale, and survival routes. A stable count of incumbent firms does not
show that the route into contracting or business ownership remained equally
open.

**Not established:** legal merits, contract awards or values, procurement
alternatives, individual discrimination, worker pay/retention, owner-household
security, prices, public-service quality, community prosperity, or the complete
mechanism behind the reported patterns. This is a working paper with no public
abstract denominator or full state/timing ledger.

## 6. A disaster can raise aggregate productivity by selecting firms out—and still reduce local consumer access

The [flood-finance record](records/us-census-flood-disaster-aid-firm-reallocation-productivity-2026.json)
finds that, after declared floods, manufacturing-plant shutdown probability
rose 3.6 percentage points within two years (more than 30% over the pre-flood
baseline), concentrated among the least productive plants. By year six,
surviving plants' productivity was more than 11% above baseline and entrants
were about 14% more productive than pre-flood entrants; bank loans rose about
3.1%, including evidence of young/small-firm private-credit crowd-in. In the
study's severe non-declared-flood comparison, aggregate manufacturing TFP fell
about 0.73% and exits rose without the same productivity selection. That is a
declared-disaster reallocation result, not recovery for each plant, worker, or
resident. The distinct
[Harvey turnover record](records/us-harvey-firm-turnover-consumer-welfare-2024.json)
follows retail consequences: roughly 1% of observed firms permanently exited,
another 3% temporarily closed at least two months, and major store closures
were associated with 15% greater customer travel distance. Its consumer-surplus
loss estimates are modeled and highly place-sensitive.

**Supported subtheme:** “productive recovery” and usable neighborhood recovery
can move in different directions because they use different units—surviving
plants, entrants, and credit versus residents' reachable stores.
**Not established:** that disaster finance caused household recovery, that each
closure was inefficient, effects for cash users or people with limited
mobility, worker continuity, a general result for repeat/coastal floods, or a
nationwide disaster rule.

## 6a. Federal disaster spending can change the financing and reallocation route without proving a shared recovery

The [flood spending and firm-reallocation record](records/us-census-flood-disaster-creative-destruction-government-spending-2026.json)
uses plant-level Census microdata around federally declared floods. It reports
that exits were concentrated among less productive plants, used machinery from
those exits moved to higher-productivity entrants, and surviving plants
upgraded machinery as they rebuilt. The working paper attributes part of this
reallocation to federal disaster spending expanding financing access for nimble
young and small firms, and reports a study-specific fiscal result in which
income gains generated tax revenue exceeding the upfront policy cost.

**Supported subtheme:** disaster spending, financing access, plant exit,
capital reallocation, survivor investment, productivity, and modeled fiscal
return can form a linked firm-system route. It is not equivalent to a claim
that the affected place recovered equitably.  
**Not established:** worker retention, wages, bankruptcy burden, household
recovery, local service continuity, displacement, market concentration, an
equal financing route, or that each closure was socially beneficial. The fiscal
interpretation is study-specific, and the source is a working paper.

## 6b. Emergency recovery loans can preserve firm survival and credit without proving worker or household recovery

The [causal small-business disaster-liquidity record](records/us-small-business-disaster-liquidity-causal-2024.json)
links more than 167,000 U.S. firm loan applications across about 1,900
disasters to Census and business-credit records. In the September 2026 working
paper's IV estimates, all firms receiving the recovery-loan treatment had a
13.2-percentage-point lower exit rate and 0.170 higher log employment; employer
firms had a 16.9-point lower exit rate, 0.376 higher log employment, and 0.719
higher log revenue. Linked credit outcomes include a lower delinquent share of
debt, about 35 fewer late-payment days, lower bankruptcy, and more private
credit. The study reports no detected capital reallocation away from neighboring
firms and some positive local-entry evidence.

**Supported subtheme:** disaster liquidity can reach a causal firm-level route
from public loan access through survival, employment, revenue, delinquency,
and private-credit capacity. Firm continuity is a stronger endpoint than a
loan award or application count, but it remains a firm-system result.  
**Not established:** worker pay, job quality or retention, owner-household
security, customer price/access, loan burden, equal loan access, community
service continuity, trust, or political meaning. Outcome universes differ by
table; the all-firm revenue estimate is not statistically starred, and the
working paper does not provide the underlying identifiers or loan terms.

## 7. Household assets can finance an employer—and transmit housing shocks into local jobs

The [housing-collateral record](records/us-census-house-prices-firm-performance-credit-collateral-2008-2009.json)
links confidential business, housing, and banking data during the Great
Recession. It reports large persistent local house-price effects on small-firm
employment, disproportionate young-firm exposure, and evidence consistent with
both bank-credit and owner-home-equity channels.

**Supported subtheme:** the owner household and the firm are sometimes the same
risk-bearing system: a property-price shock can narrow collateral and credit
before it shows up as firm employment.
**Not established:** that house prices caused every firm outcome, which channel
operated for an individual firm, worker pay or job retention, household
recovery, or consumer-service effects.

## 7a. Housing wealth can fund a different type of business entry from the entry associated with rising local prices

The [home-equity entrepreneurship record](records/us-nber-home-equity-entrepreneurship-duration-sectors-2015.json)
separates a broad house-price association from a collateral mechanism using the
1998 Texas reform that first permitted home-equity lending. House-price growth
was strongly correlated with local entrepreneurship, but much of the associated
entry was in construction and real estate and tended to be small and
short-lived. The reform-identified home-equity collateral channel instead
produced longer-lived and more sector-balanced entrepreneurship. The paper
estimates that this collateral route represented a tenth or less of
house-price-associated entry and was driven by a small group of homeowners
constrained without price growth.

**Supported subtheme:** a housing asset can serve as entrepreneurial collateral,
but raw entry counts conceal whether an entry route is short-lived
price-associated activity or a more durable, collateral-enabled business. The
owner household's housing exposure remains part of the firm-finance route.

**Not established:** individual borrowing, profitability, jobs/wages, customer
benefit, owner residence security, community capacity, the value of short-lived
entry, or transferability beyond the Texas reform and the study period.

## 7b. Public-pension funding pressure can reach firms through private-equity matching without proving public or worker benefit

The [public-pension/private-equity productivity record](records/us-census-public-pension-private-equity-productivity-2026.json)
links U.S. public-pension investments, private-equity funds, target firms, and
confidential Census firm data. The working paper reports that more severely
underfunded pensions receive lower average private-equity returns and match
with smaller general partners on average. Firms financed by the most
underfunded pensions and smallest funds show labor-productivity decreases. The
study uses public unionization as an instrument related to pension underfunding
positions, while the public abstract omits the full sample and estimate ledger.

**Supported subtheme:** public-pension funding position → fund matching and
return surface → financed-firm labor productivity. Pension governance and firm
outcomes can be connected through an investment route even when no worker,
beneficiary, or public-budget outcome is linked.
**Not established:** pension solvency, benefit adequacy, beneficiary security,
firm survival, employment, wages, work conditions, local incidence, a causal
effect of any individual fund, or whether an alternative investment policy
would improve public outcomes. Fund selection, risk tolerance, sector,
governance, and unobserved fund quality may explain part of the pattern.

## 8. Legal and administrative architecture changes survival without settling whose interests were served

The [bankruptcy-venue record](records/us-census-bankruptcy-forum-shopping-delaware-employment-1990-2021.json)
constructs 155,059 Chapter 11 cases and uses a Delaware-distance design for
nearby firms. For firms whose venue choice responded to that distance, Delaware
filing is associated with fewer job losses per establishment, lower conversion
to Chapter 7, lower closure, shorter cases, and more positive
unsecured-creditor recovery. The effect is local to that design, not a blanket
endorsement of forum shopping.

**Supported subtheme:** legal venue is an economic allocation mechanism: it can
alter which firms continue operating and retain establishments, not merely
where a case is heard.
**Not established:** individual workers' receipt or wellbeing, distribution
across creditors, customer prices and continuity, local tax effects, or social
optimality of Delaware filing.

## 8a. Assigning environmental cleanup risk to lenders can change firm pollution and credit terms together

The [environmental lender-liability record](records/us-census-environmental-lender-liability-pollution-1992-1999.json)
uses a difference-in-differences design around the 1996 federal Lender
Liability Act, comparing establishments inside the Eleventh Circuit—where a
capacity-to-control standard had expanded lender liability—with establishments
elsewhere. After liability narrowed, treated establishments increased on-site
CERCLA pollution about 15% on the intensive margin and 2% on the extensive
margin, reduced process-related abatement about 14.7%, and were about 25% more
likely to experience an unexpected environmental release relative to baseline.
For affected firms, secured-debt maturity increased about 17%; the paper's
mechanism evidence ties higher lender responsibility to shorter maturity and
less favorable pricing that can support monitoring. The air-release comparison
was near zero and statistically insignificant, consistent with the design's
CERCLA-specific mechanism boundary.

**Supported subtheme:** legal allocation of environmental risk can change a
lender-monitoring and firm-practice route. Credit terms, abatement, onsite
contamination, and release risk can move together, while small reported
production/employment distortions do not decide community wellbeing.
**Not established:** all pollution, current finance practice, household
exposure, local health, cleanup completion, worker wellbeing, or a universal
financial-governance effect. The quasi-experiment is limited to secured lending
and CERCLA contamination in 1990s establishments; establishment records do
not follow the same residents or workers.

## 9. Merchant payment infrastructure can make firm fees a household-distribution question

The [payment-rewards record](records/us-nber-payment-rewards-redistribution-2026.json)
observes settlement data for about one million merchants and reports variation
in fees by merchant, sector, size, and contract. Its incidence framework then
models an annual transfer from cash/debit users to credit-card users through
merchant prices and rewards; the dollar estimates are model outputs, not
observed household bills.

**Supported subtheme:** payment-market power operates through merchant
acceptance costs and common prices, so a firm-fee question can become a
distributional consumer question even when customers never see the fee.
**Not established:** pass-through for a particular merchant, a given
household's loss or gain, welfare after fraud/convenience/credit services, or
political response.

## 10. Recovery contracting can be local in principle while locally invisible in practice

The [local-vendor record](records/us-gao-disaster-local-vendor-use-2026.json)
reviews 40 selected FEMA and Army Corps contracts after three disasters. Agency
data identified only 3% of $2.9 billion in FEMA obligations as local-vendor
contracts, while GAO found local-area identification errors and missing Corps
documentation. The sample is not a national award rate and cannot tell whether
local firms lacked capacity or received subcontract work.

**Supported subtheme:** place-based recovery has a measurement and allocation
gate before it becomes a local-business benefit. “Money entered the disaster
area” is not evidence that locally rooted firms, workers, or households
received it.
**Not established:** local jobs, wages, survival, subcontracting, project
quality, household recovery, or agency wrongdoing.

## 11. A sector wage floor can have modest aggregate employment effects while concentrating business adjustment

The [California fast-food wage record](records/us-nber-california-fast-food-wage-small-chain-entry-exit-2026.json)
finds negative but modest overall employment effects following the April 2024
$20 fast-food wage floor. In its establishment-level analysis, entry slowed and
exit increased among small chains. The public abstract does not disclose effect
sizes, establishment counts, worker earnings/hours, customer prices, product
quality, or the same workers' later jobs.

**Supported subtheme:** aggregate employment and firm-demographic adjustment
can move differently; sector rules can concentrate entry/exit pressure within
the business population.  
**Not established:** the total policy effect, a wage–exit mechanism, worker
security, consumer price, service quality, productivity, or household welfare.

## 12. Platform vertical integration can improve short-run choice while long-run seller structure remains open

The [Amazon private-label record](records/us-nber-amazon-vertical-integration-consumer-choice-2025.json)
randomized hiding platform-owned brands in selected online-retail categories.
Shoppers chose observably similar products without more search effort or visits
to other retail sites. Its calibrated counterfactual estimates a 5.4% average
short-run consumer-surplus reduction when private labels are removed, with
larger effects in some categories; that is model output, not a long-run market
outcome.

**Supported subtheme:** platform-owned products can offer current consumer value
even as questions about ranking, seller entry, data use, future choice, and
dependence remain separate.  
**Not established:** self-preferencing, fair ranking, seller exit, future
price/quality, durable competition, or broad consumer welfare.

## 13. Rural firm innovation reflects missing complements more than location alone in the study decomposition

The [rural innovation record](records/us-census-rural-firm-innovation-endowments-2026.json)
finds rural firms reported less innovation than urban firms in the 2018 Annual
Business Survey. Its Oaxaca-Blinder decomposition attributes most of the gap to
digital infrastructure, R&D, and human-capital deficits rather than rural
location itself. This is a Census working paper; the public summary does not
give all measures, effect sizes, or causal treatment estimates.

**Supported subtheme:** place-based firm performance can depend on a bundle of
digital, knowledge, and human-capital complements rather than a rural label by
itself.  
**Not established:** that changing one complement causes innovation, or that
innovation becomes survival, jobs, wages, regional convergence, or community
wellbeing.

## 14. Founders can shape the composition of opportunity long after a firm opens

The [founder-background record](records/us-census-lehd-founder-background-hiring-homophily-2026.json)
uses LEHD data for roughly 825,000 new firms. With industry, metro, and cohort
controls, female-founded firms had a 36.4-point higher female employee share;
the corresponding differences were 51.2 points for Black employees, 37.3 for
Hispanic employees, and 11.3 for non-college employees. The differences were
visible across firm age and persisted to at least 20 years in the unbalanced
analysis; wage decompositions report relative effects for shared founder-worker
characteristics. Founder identity is proxied by top earners and the samples are
selected.

**Supported subtheme:** entrepreneurship and founder networks can structure who
gets hired into a new firm and how opportunity composition persists, even after
controlling for key local and industry context.  
**Not established:** discrimination, worker preference, workplace quality,
national rates, entrepreneurship access effects, or later household wellbeing.

## 14a. Mixed founding teams can create a firm-level complementarity without showing that gains are broadly shared

The [native-immigrant founding-team record](records/us-nber-native-immigrant-entrepreneurial-synergies-2025.json)
links U.S. startup, founder, employee, education, employment, funding, and
exit records. It reports that mixed native-immigrant teams outperform both
native-only and immigrant-only teams in employment three years after founding,
funding access and round size, and successful exits. An instrumental-variables
robustness check based on native shares in university-degree programs retains
the reported larger-team and funding patterns. The paper associates the
complementarity with diverse labor pools, venture-capital access, and broader
product markets, but does not separately identify those mechanisms.

**Supported subtheme:** founder composition can be a firm-level network and
resource channel, connecting team formation to later hiring, capital access,
and exit outcomes. Native/immigrant status, team mix, funding, employment, and
exit remain distinct evidence stages rather than a single entrepreneurship
effect.

**Not established:** a universal immigration or startup effect, the causal
weight of each proposed mechanism, worker bargaining power or wages, consumer
benefit, local community spillovers, equal access to legal/capital networks,
or performance for every sector and place. The source is a working paper and
does not disclose its complete linked sample or outcome definitions in the
public abstract.

## 14b. A property right can raise innovation and local profit while shifting cost and production pressure downstream

The [agricultural patent-rights record](records/us-nber-patent-rights-agricultural-innovation-local-profits-2026.json)
studies the 1985 introduction of plant-biotechnology patent protection, using
crop reproductive structure for differential exposure and crop/county outcome
data. The paper reports increased development of novel plant varieties and
private R&D, positive spillovers to some non-biological agricultural
technologies, higher crop yields, and higher agricultural land values and
profits in more-exposed U.S. counties. It also reports increased seed spending
and a possibility that patent rights impose technology-consumer costs and
distort downstream production.

**Supported subtheme:** intellectual-property design can create a linked
innovation, producer-return, yield, input-cost, and downstream-production
route. Higher yield or county profit is not interchangeable with bargaining
power or net welfare for the farm, tenant, worker, or food consumer.
**Not established:** farm-level adoption, farmer bargaining, food prices,
consumer welfare, labor, environmental outcomes, or household food security.
The public abstract does not disclose every crop, county, estimate, or
identification detail; higher seed cost can reflect valuable technology as well
as a shifted burden.

## Cross-subtheme finding and next test

```text
market structure, eligibility rule, wage floor, platform integration, disaster,
property shock, legal venue, or payment rail
  -> firm financing, survival, contract allocation, service location,
     merchant pricing, or local choice set
  -> distinct worker, owner, customer, creditor, and place outcomes
  -> institutional response, recovery route, market adaptation, or exit
```

The material establishes more than abstract “business impact”: it shows
allocation rules, legal design, property collateral, public finance, and
payment infrastructure changing measurable firm-side outcomes. The unresolved
join is distribution and durability: who received the retained job, contract,
service, lower price, payment benefit, or recovery—and what happened after.
Structure alone is not an end-to-end consequence.

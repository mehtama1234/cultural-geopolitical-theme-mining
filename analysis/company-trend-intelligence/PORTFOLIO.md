## Cross-domain pattern

The five records point to one recurring company problem:

> Capability and institutional output are becoming easier to produce and report,
> but the conversion into usable benefit, trust, control, and durable value is
> still the difficult middle.

The repeated conversion gap is:

```text
capability or formal offer
  -> access, governance, quality, and contestability
  -> repeated use and institutional workflow
  -> protected outcome or reliable service
  -> trust, retention, legitimacy, and practical alternatives
  -> durable business and societal value
```

This is not a claim that the five domains have one causal driver. It is a
cross-source operating lens: each record identifies a different place where
the conversion can stop.

## The five recurring business questions

### 1. Who controls the system?

Who sets the task, rule, data, price, model, route, standard, or response label?
Who can correct it, and who must accept it?

### 2. Who has a real alternative?

Can a worker refuse or switch? Can a customer leave? Can a community contest a
project? Can a language user use another tool? Can a firm replace a supplier,
route, platform, or infrastructure provider?

### 3. Does the formal output reach the user?

An AI pilot, complaint response, public service, audio archive, power project,
or security operation is not yet a user outcome. The record must identify the
last observable stage and the missing join.

### 4. Who carries the residual burden?

Track money, time, waiting, error correction, privacy, training, land, energy,
security, reputation, and political risk. A system can look resilient while
moving hidden work or risk to workers, households, communities, or public
institutions.

### 5. What would sustain trust?

Trust depends on explanation, voice, correction, fairness, cultural authority,
visible benefit, and the ability to exit or recover. It should be measured after
the encounter, not inferred from adoption, favorable opinion, or continued use.

## Portfolio monitoring dashboard

The next implementation should track these cross-record indicators:

| Indicator | Portfolio meaning |
|---|---|
| Conversion rate from formal output to completed user task | Whether capability reaches a real person, customer, worker, or community |
| Correction and appeal closure | Whether people can contest and repair system decisions |
| Repeat use after first encounter | Whether value persists beyond launch, announcement, or emergency |
| Practical alternatives and switching cost | Whether continued use represents choice or dependence |
| Residual burden | Who absorbs money, time, error, privacy, land, safety, or legitimacy cost |
| Trust after outcome | Whether explanation and remedy produce durable acceptance |
| Recovery and reversibility | Whether the company can restore service, undo harm, or change provider |

## Promotion rule

A company-facing trend should be promoted only when it has:

1. at least one direct or measured observation;
2. a stated mechanism and affected groups;
3. a counterexample or competing explanation;
4. a business exposure that is labeled as evidence or hypothesis;
5. leading indicators with an owner and cadence; and
6. a next test that could materially change the conclusion.

The portfolio should not be used to rank companies or predict returns until
company-specific exposure, timing, and outcome data are added.

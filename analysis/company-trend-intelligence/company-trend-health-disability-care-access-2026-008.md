# Health access and care burden create operating constraints before continuity is visible

**Trend ID:** `company-trend-2026-008`
**Status:** qualified trend
**Detected:** 2026-10-31
**Observation window:** 2021-2024, with disability prevalence context from 2022
**Geography:** United States
**Themes:** health, disability, aging, unequal care access; time and care; unequal exposure; public systems
**Audience or decision owner:** people, customer access, operations, benefits, safety, and service leaders

## One-sentence finding

> Disability, time, appointment, transportation, and caregiving barriers can
> prevent people from completing needed care or work-related tasks before a
> company sees a clear absence, churn, or service-failure signal.

## Signal

- **Initial signal:** Official surveys separately measure disability, nonfinancial
  access barriers, transportation, and caregiving burden.
- **Signal sources:** CDC disability and health-care access analysis, NCHS NHIS
  2022, AHRQ MEPS 2021, and CDC caregiver measurement.
- **Repetition check:** The same access problem appears through different units:
  disability type, time and appointment availability, transportation, health
  condition, and unpaid care responsibility.

## Evidence

| Source | Unit and geography | Direct observation | Evidence status | Method or limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDC disability access | U.S. adults; BRFSS disability/access measures | Disability prevalence and disability-specific differences in access measures | official record | Different years and no company pathway |
| NCHS NHIS 2022 | U.S. civilian noninstitutionalized adults | 21.7% delayed or missed care because of at least one nonfinancial barrier | measured study result | Self-report; categories not mutually exclusive |
| AHRQ MEPS 2021 | U.S. adults | About 6% reported unreliable transportation blocking care, work, meetings, or daily needs | measured study result | Combined daily-life outcome; no firm or provider link |
| CDC caregiver module | Adult BRFSS respondents in participating areas | Caregiver status, duration, and hours can be measured repeatedly | official record | Module coverage varies; no linked employer outcome |

**What the evidence supports:** Access and care burden are distributed across
disability, time, availability, transport, health condition, and unpaid care.

**What it does not support:** It does not estimate a particular company’s
absence, retention, service completion, or return on investment.

## Mechanism

```text
health, disability, or care need
  -> time, appointment, transport, accessibility, or coordination barrier
  -> delay, nonreceipt, absence, incomplete task, or unpaid care burden
  -> hidden cost for person, household, employer, provider, or customer
  -> visible operating outcome only after failure accumulates
```

The missing middle is whether a company can observe and reduce the barrier
without shifting work or risk to another affected group.

## Distribution and affected groups

- **More exposed:** People with disabilities, poor health, multiple conditions,
  limited transport, inflexible schedules, caregiving responsibilities, or
  fewer accessible alternatives.
- **Less exposed or protected:** People with flexible time, reliable transport,
  accessible providers, paid care, and a usable alternative channel.
- **Who absorbs cost:** People and carers may absorb waiting, travel, unpaid
  coordination, missed work, lost care, and repeated contact.
- **Practical access question:** Can the person complete the needed task or get
  an accommodation without disclosing more than necessary or losing service?

## Cross-domain interpretation

- **Cultural:** Disability, aging, and caregiving are interpreted through dignity,
  belonging, autonomy, and whether institutions make room for real lives.
- **Financial:** Time, transport, unpaid care, absence, and delayed treatment
  redistribute cost before a company sees a simple budget line.
- **Institutional:** Accessibility, benefits, scheduling, public capacity, and
  remedy rules determine whether formal inclusion becomes usable access.
- **Geopolitical:** Health and care capacity, workforce mobility, and provider
  dependence can change resilience and operating continuity across places.

## Time horizon

- **Now (0–12 months):** Measure access barriers, accommodation requests,
  repeat contact, absence, schedule flexibility, and completed tasks.
- **Next (1–3 years):** Link an accommodation or access intervention to care,
  work, customer, or service outcomes with a comparison group.
- **Longer term (3+ years):** Build operating models that treat accessibility,
  care, and continuity as measurable capability rather than exception handling.

## Counterevidence and limits

- A reported barrier may not prevent completion; people may compensate through
  family, paid care, flexible work, or another channel.
- Survey measures use different years, populations, and definitions and cannot
  be joined into one person-level estimate.
- Older adults can report stronger usual-care access while still facing time,
  transport, disability, or caregiving burdens.
- A company intervention may improve access for one group while shifting work or
  disclosure burden to another.
- A named workflow with a baseline, subgroup denominator, and measured outcome
  could materially strengthen or weaken this finding.

## Decision options

- **Monitor:** Access, accommodation, completion, repeat effort, absence, and
  subgroup differences.
- **Prepare:** Establish an accessibility and care-burden ledger for one named
  workflow, with privacy and disclosure limits.
- **Act:** Test a reversible schedule, access, or service-design change with a
  comparison and affected-user review.
- **Do not claim yet:** Do not call a population access estimate a company
  productivity, retention, or ROI result.

# Company trend-intelligence decision playbook

This playbook turns the trend board into a repeatable management rhythm.

## Weekly: scan and triage

Use the board and new source intake to identify signals that could change a
customer, workforce, product, financial, operating, institutional, cultural,
or geopolitical assumption.

For each signal, record:

- the source and date;
- what changed or was reported;
- the population, place, and unit;
- whether the signal is new, repeated, contested, or reversing;
- the likely decision owner; and
- the smallest evidence check needed next.

Do not call a signal a trend from one article, one vendor claim, or one high-
engagement discussion.

## Monthly: qualify and translate

For candidates that survive the first scan:

1. Add an independent source family or counter-signal.
2. Separate observation, study result, mechanism, distribution, and implication.
3. Identify who gains room, who loses room, who must wait, and who controls the
   decision.
4. Classify the trend status and confidence.
5. Translate it into the eight business exposure dimensions:
   customer, workforce, product, finance, operations, institutional,
   reputation, and geopolitical.
6. Assign leading indicators, an owner, a cadence, and a trigger.
7. State what would change the conclusion.

The output is a qualified trend record, not an automatic recommendation.

## Quarterly: decide and re-test

For qualified or confirmed trends, the decision owner should choose one or more
of four actions:

- **Monitor:** maintain the indicator and review cadence;
- **Prepare:** make a reversible investment, contingency, or governance change;
- **Pilot:** test a bounded product, process, or market response with a defined
  counterfactual or comparison;
- **Act:** commit resources because evidence and exposure justify a decision.

Every action should have a stop, rollback, or review condition. A company should
not scale a response merely because a trend is dramatic; it should scale when
the exposure, timing, alternatives, and evidence justify the cost.

## Evidence ladder

Use the following promotion ladder:

| Level | Meaning | Appropriate management language |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | One observation or early public concern | “We are watching…” |
| Repeated pattern | Similar observation across independent sources or time | “The pattern is recurring…” |
| Qualified trend | Mechanism and distribution are supported, but outcomes remain open | “The evidence suggests…” |
| Confirmed trend | Multiple source families and a meaningful outcome support the pattern | “The evidence shows…” |
| Structural shift | The pattern persists across time, places, or institutions and changes operating conditions | “We should plan around…” |
| Reversing or contested | New evidence weakens, reverses, or divides the interpretation | “The finding is changing or disputed…” |

Do not use “confirmed” to mean “important,” and do not use “structural” to mean
“interesting.”

## Decision memo format

Each executive briefing should fit on one page before linking to the evidence:

1. **Finding:** one sentence in ordinary language.
2. **Why now:** dated change or new evidence.
3. **Who is exposed:** affected groups, places, and alternatives.
4. **Mechanism:** how the change could reach behavior or business outcomes.
5. **Business exposure:** the relevant dimensions and confidence.
6. **Leading indicators:** what to watch and when.
7. **Action:** monitor, prepare, pilot, or act.
8. **Limit:** what remains unproven.
9. **Next test:** the smallest evidence that could change the decision.

## Red flags

Pause promotion or escalation when:

- a vendor or organization is the only source for a large outcome claim;
- adoption, downloads, revenue, capacity, or engagement are treated as user
  benefit without a completion or outcome measure;
- different datasets are written as if they follow the same person, customer,
  worker, firm, place, or event without identifiers;
- a complaint, survey attitude, or social-media signal is treated as a
  population rate or causal effect;
- a geopolitical operation is called deterrence without a later behavior
  measure;
- continued use is treated as loyalty when switching costs or dependence may
  explain it; or
- cultural representation is treated as cultural vitality without community
  authority, repeated use, and transmission evidence.

## Ownership model

- **Research/insight:** source, method, limits, counterevidence, and record
  quality.
- **Business owner:** exposure, decision relevance, and action choice.
- **Data owner:** indicator definition, refresh, denominator, and access.
- **Risk/legal/public affairs:** governance, rights, regulation, stakeholder
  burden, and geopolitical implications.
- **Executive sponsor:** accepts uncertainty, selects action, and funds the next
  test.

The research team should not silently convert an open question into a business
recommendation. The business owner should not silently convert a scenario into
a measured result.

# Geopolitical capability creates a permission gap before it creates leverage

**Trend ID:** `company-trend-2026-003`
**Status:** qualified trend
**Detected:** 2026-09-29
**Observation window:** 2026
**Geography:** United States, NATO, Eastern Europe, and Kosovo comparison
**Themes:** geopolitical and state consequences; infrastructure and dependency; public systems; trust and legitimacy
**Audience or decision owner:** enterprise risk, supply chain, public affairs, security, and strategy leaders

## One-sentence finding

> State capability can become operational before it becomes durable leverage:
> institutions may respond and allies may coordinate while external behavior,
> local burden, public permission, and deterrence remain unsettled.

## Signal

- **Initial signal:** Named airspace incidents, alliance responses, local
  security integration, and public-legitimacy surveys show different stages of
  the same geopolitical problem.
- **Signal sources:** NATO/Lithuania operational records, NATO airspace-response
  records, KFOR local-security integration, and Pew NATO legitimacy data.
- **Repetition check:** The capability-to-response pattern appears across an
  operational incident, repeated alliance response, local institutional
  coordination, and public-attitude evidence.

## Evidence

| Source | Unit and geography | Direct observation | Evidence status | Method or limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lithuanian NATO interception | Named airspace incident | NATO aircraft identified, intercepted, and destroyed a drone that entered Lithuanian airspace | official record | Origin, intent, deterrence, cost, and civilian-risk reduction remain open |
| NATO airspace response | Incident and alliance response | NATO documented solidarity, attribution, ISR operations, and strengthened air defence | official record | Does not show changed adversary behavior or complete burden-sharing |
| KFOR local-security integration | Mission and local institutions | KFOR posture adjustment connected Kosovo Police, EULEX, Kosovo institutions, and Serbian Orthodox Church representatives | official record | Coordination is not consent, safety, or durable legitimacy |
| Pew NATO legitimacy | 45,658 adults in 37 countries | Favorability is broad but varies by country and U.S. partisan identity | measured study result | General institution favorability does not measure support for a specific operation or cost |

**What the evidence supports:** Geopolitical capability, operational response,
institutional coordination, and public legitimacy are distinct stages. A state
or alliance can advance one stage without closing the next.

**What it does not support:** The sources do not prove deterrence, changed
adversary behavior, civilian safety, willingness to bear a specific cost, or
durable leverage.

## Mechanism

```text
incident or accepted capability
  -> operation and attribution
  -> ally, adversary, partner, or local response
  -> cooperation, refusal, adaptation, escalation, or reversal
  -> public/host burden and permission
  -> sustained operation, deterrence, legitimacy, or leverage
```

The operational and formal-response stages are observed. Later behavior,
burden, permission, and leverage remain partly open.

## Distribution and affected groups

- **First affected:** States, local communities, alliance personnel, firms with
  critical infrastructure, and populations exposed to disruption or risk.
- **More exposed:** Border and host communities, dependent supply chains,
  businesses with concentrated routes, and organizations reliant on a single
  alliance or infrastructure provider.
- **Who absorbs cost:** Local communities may absorb disruption and risk;
  governments and firms absorb readiness, insurance, compliance, and continuity
  costs.
- **Who has alternatives:** Firms with geographic, supplier, infrastructure,
  and diplomatic redundancy have more room to adapt.

## Counterevidence and limits

- A visible response may prevent harm without producing a measurable public
  outcome; non-events are difficult to attribute.
- Alliance favorability does not equal approval of a specific mission,
  deployment, cost, or risk.
- Local coordination does not prove local consent or improved civilian safety.
- Attribution can be contested, and public narratives can change before facts
  are resolved.
- Operational records are not a complete measure of private firm exposure.

## Decision options

- **Monitor:** Incidents, attribution, response cohesion, host permission,
  supplier alternatives, recovery time, and customer effects.
- **Prepare:** Build geographic, supplier, infrastructure, insurance, and
  communication redundancy before a crisis closes options.
- **Act:** Test continuity plans against named incidents and disclose which
  dependencies remain non-replaceable.
- **Do not claim yet:** Do not call an operational response deterrence,
  legitimacy, or durable geopolitical leverage without a later behavioral and
  permission record.

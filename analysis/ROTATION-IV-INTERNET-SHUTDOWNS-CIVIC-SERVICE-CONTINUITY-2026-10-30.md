# Rotation IV — Internet shutdowns, civic space and service continuity

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When authorities intentionally disrupt internet access, throttle bandwidth, block platforms or restrict VPNs, what happens to civic information, elections, journalism, health, education, work, commerce and humanitarian access—and do law, notice, oversight and redress meaningfully protect affected people?

## Why this packet matters

Connectivity is now a delivery layer for public information, learning, health, finance, work, commerce, safety and political participation. A shutdown is therefore not only a technical outage. Its effects depend on duration, geography, affected networks, language, device access, dependence on online services and the availability of safe alternatives.

```text
political crisis / election / conflict / security order
  -> shutdown, throttle, platform block or VPN restriction
  -> loss of information, communication, service and income routes
  -> coping, substitution, misinformation, delay or exposure
  -> civic participation, health, education, work, household and humanitarian effects
  -> legal challenge, restoration, compensation, trust and future behavior
```

## Evidence ledger

| ID | Named evidence | What it closes | What it does not close |
|---|---|---|---|
| IV1 | UNESCO reported in January 2026 at least 300 internet shutdowns in over 54 countries during the previous two years and linked disruptions to access to information, education, association and social, cultural and political participation. | Current global trend and rights/service mechanism. | Incident-level duration, affected users, household cost, health outcome, causal attribution or remedy. |
| IV2 | OHCHR guidance requires any shutdown to have clear law, legitimate aim, necessity, proportionality, independent prior authorization, public notice and meaningful redress; it includes throttling, platform restrictions and VPN blocking in the scope. | Legal and procedural safeguards. | Whether governments comply, court access, restoration, compensation, retaliation or actual user protection. |
| IV3 | UN reporting on digital rights states election-related shutdowns rose from five in 2023 to twelve in 2024 and that shutdowns disrupt electoral information, news media and opposition communications. | Election and information-integrity pathway. | Turnout, vote choice, result integrity, local variation, substitution or later institutional trust. |
| IV4 | OHCHR reporting on Myanmar describes a nationwide shutdown after the 2021 coup, extended platform restrictions and a near-absolute shuttering of civic space, with impacts on expression, assembly and vulnerable communities. | Named conflict/coup case and civic-space mechanism. | Household-level health, education, income, safety, humanitarian receipt or durable political effect. |
| IV5 | OHCHR reports that a 2019 shutdown in Rakhine and Chin States deprived vulnerable communities of timely public-health and humanitarian information during conflict and the COVID-19 pandemic, and says the stated security aim did not appear to improve civilian security. | Named regional health/humanitarian access case and counterexample to security rationale. | Comprehensive outcome measurement, alternative channels, later remedy or recurrence. |
| IV6 | UN human-rights materials cite India’s prolonged Jammu and Kashmir mobile-internet restrictions and concerns about website blocking without court authorization, while recommending compliance with freedom-of-expression standards. | Named legal and minority-region restriction case. | Same-person education, health, work, commerce, safety or political outcomes. |
| IV7 | OHCHR’s 2025 Tanzania communication records allegations of an internet shutdown after the October 2025 elections alongside violence, arrests, intimidation and journalist harassment. | Named election-related allegation and rights-monitoring pathway. | Final fact-finding, legal outcome, causal effect, restoration or remedy. |
| IV8 | UNESCO emphasizes that disruptions affect not only users but journalists, media operations and the chain of verified public information, potentially increasing unverified and harmful information. | Information-ecosystem and media mechanism. | Belief, sharing, harm, correction, local-language access, journalist safety or long-term trust. |

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Shutdowns are unevenly experienced. A national blanket order can have different effects in a capital, rural borderland, conflict zone, refugee settlement or minority-language region. Blocking a platform may be less visible than cutting all connectivity but still remove a community’s primary channel for language, mutual aid, news, remittances or organizing. Conversely, users may substitute radio, SMS, mesh networks, satellite links or in-person networks, though these substitutes vary in reach and safety.

The political meaning of a shutdown is also contested. Authorities may invoke security, elections or misinformation; affected communities may experience it as collective punishment, information denial or a barrier to documenting abuses. The packet therefore records official/legal standards and attributed monitoring findings without assuming every reported disruption has the same cause or downstream effect.

## Counterexample and source boundary

An outage record, shutdown order, legal standard, platform block, election allegation, public statement or restoration announcement is not by itself a measured household loss, health harm, educational interruption, electoral effect, humanitarian failure, misinformation increase, compensation or durable trust change. A shutdown may be bypassed by some users and devastating for others; a restoration may return connectivity without restoring lost income, safety or evidence.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 bounded finding:** A shutdown order, network measurement, platform block, bandwidth restriction, VPN ban, election warning, legal safeguard or restoration announcement is not by itself civic exclusion, household harm, service failure, electoral distortion, humanitarian loss or durable distrust.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** The next high-value conversion is **internet-disruption-to-civic-service-household-and-humanitarian-continuity**. The strongest next acquisition is a named place/cohort/incident chain linking order, network, duration, geography, language, device, service, information, substitution, income, health, education, safety, political participation, complaint, court review, restoration and recovery.

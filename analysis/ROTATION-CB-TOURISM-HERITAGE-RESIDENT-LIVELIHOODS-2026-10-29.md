# Rotation CB — Tourism growth, heritage and resident livelihood

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** tourism / overtourism / heritage / resident quality of life / local value capture / housing and infrastructure / biodiversity / cultural commodification  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 visitor-growth-to-resident-wellbeing question

## Research question

When destinations attract visitors through heritage designation, destination
branding, infrastructure, conservation and investment, what converts visitor
growth into locally retained income, resident quality of life, cultural
continuity, affordable housing, infrastructure capacity and ecological care
without displacement or commodification?

## Conversion chain

```text
heritage/nature/landscape and visitor demand
  -> destination branding, access, accommodation, investment and visitor flow
  -> local ownership, jobs, procurement, governance, carrying-capacity management and resident voice
  -> income, services, housing, cultural practice, infrastructure and ecosystem condition
  -> resident acceptance, dignity, belonging, conservation and durable destination legitimacy
  -> resilient, community-benefiting tourism
```

## Evidence snapshot

| Source | Direct observation | Stage closed | Stage still open |
|---|---|---|---|
| World Bank tourism evidence | Tourism supports jobs, local suppliers, rural/coastal communities and conservation when managed well; community-based programs can connect visitor spending to villages and local enterprises. | macroeconomic and local-value mechanism | who captures value, job quality, housing, resident acceptance and long-term ecosystem/cultural result |
| OECD tourism-resilience evidence | Growing visitor numbers can increase living costs, strain housing and services, and resident sentiment is increasingly important to tourism policy. | resident-pressure mechanism and governance signal | named destination rent, service capacity, displacement, benefit distribution and resident decision power |
| UN Tourism Best Tourism Villages 2025 | 52 communities were recognized for safeguarding heritage/natural resources and creating economic opportunities through tourism. | destination model and intended community/heritage mechanism | independent income retention, resident wellbeing, cultural change, carrying capacity and distribution |
| UNESCO Agra living-heritage tourism example | Community interpreters, artisans and local residents can link heritage conservation, livelihoods and visitor experience. | named community-led mechanism and cultural mediation | scale, income distribution, housing/traffic pressure, resident consent and practice continuity |
| UNEP tourism and biodiversity evidence | Tourism can finance conservation and incentivize local stewardship, but growth without management standards may harm biodiversity and fail to deliver tangible local benefits. | ecological opportunity/risk and management requirement | named visitor pressure, ecosystem condition, local benefit, access and accountability |

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** visitor numbers, heritage recognition, destination investment,
  tourism jobs, housing pressure, seasonality, congestion, ecological stress and
  resident sentiment shape the destination.
- **Mechanism:** accommodation ownership, land/rent, transport, water/waste,
  local procurement, wages, taxes, platform economy, access rules, heritage
  interpretation, visitor behavior, seasonality and governance determine who
  gains and who bears costs.
- **Operation:** visitors spend, local people own or access enterprises, workers
  receive decent income, residents retain homes and public space, institutions
  manage carrying capacity, and communities can set or contest tourism rules.
- **Material consequence:** household income, prices, rent, congestion, service
  reliability, cultural labor, privacy, public access, ecosystem health and
  waste/water/energy burdens can move in opposite directions.
- **Meaning:** a heritage site or living practice can be sacred, ordinary,
  ancestral and locally governed while being marketed as an attraction. Visitor
  interpretation can strengthen pride or turn identity into a consumable
  performance.
- **Power:** platforms, hotel chains, developers, governments, heritage bodies,
  investors, tour operators, residents, artisans, workers and customary
  authorities decide access, stories, prices, land use and value capture.

## Bounded finding

> **Visitor growth, heritage branding or sustainable-tourism certification is not
> resident wellbeing or cultural continuity.** A visitor target, destination award,
> restored site, tourism master plan, community-based-tourism program or nature-
> tourism investment does not establish that residents retain affordable housing,
> public access, decent work and local value; that cultural practices remain
> meaningful and governed by communities; or that infrastructure and ecosystems
> can absorb tourism without harm.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

The relevant unit is the destination system, not the attraction. It includes
resident households, renters, workers, artisans, farmers, fishers, informal
vendors, public services, transport, water and waste systems, protected areas,
landlords, visitors and the stories told about place.

Tourism can be genuinely regenerative when communities control interpretation,
ownership, visitor limits, procurement and benefit sharing. But “community-based”
can also become a label applied to externally designed products. A village may
receive visitors while young people cannot afford to live there, residents lose
access to common land, women’s cultural labor is unpaid, or seasonality leaves
workers insecure. The ledger must follow residents’ housing, income, time, voice
and practice—not only arrivals and receipts.

## Counterexamples and limits

- More arrivals and tourism GDP can coexist with rent inflation, displacement,
  congestion, low wages and resident opposition.
- A heritage designation can finance restoration while commodifying practice,
  restricting local access or shifting control to operators and visitors.
- Community-based tourism may create income for some households while excluding
  renters, women, youth, minority groups or poorer residents.
- Conservation tourism can improve protected-area finance while restricting
  customary livelihoods or transferring ecological risk elsewhere.
- Local procurement and job counts do not establish decent work, stable
  year-round income or ownership.
- Visitor caps or booking systems can protect sites but also shift demand,
  increase prices or exclude local cultural use.
- Resident sentiment varies by neighborhood, season, tenure and role; one survey
  or destination average cannot establish legitimacy.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: visitor flow, branding, ownership, jobs,
local procurement, housing, infrastructure, cultural practice, public access,
ecosystem capacity, resident voice, value capture and remedy are separate stages.
Keep visitor-growth-to-resident-wellbeing claims in Tier 2 until a named
destination ledger joins visitors, resident households, housing, work, value,
practice, infrastructure, ecological condition and later acceptance/retention.

## Next test

Select one heritage city, one rural heritage village and one nature/coastal
destination. Record arrivals and seasonality, accommodation ownership, rent and
resident displacement, jobs/wages, local procurement, taxes, water/waste/
transport capacity, cultural practice and access, women/youth/artisan benefit,
ecosystem indicators, visitor behavior, resident sentiment, governance and
grievance at 6/12/24 months. Compare award-recognized and non-recognized places,
and count residents who left or stopped practicing rather than only those who
benefited.

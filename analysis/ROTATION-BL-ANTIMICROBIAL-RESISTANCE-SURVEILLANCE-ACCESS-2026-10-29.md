# Rotation BL — Antimicrobial resistance, surveillance and equitable treatment access

**Date:** 2026-10-29  
**Lane:** antimicrobial resistance / public health / food and animal systems / surveillance / medicines access / cross-border health security  
**Status:** Tier 1 bounded distinction plus Tier 2 surveillance-to-treatment-and-equity question

## Research question

When antimicrobial resistance is measured through human, animal and food-system
surveillance, what converts data and national action plans into correct local
treatment, prevention, affordable access, safer animal production and reduced
cross-border health risk?

## Conversion chain

```text
antimicrobial use, infection and resistance pressure
  -> specimen, laboratory, use and outcome surveillance
  -> local guideline, stewardship, prevention and supply decision
  -> timely affordable diagnosis and effective treatment
  -> recovery, reduced transmission, safer food/animal production and trust
  -> durable health security across borders
```

## Stage audit

- **Signal:** AMR is an expanding global health-security threat with increasingly
  standardized surveillance across human, animal and food systems.
- **Mechanism:** laboratory access, specimen quality, reporting completeness,
  local resistance patterns, diagnostic turnaround, guideline authority,
  medicine supply, prices, prescribing behavior, prevention and cross-border
  coordination determine whether data changes care.
- **Operation:** a patient, farm, food chain or community is sampled; a result is
  reported; a clinician, veterinarian, producer or public-health authority acts;
  medicine, prevention or isolation reaches the relevant person or system.
- **Material consequence:** effective treatment, mortality, hospital duration,
  household cost, food safety, animal productivity, worker exposure and travel or
  trade continuity may change independently.
- **Meaning:** antibiotic use is shaped by trust, uncertainty, care-seeking,
  household affordability, veterinary practice, farming culture and local
  histories of unequal access—not only by technical stewardship rules.
- **Power:** laboratories, ministries, clinicians, pharmaceutical suppliers,
  farms, insurers, hospitals and international bodies decide what is measured,
  treated, affordable and visible across borders.

## Bounded finding

> **AMR surveillance or a national action plan is not effective local treatment
> or equitable health security.** A resistance estimate, dashboard, laboratory
> network, stewardship guideline, medicine target, animal-use reduction or One
> Health commitment does not establish that a named patient or farm received a
> correct affordable intervention, recovered, avoided transmission, retained
> trust or experienced a reduced burden.

## Cultural and geographical interpretation

Resistance is biological but access is geographical. A country can report data
without every rural clinic having diagnostics; a national guideline can be
correct while medicines are unavailable or unaffordable; animal-use reduction
can improve aggregate stewardship while shifting costs to small farmers or
changing food prices. Cross-border health security therefore depends on local
translation: specimen, result, decision, medicine, adherence and outcome.

Surveillance also creates a politics of visibility. High-capacity countries may
appear to have more resistance because they test more, while under-observed
communities can look safer. The atlas must retain reporting completeness,
denominators, care-seeking and missingness rather than rank places as if the
measurements were equally generated.

## Counterexamples and limits

- More surveillance can initially increase the measured burden by revealing
  previously unseen resistance; that is not necessarily deterioration.
- Lower antimicrobial use can reflect better prevention, but it can also reflect
  reduced access or under-treatment; use reduction alone is ambiguous.
- A resistant laboratory result does not establish treatment failure if an
  effective alternative was available and received.
- National action-plan adoption can coordinate resources even before outcome
  changes are observable; policy presence is still not outcome evidence.
- Human, animal and environmental resistance pathways are connected but require
  separate units and sampling; a One Health label does not pool them validly.

## Promotion decision

Promote the distinction to Tier 1: surveillance, completeness, guideline,
diagnosis, medicine access, treatment, recovery, prevention and health security
are separate stages. Keep surveillance-to-treatment and equity claims in Tier 2
until a named place or cohort ledger joins laboratory result, clinical/veterinary
decision, medicine receipt, cost, adherence, outcome and later transmission.

## Next test

Build a matched human-animal-food ledger for one urban referral system, one rural
district and one cross-border food or livestock pathway. Record sampling,
laboratory turnaround, resistance result, guideline, medicine stock/price,
prescriber or farmer action, treatment receipt, adherence, outcome, recurrence,
worker exposure, reporting completeness and community trust. Compare high- and
low-surveillance places without interpreting missing data as low resistance.

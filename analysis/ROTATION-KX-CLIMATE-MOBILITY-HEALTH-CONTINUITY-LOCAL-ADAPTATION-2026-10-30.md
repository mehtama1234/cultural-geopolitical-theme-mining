# Rotation KX — Climate mobility, health continuity and locally grounded adaptation

**Date:** 2026-10-30  
**Research question:** When climate change shapes migration and displacement, can research priority-setting and health-system adaptation preserve continuity of care, cultural safety and agency across journeys, host communities and protracted displacement?

## Why this packet matters

The climate–mobility–health nexus is often split across climate adaptation,
migration governance, humanitarian response and health planning. WHO's 2026
research-prioritization exercise makes the split visible: it identifies social
determinants, universal health coverage, emergencies, data/policy/interventions
and health outcomes as linked priorities, while naming gaps in access, inclusion,
long-term effects and intersectional data. IOM's climate-mobility work adds the
geographical and cultural warning that global projections can miss local realities,
especially for marginalized groups.

```text
climate shock / slow-onset degradation / conflict / livelihood pressure
  -> movement, immobility or protracted displacement
  -> disrupted documents, language, income, housing and health-care continuity
  -> host-system response, local adaptation and community participation
  -> care received, health outcome, social protection, belonging and remedy
```

## Cultural, geographical and geopolitical reading

Climate mobility is not only movement between coordinates. It can separate a
person from a language, healer, family network, livelihood, land, identity
documents and familiar route to care. The same displacement label can conceal
different experiences: a coastal household that moves temporarily, a pastoralist
whose route becomes unsafe, a refugee in protracted urban settlement, an
internally displaced person without recognized status, or a migrant worker whose
health coverage stops at a border.

The geography of responsibility is distributed. A source region may carry loss
of land and livelihood; a transit region may carry emergency care and detention
risk; a destination region may carry housing, school and primary-care pressure;
and a diaspora may carry remittances and informal care. National policy inclusion
can therefore coexist with exclusion at the clinic, pharmacy, border checkpoint,
registration desk or language encounter.

The geopolitical signal is a move from emergency-only response toward portable,
migrant-inclusive and climate-resilient systems. But portability can also expose
status, increase surveillance or make people legible to exclusion. Data systems
must distinguish continuity of care from continuity of control.

## Counterexample and source boundary

A global research agenda, policy inclusion statistic, climate projection,
stakeholder consultation, emergency plan, health-system framework or community
participation claim does not prove that a named displaced person received safe,
affordable and culturally appropriate care across a journey. Movement is not
always caused by climate; not all climate-affected people move; mobility can be
protective, harmful, voluntary, forced or temporary; and host communities can
experience both benefit and pressure. Data exclusions and differing definitions
make cross-country comparisons uncertain.

## Promotion decision

**Tier 1 deepened finding:** Climate mobility and health are a coupled systems
problem with clear research, access, cultural and governance gaps; local and
community action is necessary to prevent global priorities from becoming detached
from lived journeys.

**Tier 2 mechanism candidate:** **climate-mobility-to-portable-culturally-safe-health-continuity-and-local-adaptation**. The next acquisition should follow one person, household or community from climate exposure or mobility status through documents, language, housing, work, registration, facility encounter, referral, payment, treatment, mental-health support and later outcome, with a stable-host, non-migrant or differently exposed comparator.

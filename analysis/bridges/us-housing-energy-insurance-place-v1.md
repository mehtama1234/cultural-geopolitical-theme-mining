# Bridge 4: housing, energy, and insurance to health, mobility, and place

**Status:** cross-source synthesis, not a causal estimate  
**Checked:** 2026-09-12

## Question

When the cost or condition of a home changes, how does the pressure move through energy, insurance, health, credit, work, mobility, and the ability to stay in a place?

## Evidence carried by the existing records

| Step | Existing evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy burden | [An energy bill can take food, medicine, or safe temperature first](../findings/us-energy-household-burden-matched-evidence-001.md) records food or medicine sacrifice, notices, unhealthy temperatures, and equipment limits. | Energy cost can become a basic-needs and health problem. | Which household made which cut or recovered afterward. |
| Recovery time | [A shutoff rule changes the time a household has to recover](../findings/us-utility-shutoff-reconnection-rules-matched-evidence-001.md) separates notice, payment plan, shutoff, reconnection, and repeat debt. | Rules change the time and cost available before service is lost. | Which rule produces durable recovery across states and households. |
| Building intervention | [Efficiency savings are real but smaller than the promise](../findings/us-weatherization-health-household-outcomes-matched-evidence-001.md) finds a gap between modeled savings and actual savings and separates use, temperature, safety, and health. | A lower modeled or measured bill is not automatically a safer or healthier home. | Whether a particular retrofit expanded household room or merely reduced use. |
| Place risk | [Insurance can turn a place risk into a staying problem](../findings/us-home-insurance-matched-evidence-001.md) links risk to premiums, coverage, credit pricing, home value, repair, and financing. | Place risk can become a housing and asset constraint. | That a particular family moved, defaulted, or became uninsured because of insurance. |
| Mobility context | The [weighted 2022 NHTS transport comparison](../projects/us-household-calendar-integration/nhts-transport-comparison-v1.md) finds zero-vehicle households and rideshare exposure are more common in urban than rural records, while rural work trips show more pickup use. | Place changes the set of usable routes to work and services. | It does not connect housing cost, energy burden, insurance, fare, missed work, or recovery for the same household. |

## Working chain

```text
home condition, energy price, disaster risk, insurance rule, or housing cost
  -> bill, coverage, safety, repair, or service access changes
  -> household cuts use, borrows, delays repair, accepts risk, seeks aid, stays, or moves
  -> landlord, insurer, utility, lender, agency, or local government controls a remedy
  -> health, work, care, credit, home value, and next-month room change
  -> local inequality, public spending, trust, and place-based political demand may change
```

The strongest supported point is that housing costs are a stack of conditions and risks, not only rent or mortgage. The unresolved point is household recovery: service restored, coverage renewed, or work completed does not prove that debt, health, safety, or mobility returned to its prior position.

The NHTS comparison supplies a measured mobility layer for this broader bridge. It shows that “can reach work or care” is partly a place and vehicle-access question. It must remain a context or comparison until it is joined to housing, energy, insurance, cost, and outcome records.

## Five housing outcomes that must stay separate

1. **Affordability:** can the household pay the recurring bill?
2. **Protection:** is insurance or service available and sufficient when needed?
3. **Condition:** is the home safe, usable, and repairable?
4. **Mobility:** can the household stay, move, sell, or reach work and care?
5. **Recovery:** after the shock, did cash room, health, debt, and access improve?

A household can remain housed but be underinsured, unsafe, indebted, or unable to move. A lower energy bill can reflect efficiency or rationing. A service restoration can preserve access while increasing the balance.

## First comparison to run

Compare similar households or properties across risk, tenure, and control:

1. renter versus owner, including who pays and who can repair;
2. high- versus low-energy-burden homes with similar income;
3. private insurance, public coverage, and no coverage across comparable risk zones;
4. notice and payment-plan rules with different deadlines and assistance timing;
5. bill aid, repair, weatherization, allowance changes, and relocation support by the home’s first failure.

Record bill, premium, deductible, coverage, arrears, notice, shutoff, equipment, temperature, repair, landlord or insurer action, food and medicine tradeoff, health, missed work, credit, sale, move, and next-home access. Keep ZIP-year, housing-unit, household, and individual records separate unless a valid linkage exists.

## What would weaken the bridge

The working bridge would weaken if energy or insurance costs did not alter usable household room after accounting for income and alternatives; if shutoff protections reliably prevented both service loss and repeat debt; if weatherization’s actual savings and safety outcomes matched or exceeded modeled claims across home types; or if place risk did not change coverage, financing, repair, mobility, or public burden.

## Next end-to-end artifact

The next version should follow one place-based intervention or shock through the bill or risk, household response, controlling institution, health/work/housing result, and distribution of public or private cost. The result must distinguish staying from secure staying, service restoration from recovery, and lower use from safe comfort.

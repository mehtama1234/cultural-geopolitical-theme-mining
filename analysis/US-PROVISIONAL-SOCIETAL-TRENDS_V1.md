# US provisional societal trend register v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-12  
**Purpose:** synthesize the current source layers into broad, testable trends  
**Scope:** US households, consumers, workers, firms, places, institutions,
political culture, infrastructure, and cross-border state power

This register is the current broad picture, not a claim that every trend is
rising at the same time or happening to the same people. The evidence comes
from different units and survey frames. A trend is included because multiple
source layers make a recurring pattern visible; its missing join is stated
beside it.

## The current provisional trends

| # | Provisional trend | Evidence currently supports | It does not yet establish | Next test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **People preserve immediate functioning by moving costs elsewhere.** | SHED records substitution, reduced use, saving cuts, borrowing, delayed purchases, and extra work; USDA shows the basic-security endpoint in food. | That one household makes all of these adjustments or that a particular price caused one sacrifice. | Follow the same people across price, food, debt, health, and recovery. |
| 2 | **Time is an unequal form of money.** | ATUS shows paid work, household production, care, multiple-job work, workplace location, and reduced socializing distributed differently across groups. | Which employer, price, or policy created a particular schedule or whether time losses compound with cash losses. | Link time diaries to work transitions, travel, care need, service effort, and income. |
| 3 | **Access is increasingly judged by the ability to get a remedy.** | CFPB records complaints, explanations, and non-monetary responses that vary by product; platform and consumer findings separate first answers from resolution. | That a recorded response solved the problem, restored trust, or prevented exit. | Follow the same case from first contact through remedy, repeat effort, abandonment, and switching. |
| 4 | **Basic security is a distributional outcome, not just a market outcome.** | USDA identifies low versus very low food security, child/adult shielding, poverty and subgroup differences, and assistance participation. The SIPP SNAP layers place current-month receipt beside food security, work, race, child presence, and resource bands, then add adjacent-month receipt states, recorded start/end reasons, and resource/job changes around transitions. | Whether assistance fully restores security or whether food hardship is driven by one current price trend; exits sometimes coincide with lower measured resources, but the adjacent comparison does not identify notice, effort, or causation. | Align benefits, earnings, prices, local access, diet, health, and administrative burden; use SNAP spell starts, ends, interruptions, and reasons in a longitudinal test. |
| 5 | **Political judgment is made from material conditions through identity and information.** | ANES associates financial worry with national economic judgment, trust, institutional meaning, party identity, and reported vote; Pew identifies distinct civic participation types and partisan source ecosystems. | That one bill or source caused a belief, vote, or withdrawal. | Use a longitudinal respondent design measuring exposure, attribution, source, interpretation, action, and vote separately. |
| 6 | **Civic participation is segmented, not one simple scale.** | Pew’s Mobilizers, Connectors, Spectators, and Outsiders combine voting, volunteering, contacting officials, religion, news attention, and discussion in different ways. | Permanent social types, a universal decline in participation, or a single platform effect. | Re-measure the groups over time and compare offline ties, resources, source use, and institutional trust. |
| 7 | **Local economic rootedness is weakening while business entry remains staged.** | HBS reports a declining hometown advantage for entrepreneurs; Census and linked administrative research distinguish applications from employer-firm formation. | That local services, jobs, belonging, or political voice declined in the same places. | Match applications to lasting firms, establishments, jobs, services, rents, prices, and local civic measures. |
| 8 | **Firm and public success can coexist with transferred household or community risk.** | Payment, insurance, utility, platform, aid, and complaint findings repeatedly separate the organization’s headline outcome from the recipient’s later security or effort. | A single general mechanism or the total size of transferred risk. | Measure who pays, waits, loses access, or carries future risk after each organizational decision. |
| 9 | **Technology expands capability and dependence together.** | World Bank, IEA, and the Romania/Malaysia cases connect AI to local complements, providers, capital, chips, electricity, data, and portability. | That infrastructure investment creates local skills, public control, jobs, or geopolitical leverage. | Compare ownership, local learning, energy/water costs, interoperability, service quality, and exit options. |
| 10 | **The central scarce resource is optionality.** | Across SHED, SIPP, ATUS, CFPB, housing, work, food, business, and AI layers, security repeatedly depends on the ability to delay, switch, appeal, move, refuse, or absorb a shock. The SIPP race × resource layer now shows that material pressure and work status are distributed differently across intersecting resource and race groups, with replicate-weight uncertainty and documented field universes. | That optionality is one measurable latent factor, that it is uniformly declining, or that the SIPP intersections explain the mechanisms behind the differences. | Build separate indicators for cash, time, alternatives, rights, social help, and institutional voice; test their joint distribution and follow the strongest differences into remedy, recovery, trust, and action. |
| 11 | **A cost becomes political through a chain of market power and interpretation.** | Tariff, monetary-policy, household-adaptation, inflation-belief, ANES, and real-wage/election layers separate direct price, delayed domestic markup, effective household cost, adaptation, named cause, source, trust, and political outcome. | That any particular tariff, firm, rate, price, belief, or adaptation caused an individual vote or that domestic capacity automatically creates geopolitical independence. | Follow one exposed product or policy through firm pricing and sourcing, household basket and tradeoffs, attribution and information, action, and later capacity or policy response. |
| 12 | **Digital capability and dependency grow together.** | World Bank, IEA, BIS, IMF, OFR, Romania, and Malaysia layers connect AI/cloud adoption to power, capital, skills, data, ownership, public procurement, local benefits, and provider dependence. | That announced investment creates durable local capability, fair distribution, public control, or geopolitical leverage. | Compare operational outcomes, ownership, energy/water burden, local learning, service quality, portability, provider switching, and state bargaining. |
| 13 | **Care costs are redistributed through work, family time, and other needs.** | Federal Reserve care-skipping data, hospital-admission research, Medicaid and medical-debt studies, dependent-coverage/job-lock evidence, caregiving/work accounts, ATUS, and USDA food-security measures separate bills, care, work, unpaid time, credit, food, and family outcomes. | That one health event caused every later hardship or that financial protection restored health, time, and job freedom equally. | Follow a dated care event across price, coverage, delay, paid/unpaid labor, work, debt, food/housing tradeoffs, health, dignity, and later trust. |
| 14 | **Place risk changes the ability to stay, move, work, and participate.** | Treasury/FIO, FEMA/NFIP, ACS, FHFA, HMDA, energy/utility, NHTS, and local-business layers separate protection, affordability, repair, transport, business entry, services, and local attachment. | That a particular premium, hazard, bill, business change, or transport gap caused a move, public burden, or loss of belonging. | Follow one place-based event across household and firm records, including coverage, repair, transport, services, work, health, move/stay, local trust, and political demand. |
| 15 | **Optionality is unequally distributed and cannot be reduced to income.** | SIPP intersectional layers, SHED, ATUS, USDA, NHTS, CFPB, and safety-net records show different combinations of cash, time, family support, transport, institutional access, food security, and exit options. | That optionality is one declining latent factor or that any demographic label alone explains the mechanism. | Measure cash, time, alternatives, rights, social support, institutional voice, and practical exit separately, then follow their joint effects in same-unit designs. |
| 16 | **Consumer participation can create trust and dependence at the same time.** | Fraud, privacy, reviews, discrimination, habit, older-consumer, and recourse layers separate use, sharing, visibility, help-seeking, complaint, recovery, trust, status, and exit. | That continued use means satisfaction, that trust means informed control, or that a platform or firm caused a consumer’s identity or behavior. | Follow one customer through entry, data or purchase choice, failure or benefit, remedy, later trust, and exit; include non-users and non-reporters. |

The cross-source [trust, meaning, and action layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/cross-source-trust-action-layer-v1.md)
now makes the broad interpretation explicit: material and institutional
conditions do not become societal or political trends automatically. They are
filtered through attribution, identity, information, trust, and available
alternatives, then expressed through consumer, civic, and political action.
This is a cross-source arrow map, not a same-person join; the next depth test
is longitudinal measurement of exposure, interpretation, action, remedy, and
later security or legitimacy.

## The broad causal shape to test

```text
condition, price, rule, technology, or shock
  -> money, time, access, control, status, health, or security changes
  -> people, firms, and institutions adapt, delay, borrow, switch, stay, organize, or go without
  -> the next actor responds and redistributes cost, risk, data, assets, or power
  -> culture, trust, identity, public demand, political action, firm capacity, or state leverage changes
```

The register does not claim that every arrow is observed. It is a guide for
choosing the next source and for refusing to treat an attractive narrative as a
completed explanation.

## What cuts across the trends

1. **Headline outcomes hide the adjustment.** Employment, housing, service
   access, food purchases, firm formation, and AI adoption can look stable while
   time, quality, autonomy, safety, or future room deteriorates.
2. **The same response can mean opposite things.** Borrowing can be resilience
   or a trap; staying can be preference or constraint; automation can assist or
   intensify control; aid can restore choice or restrict it to one need.
3. **Power appears at the next decision.** The important actor is whoever can
   set the price, rule, schedule, data use, remedy, ownership, or exit terms.
4. **Distribution is part of the trend.** Income, race, age, gender, disability,
   family structure, migration, geography, firm size, and provider access change
   both exposure and available alternatives.
5. **Cultural and political effects require their own evidence.** Hardship does
   not automatically become distrust, identity, participation, or a vote.

## What would change the broad picture

The working picture would weaken if repeated population and institutional data
showed that adjustments rarely displace other needs; remedy and exit were common
after measured service failures; local business entry translated reliably into
lasting local capacity; technology adoption consistently reduced dependence; or
material conditions did not vary with interpretation and action after identity,
information, and alternatives were measured.

## Relationship to the program

This register summarizes, but does not replace, the [14-theme inventory](US-BROAD-THEME-INVENTORY_V1.md),

# US provisional societal trend register v1

**Checked:** 2026-09-17
**Purpose:** synthesize the current source layers into broad, testable trends  
**Scope:** US households, consumers, workers, firms, places, institutions,
political culture, infrastructure, and cross-border state power

This register is the current broad picture, not a claim that every trend is
rising at the same time or happening to the same people. The evidence comes
from different units and survey frames. A trend is included because multiple
source layers make a recurring pattern visible; its missing join is stated
beside it.

The companion [counterexample register](US-BROAD-COUNTEREXAMPLE-REGISTER_V1.md)
defines the reversal, boundary, or alternative-protection test for each theme.
Unless a row names measured counterevidence, its counterexample remains a
required next test rather than an established result.

**Latest care/work conditioning:** the [SIPP care/work tenure × poverty layer](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/sipp-care-work-tenure-poverty-layer-v1.md)
adds a many-person, month-level screen showing that paid care, help paying, and
reported work constraint are different distributions. Renter records show
higher reported work constraint than owner/buyer records across the displayed
income bands, while paid-care incidence rises at higher income and help is more
concentrated in lower-income bands. This is a conditional-universe diagnostic,
not evidence that tenure or income caused a work constraint.

**Latest sector-flow conditioning:** the [BFS–BDS sector divergence layer](projects/us-local-business-place/bfs-bds-sector-divergence-layer-v1.md)
shows that application volume, realized establishment flow, and net-job
intensity diverge across the full 19-sector national file. Retail leads on
applications but not net-job intensity; health care/social assistance and
accommodation/food are much higher on the latter measure; administrative/
support combines positive entry-minus-exit with negative net jobs. This
strengthens the provisional claim that business activity has different social
meanings by sector, while leaving local access, quality, ownership, and worker
power unmeasured.

**Latest state conditioning:** the [BFS–BDS state archetype layer](projects/us-local-business-place/bfs-bds-state-archetype-layer-v1.md)
groups 51 matched states/DC records by application intensity. Higher-intensity
groups have higher median establishment entry, exit, and net-job rates, but the
top group contains high-churn places and different job outcomes; low-
application/high-net-job states remain counterexamples. This strengthens the
place-conditioned trend while leaving local service access, ownership, job
quality, and political meaning open.

**Latest public-system conditioning:** the [SIPP stable-state child-care
bridge](records/us-sipp-snap-stable-state-childcare-2024.json) adds a same-person
November-to-December SNAP status comparison to annual fall child-care fields.
Continued receipt coexists with higher reported child-care assistance and work
prevention but lower paid child care than stable nonreceipt. The comparison is
composition-sensitive, and sparse entry/exit cells remain open. The related
[child-care time-loss finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-018.md)
adds 7.43% versus 3.42% reported work prevention among stable SNAP versus
stable non-SNAP respondents, with exact Fay-BRR reproduction documented. The
[following-hardship finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-019.md)
adds higher descriptive rent/utility hardship in work-prevention cells, while
keeping annual fall care fields separate from the adjacent-month SNAP bridge.

**Latest mixed-safety-net conditioning:** the [WBNS charitable-food access
layer](projects/us-safety-net-access/wbns-charitable-food-access-layer-v1.md)
adds a 2019–2025 participation series and 2025 access, treatment, and unmet-need
measures. Working-age charitable-food participation remained elevated at 16.7%
in 2025, while 28.8% of adults in food-insecure households reported unmet
charitable-food need; hours, awareness, comfort, variety, transportation, safety,
and perceived treatment form overlapping route constraints. This strengthens the
mixed public/private safety-net arrow without treating charitable receipt as
adequacy, provider capacity, trust, or political action.

**Latest consumer-recourse conditioning:** the [CFPB case-route sample](projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/cfpb-case-route-sample-2024-v1.md)
shows that the first institutional handoff and visibility fields differ across
financial products. It is a capped retrieval-order diagnostic, not a remedy
rate; the same-case effort, outcome adequacy, trust, and exit arrow remains open.

**Latest platform/attention conditioning:** the [Pew news, platform, and
civic-engagement record](records/us-pew-news-platform-civic-engagement-2025.json)
adds four descriptive engagement types, partisan news-source ecosystems, and
social-media influencer exposure. These are separate attention, trust, and
action measures—not evidence that a platform caused a belief or vote.

**Latest macro/geopolitical conditioning:** the [IMF April 2026 WEO layer](projects/ai-work-control/imf-weo-geopolitical-fiscal-shock-2026-layer-v1.md)
places conflict and defense mobilization alongside growth, inflation, debt,
deficits, and social-spending trade-offs. It adds conditional global and US
projections to the state-consequence map, but does not identify household
incidence, legitimacy, unrest, or alliance behavior.

**Latest household-room conditioning:** the [Federal Reserve income-volatility
and support layer](projects/us-cost-trust-politics/federal-reserve-income-volatility-support-2025-layer-v1.md)
adds income timing, outside support, public assistance, month-end margin, and
the distinct adaptation menu around price pressure. These distributions deepen
the household room trend without establishing a same-person dated event or
recovery path.

**Latest automatic-saving conditioning:** the [Auto-IRA balance-sheet layer](projects/us-auto-ira-household-balance-sheets/auto-ira-balance-sheet-layer-v1.md)
adds a policy-exposure result in which retirement ownership/assets and
checking/savings ownership/balances rise alongside a modest increase in
credit-card debt. The new [retirement-timing layer](projects/us-auto-ira-household-balance-sheets/auto-ira-retirement-claiming-layer-v1.md)
reports later work and Social Security claiming in a separate SIPP policy-timing
comparison. The [cross-paper reconciliation](projects/us-auto-ira-household-balance-sheets/auto-ira-cross-paper-reconciliation-v1.md)
keeps these designs and balance-sheet definitions separate: institutional design
may move current liquidity, future assets, and life-course timing together, but
the apparent checking, debt, and retirement-asset disagreement is not a pooled
net-security result. Debt cost, take-home pay, health, job quality, choice versus
constraint, and net security remain open.

**Latest same-person material/work conditioning:** the [SIPP monthly transition
finding](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-021.md)
reruns the 2024-reference-year person-month backbone and shows that explicitly
monthly income-to-poverty bands and job-count categories move on different
clocks. It keeps annual/reference-period hardship fields out of the monthly
event interpretation and does not claim a volatility ranking, causal work
effect, or household-weighted estimate.

The companion [SNAP transition-context finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/findings/us-safety-net-access-007.md)
adds Fay–BRR uncertainty around the public-system state itself: resource ratios
often decline around entry and exit, while job counts are usually unchanged in
the smaller valid context universe. This is not a SNAP-effect or “exit to work”
estimate; it is a timing and measurement safeguard for the same-person bridge.

The new [three-month SNAP following-context finding](projects/us-safety-net-access/findings/us-safety-net-access-009.md)
extends that safeguard one month forward: household resources often continue
to decline after entry or exit while job counts and hours are usually unchanged,
and person earnings move in both directions. It remains a selected descriptive
sequence until notice, effort, benefit timing, remedy, and recovery are observed
in the same episode.

The [work/care/health bridge](projects/us-household-calendar-integration/findings/us-household-calendar-integration-027.md)
now makes the next measurement boundary explicit: SIPP monthly work/resource
movement, ATUS care-time allocation, and MEPS health-cost/coverage change each
measure a distinct currency of household room. Their alignment strengthens the
architecture of the material/time/care trend, but they are not the same-person
chain; dated care or health onset, schedule control, treatment continuity,
trade-offs, recovery, and later meaning/action remain open.

 ## The broad causal shape to test

```text
condition, price, rule, technology, or shock
  -> money, time, access, control, status, health, or security changes
  -> people, firms, and institutions adapt, delay, borrow, switch, stay, organize, or go without
  -> the next actor responds and redistributes cost, risk, data, assets, or power
  -> culture, trust, identity, public demand, political action, firm capacity, or state leverage changes
```

The register does not claim that every arrow is observed. It is a guide for
choosing the next source and for refusing to treat an attractive narrative as a
completed explanation.

## What cuts across the trends

1. **Headline outcomes hide the adjustment.** Employment, housing, service
   access, food purchases, firm formation, and AI adoption can look stable while
   time, quality, autonomy, safety, or future room deteriorates.
2. **The same response can mean opposite things.** Borrowing can be resilience
   or a trap; staying can be preference or constraint; automation can assist or
   intensify control; aid can restore choice or restrict it to one need.
3. **Power appears at the next decision.** The important actor is whoever can
   set the price, rule, schedule, data use, remedy, ownership, or exit terms.
4. **Distribution is part of the trend.** Income, race, age, gender, disability,
   family structure, migration, geography, firm size, and provider access change
   both exposure and available alternatives.
5. **Cultural and political effects require their own evidence.** Hardship does
   not automatically become distrust, identity, participation, or a vote.

## What would change the broad picture

The working picture would weaken if repeated population and institutional data
showed that adjustments rarely displace other needs; remedy and exit were common
after measured service failures; local business entry translated reliably into
lasting local capacity; technology adoption consistently reduced dependence; or
material conditions did not vary with interpretation and action after identity,
information, and alternatives were measured.

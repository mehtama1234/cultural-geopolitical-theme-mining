# The broader goal: a living map of American change

**Status:** governing program statement  
**Scope:** the full US-centered Cultural, Social, and Geopolitical Theme
Mining program  
**Working rule:** preserve breadth; deepen one defensible link at a time

## The goal

Build a living, source-traceable map of the forces changing American life and
American power.

We are looking for recurring societal, cultural, political, consumer,
financial, institutional, firm, labor, infrastructure, technological, and
geopolitical trends—not isolated headlines and not a single stress score. The
map should explain what is changing, who experiences it, who has alternatives,
who absorbs the residual risk, who controls the next decision, and what the
change may do to behavior, trust, legitimacy, markets, public capacity, and
state or geopolitical room to act.

## The end-to-end question

For each important trend, follow the longest defensible path:

```text
condition, shock, decision, or capability
  -> exposure and incidence
  -> change in money, time, access, health, status, control, or security
  -> practical alternatives and room to maneuver
  -> adaptation by people, consumers, workers, firms, and institutions
  -> redistribution of cost, risk, ownership, information, waiting, and power
  -> trust, identity, cultural meaning, legitimacy, or social behavior
  -> political judgment, collective action, switching, exit, or non-use
  -> firm/sector capacity, public capacity, dependence, state leverage,
     alliance position, or geopolitical consequence
```

This is a research path, not a claim that every trend follows the same causal
chain. Every link must be labeled as measured, reported, estimated,
experimentally identified, longitudinally associated, compared, inferred
across sources, or still open.

## The 14-theme breadth requirement

Keep the atlas broad across:

1. household room, prices, debt, and financial security;
2. time, care, family life, and social reproduction;
3. consumer power, recourse, and service dependence;
4. platforms, data, attention, and digital agency;
5. work, control, bargaining, and labor-market change;
6. health, disability, aging, and unequal care access;
7. housing, place, mobility, and insurance;
8. unequal exposure, status, race, gender, age, and belonging;
9. trust, identity, cultural meaning, and changing norms;
10. public systems, administrative access, and policy feedback;
11. political judgment, civic participation, and collective action;
12. firm, market, ownership, and concentration power;
13. infrastructure, energy, technology, and dependency; and
14. geopolitical and state consequences.

No deep lane—health costs, household constraints, AI/work, consumer recourse,
housing, finance, migration, public systems, or defense/infrastructure—defines
the whole program. Each is an evidence lane in the broader map.

## What each research pass must deliver

Choose one bounded question and record its unit, time window, geography,
denominator, universe, weights, missingness, source vintage, mechanism,
counterexample, uncertainty, and stopping rule. Then produce a traceable route:

```text
source -> measured claim -> method and limit -> mechanism
      -> affected groups and alternatives -> response and redistribution
      -> meaning, trust, or action -> wider capacity or power consequence
      -> counterevidence -> next decisive test
```

Separate sources may form a useful layered bridge, but different respondents,
households, complaints, workers, firms, places, and countries must never be
written as one synthetic causal episode. Missing identifiers, incompatible
clocks, inaccessible files, and open arrows are findings about the boundary
of knowledge and must remain visible.

## The product we are building

The output is a searchable trend atlas that lets a reader move from a broad
theme to recurring evidence, affected groups and places, available choices,
institutional and firm responses, cultural or political meaning, state or
geopolitical implications, counterexamples, and the next test.

The atlas should help answer three plain questions:

1. What changed, and how do we know?
2. What did it make possible or impossible for different people and
   organizations?
3. What changed in behavior, meaning, institutions, firms, political action,
   or state capacity—and which link remains unproven?

Success means the explanation becomes more durable, comparable, and honest
over time. It does not mean every arrow is closed or that the program has been
reduced to one dataset, one population, or one grand theory.

## Current operating focus

Rotate depth across the 14 themes while prioritizing the open middle between
material exposure and consequence:

```text
dated event or condition
  -> actual alternative and decision route
  -> money/time/behavioral adaptation
  -> protected or sacrificed outcome
  -> remedy, persistence, recovery, meaning, or action
```

Use existing local artifacts first and keep acquisition storage-aware. The next
pass should strengthen one bridge that can change the atlas while preserving
the broader scope and explicitly stating what remains open. The current bridge
status is tracked in the [material-to-action status ledger](analysis/US-BROAD-MATERIAL-TO-ACTION-STATUS_V1.md).

## Verified resumption checkpoint — 2026-09-17

The publication gate currently passes all 20 checks: 310 canonical records,
1,103 observations, 103 source packets, 14 themes, 644 published HTML pages,
and 1,269 Markdown files are synchronized and locally linked. The broad
same-case episode audit confirms that the atlas has substantial exposure,
adaptation, institutional-response, and selected follow-up evidence, but no
retained source closes the complete sequence from dated exposure and usable
alternatives through verified remedy, meaning/action, recovery, and exit.

The current cross-domain proposition is conditional rather than universal:
institutional visibility and response are easier to observe than the affected
person’s final condition. A complaint can close without a repair, a recall can
advance without purchaser restoration, a firm can recover without proving
community recovery, and an AI productivity or time effect can occur without
worker control. The [counterexample register](analysis/US-BROAD-COUNTEREXAMPLE-REGISTER_V1.md)
and [practical-exit synthesis](analysis/projects/us-customer-automation-recourse/practical-exit-cross-domain-synthesis-v1.md)
now make that boundary explicit.

The active execution rule is therefore: use existing local artifacts first,
make no bulk download, and advance one lawful same-unit or valid matched arrow
at a time. The immediate test is to identify a compact episode source with a
stable key that can observe actor, alternatives, effort or trade-off,
protected/sacrificed outcome, remedy receipt, and later meaning, action,
recovery, or exit. If a candidate lacks those fields, record the acquisition
boundary and rotate to the next theme rather than promote a cross-source story
into a causal result.

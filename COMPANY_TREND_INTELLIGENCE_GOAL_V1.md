# Company trend-intelligence goal v1

**Program:** Cultural, Social, and Geopolitical Theme Mining
**Audience:** Companies, strategy teams, investors, risk leaders, product
leaders, and public-affairs teams
**Status:** Proposed governing goal

## The goal

Build and maintain a living, source-traceable trend-intelligence system that
helps companies understand what is changing beneath visible events, why it is
changing, who is affected, how the change may spread across markets and
institutions, and what the company should monitor, prepare for, or do next.

The system should identify emerging and structural change across society,
culture, consumers, finance, technology, work, institutions, infrastructure,
and geopolitics. It should test important signals against multiple independent
sources, explain the mechanisms and groups affected, show how costs, risks,
power, trust, and opportunity are redistributed, and translate the result into
business exposure, scenarios, leading indicators, and decision options.

This is not a news feed, a pile of summaries, a universal stress index, or a
false prediction engine. It is an evidence-based early-warning and strategic-
interpretation system.

## The plain-language promise

Help a company see important change early, understand it correctly, and make
better decisions before the change becomes obvious.

## The end-to-end intelligence chain

```text
public signal or source
  -> measured observation
  -> repeated or contested trend candidate
  -> mechanism and drivers
  -> affected people, markets, sectors, and places
  -> behavior, financial, cultural, institutional, or geopolitical consequence
  -> company exposure, opportunity, and vulnerability
  -> scenarios and leading indicators
  -> decision options and next evidence test
```

For each important trend, the system should answer:

1. What is changing, and how strong is the evidence?
2. Is the change temporary, cyclical, emerging, structural, reversing, or
   contested?
3. Who is affected first, and how does exposure vary by place, income,
   generation, identity, sector, or institution?
4. What behavior, cost, constraint, or opportunity is changing?
5. Which firms, public bodies, communities, or geopolitical actors are
   responding?
6. How might trust, identity, culture, legitimacy, or political judgment
   change?
7. What does this mean for the company’s customers, workforce, products,
   operations, supply chain, reputation, capital, and regulatory position?
8. What should the company monitor next, and what evidence would change the
   conclusion?

## The 14-theme coverage

The atlas should maintain breadth across:

1. household room, prices, debt, and financial security;
2. time, care, family life, and social reproduction;
3. consumer power, recourse, and service dependence;
4. platforms, data, attention, and digital agency;
5. work, control, bargaining, and labor-market change;
6. health, disability, aging, and unequal care access;
7. housing, place, mobility, and insurance;
8. unequal exposure, status, race, gender, age, and belonging;
9. trust, identity, cultural meaning, and changing norms;
10. public systems, administrative access, and policy feedback;
11. political judgment, civic participation, and collective action;
12. firm, market, ownership, and concentration power;
13. infrastructure, energy, technology, and dependency; and
14. geopolitical and state consequences.

No single deep project defines the program. AI and work, language and
cultural vitality, health costs, housing, consumer recourse, public systems,
infrastructure, finance, and geopolitics are evidence lanes inside the wider
map.

## The business translation

Every mature trend record should translate evidence into the following company
questions:

- **Customer:** Who changes behavior, demand, willingness to pay, or trust?
- **Workforce:** What changes in skills, control, bargaining, retention, or
  employee meaning?
- **Product:** Which needs, features, services, or access barriers change?
- **Finance:** What changes in affordability, margins, capital, insurance,
  credit, or valuation?
- **Operations:** What changes in suppliers, infrastructure, data, energy,
  logistics, or resilience?
- **Institutional:** What changes in regulation, procurement, standards,
  enforcement, or public capacity?
- **Reputation:** Which identities, communities, values, or trust relationships
  affect legitimacy and market access?
- **Geopolitics:** What changes in trade, sanctions, alliances, dependence,
  national capability, or state leverage?

The output must distinguish between a plausible implication and a supported
business effect. A scenario is not a result, and a result is not a forecast.

## Source and evidence architecture

Use the source family that can observe each stage:

- surveys and household records for lived experience, attitudes, and behavior;
- administrative, complaint, court, and enforcement records for institutional
  action and remedy;
- company filings, market data, and firm records for strategy, exposure,
  concentration, and operating capacity;
- infrastructure, energy, housing, health, and place records for capacity,
  access, reliability, and local incidence;
- cultural, community, and language sources for identity, meaning, trust,
  legitimacy, and governance;
- research papers and evaluations for mechanisms, designs, and causal limits;
- trade, defense, sanctions, procurement, alliance, and security sources for
  state power, dependence, and geopolitical consequence;
- public discussion for early signals and lived interpretation, never as a
  representative survey by itself.

Each record must preserve the source, date, geography, unit, denominator,
method, revision status, access limits, evidence status, counterevidence, and
next test. Separate datasets must not be written as if they followed the same
person, household, firm, place, or event unless the identifiers and timing
support that claim.

## Trend-identification workflow

```text
signal
  -> candidate
  -> repeated-signal and counter-signal check
  -> independent research and official-data check
  -> mechanism and distribution analysis
  -> business exposure translation
  -> confidence and horizon rating
  -> scenario and leading-indicator design
  -> monitored finding or open question
```

Classify each candidate as an emerging signal, repeated pattern, confirmed
trend, structural shift, reversing trend, or contested finding. Record the time
horizon, geographic scope, affected groups, confidence, and missing evidence.

The system should favor earlier detection, clearer uncertainty, and better
preparation over dramatic claims. It should explicitly state what would prove
the interpretation wrong.

## Standard output for a company reader

Each published trend should provide:

1. a one-sentence finding;
2. the strongest direct evidence;
3. the mechanism in plain language;
4. who gains room and who loses room;
5. cultural, financial, institutional, and geopolitical interpretation;
6. company exposures and possible opportunities;
7. three horizons: now, next, and longer term;
8. leading indicators to monitor;
9. counterexamples and limitations; and
10. the smallest next test or decision-relevant action.

## What success means

Success is a maintained, searchable, source-traceable atlas that lets a
company move from a broad theme to a measured observation, affected groups,
mechanism, available alternatives, institutional or firm response,
redistribution of cost and power, cultural or political meaning, geopolitical
consequence, and next decision.

The program is successful when it helps a company:

- notice meaningful change before it is obvious;
- distinguish noise from a repeatable pattern;
- understand who is affected and why;
- identify hidden costs, dependencies, and loss of alternatives;
- prepare products, operations, workforce, and policy responses;
- avoid overreacting to weak or promotional evidence; and
- keep learning as new data, events, and counterexamples arrive.

## Relationship to the existing work

The existing 14-theme atlas remains the research foundation. The cultural-
language and Indigenous-language work is a deep evidence lane within trust,
identity, cultural meaning, technology, public systems, accessibility, place,
and legitimacy. Financial, consumer, workforce, infrastructure, and
geopolitical work should use the same evidence and handoff discipline.

The company-facing layer does not replace the research goal. It translates the
research into decision relevance while keeping observation, inference,
uncertainty, and open questions visible.
